Legal Matters in Türkiye for Turkish Citizens Living Abroad

+90 258 241 55 54 · info@capar.av.tr · Contact form ↓

Short answer: A person living abroad can have their court, enforcement and land registry matters in Türkiye handled by a lawyer under a power of attorney drawn up at the Turkish consulate in their country (Notaries Act art. 191, art. 192) or by a local notary with an apostille or consular certification (HMK art. 224). The power of attorney is filed in the court or enforcement file (HMK art. 76), and in matters handled through a lawyer, documents are served on the lawyer (Notification Act art. 11). Settlement, waiver, accepting the other side's claim and applying to mediation require express authority in the power of attorney (HMK art. 74); for the land registry, it must authorise the specific transaction (Land Registry Regulation art. 18(4)). In an uncontested divorce the judge hears the spouses in person (TMK art. 166(3)), so attendance may be needed. Av. Many legal matters of Turkish citizens living abroad — property, inheritance, family, lease, debt enforcement and criminal cases in Türkiye — can largely be handled without travelling to Türkiye, through a power of attorney given to a lawyer; some steps may still require the person to be present. This page summarises how each matter works under Turkish law, with the statutory provisions it rests on. Av. Bayram Can Çapar handles these matters for Turkish expats and members of the diaspora, acting under a power of attorney.

Power of attorney from abroad: Turkish consulate, foreign notary and apostille

In foreign countries, notarial acts are performed by Turkish consulates under the provisions of the Turkish Notaries Act on notarial acts (Notaries Act art. 191, art. 192). A power of attorney drawn up at the Turkish consulate general or consulate in your country of residence can therefore be filed in a Turkish court or enforcement file in the same way as one drawn up by a notary in Türkiye (HMK art. 76).

The power of attorney can also be drawn up by a notary in your country of residence. A document issued by foreign state authorities has the character of an official document in Türkiye only if it is certified by the competent authority of the issuing state or by the relevant Turkish consulate; the provisions of international conventions to which Türkiye is a party are reserved (HMK art. 224). In countries party to the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents, this certification is the apostille issued by that country's competent authority; in other countries, the signature and seal of the foreign authority are certified by the Turkish consulate (Notaries Act art. 195).

In practice, a power of attorney drawn up in a foreign language must be translated into Turkish before it is used in Türkiye, and the translation is usually made by a sworn translator and certified by a notary. A power of attorney drawn up in Turkish at a consulate needs no translation.

The powers given to the lawyer must be stated expressly in the power of attorney. Without express authority, the representative cannot settle, amend the entire claim, resort to alternative dispute resolution, waive the claim or a legal remedy, accept the opposing party's claim, lift an attachment, or — unless the document specifies which ones — bring proceedings concerning strictly personal rights (HMK art. 74). For matters such as a divorce action or an application to mediation, the authority must therefore be written into the power of attorney separately.

If a land registry transaction is to be carried out through a representative, the land registry office requires a power of attorney drawn up under the Notaries Act showing that the representative is authorised to carry out the requested transaction, and a power of attorney for a land registry transaction must bear the principal's signature (Land Registry Regulation art. 18(4)). The transaction to be carried out — sale, purchase, mortgage or gift — is therefore stated expressly. A power of attorney drawn up at a Turkish consulate is made under the Notaries Act and so meets this requirement (Notaries Act art. 192).

Inheritance: certificate of inheritance, disclaimer and division

Succession is governed by the national law of the deceased, but Turkish law applies to immovable property located in Türkiye (MÖHUK art. 20(1)). Who inherits a property in Türkiye, and in what shares, is therefore determined under Turkish law wherever the heirs live.

A certificate of inheritance (veraset ilamı) is issued by a civil court of peace or by a notary (TMK art. 598). Where issuing it requires a court hearing, the civil registry records are insufficient or the certificate is requested by foreign nationals, a notary cannot issue it and the application goes to the civil court of peace (Notaries Act art. 71/B). An heir who does not hold Turkish citizenship therefore applies to the court.

An inheritance may be disclaimed within three months; for statutory heirs the period runs from the date they learned of the death, unless they prove that they learned only later that they were heirs (TMK art. 606). The disclaimer is made by an oral or written, unconditional declaration to a civil court of peace (TMK art. 609). It can also be made through a lawyer; in practice, the power of attorney must expressly authorise the lawyer to disclaim the inheritance.

Each heir may at any time request the division of the estate unless bound by contract or law to continue the joint ownership; failing agreement, the civil court of peace is asked to divide the assets in kind or, where that is not possible, by sale (TMK art. 642). Before such a case is filed, applying to a mediator is a mandatory precondition (HUAK art. 18/B(1)(b)).

Land registry: sale, partition of co-owned property, annulment of title

A contract transferring ownership of immovable property is valid only if made in official form (TMK art. 706). If the seller or buyer lives abroad, the transaction at the land registry office can be carried out by a representative whose power of attorney covers that transaction (Land Registry Regulation art. 18(4)).

If co-owners of a property held in shares cannot agree on how to divide it, the judge, at the request of one co-owner, orders division in kind or, where that is not appropriate, sale by public auction (TMK art. 699). Partition actions are heard by the civil court of peace (HMK art. 4(1)(b)), and mediation must be tried first (HUAK art. 18/B(1)(b)). Co-owners living abroad do not prevent the action; documents are served on them under the provisions of the Notification Act on service abroad (Notification Act art. 25, art. 25/a).

If a right in rem has been registered without legal basis, the person whose right is harmed may sue for correction of the land register; rights in rem acquired in good faith by third parties relying on the entry, and claims for compensation, are reserved (TMK art. 1025). A person who learns while abroad that their property was transferred through misuse of a power of attorney or a forged document can bring an action for annulment of title and re-registration through a lawyer.

Recognition of divorce and other foreign judgments in Türkiye

A foreign civil judgment that has become final under the law of the state where it was given can be enforced in Türkiye only after a Turkish court grants exequatur (tenfiz) (MÖHUK art. 50). For it to count as conclusive evidence or res judicata, recognition (tanıma) is needed; reciprocity is not required for recognition (MÖHUK art. 58(1)). The same procedure applies where an administrative act in Türkiye is to be based on a foreign judgment (MÖHUK art. 58(3)).

The court grants exequatur on the following conditions (MÖHUK art. 54):

  • There must be reciprocity between Türkiye and the state where the judgment was given; this condition does not apply to recognition.
  • The judgment must not concern a matter within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Turkish courts.
  • The judgment must not be manifestly contrary to public policy.
  • The person against whom it is sought must not have objected before the Turkish court that they were not duly summoned or represented, or that judgment was given in their absence contrary to the applicable law.

The application must be accompanied by the original judgment duly certified by the authorities of that country, or a certified copy, with a certified translation, and by a certified document showing that the judgment is final, with its certified translation (MÖHUK art. 53).

The competent court is the civil court of first instance (MÖHUK art. 51(1)); recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments in family matters such as divorce fall to the family court (Law No. 4787 art. 4(2)). The action is brought where the person against whom recognition or enforcement is sought is domiciled in Türkiye or, failing that, resides; if that person has neither domicile nor residence in Türkiye, it may be brought before a court in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir (MÖHUK art. 51(2)). The case can be heard in a particular province, for example Denizli, only if that province's court has jurisdiction under this rule.

For a divorce granted abroad there is also a route without court proceedings: a divorce decision given by a foreign judicial or administrative authority is registered in the Turkish civil registry if both parties apply together, in person or through representatives, the decision was given by the competent authority of that state and has become final, and it is not manifestly contrary to Turkish public policy; if one party has died or is a foreign national, the Turkish party or their representative may apply alone (Population Services Act art. 27/A(1)). Registration is carried out abroad by the Turkish mission in the country where the decision was given and, in Türkiye, by civil registry offices designated by the Ministry (Population Services Act art. 27/A(2)). If registration is refused because these conditions are not met, recognition is sought from the court (Population Services Act art. 27/A(3)).

Where Turkish citizens living abroad have not brought, or cannot bring, a personal-status case such as divorce before a foreign court, the case is heard in Türkiye by the court with territorial jurisdiction or, failing that, the court where the person resides, or — if they do not reside in Türkiye — the court of their last domicile in Türkiye, and failing that a court in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir (MÖHUK art. 41). In divorce cases, the competent court is that of either spouse's domicile or of the place where the spouses lived together for the last six months before the action (TMK art. 168).

Leases and eviction

Disputes over rent, eviction and rent arrears for a home or business premises let in Türkiye are heard by the civil court of peace, except for eviction through enforcement proceedings without a judgment (HMK art. 4(1)(a)). Applying to a mediator before filing suit is a mandatory precondition in these disputes (HUAK art. 18/B(1)(a)); even where a party is represented by a lawyer, the mediator also informs the party in person (HUAK art. 18/A(7)).

For renewed lease periods, rent may not rise by more than the twelve-month average change in the consumer price index for the previous lease year; for leases longer than five years or renewed after five years, the judge sets the rent for the new lease year fairly, also taking comparable rents into account (TBK art. 344).

A landlord who needs to use the premises as a home or workplace for themselves, their spouse, descendants, ascendants or dependants may end the lease by an action brought within one month from the date set by law (TBK art. 350(1)). A landlord living abroad who plans to return to Türkiye may rely on their own housing need on this ground; whether the need exists is assessed by the court on the facts.

A tenant's failure to vacate on a date undertaken in writing, or having caused two justified written warnings for unpaid rent, are further grounds for eviction that must be pursued within the one-month periods set by law (TBK art. 352(1), art. 352(2)).

Debt enforcement and objection to a payment order

A creditor living abroad can start enforcement proceedings against a debtor in Türkiye through a lawyer, who files the power of attorney in the enforcement file (HMK art. 76).

In ordinary enforcement without a judgment, the debtor must object to the enforcement office within seven days of service of the payment order; the objection may also be lodged with an enforcement office other than the one conducting the proceedings (İİK art. 62(1)). The debtor or their representative must give an address in Türkiye with the objection; if no new address in Türkiye is notified and none can be found, service at the address given in the enforcement request is deemed made to the debtor (İİK art. 62(3)).

A payment order is served on a debtor abroad under the provisions of the Notification Act on service abroad, and the objection period runs from the date of service (İİK art. 62(1); Notification Act art. 25, art. 25/a).

Criminal cases

A suspect or accused may have the assistance of one or more defence lawyers at every stage of the investigation and prosecution (CMK art. 149(1)); a person living abroad can appoint a lawyer as defence counsel in a criminal case in Türkiye.

Except where the law provides otherwise, no hearing is held against an accused who is not present (CMK art. 193(1)). If the evidence collected shows that a decision other than a conviction, a decision not to impose a penalty or a security measure should be given, the case may be concluded in the accused's absence even without questioning (CMK art. 193(2)). Where the offence carries only a judicial fine or confiscation, alone or together, the hearing may proceed even if the accused does not attend (CMK art. 195).

If an accused who is abroad would find it difficult to attend on the scheduled date, they may be questioned at a hearing opened earlier or through letters rogatory (istinabe) (CMK art. 196(6)). Once questioned, the accused — or defence counsel authorised for this purpose — may ask to be exempted from attending the hearings (CMK art. 196(1)). Questioning and participation by simultaneous audio-video link are provided for in the Code for accused persons located in Türkiye (CMK art. 196(4)).

An accused who is abroad and cannot be brought before the competent court, or whose appearance is not considered appropriate, is deemed absent (gaip); no hearing is opened against them, the court takes the steps needed to obtain or preserve evidence, and defence counsel may attend those steps (CMK art. 244).

Service of documents abroad and time limits

Service in a foreign country is effected through that country's competent authority; where a treaty or that country's law allows, the Turkish mission there asks the competent authority to effect service (Notification Act art. 25).

If the addressee is a Turkish citizen, service may also be effected through the Turkish embassy or consulate. The person is then sent a notice stating the subject of the document and the issuing authority and warning that service will be deemed effected if they do not apply within thirty days; if they do not apply to the mission within thirty days of receiving the notice, service is deemed effected at the end of the thirtieth day (Notification Act art. 25/a). Because time limits for actions and objections may start from that date, the date of the notice is worth recording.

Where service by public notice is required for a person living abroad, the documents and the notice are also sent to their known address abroad by registered letter with return receipt (Notification Act art. 28).

In matters handled through a lawyer, service is made on the lawyer; the rules on serving criminal judgments on the accused are reserved (Notification Act art. 11). In mandatory mediation, limitation periods are suspended and time limits for forfeiture do not run from the application to the mediation office until the final report is drawn up (HUAK art. 18/A(15)).

Following the case remotely through UYAP

UYAP (the National Judicial Network Information System) is the IT system set up to deliver judicial services electronically; using a secure electronic signature, actions can be filed, fees and advances paid and case files inspected electronically (HMK art. 445(1), art. 445(2)). For acts performed electronically, a time limit ends at the end of the day (HMK art. 445(4)).

The lawyer follows the file on UYAP and files petitions and documents electronically with a secure electronic signature (HMK art. 445(2)); a client abroad learns of hearings, expert reports and decisions through the lawyer.

At a party's request, the court may allow that party or their representative to attend the hearing and take procedural steps from where they are by simultaneous audio-video transmission (HMK art. 149(1)). This is an option, and whether it is used depends on the court's decision.

When might you need to travel to Türkiye?

In practice, once a power of attorney has been given, most of the work proceeds through the lawyer. Whether you need to be present depends on the matter:

  • Uncontested divorce: the judge must hear the parties in person and be satisfied that they expressed their will freely (TMK art. 166(3)), so the spouses may need to attend the hearing. Participation by audio-video link depends on the court's decision (HMK art. 149(1)).
  • Criminal proceedings: as a rule, no hearing is held against an absent accused (CMK art. 193(1)); for an accused abroad, questioning at an earlier hearing or through letters rogatory is available (CMK art. 196(6)).
  • Power of attorney: it is drawn up at the Turkish consulate in your country (Notaries Act art. 191) or by a local notary with an apostille or consular certification (HMK art. 224); you do not need to travel to Türkiye for this.
  • Land registry transactions: sales and transfers can be carried out by a representative whose power of attorney covers them (Land Registry Regulation art. 18(4)).
  • Registering a foreign divorce: the application can also be made to the Turkish mission in the country where the decision was given (Population Services Act art. 27/A(2)).
  • Disclaiming an inheritance: the declaration may also be made in writing to the civil court of peace (TMK art. 609) and can be made through a lawyer authorised for it in the power of attorney.

Frequently Asked Questions

I live abroad. Do I need to travel to Türkiye for a property, inheritance or court matter there?

In most matters, no. With a power of attorney drawn up at the Turkish consulate in your country (Notaries Act art. 191) or by a local notary with an apostille or consular certification (HMK art. 224), your lawyer can handle court, enforcement and land registry matters. In an uncontested divorce the judge must hear the parties in person (TMK art. 166(3)), and in criminal proceedings the accused's presence is the rule (CMK art. 193(1)), so attendance may be needed in those cases.

How do I give a power of attorney to a lawyer in Türkiye from abroad?

There are two routes: have it drawn up at a Turkish consulate general or consulate under the Notaries Act (Notaries Act art. 191, art. 192), or have it drawn up by a notary in your country and certified by that country's competent authority (with an apostille in countries party to the Hague Apostille Convention) or by the Turkish consulate (HMK art. 224; Notaries Act art. 195). A power of attorney in a foreign language also needs a Turkish translation.

Is a power of attorney made by a foreign notary valid in Türkiye?

A document issued by foreign state authorities counts as an official document in Türkiye if it is certified by the competent authority of the issuing state or by the Turkish consulate, subject to the international conventions to which Türkiye is a party (HMK art. 224); in Apostille Convention countries the certification is the apostille. For land registry transactions a power of attorney drawn up under the Notaries Act is required (Land Registry Regulation art. 18(4)), which a consular power of attorney meets directly.

What should a power of attorney for selling property in Türkiye say?

The land registry office requires a power of attorney showing that the representative is authorised to carry out the requested transaction and bearing the principal's signature (Land Registry Regulation art. 18(4)). The transaction — sale, purchase, mortgage or gift — is therefore stated expressly.

How can an heir living abroad disclaim an inheritance, and what is the time limit?

An inheritance may be disclaimed within three months, which for statutory heirs generally runs from the date they learned of the death (TMK art. 606). The disclaimer is an oral or written, unconditional declaration to a civil court of peace (TMK art. 609). It can also be made through a lawyer whose power of attorney expressly authorises it.

How do I obtain a certificate of inheritance (veraset ilamı) from abroad?

It is issued by a civil court of peace or a notary (TMK art. 598); where a court hearing is needed, the civil registry records are insufficient or the certificate is requested by foreign nationals, a notary cannot issue it and the application goes to the civil court of peace (Notaries Act art. 71/B). The application can be made through a lawyer.

Is my foreign divorce automatically valid in Türkiye?

No. A foreign judgment needs recognition to count as res judicata in Türkiye and exequatur to be enforced (MÖHUK art. 50, art. 58). Recognition of foreign judgments in family matters falls to the family court (Law No. 4787 art. 4(2)). If the conditions are met, a foreign divorce can also be registered in the civil registry without a court case (Population Services Act art. 27/A).

How can I register a foreign divorce in the Turkish civil registry without going to court?

Both parties must apply together, in person or through representatives; the decision must have been given by the competent authority of that state and be final, and must not be manifestly contrary to Turkish public policy. If one party has died or is a foreign national, the Turkish party may apply alone. Registration is done by the Turkish mission in the country where the decision was given or, in Türkiye, by a civil registry office designated by the Ministry (Population Services Act art. 27/A). If registration is refused, a recognition action is filed.

I live abroad. Where can I file for divorce in Türkiye?

The competent court in divorce cases is that of either spouse's domicile or of the place where the spouses lived together for the last six months (TMK art. 168). For personal-status cases of Turkish citizens, if no Turkish court has territorial jurisdiction, the case is heard where the person resides or — if they do not reside in Türkiye — at their last domicile in Türkiye, and failing that in Ankara, Istanbul or Izmir (MÖHUK art. 41).

Can I evict my tenant in Türkiye while living abroad?

If one of the statutory grounds exists, the action can be brought through a lawyer: your own housing need (TBK art. 350), a written undertaking to vacate or two justified warnings (TBK art. 352) are among them. The civil court of peace hears the case (HMK art. 4(1)(a)) and mediation must be tried first (HUAK art. 18/B(1)(a)); for the lawyer to apply to mediation, the power of attorney must expressly authorise it (HMK art. 74).

How are court documents served at my address abroad, and when do time limits start?

Service is made through the competent authority of your country of residence (Notification Act art. 25) or, for Turkish citizens, through the Turkish embassy or consulate. On the consular route, if you do not apply to the mission within thirty days of receiving the notice, service is deemed effected at the end of the thirtieth day (Notification Act art. 25/a). In matters handled by a lawyer, service is made on the lawyer (Notification Act art. 11).

There is a criminal case against me in Türkiye and I am abroad. What happens?

As a rule, no hearing is held against an absent accused (CMK art. 193(1)). An accused abroad may be questioned at a hearing opened earlier or through letters rogatory (CMK art. 196(6)) and, once questioned, may be exempted from attending (CMK art. 196(1)). An accused who cannot be brought before the court is deemed absent and no hearing is opened (CMK art. 244). Defence counsel can assist at every stage (CMK art. 149).

How can I follow my case from abroad?

Your lawyer follows the file on UYAP, where actions can be filed, fees paid and files inspected (HMK art. 445). At a party's request, the court may allow the party or their representative to attend the hearing by audio-video link (HMK art. 149(1)).
