Higher Education Law

Higher education law attorney Bayram Can Çapar: disciplinary investigations of students and academic staff; acts of the Council of Higher Education and universities.

This page explains Turkish law. It is an English version of the Turkish page; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In higher education, students and academic staff may face disciplinary investigations, examination and credit-transfer decisions and various administrative measures. Reviewing these acts in terms of procedure and substance is of great importance.

The office represents clients in disciplinary proceedings concerning students and academic staff, objections to acts of the Council of Higher Education (YÖK) and universities, and equivalence and credit-transfer disputes.

Associate professorship (doçentlik)

Higher Education LawAssoc. Prof. Appointment Unlawful Over Co-Author Juror: Turkish Council of State on Joint Publications7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers of the Council of State finally held, by majority, that an associate professor appointment was unlawful where it relied on the reports of a jury including a professor with several joint articles and papers with the appointee, and was made as if there were a single candidate although an expert had found that both candidates met the minimum conditions.Higher Education LawResidence Rule Upheld for Assoc. Prof. Language Exams Abroad: Turkish Council of State on the 2022 Guide7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State found the provision of the 2022 March Term Associate Professorship Application Guide requiring associate professor candidates who sit a foreign language exam abroad to document at least six months’ residence in the exam country up to and including the exam date, not contrary to the public interest and the requirements of the service, and also held lawful the cancellation of the application of a candidate who did not submit a residence certificate. The decision was given at first instance and unanimously; since an appeal is open, it may not be final.Higher Education LawExcluding Associate Degree Courses Is Unlawful: Turkish Council of State on 2023 Associate Professor Criteria4 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State annulled the omission of associate degree courses from the 2023 associate professorship application requirements and upheld the other contested rules. The decision is subject to appeal in cassation.Higher Education LawAssociate Professorship in Türkiye: Application Requirements and Annulment Action Against a Negative Result4 November 2026Under Turkish law, an application for associate professorship requires a doctorate, at least 55 points in a central foreign language examination and original publications set for each field; the Interuniversity Board's jury assesses the file through detailed and reasoned reports. An annulment action against a negative result is brought against the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board within sixty days before the administrative courts of Ankara; Higher Education Law No. 2547 art. 24, the Associate Professorship Regulation and Council of State decisions.

Appointment of faculty members and vacancy announcements

Higher Education LawAppointing a Markedly Lower Scorer Is Unlawful: Turkish Council of State on Assistant Professor Rankings7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State, holding unlawful the appointment of a candidate with a markedly lower score to an assistant professor post by departing from the objective ranking in the expert report, quashed the regional administrative court’s dismissal finally and by majority; as the top-ranked candidate had not sued, the Chamber held that the administration must annul the appointment and re-advertise the post.Higher Education LawSplit Experts in Professor Case Need New Panel: Turkish Council of State on Conflicting Expert Reports7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State stated that, in a professor appointment where both candidates met the advertised requirements, the administration must compare them on objective and verifiable criteria of merit and scientific competence. As the expert panel was divided on which candidate was superior, it held that the dispute must be resolved with the report of a new panel of different persons and quashed the regional administrative court’s dismissal finally and unanimously.Higher Education LawAnnulling Faculty Appointments in Türkiye: Appointment and Promotion Criteria, Non-Appointment, Non-Renewal7 November 2026Under Turkish law, annulment actions against non-appointment to assistant professor, associate professor and professor posts and against non-reappointment: criteria under Law No. 2547 and the Regulation, YÖK-approved institutional criteria, limits of discretion, time limits and Council of State decisions.Higher Education LawAnnulling Tailor-Made Academic Job Postings in Türkiye: University Vacancies Drafted for One Candidate25 October 2026Annulment actions against academic job postings tailored to one candidate: Constitution, Law No. 2547, regulations, time limits, standing, Council of State rulings.

Terms of office; research assistant and lecturer posts

Higher Education LawResearch Assistant Requirements, 50/d and 33/a Posts, Term9 November 2026Under Turkish law, applying for a research assistant post requires at least 70 in ALES, at least 50 points in a central foreign language examination, not having reached the age of 35 on 1 January of the year of the entrance examination and, at state universities, postgraduate studentship; candidates go through a pre-assessment and a written entrance examination. Appointments are made under Article 50/d of Law No. 2547 using the procedure in Article 33; a 50/d post depends on postgraduate education, while a 33/a appointment lasts at most three years; the Central Examination Regulation and Council of State decisions.Higher Education LawResearch Assistant Non-Renewal Needs Concrete Reasons: Turkish Council of State Requires Proportionate Grounds7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State held, by majority and as a final decision, that not renewing a research assistant's contract requires concrete and proportionate reasons, that academic staff at foundation universities enjoy the same professional security as those at state universities, and that a new ground raised during the proceedings does not on its own save the act.Higher Education LawYÖK Paragraph Barring Court-Backed Appointments Annulled: Turkish Council of State on YLSY (Law No. 1416)7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers of the Council of State upheld, on the same reasoning, the annulment of the paragraph of a YÖK general letter stating that those deemed unsuccessful under Law No. 1416 could not be appointed to teaching staff posts even by court decisions, as contrary to the administration’s duty to comply with judicial decisions. The Assembly also accepted that the scholarship holder whose ties with the university were severed had standing to challenge the whole letter, quashed the partial dismissal for lack of standing and sent the file back to the 8th Chamber for that part.Higher Education LawRector Holds Power Not to Extend Assistant Professor Terms: Turkish Council of State on Vice-Rector Decision7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State, in a final and unanimous decision quashing a regional administrative court judgment that had rejected the appeal, held that an act not extending an assistant professor's term, signed by a vice-rector, was unlawful for lack of authority because Article 23 of Law No. 2547 gives that power to the rector and contains no express provision for its delegation; as the merits were not examined, lost financial rights could not be granted directly at this stage.Higher Education LawDoctoral Withdrawal Ends a 50/d Research Assistant Post: Turkish Council of State; 33/a Request Is Separate7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State held that a post under Article 50/d of Law No. 2547 is support limited to the duration of postgraduate education and that ties with the post must be severed when that education ends for any reason, finding lawful the severance of a research assistant who had the doctoral registration deleted at their own request; it left the 33/a request made around the same time outside the case. The Chamber quashed, finally and by majority, the regional administrative court decision rejecting the appeal, and the file was sent back to that court for a new decision.Higher Education LawNo Regulatory Gap for Staff Already Over the Norm Cadre: Turkish Council of State on University Staff7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers of the Council of State upheld the 8th Chamber’s decision rejecting the claim of incomplete regulation, because provisional Article 1 of the norm cadre regulation provides that teaching staff in excess of the norm cadre when it entered into force, and those appointed to posts advertised before then, continue to serve in their posts. The Chamber had also found lawful planning up to twice the minimum cadre and the maximum ratio for the same title; the ratio provision was amended both before and after the decision.Higher Education LawLecturer Term Non-Extension Held Lawful on Combined Grounds: Turkish Council of State on Three Facts7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State held lawful the non-extension of a lecturer’s term of office, assessing together that the precondition in Article 31 of Law No. 2547 had ceased to exist with the later appointment of an assistant professor to the field taught, that the action against a disciplinary penalty had been dismissed and that only 6 hours of the 12-hour teaching load had been fulfilled. The Chamber quashed, finally and by majority, the regional administrative court decision rejecting the appeal, and the file was sent back to that court.Higher Education LawNo Age-35 Limit on Research Assistant Reappointment: Turkish Council of State on Article 33 of Law No. 25474 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State unanimously and finally held that the age-35 condition in Article 33 of Law No. 2547 applies only to the first application for a research assistant post, not to the reappointment of a research assistant who met it on taking up the post.

Discipline: academics and students

Higher Education LawArt. 54 Annulment Alone Does Not Lift Student Penalties: Turkish Council of State, Review Under Law No. 74377 November 2026Under Turkish law, the Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers of the Council of State did not regard the Constitutional Court’s annulment of Article 54 of Law No. 2547 as sufficient on its own to annul a student disciplinary penalty imposed under the former Regulation, and required a review on the merits, under the provision re-enacted by Law No. 7437 before the annulment took effect, of the proof of the act, whether it warrants the penalty and the more favourable provision. The Assembly quashed, finally and by majority, the regional administrative court’s decision of insistence that had annulled the penalty solely because of the annulment decision, and the file was sent back to that court for a new decision.Higher Education LawMissing Rules on Deleting Student Penalties Unlawful: Turkish Council of State on Disciplinary Records7 November 2026Under Turkish law, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State, ruling at first instance and by majority, held that the absence of a clear rule on keeping higher education students’ disciplinary penalties on record and deleting them after certain conditions and a certain period breaches legal security, legal certainty and proportionality, and annulled the implied refusal of the application asking the Council of Higher Education to regulate the matter and the dean’s act based on it.Higher Education LawDisciplinary Penalties for Student Cheating: Unlawfulness and Annulment under Turkish Law17 July 2026Unlawfulness and annulment of disciplinary penalties imposed for a student cheating in an exam: the disciplinary treatment of cheating (Higher Education Law No. 2547, art. 54 — reprimand for attempted cheating, one-semester suspension for cheating or enabling cheating, two-semester suspension for cheating by threat; the provision that replaced the Student Discipline Regulation repealed in 2023), the right of defence and investigation procedure (art. 54/5-6), proof of cheating by concrete evidence and expert report, proportionality and the problem of a separate penalty per course, the criminal-disciplinary relationship (art. 54/6-ğ), objection, the annulment action, the sixty-day time limit (Administrative Procedure Code art. 7) and the appeal route (art. 46, including the amendment by Law No. 7589), with decisions from files upheld by the 8th Chamber of the Council of State.Higher Education LawDisciplinary Penalties on Academics and Annulment Actions2 July 2026A guide to disciplinary penalties on academics in Türkiye and annulment actions: the special regime of Law No. 2547 and its differences from the civil-servant regime; penalty types (art. 53/b); the legality principle shaped by Constitutional Court annulments; investigation procedure, statement-taking and the right of defence (art. 53/A, as amended by Law No. 7592); limitation (art. 53/C); mandatory ethics-board and expert examination in plagiarism cases; the 60-day period, regional appeal and cassation only for removal penalties (APL art. 46) - with verified Council of State (8th Chamber) decisions in the university cases.

Frequently Asked Questions

I received a student disciplinary penalty. What can I do?

It is examined whether the investigation was conducted in accordance with the procedure; the penalty can be reviewed through an administrative objection and an action for annulment brought in time.

Where can a university's decision be challenged?

Depending on the nature of the act, an administrative objection and the administrative courts are available. As the periods are short, the date of notification matters.

Can I challenge a refusal of equivalence or credit transfer?

Yes, an administrative action is available against unlawful refusals.
