This page explains Turkish law. It is an English version of the Turkish page; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

Contract law deals with the rights and obligations that parties create by their own will. Its main source is the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 (Türk Borçlar Kanunu); in commercial matters the provisions of the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 (Türk Ticaret Kanunu) also apply.

This page brings together articles on the formation and validity of contracts, performance and default, adaptation for excessive hardship, penalty clauses and limitation, and on types of contract such as mandate, contracts for work, suretyship, brokerage and financial leasing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a contract need to be in writing to be valid?

As a rule, contracts are not subject to any form; where the law prescribes a form (for example for suretyship and the sale of immovable property), a contract made without complying with that form is not valid (Law No. 6098, Art. 12).

Can a contract be adapted if circumstances change unexpectedly?

If an extraordinary situation, unforeseeable when the contract was made and not caused by the debtor, has excessively upset the balance to the debtor's detriment, the debtor may ask the judge to adapt the contract; if adaptation is not possible, the debtor may withdraw from the contract or, in contracts for continuing performance, terminate it (Law No. 6098, Art. 138).

When does a contractual claim become time-barred?

Unless the law provides otherwise, every claim is subject to a ten-year limitation period (Law No. 6098, Art. 146); a five-year period applies to certain claims such as rent, interest and other periodic payments (Art. 147).
