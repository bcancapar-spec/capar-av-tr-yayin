Turkish Condominium Management: Owners' Board, Manager, Management Plan, Service Charges, Annulling Decisions

Short answer: Under Turkish law, a building subject to condominium ownership is managed by the board of condominium owners. Each condominium owner has a single vote regardless of the size of their land share, and the board meets with more than half of the owners by both number and land share and decides by majority vote (Condominium Law art. 27, Condominium Law art. 30, Condominium Law art. 31). Where there are eight or more independent units, appointing a manager is mandatory; the manager is appointed every year by a majority of the owners by both number and land share, and if the owners cannot appoint one, the civil court of peace appoints a manager on the application of an owner (Condominium Law art. 34). The management plan may be amended with the votes of four fifths of all condominium owners (Condominium Law art. 28). Unless otherwise agreed, owners contribute equally to the costs of the doorkeeper, heating operator, gardener and watchman and to other common expenses in proportion to their land shares; an owner who is late in paying owes late-payment compensation of five per cent per month (Condominium Law art. 20). Board decisions may be challenged by an annulment action in the civil court of peace within the one-month and six-month time limits, after first applying to mediation; no time limit applies to decisions that are non-existent or absolutely null (Condominium Law art. 33).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Condominium management under the Turkish Condominium Ownership Law (arts. 27-41): the board of condominium owners, voting rights and proxies, notice of meetings and quorums, amending the management plan by four fifths and its limits, the election, court appointment, duties, accounting and agent-like liability of the manager, common expenses (service charges), the operating plan as amended by Law No. 7579 and late-payment compensation of five per cent per month, the one-month and six-month time limits for annulling board decisions, non-existence and absolute nullity, mandatory mediation and the competent civil court of peace, with recent decisions of the 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation.

A guide to the management of condominiums under Turkish law: the board of condominium owners and voting rights (Condominium Law art. 27, Condominium Law art. 31), notice of meetings and quorums (Condominium Law art. 29, Condominium Law art. 30), the management plan and the four-fifths rule (Condominium Law art. 28), the election of the manager, appointment by the civil court of peace and the manager’s liability as an agent (Condominium Law arts. 34-41), common expenses, the operating plan and late-payment compensation of five per cent per month (Condominium Law art. 20, Condominium Law art. 22, Condominium Law art. 37), the one-month and six-month time limits for annulling board decisions, non-existence and absolute nullity (Condominium Law art. 33), mandatory mediation and the competent civil court of peace; with verified decisions of the Court of Cassation.

The Board of Condominium Owners and Voting Rights

In a building subject to condominium ownership (the main immovable), management is centred on the board of condominium owners: the main immovable is managed by this board, and the manner of management is decided by the board, subject to the mandatory provisions of the law (Condominium Law art. 27). Board decisions are entered in a decision book whose every page is certified with a notary’s seal and are signed by all condominium owners present at the meeting; those voting against sign by stating the reason for their dissent. All condominium owners and their universal and singular successors, as well as the manager and the auditors, are bound to comply with the board’s decisions (Condominium Law art. 32).

Voting rights: Each condominium owner has a single vote regardless of the size of his land share; an owner of more than one independent unit votes separately for each unit, but his votes may not exceed one third of all votes. An owner whom a decision directly concerns may attend the discussion but may not vote. Votes may also be cast through an authorised proxy; no one may be appointed as proxy to cast more than five per cent of the votes, and in immovables with forty or fewer units subject to condominium ownership a person may act as proxy for at most two persons (Condominium Law art. 31).

Even where only condominium servitude has been established, if the building has actually been completed and two thirds of the independent units have actually been put into use, the condominium rules apply to the management of the main immovable although the transition to condominium ownership has not been made (Condominium Law art. 17). The Law’s provisions on the obligations of condominium owners also apply to tenants, holders of a right of residence and others who continuously benefit from an independent unit; those who fail to fulfil these obligations are jointly and severally liable together with the owner (Condominium Law art. 18).

Notice of Meetings, Attendance and Decision Quorums

The board holds an ordinary meeting at least once a year, and in housing estates (toplu yapı) at least once every two years; where an important reason arises, an extraordinary meeting may be convened at the request of the manager, the auditor or one third of the owners, with at least fifteen days’ notice stating the reason for the meeting.

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 29 (Time of meetings and notice) (unofficial translation)

“The board of condominium owners shall meet not less than once a year at the times specified in the management plan or, if no such time is specified, within the first month of each calendar year.… Where an important reason arises, the board of condominium owners may meet at any time upon the request of the manager, the auditor or one third of the condominium owners, by a notice to be signed by all condominium owners or by a registered letter at least fifteen days before the date requested for the meeting, provided that the reason for the meeting is also stated. When the first notice is made, it shall also be specified where and on what date the second meeting will be held if the quorum cannot be reached at the first meeting. The interval between the first and the second meeting may not be less than seven days.”

The attendance quorum is more than half of the owners both in number and in land share; decisions are taken by majority vote. If this quorum is not reached, at the second meeting, held at the latest fifteen days later, the decision quorum is the absolute majority of those attending. Matters for which the Law lays down special majorities are reserved.

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 30 (Quorum) (unofficial translation)

“The board of condominium owners shall convene with more than half of the condominium owners both in number and in land share and shall decide by majority vote. If the first meeting cannot be held because the quorum is not reached, the second meeting shall be held at the latest fifteen days later. At this meeting the decision quorum is the absolute majority of those attending. The provisions laid down separately in this Law on quorums are reserved.”

The main special majorities are: a majority in number and land share for electing the manager and the auditor (Condominium Law art. 34, Condominium Law art. 41); a majority in number and land share for innovations and additions facilitating the use of, or increasing the benefit from, common areas (Condominium Law art. 42); the written consent of four fifths of all owners for an owner to carry out construction, repairs or installations in common areas or to paint the exterior in a different colour (Condominium Law art. 19); four fifths for amending the management plan (Condominium Law art. 28); and unanimity for opening entertainment, food or manufacturing premises in a residential unit, adding a storey, encumbering the main immovable with a right or leasing the exterior walls and roof for advertising (Condominium Law art. 24, Condominium Law art. 44, Condominium Law art. 45). The 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation held that, of the decisions taken at a second meeting, only those on electing the manager and the auditor require a majority in number and land share, while the others can be taken by a majority of those attending; the court should therefore have annulled only those decisions rather than the whole meeting (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/12698, K. 2021/15129).

The Management Plan and Its Amendment

The management plan has the nature of a contract binding all owners and their successors, the manager and the auditors, and it is one of the documents submitted to the land registry when condominium ownership is established (Condominium Law art. 12).

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 28 (Management plan) (unofficial translation)

“The management plan regulates the manner of management, the purpose and manner of use, the fees to be received by the manager and the auditors and other matters relating to management.… The management plan has the nature of a contract binding all condominium owners. In cases not covered by the management plan, disputes arising from the management of the main immovable shall be decided in accordance with this Law and the general provisions. Amendment of the management plan requires the vote of four fifths of all condominium owners. The right of the condominium owners to apply to the court under article 33 is reserved. The management plan and the amendments made to it bind all condominium owners and their universal and singular successors, as well as the manager and the auditors.”

A four-fifths majority cannot change everything. The 5th Civil Chamber stated that owners may at any time apply to the court for the annulment of provisions of the management plan that breach mandatory provisions of the Law, that since the annulment of the plan concerns all owners, all of them must be joined to the proceedings, and that an amendment adopted by four fifths cannot override matters for which the Law requires unanimity (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/5533, K. 2022/11993).

In housing estates a single management plan covering all buildings and areas is drawn up. Following the amendment made by Law No. 7579, the vote of two thirds of the total number of independent units represented by the members of the estate’s representatives board is required to amend that plan, and provisions of management plans contrary to this rule do not apply (Condominium Law art. 70). Law No. 7579 changed only this ratio for housing estates; it did not alter the general four-fifths rule shown in the box above.

The Manager: Election, Appointment, Duties and Liability

Management may be entrusted to a manager chosen from among the owners or from outside, or to a three-member management board; where there are eight or more independent units, appointing a manager is mandatory. A manager appointed by the civil court of peace cannot be replaced by the board for six months, although the court may permit a replacement for just cause.

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 34 (Appointment of the manager) (unofficial translation)

“If the main immovable has eight or more independent units, the appointment of a manager is mandatory.… The manager shall be appointed by a majority of the condominium owners both in number and in land share. The manager is re-appointed every year at the statutory annual meeting of the board of condominium owners; the former manager may be re-appointed. If the condominium owners cannot agree on the management of the main immovable or cannot meet and appoint a manager, a manager shall be appointed to the immovable by the civil court of peace of the place where the immovable is located, upon the application of one of the condominium owners and, if possible, after the others have also been heard. This manager has exactly the powers of a manager appointed by the condominium owners and is liable to the condominium owners.”

In a regional court of appeal decision upheld by the Court of Cassation, the election of a manager made without a majority in number and land share was treated as valid until annulled; since a manager was in office on the date of the action and no annulment action had been brought against the election, the conditions for the court to appoint a manager were held not to be met (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/8850, K. 2026/3482). Where an election is alleged to be irregular, the annulment of the election decision should therefore be sought first.

Duties and liability: Unless the management plan provides otherwise, the manager implements board decisions, takes the measures needed to protect, maintain and repair the main immovable, insures it, collects advances until the operating plan is approved, brings actions and enforcement proceedings against owners who fail to perform their obligations and has the statutory mortgage registered, keeps the funds collected in a bank account opened in his capacity as manager and convenes the board (Condominium Law art. 35). He must also record board decisions and all expenses in the decision book, keep the supporting documents and have the book closed by a notary within one month after the end of each calendar year (Condominium Law art. 36). The manager is liable to the owners exactly as an agent (Condominium Law art. 38); he must render accounts of income and expenditure to the board at the times stated in the management plan or, failing that, in the first month of each calendar year, and must also show the accounts at other times if half of the owners so request (Condominium Law art. 39). The board supervises the manager continuously and may replace him at any time for just cause; supervision may be entrusted to an auditor or a three-member audit board elected by a majority in number and land share (Condominium Law art. 41).

The manager cannot bring every action on his own initiative: according to the 5th Civil Chamber, actions to prevent interference with common areas and to remove deviations from the approved project are based on ownership and are not among the manager’s statutory duties; a manager who is not an owner may bring such an action only if authorised by the board (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2826, K. 2024/6978).

Common Expenses (Service Charges), the Operating Plan and Late-Payment Compensation

Unless otherwise agreed, owners contribute equally to the expenses of the doorkeeper, boiler operator, gardener and watchman and in proportion to their land shares to other common expenses; claiming not to use a common area is no ground for refusing to pay. Only an owner who does not wish to benefit from very costly or luxurious innovations and additions is not obliged to contribute to their cost (Condominium Law art. 43).

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 20 (Contribution to the general expenses of the main immovable) (unofficial translation)

“Unless otherwise agreed among them, each condominium owner is obliged to contribute: a) equally to the expenses of the doorkeeper, boiler operator, gardener and watchman and to the advances to be collected for them; b) in proportion to his own land share to the insurance premiums of the main immovable, to the maintenance, protection, strengthening and repair expenses of all common areas, to other expenses such as the manager’s salary, to the operating expenses of common facilities and to the advances to be collected for such expenses;… c) Condominium owners may not avoid paying this share of expenses and advances by waiving their right to use the common areas or facilities or by claiming that, owing to the situation of their own independent unit, there is no need to benefit from them. An action may be brought or enforcement proceedings initiated against a condominium owner who does not pay his share of expenses or advances by each of the other condominium owners or by the manager, in accordance with the management plan, this Law and the general provisions. A condominium owner who does not pay the full share of expenses and advances is obliged to pay late-payment compensation calculated at five per cent per month for the days of delay.”

The 20th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation noted that the rate of late-payment compensation was ten per cent before the amendment made by Law No. 5711 and determined its starting point as follows: if the owner attended the board decision on which the common expense is based, the date of the decision; if not, the date on which the decision or operating plan was notified to him or on which he otherwise learned of the debt. The decision concerned an action for annulment of an objection brought after an objection to enforcement proceedings for a common-expense claim (20th Civil Chamber, E. 2019/3030, K. 2019/5878).

Liability of tenants and the statutory mortgage: Persons continuously using an independent unit under a lease, a right of residence or for another reason are jointly and severally liable with the owner for his share of expenses and advances and for late-payment compensation; however, a tenant’s liability is limited to the rent he owes, and payments he makes are deducted from the rent. If the debt still cannot be collected, a statutory mortgage is registered on the defaulting owner’s unit in favour of the other owners for the debt established by the court; these claims rank in priority (Condominium Law art. 22).

The operating plan and the 2026 amendment: Law No. 7579, in force since 22.05.2026, provides that the operating plan is approved at the general meeting of owners; if there is no approved plan, the manager must without delay prepare a provisional plan, and the general meeting must decide on the plan within three months at the latest. If an operating plan already exists, the amount in the provisional plan is set on the basis of the current plan without exceeding the revaluation rate announced for the previous year under the Tax Procedure Law.

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 37 (Preparation of the operating plan) (unofficial translation)

“The operating plan is approved at the general meeting of condominium owners. If there is no operating plan accepted by the board of condominium owners, the manager shall without delay prepare a provisional operating plan until it is approved by the board of condominium owners within three months at the latest.… This plan shall be notified to the condominium owners or to those actually using the independent units against signature or by registered letter, and a decision on its acceptance, as it is or with amendments, shall be taken at the general meeting within three months at the latest.… Finalised operating plans or decisions of the board of condominium owners concerning operating expenses count as documents listed in paragraph 1 of article 68 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law.”

Because a finalised operating plan or a board decision on operating expenses is a document listed in Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 68/1, the creditor may also apply for the removal of an objection raised against enforcement proceedings based on such documents.

Annulment of Board Decisions: Time Limits, Non-Existence and Absolute Nullity

The remedy against board decisions is an action for annulment. An owner who attended the meeting and voted against may sue within one month from the date of the decision, and an owner who did not attend within one month from learning of it, and in any event within six months from the date of the decision, before the civil court of peace of the place where the main immovable is located. No time limit applies where the decision is non-existent or absolutely null and void.

Statutory Provision — Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 634) art. 33/1 (Intervention of the judge and action for annulment) (unofficial translation)

“Against decisions taken by the board of condominium owners, each condominium owner who attended the board meeting but voted against in accordance with article 32 may bring an action for annulment before the civil court of peace of the place where the main immovable is located within one month from the date of the decision, each condominium owner who did not attend the meeting within one month from learning of the decision, and in any event within six months from the date of the decision; no time limit applies where decisions of the board of condominium owners are deemed non-existent or absolutely null and void. A condominium owner or owners harmed because one of the condominium owners, or a person continuously benefiting from his unit on the basis of a lease, a right of residence or another reason, fails to fulfil his debts and obligations may apply to the civil court of peace of the place where the main immovable is located and request the intervention of the judge.”

Which defects are grounds for annulment subject to time limits and which amount to non-existence or nullity that can be raised at any time is the most disputed question in practice. The 5th Civil Chamber did not treat the failure to give some owners the notice of an extraordinary meeting required by Condominium Law art. 29/2 as a ground of non-existence or absolute nullity; it held that, owing to the breach of a mandatory provision, the meeting and its decisions had to be annulled in an action brought within the one-month and six-month limits (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/10023, K. 2021/1246). The same decision also states that, as regards a request to establish irregularities in the accounts, no declaratory judgment binding on a possible action for the manager’s liability can be given before the accounting and supervision mechanisms of Condominium Law art. 39 and Condominium Law art. 41 have been used.

Parties: Actions to annul board decisions may be brought against the manager as the representative of the owners; the manager informs all owners of the action, and if the decision is annulled the costs are met from the common expenses (Condominium Law art. 38). Where the annulment of the management plan is sought, however, the 5th Civil Chamber decision mentioned above requires all owners to be joined. Tenants and others continuously using a unit do not have rights as broad as those of an owner: according to a regional court of appeal decision upheld by the Court of Cassation, they may request the intervention of the judge, but to sue against a board decision the decision must concern their particular interest and directly harm their rights and interests (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/13534, K. 2025/6437).

Mediation, the Competent Court and Intervention of the Judge

All disputes arising from the application of the Condominium Ownership Law are resolved by the civil court of peace (Condominium Law additional art. 1). This presupposes that the Condominium Ownership Law applies to the dispute: according to the 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, where a site is built on more than one parcel and has not yet moved to housing-estate management, a dispute over interference with common areas is governed by the general provisions rather than that Law, jurisdiction is likewise determined under the general provisions, and the action is heard by the civil court of first instance (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/9250, K. 2026/3075; see General Rules for Multi-Parcel Sites Until Estate Management: Turkish Court of Cassation on Common-Area Actions, Jurisdiction and a Manager’s Authority to Sue). Under the provision added by Law No. 7445 to the Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes, applying to a mediator before suing is a precondition to suit in disputes arising from the Condominium Ownership Law (Law No. 6325 art. 18/B). If it emerges that the action was brought without applying to a mediator, it is dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a precondition; from the application to the mediation office until the final minutes are drawn up, limitation periods are suspended and preclusive periods do not run (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A).

In a decision upheld by the Court of Cassation, an action to annul a board decision alleged to be non-existent was dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a precondition because it had been brought without applying to a mediator; the first-instance court’s reasoning that this defect could not be cured later passed appellate and cassation review (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/8970, K. 2026/2476). It is therefore important to apply for mediation in good time, bearing in mind the one-month and six-month annulment periods.

Intervention of the judge: If an owner or a person continuously using a unit fails to perform his debts and obligations, an owner harmed by this may ask the civil court of peace for the intervention of the judge. After hearing those concerned, the judge decides immediately according to the Law and the management plan or, failing provisions there, according to the general provisions and the rules of equity, and notifies the person concerned that the decision must be complied with within a short period that he sets; those who fail to comply within that period are given an administrative fine by the same court (Condominium Law art. 33).

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below have been verified against their full texts in the official case-law database. Over time, appeals in condominium disputes have been reviewed by different chambers; the decisions belong to the chambers competent under the division of work in force when they were rendered. A 2022 decision of the 5th Civil Chamber states that the 20th Civil Chamber, which had earlier reversed the judgment in the same file, had been abolished (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/5533, K. 2022/11993).

An absolute majority suffices at the second meeting; electing the manager and the auditor requires a majority in number and land share

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/12698, K. 2021/15129, 15.12.2021 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning)

“Under the provisions explained, the decision quorum at the 2nd ordinary meeting is the absolute majority of those attending. Since the provisions laid down in the Law on quorums are reserved, decisions on electing the manager and the auditor must be taken by a majority of the condominium owners in number and land share, whereas decisions for which the Law sets no quorum may be taken at the 2nd meeting by a majority of those attending.” (translation)

Management plan: breaches of mandatory provisions may be raised at any time; a four-fifths majority cannot override matters requiring unanimity

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/5533, K. 2022/11993, 14.09.2022 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning)

“…it was not correct to disregard that, where the management plan is contrary to the mandatory provisions of the Law, the condominium owners may apply to the court at any time for the annulment of those provisions and that, since the annulment of the management plan closely concerns the rights of the condominium owners, all of the condominium owners must be joined to the action and, after the parties have been properly constituted, the merits must be examined and a decision given according to the outcome,… although a 4/5 consent is sufficient to amend the management plan, an amendment made to the management plan by a 4/5 majority cannot override matters for which the law requires unanimity…” (translation)

Failure to give notice of an extraordinary meeting is not non-existence but a ground for annulment subject to the one-month and six-month limits

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/10023, K. 2021/1246, 10.02.2021 (decision upholding the judgment; the Chamber’s reasoning for upholding)

“…that the meeting was an extraordinary meeting, that the requirement of notice of the meeting laid down in Article 29/2 of the Law was not fulfilled with respect to some of the claimants, and that, although failure to comply with the notice requirement is not a ground of non-existence or absolute nullity, the meeting and its decisions must be annulled, owing to the breach of mandatory provisions, in an action brought within the 1-month and 6-month periods…” (translation)

The election of an irregularly elected manager is valid until annulled; while a manager is in office the conditions for appointment by the judge are not met

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/8850, K. 2026/3482, 02.03.2026 (decision upholding the judgment; finding of the regional court of appeal that was upheld)

“As accepted in the settled decisions of the Court of Cassation, where the manager has not been elected by a majority of the condominium owners in number and land share, this decision is valid until it is annulled; the claimant did not bring an action for the annulment of the meetings at which the manager was elected or of the items concerning the election of the manager;… on the date of the action the site had a manager, and therefore the statutory conditions were not met…” (translation)

Mediation is a precondition to suit in condominium disputes; an annulment action brought without applying to a mediator is dismissed on procedural grounds

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/8970, K. 2026/2476, 16.02.2026 (decision upholding the judgment; finding of the first-instance court that passed appellate and cassation review)

“…on the grounds that the dispute between the parties concerned the annulment of a decision of the board of condominium owners and the mediation route had not been exhausted before the action was brought, that disputes arising from condominium ownership are subject to mediation as a precondition to suit, that the claimant did not apply to mediation, that the purpose introduced by the statutory amendment is the exhaustion of the mediation route before suing, that the action was brought without fulfilling the precondition and that this defect could not be cured later, the action was dismissed on procedural grounds for lack of a precondition to suit.” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Within what time must an action be brought against a decision of the board of condominium owners?

An owner who attended the meeting and voted against may bring an action for annulment before the civil court of peace within one month from the date of the decision, an owner who did not attend within one month from learning of it, and in any event within six months from the date of the decision (Condominium Law art. 33). No time limit applies to decisions that are non-existent or absolutely null and void. Applying to a mediator before suing is a precondition to suit; preclusive periods do not run during mediation.

How is a building manager elected, and what happens if none can be elected?

Appointing a manager is mandatory in buildings with eight or more independent units. The manager is appointed by a majority of the owners both in number and in land share and is re-appointed every year; if the owners cannot agree or fail to appoint a manager, the civil court of peace appoints one upon the application of an owner (Condominium Law art. 34). Where a manager alleged to have been irregularly elected is in office, a decision upheld by the Court of Cassation found that the conditions for appointment are not met unless the election decision is annulled.

What can be done against an owner who does not pay service charges?

The manager or any of the owners may sue or initiate enforcement proceedings; the defaulting owner pays late-payment compensation of five per cent per month (Condominium Law art. 20). Finalised operating plans and board decisions on operating expenses count as documents under Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 68/1 (Condominium Law art. 37). A tenant is also jointly and severally liable up to the rent he owes; if the debt still cannot be collected, a statutory mortgage may be registered on the unit (Condominium Law art. 22).

I do not use the lift or the common area; must I still pay service charges?

As a rule, yes. An owner cannot avoid paying his share of expenses and advances by waiving his right to use a common area or facility or by claiming that, owing to the situation of his unit, he has no need for it (Condominium Law art. 20). Very costly or luxurious innovations and additions are an exception: an owner who does not wish to benefit from them is not obliged to contribute to their cost (Condominium Law art. 43).

Can a tenant seek the annulment of a decision of the board of condominium owners?

Tenants and others continuously using a unit may request the intervention of the judge, but they do not have rights as broad as an owner’s. In a decision upheld by the Court of Cassation it was accepted that, for a tenant to sue against a board decision, the decision must concern his particular interest and directly harm his rights and interests.

How is the management plan amended?

Amending the management plan requires the vote of four fifths of all owners (Condominium Law art. 28). According to the Court of Cassation this majority cannot override matters for which the Law requires unanimity, and the annulment of plan provisions contrary to mandatory provisions of the Law may be sought at any time. In housing estates, following the amendment made by Law No. 7579, the vote of two thirds of the units represented by the members of the estate’s representatives board is required (Condominium Law art. 70).
