Action for Determination of Rent: The Increase Ceiling (CPI Limit), Equity After Five Years, Time to Sue

Short answer: Under Turkish law, the action for determination of rent allows the court to fix the rent applicable in the renewed lease period of residential and roofed-workplace leases, and it may be brought by either the landlord or the tenant. In renewed periods the rent increase may not exceed the twelve-month average change in the consumer price index (CPI) for the previous lease year (TBK art.344). In leases longer than five years or renewed after five years, and at the end of every five years thereafter, the judge sets the rent equitably having regard to that rate, the condition of the premises and comparable rents; in practice a reasonable equity reduction is made from the market rent determined by the expert. The action may be brought at any time, but for the rent fixed by the court to bind the tenant from the start of the new period, the action must be filed at least thirty days before the new period begins, or written notice of the increase must be given to the tenant within that time and the action filed by the end of the new period, while an action filed within the new period suffices if the contract contains an increase clause (TBK art.345). The competent court is the civil court of peace, regardless of the value in dispute (HMK art.4).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

The action for determination of rent is a public-order action by which the court fixes the renewed-period rent in residential and roofed-workplace leases. This article explains, under TBK arts.343-346, the CPI twelve-month-average ceiling on rent increases, the equity-based (comparable-rent) determination after five years, the time to sue and the thirty-day notice condition (TBK art.345), the competent civil court of peace, the difference between rent determination and the TBK art.138 adaptation action, and the expiry of the temporary 25% ceiling applied to residential rents in 2022-2024, in light of decisions of the Yargıtay Assembly of Civil Chambers and 3rd Civil Chamber.

What Is the Action for Determination of Rent?

The action for determination of rent is an action in residential and roofed-workplace leases by which the court fixes the rent to be applied in the renewed lease period. Where the parties cannot agree on the new-period rent, or the applied rent falls below/above market rates, either the landlord or the tenant may bring this action to have the rent fixed according to current economic conditions and statutory limits.

In the settled practice of the Court of Cassation, rent-determination actions concern public order and are sui generis. The judge must follow the methods set by statute, unification-of-precedents decisions and the Court's case-law, and is not bound by methods chosen by the parties. The judgment fixes, clearly and completely, the rent for one lease year.

The action presupposes an existing lease and does not terminate it; it merely fixes the new-period rent. It is distinct from the action for adaptation of the lease (TBK art.138), as explained below.

Legal Basis

The basis of rent determination lies in TBK arts.343-346, governing the fixing of rent in residential and roofed-workplace leases. TBK art.343 provides that, other than the determination of rent, no change may be made against the tenant; art.344 sets the limits of the rent applicable in renewed periods.

Relevant Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations art.344 (unofficial translation)

“The parties' agreements on the rent to be applied in renewed lease periods are valid provided they do not exceed the rate of change in the twelve-month averages of the consumer price index of the previous lease year. This rule also applies to leases longer than one year. Where no such agreement has been made, the rent is fixed by the judge, having regard to the condition of the leased property and according to equity, provided it does not exceed that CPI rate. Regardless of whether such an agreement exists, in leases longer than five years or renewed after five years, and at the end of each subsequent five years, the rent to be applied in the new lease year is fixed by the judge in an equitable manner, having regard to the twelve-month average CPI change, the condition of the leased property and comparable rents...”

Relevant Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations art.343 (unofficial translation)

“Other than the determination of the rent, no change may be made against the tenant in lease agreements.”

Relevant Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations art.346 (unofficial translation)

“No payment obligation other than the rent and ancillary charges may be imposed on the tenant. In particular, agreements providing for a penalty clause upon late payment of rent, or for the subsequent rents becoming due, are void.”

The action originates in the Court of Cassation's case-law: the gap created when the Constitutional Court's annulment of arts.2 and 3 of the former Law No. 6570 took effect on 26.09.1963 was filled by the Unification-of-Precedents Decision of 18.11.1964 no.2/4, which adopted that the rent be fixed by the judge, on a limited basis, according to equity. With the entry into force of Law No. 6098 (TBK) on 01.07.2012, the method and criteria for fixing rent were regulated at statute level.

An important practical detail: some residential/roofed-workplace lease provisions such as TBK arts.343-346 did not apply, for eight years from 1/7/2012 (i.e. until 1/7/2020), to workplace leases where the tenant is a merchant or a legal person; freedom of contract governed until then. From 1/7/2020 these provisions began to apply to such workplace leases too.

How the Rent Is Determined (TBK art.344)

1. First five years: the CPI twelve-month average ceiling

The parties' increase agreement for the renewed period is valid only if it does not exceed the rate of change in the twelve-month averages of the CPI of the previous lease year. Agreements exceeding this ceiling are void as to the excess. Where there is no agreement, the rent is fixed by the judge, again not exceeding the CPI rate, according to equity considering the property's condition.

2. After five years: equity-based (comparable-rent) determination

In leases longer than five years or renewed after five years, and at the end of each subsequent five years, the new-year rent is fixed by the judge in an equitable manner, having regard to the twelve-month average CPI change, the condition of the property and comparable rents. In practice this is the “equity period”: the rent is no longer bound solely by the CPI and may be brought closer to current market value.

3. Rent in foreign currency

Where the rent is agreed in foreign currency, subject to the Law on the Protection of the Value of Turkish Currency, no change may be made for five years; the TBK art.138 hardship provision is reserved. After five years, the rent is fixed also taking into account changes in the value of the foreign currency.

The Temporary 25% Ceiling on Residential Rents and Its Expiry

Owing to high inflation a temporary ceiling was imposed on residential rents. By Provisional Article 1 added to the TBK by Law No. 7409, for the period 08.06.2022 - 01.07.2023, and by Provisional Article 2 added by Law No. 7456, for the period 02.07.2023 - 01.07.2024, the increase in renewed residential leases was capped at 25% of the previous rent (where the CPI rate was below 25% in that period, the CPI applied).

This temporary measure applied only to residential rents and expired on 01.07.2024. Currently, for both residential and roofed-workplace rents, the only statutory ceiling is the twelve-month average CPI change under TBK art.344. As the 25% cap is a past temporary measure, in rent-determination actions filed today the new-period rent is fixed under the criteria of TBK art.344.

Conditions and Who May Sue

A valid residential or roofed-workplace lease and a new lease period whose rent is sought to be determined are required. Both the landlord (to increase the rent) and the tenant (to have the rent fixed/reduced to market) may sue. Even without an increase clause in the contract, determination of rent under TBK art.344 may always be sought.

Because the action seeks to change the current rent, there must be a dispute over the contractual rent; the action proceeds insofar as the claimant has a legal interest.

Time to Sue and Effect of the Judgment (TBK art.345)

The action to determine rent may be brought at any time. However, which period the outcome binds depends on the timing of the action and any notice:

Relevant Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations art.345 (unofficial translation)

“An action to determine the rent may be brought at any time. However, where this action is brought at the latest thirty days before the start of the new period, or provided the landlord has, within that time, given the tenant written notice of an increase in rent, and the action is brought by the end of the following new lease period, the rent to be fixed by the court binds the tenant from the start of that new lease period. If the contract contains a provision that the rent will be increased in the new period, the rent to be fixed in an action brought by the end of the new period is likewise effective from the start of that new period.”

Thus, if the action is brought at least thirty days before the new period, or written notice of an increase (ihtar) is given to the tenant within that time, the court-fixed rent applies from the start of the new period. Moreover, if the contract contains an increase clause for the new period, according to the Court of Cassation the thirty-day/notice condition is not required; an action brought within the new period suffices and the fixed rent applies from the start of the period.

Competent Court and Venue

Like all disputes arising from a lease, the action to determine rent falls, irrespective of the amount in dispute, within the civil court of peace (sulh hukuk mahkemesi) (HMK art.4/1-a).

Relevant Provision — Code of Civil Procedure art.4 (unofficial translation)

“(1) Regardless of the value or amount in dispute, the civil courts of peace hear: (a) actions concerning all disputes arising from the lease relationship, including debt claims arising therefrom — except provisions on eviction by unenforced-judgment execution under Law No. 2004 — and actions brought against them ...”

As to venue, being an action arising from a contract, besides the defendant's domicile (HMK art.6) the court of the place of performance of the contract is also competent (HMK art.10). Since the place of performance of a lease is, as a rule, where the leased property is located, in practice the civil court of peace of the property's location is competent.

Relevant Provision — Code of Civil Procedure art.10 (unofficial translation)

“(1) Actions arising from a contract may also be brought in the court of the place where the contract is to be performed.”

Rent Determination vs Adaptation (TBK art.138)

Rent determination (TBK art.344) and adaptation of the lease (TBK art.138 — excessive hardship) are subject to different aims and conditions. Rent determination aims to re-fix the new-period rent considering inflation, the CPI, the property's condition and comparable rents. Adaptation aims to adjust the contract to new conditions where an extraordinary situation unforeseen at conclusion has collapsed the basis of the transaction.

The Court of Cassation holds that in residential and roofed-workplace leases TBK art.344 is a special and mandatory provision; therefore an adaptation action under TBK art.138 cannot be brought before the five-year period elapses merely on grounds of inflation or an inadequate rent increase. Where a special provision (art.344) exists, the general provision (art.138) cannot be invoked so as to render it ineffective. Conversely, where genuine extraordinary circumstances satisfying the conditions of art.138 exist — earthquake, epidemic, war, sudden and extreme currency swings — an adaptation action may be brought at any time.

Equity (Hak ve Nesafet) Reduction

In post-five-year (equity-period) determinations and in actions involving long-standing tenants, the court applies a suitable and reasonable equity reduction to the free-market (vacant-letting) value found by the expert. This reduction rests on criteria such as the tenant's long tenure, upkeep of the property and the stability of the ongoing lease.

Per the Court's settled practice, the reduction must be applied to the market value determined by the expert, not to the amount claimed; and the fixed rent must not exceed the claim. The rate varies with the case, but in practice a moderate rate is applied; in any event the judgment must state reviewable reasons.

Proof

The lease, the current rent, payment records, any notice (ihtar) and its service date are the key documents. The market rent for the new period is determined by on-site inspection and expert examination, evaluating comparable leases, the property's location, size, nature and environmental-economic factors.

In post-five-year (equity) determinations, comparable rents must be investigated. Satisfaction of the thirty-day/notice condition must be shown by service and date; otherwise the fixed rent applies only from the following period.

Decisions of the Court of Cassation

Legal nature of the action and reasonable reduction for long-standing tenants

Court of Cassation Decision
Yargıtay Assembly of Civil Chambers, 2017/974 E., 2021/1058 K., 21.09.2021

“The most striking feature of rent-determination actions is that they concern public order. ... In these actions the judge must determine the rent in conformity with the methods set by statute, unification-of-precedents decisions and the Court's case-law. ... From the rent the experts state the property could yield if let vacant under free-market terms, the court must make a suitable and reasonable reduction in fixing the rent.” (Unofficial translation.)

Reduction is applied to the market value; the claim may not be exceeded

Court of Cassation Decision
Yargıtay Assembly of Civil Chambers, 2017/977 E., 2021/1168 K., 05.10.2021

“In that case, the court should apply a reduction under the principle of equity to the rent determined by the expert … in a manner that also reflects the settled practice of the Court of Cassation, and, also taking the claimant’s request into account, should determine the rent without exceeding the claim. … Whereas the Special Chamber’s reversal judgment, which is also adopted by the Assembly of Civil Chambers, should have been complied with, persisting in the earlier judgment is contrary to procedure and law.” (Unofficial translation.)

Time to sue and the contractual increase clause (TBK art.345)

Court of Cassation Decision
Yargıtay 3rd Civil Chamber, 2019/1029 E., 2019/1790 K., 06.03.2019

“… an action for the determination of rent may be brought at any time. However, provided that this action is brought on a date no later than thirty days before the start of the new period, or that the landlord has, within that period, notified the tenant in writing that the rent will be increased, where the action is brought by the end of the following new rental period, the rent determined by the court binds the tenant from the start of that new rental period. If the contract contains a provision that the rent will be increased in the new rental period, the rent determined by the court in an action brought by the end of the new rental period also applies from the start of that new period. (TBK art. 345) … Where the parties have expressed in the contract their will to increase the rent for the new period, it suffices that the action be brought within the new period to which the determination relates.”

Distinction between rent determination and adaptation; the special character of TBK art.344

Court of Cassation Decision
Yargıtay 3rd Civil Chamber, 2024/1083 E., 2025/1439 K., 10.03.2025

“In residential and roofed-workplace leases, the grounds of the rent-determination action under Article 344 of Law No. 6098 and the principles to be applied therein do not constitute the ground and subject of the adaptation action for excessive hardship under Article 138 of the same Law; where the other grounds and conditions in Article 138 are met, an adaptation action may always be brought.” (Unofficial translation.)

Can a rent-determination dispute over a residential or roofed business lease go to arbitration? (Assembly of Civil Chambers, 2026)

Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers (the Assembly’s reasoning)
Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu, 2025/502 E., 2026/15 K., 21.01.2026

“That being so, it has been concluded that it must first be determined, according to the nature and type of lease contracts, whether the subject of a dispute that may arise in the lease relationship is a matter subject to the will of the tenant and the landlord.” (unofficial translation)

The Assembly stated that, for residential and roofed business premises, TBK art.344 sets a ceiling on the parties’ will for a public purpose, and that treating such rent-determination disputes as arbitrable without distinction could, because of the limited review in an action to set aside an arbitral award, take this rule outside the supervision of the state courts. It stressed that mandatory mediation in lease disputes does not by itself make them arbitrable, since a mediator cannot give a binding decision; it required the court to establish by on-site inspection and expert report whether the premises are subject to the rules on ordinary leases, residential and roofed business premises leases, or usufructuary leases, and reversed the insistence judgment by majority. The dissent considered that the provisions limiting rent are not a matter of public order. Accordingly, even where the contract contains an arbitration clause, whether a rent-determination dispute over residential or roofed business premises can be resolved by arbitration is assessed separately according to the nature of the premises.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who may bring the rent-determination action?

Both landlord and tenant. The landlord usually sues to increase the rent, the tenant to have it fixed/reduced to market. Even without an increase clause, determination under TBK art.344 may always be sought.

What is the maximum new-period increase?

The increase in renewed periods cannot exceed the twelve-month average CPI change of the previous lease year (TBK art.344); agreements exceeding it are void as to the excess. The temporary 25% ceiling on residential rents expired on 01.07.2024.

How is the rent fixed after five years?

After five years and at the end of each five-year period, the rent is fixed by the judge according to equity — no longer bound solely by the CPI — having regard also to the property's condition and comparable rents. This is when the rent may be brought closer to current market value.

When should I file the rent-determination action?

It may be filed at any time. But for the fixed rent to apply from the start of the new period, the action must be filed at least thirty days before the new period, or written notice of increase (ihtar) must be given to the tenant within that time. If the contract has an increase clause, an action within the new period suffices.

Which court is competent?

Disputes arising from a lease fall within the civil court of peace irrespective of the amount (HMK art.4). Venue is the defendant's domicile or the place of performance (as a rule, where the property is located).

Can I bring an adaptation action before five years because inflation is high?

According to the Court of Cassation, no. In residential and roofed-workplace leases TBK art.344 is a special and mandatory provision, so an art.138 adaptation action cannot be brought before five years merely on grounds of inflation/inadequate rent. But where genuine extraordinary circumstances (earthquake, epidemic, war) exist, an adaptation action may always be brought.
