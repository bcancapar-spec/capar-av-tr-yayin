Türkiye’s “Full-Time Law” for Physicians: Private Practice Restrictions from 1928 to 2026 and Today

Short answer: As of October 2026, physicians holding a permanent post in the public sector may not open a private clinic or work in a private hospital or foundation university, save for the statutory exceptions. This covers the Ministry of Health, other public bodies and state universities (Law No. 657 art. 28, Law No. 1219 art. 12 and Law No. 2547 art. 36). Professors and associate professors at state universities may work in private hospitals outside working hours only under an institutional contract concluded by the university and within quotas. Faculty members who were lawfully running a private clinic on 18.01.2014 may, according to Council of State case law, continue that activity. Physicians with no tie to the public sector may practise freely; in private health institutions and foundation universities they may work in no more than two institutions and as employees insured under Law No. 5510 art. 4/1-a.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

What Is “Full Time”? Key Concepts

The “Full-Time Law” (Tam Gün Yasası) is, in the narrow sense, Law No. 5947 of 21.01.2010 on Full-Time Work of University and Health Personnel and Amendments to Certain Laws (Official Gazette 30.01.2010, No. 27478). The problem, however, neither began nor ended with that Law. Its core is this: may a physician paid by the State practise privately outside working hours — in a private clinic, a private hospital or a foundation university? Turkish law has answered that question differently over almost a century, and the back-and-forth between the legislature and the Constitutional Court intensified between 2010 and 2016.

Full-time work means that a public-sector physician devotes all of his or her professional work to the institution of employment and carries on no paid professional activity outside it. Part-time status was the pre-2010 status in which professors and associate professors worked part-time at the university while practising privately. The revolving-fund supplementary payment is a performance-based payment distributed to staff from institutional income, subject to ceilings set by law. Out-of-hours work is work done at the physician’s own institution outside working hours, for which a separate supplementary payment is made.

The provisions that carry today’s system are found in the Civil Servants Law No. 657, Law No. 1219 on the practice of medicine and the Higher Education Law No. 2547; the financial side is governed by the revolving-fund legislation.

1928–1980: Law No. 1219, Socialisation and the 1978 Full-Time Law

Law No. 1219, the basic law on the practice of medicine, has been in force since 1928. The first paragraph of Law No. 1219 art. 12, as amended in 1949, prohibits a registered physician from engaging in trade by opening a shop. That rule concerns the separation of medicine from commerce, not the public–private divide; the limits on out-of-hours practice by public physicians were added to the article later.

Law No. 224 on the Socialisation of Health Services, adopted in January 1961, provided for health services in socialised regions to be delivered by contracted personnel. It did not abolish private medicine and expressly recognised that physicians receiving no salary or benefit from the public sector were free to practise.

Statutory Provision — Law on the Socialisation of Health Services (Law No. 224) art. 4 (unofficial translation)

“Physicians and members of the medical professions who receive no salary or wage from institutions within the public sector and obtain no benefit in kind or in cash in any manner whatsoever are entitled, within the provisions of the laws in force, to practise their professions freely and to establish private health institutions and pharmacies.”

The Law also provided that the contracts of socialisation personnel who abandoned their posts would be terminated and that they could not practise privately in the province concerned for one year (Law No. 224 art. 24). Its provision headed “Basic principles” was repealed in 1980; as its original text is not part of the current legislation, no view is expressed here on its content.

A full-time scheme covering public health personnel as a whole was introduced by Law No. 2162 of 29.06.1978 on the Principles of Full-Time Work of Health Personnel (OG 09.07.1978, No. 16341). It covered health personnel in budget institutions, municipalities, state economic enterprises and the Social Insurance Institution, as well as staff of the human health sciences faculties of universities. These staff were barred from practising privately, from taking paid professional posts in public or private institutions and from earning professional income beyond that provided by the Law; in return they received professional, hardship, on-call and emergency allowances.

The universities’ action for annulment was rejected by majority in Constitutional Court, E. 1978/59, K. 1979/11, 27.02.1979 (OG 10.06.1979). In the Court’s view the Law sought to have public health personnel devote all their work to public service, an aim consistent with the social state principle and the State’s health duty; subjecting medical faculty staff to heavier obligations together with higher allowances did not breach the equality and university autonomy provisions of the 1961 Constitution. A dissenting opinion recalled that the full-time principle was not new and that the Universities Law No. 1750 also contained a provision headed “Full-time work”. Law No. 2162 was repealed, together with the “Basic principles” provision of Law No. 224, by Law No. 2368 art. 6.

1980–2009: The Choice Between Allowance and Private Practice; Part-Time Status

Law No. 2368 of 31.12.1980 on the Allowances and Working Principles of Health Personnel replaced the ban with a choice. Under its text before repeal, staff receiving the allowance could not take paid professional posts or practise privately (Law No. 2368 art. 3), whereas staff entitled to private practice who waived the allowance could practise outside working hours, provincial health directors and chief physicians excepted (Law No. 2368 art. 4). For some thirty years, out-of-hours private practice by public physicians thus remained possible at the cost of the allowance.

At universities, Law No. 2547 art. 36 (1981) provided for two forms of work. As recounted in the Constitutional Court’s 2010 decision, an associate or full professor with five years of permanent service could be appointed to part-time status for two years, renewable every two years, on the opinion of the university board and the decision of the rector. A part-time faculty member could run a private clinic.

The financial instrument of the period was the revolving fund. Before Law No. 5947, the third paragraph of Law No. 209 art. 5 listed whether staff “practise privately or not” among the criteria for supplementary payments; Law No. 5947 replaced that phrase with “examination, teaching and research activities”. Full-time work was therefore encouraged through a larger share of the fund even before any ban. The Basic Law on Health Services No. 3359 of 1987 made Ministry planning of health institutions and personnel the governing principle (Law No. 3359 art. 3), which today underpins the quotas applied in private hospitals.

2010: The Full-Time Law No. 5947

Law No. 5947 abolished the choice and drew a sharp line between the public and private sectors:

  • Universities: Law No. 2547 art. 36 was rewritten. Academic staff were to work in permanent status and, apart from statutory duties and copyright, could not do any paid or unpaid, public or private work outside higher education institutions or practise privately. Part-time faculty members who did not apply within one year were to be “deemed to have resigned”.
  • All physicians: Law No. 1219 art. 12 divided health institutions into three groups: (a) public institutions; (b) private institutions and foundation universities contracted with the Social Security Institution (SGK) or public bodies; (c) non-contracted private institutions and foundation universities and private practice. Physicians could practise only in institutions within “only one of the following sub-paragraphs”.
  • Payments: Law No. 209 art. 5 and Law No. 2547 art. 58 were recast; ceilings were raised and a separate payment for out-of-hours income-generating work was introduced.
  • Other: Compulsory professional liability insurance was introduced (Law No. 1219 additional art. 12) and Law No. 2368 was repealed.

The full-time regime was to apply to Ministry of Health institutions from 30.07.2010 and to universities from 30.01.2011. As quoted in a dissenting opinion to the Constitutional Court’s decision, the legislative reasoning stated that the existing system of work led to a decline in efficiency in the public sector and to patients being steered to the private sector, and that the direct financial relationship between physicians and their patients made it harder for patients to access health care (Constitutional Court, E. 2010/29, K. 2010/90, dissenting opinion of Justice Nuri Necipoğlu).

The Constitutional Court Decision of 16.07.2010

The action brought by the main opposition party was decided in Constitutional Court, E. 2010/29, K. 2010/90, 16.07.2010 (OG 04.12.2010). By a stay of execution published in the Official Gazette of 22.07.2010, the Court also suspended the phrase “only one of the sub-paragraphs” until publication of the reasoned decision. The operative part provides:

  • Outside-work ban: The first sentence of Law No. 2547 art. 36/2 was annulled by majority. The legislature may impose limits on academic staff suited to their titles and status in order to address health problems, but such limits may not obstruct scientific activity; prohibiting all work after hours, even unpaid, without distinguishing between faculty members and other academic staff is incompatible with Constitution art. 130. Permanent status and teaching-load rules were upheld.
  • “Deemed resignation”: annulled on grounds of legal certainty; part-time staff had, if not an acquired right, a legitimate expectation regarding the two-year term granted by law.
  • Single-group rule: the phrase “only one of the sub-paragraphs” in Law No. 1219 art. 12/2 was annulled by majority under Constitution art. 17 and Constitution art. 56, the word “following” being retained; dissenters criticised the split annulment for creating a new rule.
  • Payment ceiling: Law No. 209 art. 5/4 was annulled unanimously because it set only an upper limit with no minimum guarantee; the annulment took effect nine months later.
  • Rejected: provisions on secondment and the payment regulation, the occupational-physician sentence, the half-share of insurance premiums borne by physicians and the 35-hour rule for radiation staff.

As a result, as the Court itself noted in 2014, full-time faculty members could practise privately outside working hours, and physicians were no longer confined to one group of institutions. The legislation of the following six years sought to close these gaps.

2011–2016: Decree-Laws, Laws No. 6514 and 6569 and Further Annulments

Decree-Law No. 650 and the enabling-law problem

The first response was Decree-Law No. 650 (OG 26.08.2011), issued under Enabling Law No. 6223. It added to Law No. 657 art. 28 a sentence barring civil servants from opening clinics or working in foundation universities, made Law No. 1219 art. 12/2 subject to the civil-service and higher-education rules, and allowed faculty members to practise outside working hours provided that at the university they only taught and researched and did not work in revolving-fund services; those choosing this route received no supplementary payment and could hold no administrative post. Constitutional Court, E. 2011/113, K. 2012/108, 18.07.2012 (OG 01.01.2013) annulled these provisions as falling outside the Enabling Law, with a six-month deferral. The payment provision annulled in 2010 was re-enacted by Decree-Law No. 663 (OG 02.11.2011).

Law No. 6514: the ban re-enacted by statute

The ban was then enacted by statute in Law No. 6514 of 02.01.2014 (OG 18.01.2014). A clinic ban was added to Law No. 657 art. 28 and Law No. 1219 art. 12/2 was rewritten with a “without prejudice to” clause.

Statutory Provision — Civil Servants Law (Law No. 657) art. 28 (Prohibition of trade and other gainful activities) (unofficial translation)

“…Civil servants may not open offices, bureaux, clinics or similar premises for the purpose of professional activity or private practice, and may not work at any workplace belonging to natural persons, legal persons governed by private law or professional bodies having the status of public institutions, or at foundation higher education institutions.”

For universities, a seventh paragraph added to Law No. 2547 art. 36 made physician academic staff subject to Law No. 657 art. 28 and opened a supervised institutional-contract route for professors and associate professors.

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 36/7 (Working principles) (unofficial translation)

“Academic staff who are physicians, dentists or specialists under the medical specialisation legislation are subject to article 28 of Civil Servants Law No. 657, save as provided by law. However, those holding professorial or associate professorial posts may, with their consent, be employed outside working hours in private hospitals or foundation university hospitals, provided that their number does not exceed 50 per cent of the professors and associate professors on the staff of each department, that an institutional contract of up to one year is concluded and that the income is recorded in the university revolving-fund account.”

Faculty members working on this basis are selected by their previous year’s performance, may not hold more than one contract or any administrative post, and hospitals may contract up to 20 per cent of their physician and dentist posts (Law No. 2547 art. 36/8); half of the income is paid to the faculty member without a ceiling. Law No. 6514 also required faculty members to close their clinics within three months on pain of severance from the university (Law No. 2547 provisional art. 64).

The Constitutional Court first suspended the transitional provision by a decision of 09.04.2014 and then, in Constitutional Court, E. 2014/61, K. 2014/166, 07.11.2014 (OG 19.06.2015), upheld the ban in Law No. 657 art. 28 and Law No. 2547 art. 36/7 but annulled the transitional provision. According to the reasoning quoted verbatim in Council of State decisions, prospective expectations are not acquired rights, but faculty members who, relying on the court rulings, had organised their lives around out-of-hours practice had a legitimate expectation that this would continue for some time, protected by the principle of legal certainty; the Court also stressed that the many successive laws on the subject had created uncertainty.

Law No. 6569 and the 2016 annulment

Law No. 6569 of 19.11.2014 (OG 26.11.2014), adopted twelve days later, tried a financial sanction: physician faculty members practising outside would receive no university allowance or supplementary payment until they stopped; those wishing to stop were to notify by 31.12.2014 and their activity would be deemed ended by 31.05.2015 at the latest (Law No. 2547 provisional art. 70). Constitutional Court, E. 2016/13, K. 2016/127, 22.06.2016 (OG 21.09.2016) annulled the payment ban and a similar sentence on military faculty members: according to the reasoning quoted by the Council of State, these payments are not consideration for overtime or performance, and depriving faculty members of them entirely for using an option offered by the legal system was neither proportionate nor fair (Constitution art. 2). Within six years, three transitional provisions — deemed resignation, severance and loss of allowances — were thus annulled.

2018–2026: Recent Amendments

After 2016 the legislature left the ban itself untouched; amendments focused on controlled channels within the ban and on the payment system.

2018 — Law No. 7146: Law No. 1219 art. 12/3 now provides that work in more than one institution takes place “within the workforce planning of the Ministry of Health”; protocol-based cooperation in international health tourism was allowed, but Ministry or state-university staff may not serve in private-sector or foundation-university facilities through this route (Law No. 1219 art. 12/4). Payments for designated complex procedures may be increased up to five-fold.

2020 — Law No. 7243: The disciplinary rules in Law No. 2547 art. 53 were renewed; engaging in prohibited gainful activities became an offence punishable by suspension of step advancement.

2022–2024 — payments: Law No. 7411 of 16.06.2022 raised the faculty-member ceiling in Law No. 2547 art. 58 from 800 to 950 per cent and recast Law No. 209 art. 5. Constitutional Court, E. 2022/102, K. 2023/154, 13.09.2023 (OG 27.10.2023) annulled, with a nine-month deferral, a sentence of Law No. 209 art. 5/1 leaving the principles of payment to regulation; the sentence was re-enacted by Law No. 7496 of 21.02.2024, which also introduced separate ceilings for subspecialists.

January 2025 — Private Hospitals Regulation: The new Regulation of 30.01.2025 governs the out-of-hours employment, within 20 per cent of posts and under an institutional contract, of professors approved by state universities under Law No. 2547 art. 36/7, and allows clinic physicians to treat their patients in contracted private hospitals within a quota of one third of the specialty’s posts, with an exception for physicians over 60.

July 2025 — Law No. 7557: Law No. 7557 of 21.07.2025 (OG 24.07.2025) replaced “more than one” in Law No. 1219 art. 12/3 with “no more than two” and tied private-sector work to employee insurance under Law No. 5510 art. 4/1-a.

Statutory Provision — Law on the Practice of Medicine and its Branches (Law No. 1219) art. 12/3 (unofficial translation)

“Physicians, dentists and specialists under the medical specialisation legislation may work in no more than two health institutions, within the workforce planning of the Ministry of Health and provided that each falls within one sub-paragraph of the second paragraph. …Physicians, dentists and specialists under the medical specialisation legislation work in private health institutions and foundation universities, whether or not these have a contract with the Social Security Institution or public bodies, as insured persons under article 4(1)(a) of Social Insurance and General Health Insurance Law No. 5510 of 31/5/2006. …Those practising privately may also diagnose and treat their patients in the health institutions listed in sub-paragraph (b), provided that the fee is paid by the patient and not claimed from the Social Security Institution.”

Under the transitional provision added by the same Law, physicians in private institutions and foundation universities had to apply for a new work permit by 01.06.2026; the permits of those who did not apply were cancelled (Law No. 1219 provisional art. 16).

August 2026 — Law No. 7592: Law No. 7592 of 30.07.2026 (OG 09.08.2026) tied the out-of-hours payment for faculty members to services rendered “in person” and introduced ceilings of 1150 per cent, and 2300 per cent in total, for subspecialist faculty members. It also allows faculty members who have reached the age of 67, hold a specialty under the legislation on specialisation in medicine and dentistry and have served at state higher education institutions to be employed on contract if they apply within three years (Law No. 2547 provisional art. 86); the prohibitions applicable to faculty members apply to them as well (Law No. 2547 art. 30/2).

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 58/h (Revolving fund) (unofficial translation)

“Additional fees collected under the third paragraph of article 73 of Social Insurance and General Health Insurance Law No. 5510 of 31/5/2006 for health services rendered in person by faculty members at the university outside working hours shall be credited to a separate account of the revolving-fund enterprise.”

August 2026 — amending regulation: Under the amendment of 29.08.2026, private hospitals may employ professors if they run a Ministry-approved specialty training programme, instead of having a cooperation protocol with a foundation university; the procedure for case-based permits for clinic patients was left to the Ministry.

Current Position: Who May Work Where, and on What Terms?

As of 25.10.2026 the framework provision is Law No. 1219 art. 12/2, which reserves the civil-service and higher-education rules for public physicians.

Statutory Provision — Law on the Practice of Medicine and its Branches (Law No. 1219) art. 12/2 (unofficial translation)

“Physicians, dentists and specialists under the medical specialisation legislation may practise their professions in the following health institutions, without prejudice to article 28 of Civil Servants Law No. 657 of 14/7/1965, additional article 27 of Turkish Armed Forces Personnel Law No. 926 of 27/7/1967, article 36 of Higher Education Law No. 2547 of 4/11/1981 and article 32 of Gülhane Military Medical Academy Law No. 2955 of 17/11/1983: a) Public institutions. b) Private health institutions contracted with the Social Security Institution and public bodies, and foundation universities so contracted. c) Private health institutions not contracted with the Social Security Institution and public bodies, foundation universities not so contracted, and private practice.”

Physicians in the Ministry of Health and other public bodies

A public physician may not open a clinic or work in a private workplace or foundation higher education institution (Law No. 657 art. 28). The exceptions are narrow: public physicians without managerial duties and family physicians may act as occupational physicians outside working hours, with permission, for up to thirty hours a month (Law No. 1219 art. 12/3); health tourism protocols are confined to public and university facilities. Secondment to another public health institution is possible at the physician’s request with the employer’s consent. Out-of-hours work is done at the physician’s own institution and paid as a supplementary payment.

Physician faculty members at state universities

Physician academic staff are also subject to Law No. 657 art. 28 (Law No. 2547 art. 36/7). A faculty member may treat patients out of hours at his or her own university hospital and, since Law No. 7592, receives a supplementary payment only for services rendered in person. Professors and associate professors may be employed out of hours in private or foundation university hospitals under an institutional contract of up to one year, within the 50 per cent quota, with the income recorded in the revolving fund and with their consent. Assistant professors, lecturers and research assistants cannot use this route.

Faculty members with a clinic on 18.01.2014

Since the transitional provision was annulled, faculty members who were lawfully running a clinic or working in a private institution on 18.01.2014 may continue. The Council of State’s Plenary Session of the Administrative Law Chambers extends this protection not to everyone who was a faculty member on that date, but only to those actually and lawfully practising privately on that date. However, since the regulatory amendment that entered into force on 15.09.2022, the “base payment” component of the revolving-fund supplementary payment is not paid to a faculty member who continues such private activity; the Plenary Session’s decision of 11.06.2025 to that effect is set out in the section on sanctions.

Foundation universities, private institutions and private clinics

A physician with no public post may work in the institutions listed in Law No. 1219 art. 12/2, but since Law No. 7557 in no more than two, within one sub-paragraph each and within Ministry workforce planning. Work in private institutions and foundation universities is insured employment under Law No. 5510 art. 4/1-a; permits of those who did not apply anew by 01.06.2026 were cancelled. A clinic physician may also treat patients in SGK-contracted private institutions if the patient pays and nothing is claimed from the SGK (Law No. 1219 art. 12/3); the Regulation requires a contract with the hospital and applies the one-third quota (Private Hospitals Regulation art. 24).

Sanctions for Breach of the Prohibitions

For a civil-servant physician, Law No. 657 art. 125 applies: engaging in gainful activities prohibited to civil servants is punishable by suspension of step advancement.

Statutory Provision — Civil Servants Law (Law No. 657) art. 125 (Disciplinary penalties: suspension of step advancement) (unofficial translation)

“D - Suspension of step advancement: the suspension of the civil servant’s advancement within his or her grade for 1 to 3 years, according to the gravity of the act. …h) Engaging in trade or other gainful activities prohibited to civil servants,”

For academic staff, Law No. 2547 art. 53 has since 2020 made the same act punishable by suspension of step advancement or a multiple salary deduction.

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 53/b-4 (Disciplinary penalties) (unofficial translation)

“(4) Suspension of step advancement or multiple salary deduction: …Engaging in trade, other than under exceptions recognised by the relevant laws, or in other prohibited gainful activities.”

Specific sanctions also apply: a faculty member breaching the institutional contract may not be so employed for one year, or five years on repetition, and a hospital in breach loses the contract and may not contract for one year, or five years on repetition (Law No. 2547 art. 36/7-8); physicians who did not apply for a new permit by 01.06.2026 had their work permits in private institutions cancelled (Law No. 1219 provisional art. 16). The harsher consequences provided between 2010 and 2014 — deemed resignation, severance and loss of allowances — were annulled by the Constitutional Court.

However, under a regulatory amendment that entered into force on 15.09.2022, the “base payment” component of the revolving-fund supplementary payment is not paid to faculty members who engage in private practice outside working hours or work in a private health institution; the Council of State’s Plenary Session of the Administrative Law Chambers resolved the conflict between regional administrative court decisions accordingly.

Council of State Decision
Plenary Session of the Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2025/31, K. 2025/38, 11.06.2025 (the Plenary Session’s decision resolving the conflict; unanimous)

“For these reasons, it has been concluded that the conflict must be resolved to the effect that no ‘base payment’ may be made, under sub-paragraph (b) added to paragraph 9 of article 5 of the principal Regulation by article 3 of the Regulation Amending the Regulation on the Procedures and Principles for the Distribution of Supplementary Payments from Revolving-Fund Income at Higher Education Institutions, which entered into force upon publication in Official Gazette No. 31954 of 15/09/2022, to those serving as professors at university faculties of medicine who at the same time engage in private practice outside working hours or work in private health institutions.” (translation)

Constitutional Court and Council of State Decisions

The Constitutional Court’s principal decisions are cited with their references in the chronological table and the period sections. Below are four Council of State decisions, examined in full text from the official case-law database, which determine the practical effect of those decisions.

Annulment does not operate retroactively: the period of withheld allowances

Council of State Decision
8th Chamber, E. 2019/6371, K. 2022/2671, 13.04.2022 (the Chamber’s reasons for reversal; unanimous)

“Since the revolving-fund supplementary payment governed by sub-paragraph (c) of article 58 of Law No. 2547 was not included within the scope of provisional article 70, it is understood that those revolving-fund payments cannot be linked to provisional article 70 and therefore to the Constitutional Court’s annulment decision. …faculty members who were prevented from receiving the university allowance under provisional article 70 … may apply to the relevant administrations for payment from 21/09/2016, the date on which the Constitutional Court’s annulment decision on provisional article 70 took effect” (translation)

The university allowance withheld between 15.12.2014 and 21.09.2016 therefore cannot be claimed retroactively, whereas the revolving-fund payment, outside the provision’s scope, must be paid according to actual contribution.

The test: the actual position on 18.01.2014

Council of State Decision
Plenary Session of the Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2021/3791, K. 2022/1545, 20.04.2022 (the Plenary Session’s reasons for affirmance; by majority)

“For in the concrete case the factor determining status is not ‘being a physician’ but lawfully running a clinic as at 18/01/2014. …Accordingly, under the Constitutional Court’s decision, a justified expectation can be spoken of only for faculty members lawfully practising privately by running a clinic as at 18/01/2014, and only their expectations must be protected.” (translation)

The Plenary Session did not follow the 10th Chamber’s 2021 approach, which would also have protected faculty members without a clinic.

A physician who became a faculty member after 2014 may not open a clinic

Council of State Decision
10th Chamber, E. 2022/1885, K. 2025/5053, 06.11.2025 (the Chamber’s reasons for reversal; unanimous)

“In that case, the claimant, who was not serving as a faculty member at a higher education institution as at 18/01/2014, when Law No. 6514 entered into force, and therefore fell outside provisional article 64 added to Law No. 2547, and who was subject to the rules in force on 05/10/2015, when he began working as a faculty member, namely article 36 of Law No. 2547 as amended by Law No. 6514, cannot lawfully practise privately by opening a clinic.” (translation)

The claimant had been a specialist at a public hospital on 18.01.2014 and was appointed assistant professor in 2015; the Chamber reversed the contrary regional court decision.

Clinic physicians treating patients in private hospitals: the one-third quota

Council of State Decision
Plenary Session of the Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2025/2338, K. 2025/3537, 22.12.2025 (the Plenary Session’s reasons for reversal; by majority, with dissents)

“…it has been concluded that the contested rules, understood to have been adopted in order to prevent planning from becoming ineffective, to ensure certainty in employment, to enable the monitoring and supervision of patients diagnosed and treated in private hospitals and of the physicians providing this service and to bring these activities under discipline, thereby protecting patient health and preventing possible abuse, are not contrary to higher legal norms, the public interest or the requirements of the service.” (translation)

The 10th Chamber had annulled the quota on 30.12.2024 as unsupported by any study or criteria such as operating-theatre capacity. The Plenary Session reversed, relying on Ministry planning powers and the workforce-planning clause in Law No. 1219; a dissent argued that the regulation unlawfully narrowed a statutory right. The decision concerns the former regulation, but a similar quota appears in the new one.

Assessment: The Constitutional Debate

The full-time debate concerns the balance between freedom of work and the State’s duty to plan health services.

Statutory Provision — Constitution of the Republic of Türkiye (Law No. 2709) art. 48 (Freedom of work and contract) (unofficial translation)

“Everyone has the freedom to work and conclude contracts in the field of his or her choice. Establishment of private enterprises is free.”

On the other side, the State must plan health institutions centrally (Constitution art. 56). The Constitutional Court’s decisions from 1979 to 2016 follow a consistent line between these poles.

The ban itself: The Court has in principle regarded a ban on outside work by public health personnel as within the legislature’s discretion, upholding Law No. 2162 in 1979, permanent status in 2010 and the bans in Law No. 6514 in 2014. Under the equality principle (Constitution art. 10), heavier obligations paired with higher allowances were treated as reflecting a different legal situation.

The special position of universities: The 2010 decision showed that the limit of the ban is the scientific autonomy of universities. A ban on all outside work by academic staff, even unpaid, was held contrary to Constitution art. 130; the applicants also relied on freedom of science and the arts under Constitution art. 27, but the Court based the annulment on the provision specific to universities. The 2014 scheme was upheld at a time when the ban targeted only the professional activity of physician academics and offered an exception channel.

Legal certainty in transition: All the annulled provisions concerned transition. While denying any acquired right, the Court three times protected the legitimate expectation of those who had planned their lives on the basis of the existing law and court rulings, and applied proportionality to the withholding of allowances. The Council of State’s use of the actual position on 18.01.2014 as the decisive test follows from this.

The instrument of regulation: The 2012 annulment showed that such limits cannot be imposed by a decree-law outside its enabling statute. Today the debate has moved to regulations: on private-hospital quotas the Council of State’s Plenary Session emphasised Ministry planning powers, while dissents held that a statutory right cannot be restricted by regulation. Since 2018 the legislature has preferred supervised channels to lifting the ban; their effect on access to care and medical employment is a question of health policy.

Conclusion

In Turkish law, private practice by physicians has moved from an express freedom for those not paid by the State (1961), through a short-lived full-time ban (1978–1980) and a choice paid for by forgoing an allowance (1980–2010), to a sharp public–private divide since 2010. In the period opened by Law No. 5947, the Constitutional Court accepted the substance of the ban but, protecting university autonomy and legal certainty in transition, forced the legislature to retreat three times. Today a public physician may not open a clinic or work in a private institution; a state-university professor or associate professor may work outside only under an institutional contract; faculty members who had a clinic on 18.01.2014 are protected; and in the private sector a physician may work in no more than two institutions as an insured employee. In any specific case, the person’s post, title and actual position on 18.01.2014 must be examined together.

This article has been prepared for general information purposes and does not constitute a legal opinion on specific disputes. The particular circumstances of each case must be assessed separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

May a specialist with a permanent post at a state hospital open a private clinic after hours?

No. Law No. 657 art. 28 prohibits civil servants from opening clinics and working in private workplaces. Public physicians without managerial duties may act as occupational physicians only with permission and for up to thirty hours a month.

May a physician faculty member at a state university work in a private hospital?

As a rule, no. Professors and associate professors may be employed out of hours under an institutional contract of up to one year concluded by the university and within quotas (Law No. 2547 art. 36/7). A personal clinic or individual contract is not covered.

What is the position of faculty members who had a clinic before 2014?

Faculty members who were lawfully running a clinic or working in a private institution on 18.01.2014 may continue, because the transitional provision was annulled. According to the Council of State, this protection does not extend to those without a clinic on that date or who became faculty members later.

In how many places may a physician working in a private hospital work at the same time?

Since Law No. 7557, in no more than two health institutions, within Ministry workforce planning and within one sub-paragraph each (Law No. 1219 art. 12/3); private-sector work is insured employment.

May a clinic physician operate on a patient in a private hospital?

Yes, if the patient pays and nothing is claimed from the SGK. The Private Hospitals Regulation requires a contract with the hospital, a quota of one third of the specialty’s posts and an exception for physicians over 60; case-based permits may be granted where needed.

What is the sanction for working in breach of the ban?

Suspension of step advancement for civil-servant physicians (Law No. 657 art. 125), and suspension of step advancement or a multiple salary deduction for academic staff (Law No. 2547 art. 53). A faculty member breaching an institutional contract may not be so employed for one year, or five years on repetition.
