Tera, Pusula and Liquidated Fund Investors in Türkiye: Legal Roadmap on Liquidation, Claims, Full Remedy

Short answer: If you hold units in a fund under liquidation, the first channel is the liquidation ordered by the Capital Markets Board (CMB): fund assets are separate from the portfolio management company, cannot enter its bankruptcy estate and the liquidation balance is distributed only to unitholders. The Investor Compensation Center (ICC) does not cover a fall in fund value; it intervenes only where a brokerage cannot return cash or instruments held on your behalf, upon a CMB compensation decision. For the remaining loss, joining the criminal case, damages actions against those responsible, claim registration and the claim registration action in bankruptcy, and a prior application and full-remedy action against the CMB for failures of supervision may come into play. Each route has its own conditions, time limits and risks; none guarantees a result.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

A guide for investors harmed in the Tera, Pusula and liquidated fund cases: CMB decisions, compensation, criminal complaints, bankruptcy claims, full-remedy actions.

A step-by-step legal roadmap for investors harmed in the Tera and Pusula cases and in the 131 investment funds placed into liquidation by the Capital Markets Board: facts verified from official CMB bulletins, evidence, fund liquidation and interim payments, the Investor Compensation Center, criminal complaints for market fraud and joining the prosecution, claim registration and the claim registration action in bankruptcy, damages actions, full-remedy actions against the CMB, a table of time limits and verified decisions of the Court of Cassation and the Council of State.

What Is Officially Known About the Tera and Pusula Cases?

This section relies on decisions published in CMB weekly bulletins up to bulletin no. 2026/73 of 22.10.2026; information from the press is identified as such. Criminal complaints and trading bans are administrative findings and measures; there is no final conviction against any person or entity and the presumption of innocence applies.

Liquidation of the funds. By decisions no. 57/1706 and 57/1707 of 17.09.2026 (bulletin 2026/60), the CMB closed to trading all funds founded by the Tera, Pusula, Hedef, Atlas, A1, Pardus and Bulls portfolio management companies and traded on the electronic fund platform TEFAS, and ordered the listed funds to be liquidated “by a method to be determined by the Board”, citing Law No. 6362 art. 1, art. 128/1-a and art. 96/1. Bulletin 2026/61 of the same day (decision 57/1708) set the liquidation principles and updated the list to 131 funds, of which 12 are Pusula Portföy and 6 are Tera Portföy funds. T. İş Bankası AŞ was authorised for the Tera funds and TC Ziraat Bankası AŞ for the other companies’ funds. Reconciliation is made between the Central Registry Agency (MKK) and the banks, assets are converted into cash and distributed pro rata, and unexecuted TEFAS redemption orders are paid first. The liquidation period was extended from three to six months by decision 59/1710 of 20.09.2026 (bulletin 2026/62) and may be extended again.

Interim payment. By decision 64/1775 of 30.09.2026 (bulletin 2026/67) the CMB ordered an interim payment, on account of the final liquidation entitlement, to reconciled unitholders of the Tera, Pusula, Atlas and Hedef funds. The net investment amount calculated by the MKK is used; if below TRY 1 million it is paid in full, otherwise up to TRY 1 million, starting with money market funds, while units are redeemed only at final liquidation. The Hedef funds were removed from this procedure by decision 70/1821 of 15.10.2026 (bulletin 2026/70) and made subject to the order set for the A1 Capital, Bulls and Pardus funds.

Brokerages. The activities of Tera Yatırım Menkul Değerler AŞ were temporarily suspended under Law No. 6362 art. 96/1 as of 18:10 on 30.09.2026 and three persons’ licences were permanently cancelled (bulletin 2026/67). The activities of Pusula Yatırım, Bulls Yatırım and Alnus Yatırım Menkul Değerler AŞ were suspended under the capital adequacy rules and Law No. 6362 art. 97/1 as of 00:01 on 21.10.2026 (bulletin 2026/72). According to the press, on 22.09.2026 the CMB made unusual cash and asset outflows at six brokerages, including Tera and Pusula Yatırım, subject to approval (Bloomberg HT, 23.09.2026). The bulletins reviewed contain no investor compensation decision under Law No. 6362 art. 82 for these firms.

Criminal complaints and bans. The CMB decided to file criminal complaints under Law No. 6362 art. 107/1 against many persons over trading in shares such as KTLEV, GUNDG and DSTKF (bulletin 2026/59, 16.09.2026), OZATD (2026/65), TRHOL (2026/66), VSNMD (2026/70), ALKLC and ANELE (2026/73). In connection, Pusula Portföy (KTLEV), Tera Portföy, Tera Yatırım (OZATD, TRHOL, VSNMD, ALKLC/ANELE) and certain Tera group companies received two-year trading bans limited to the shares concerned (Law No. 6362 art. 101/1). Bulletin 2026/71 announced that, because the mandatory tender offer in Peker GYO was not made, shareholders may bring claims against Tera Yatırım Holding AŞ.

Investigation and trustee (press and company disclosures). The press reports that the investigation is conducted by the Istanbul Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office, with numerous detentions and arrests in September and October 2026. According to Tera Yatırım Teknoloji Holding AŞ’s disclosure of 19.10.2026, the Istanbul 5th Criminal Judgeship of Peace appointed the Savings Deposit Insurance Fund (TMSF) as trustee by decision 2026/10332 D. İş of 14.10.2026. According to the press, the banking regulator BDDK also vested shareholder rights in certain institutions, including Tera Yatırım Bankası, in the TMSF on 30.09.2026. As of 25.10.2026 no official information on an indictment was found.

Diagnosis First: Which Loss, Which Route?

  1. Unitholder of a fund in liquidation: the money is in the fund’s separate estate and is paid first from the liquidation. A fall in value is not covered by the ICC; damages may be sought from those responsible.
  2. Account holder at a suspended brokerage: shares are registered in your name with the MKK; a cash balance is the firm’s debt. If it cannot be returned, the ICC acts on a CMB compensation decision; any excess is pursued in gradual liquidation or bankruptcy.
  3. Direct investor in shares alleged to have been manipulated: the loss arises from price movements; the route is the criminal file and tort damages. Where public disclosure documents were false, a special liability subject to a short six-month limitation exists (Law No. 6362 art. 32).
  4. Shareholder denied a tender offer: the claim is brought by action against the party obliged to make the offer.

First Steps and the Evidence File

Many claims in these cases fail on proof rather than law. The Council of State upheld the dismissal of a claim by an investor who could not document the assets held at a brokerage whose activities were terminated (Council of State, 13th Chamber, E. 2015/5608, K. 2021/5348). Collect now, from official sources and dated:

  1. Account statements, fund purchase and sale receipts, TEFAS records and confirmations of unexecuted redemption orders.
  2. MKK records: balance and movement statements obtainable via e-YATIRIMCI and e-Government.
  3. The framework agreement, risk disclosure form, key investor information, the prospectus in force at the time; for hedge (serbest) funds, the qualified investor document.
  4. Correspondence, messages and recordings with the customer representative, and marketing material.
  5. Public disclosures, CMB bulletins, notices of the liquidating bank and the interim payment receipt.

Keep a timeline of knowledge, payment and notice dates; receiving an interim payment is not a waiver of claims, but no document containing a release should be signed.

Fund Liquidation, Interim Payments and Segregation of Fund Assets

An investment fund is an estate without legal personality established by a portfolio management company to operate a portfolio with money collected against units (Law No. 6362 art. 52/1). The investor’s strongest protection is segregation:

Statutory Provision — Capital Markets Law (Law No. 6362) art. 53 (Segregation of fund assets) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The assets of the fund are separate from the assets of the portfolio management company and of the institution providing portfolio custody. (2) … Even where the management or supervision of the portfolio management company or of the custodian is transferred to public authorities, the fund assets may not be disposed of for any other purpose, may not be attached, including for the collection of public receivables, may not be subject to an interim injunction and may not be included in a bankruptcy estate. (3) In the liquidation of the fund assets, payment may be made only to unitholders.”

The company’s bankruptcy, a trustee or a transfer to the TMSF does not expose fund assets to its creditors; but segregation protects against other people’s debts, not against loss of value. The interim payment is an advance on the final entitlement.

The portfolio management company must manage the fund so as to protect unitholders, and, absent special rules, the provisions on mandate apply by analogy (Law No. 6362 art. 52/3-4 and art. 55/6). Monitoring the portfolio’s compliance and the calculation of unit value under the valuation rules is the custodian’s task (Law No. 6362 art. 56/1-c, f):

Statutory Provision — Capital Markets Law (Law No. 6362) art. 56/2-3 (Portfolio custody and resulting liability) (unofficial translation)

“(2) The institution providing portfolio custody under this article is liable, for failure to perform its obligations, to the portfolio management company and unitholders in investment funds, and to the company in investment companies, for the damage it causes. (3) … The right of shareholders or unitholders to bring an action is reserved.”

An unusual concentration and valuation of allegedly manipulated shares in fund portfolios also raises the liability of the custodian of the pre-liquidation period. The liquidating banks assumed their role by CMB decision; the former custodian and the liquidating bank are different persons and liability must be examined period by period.

An investor who considers a CMB decision in the liquidation unlawful (for example, the interim payment cap or the order of liquidation) may bring an annulment action in the administrative court; such applications are treated as urgent (Law No. 6362 art. 134). The time limit is in principle 60 days (Law No. 2577 art. 7) and its start must be calculated from notification or publication. If the MKK reconciliation contains an error, apply in writing to the distributor and the liquidating bank, then complain to the CMB.

Brokerage Accounts and the Investor Compensation Center

The ICC is a public legal entity that implements CMB compensation decisions where investment firms cannot meet their obligations to pay cash or deliver capital market instruments (Law No. 6362 art. 3/1-y and art. 83). The precondition is a CMB “investor compensation decision”, to be taken within three months of establishing the failure (Law No. 6362 art. 82/1). A temporary suspension is not in itself a compensation decision.

Statutory Provision — Capital Markets Law (Law No. 6362) art. 84 (Scope of compensation) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Compensation covers claims arising from the failure to meet obligations to pay cash or deliver capital market instruments belonging to investors and held or managed on their behalf by the investment firm in connection with investment services and activities or ancillary services. (2) Investors of firms subject to an investor compensation decision are entitled to claim compensation under this article. Losses arising from investment advice or price movements in the market are not covered. … (5) The maximum compensation payable to each eligible investor is one hundred thousand Turkish lira. This amount is increased each year by the announced revaluation rate.”

Because the ceiling is reset every year, the amount applicable at the date of application should be confirmed from the official announcements of the Investor Compensation Center (ICC). The ceiling covers all claims of one investor against the same firm. The firm’s managers, shareholders with five per cent or more and their relatives, group companies and those responsible for the financial difficulty are excluded, and payments to persons subject to a criminal complaint for market fraud or laundering are suspended until a final judgment (Law No. 6362 art. 84/3-4).

If a compensation decision is announced, the application is made to the ICC in writing; the right is time-barred one year after the announcement and the ICC pays within three months of determining entitlements (Law No. 6362 art. 85/1-2). The creditors’ list and objections to it are governed by the Regulation on the Procedures and Principles of Investor Compensation and Gradual Liquidation; the objection period is short and is stated in the announcement. The excess over the ceiling is not lost: the CMB may order gradual liquidation, in which customer claims are paid first from the assets and pro rata if insufficient, no bankruptcy may be ordered and no enforcement under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law may be pursued until it is closed (Law No. 6362 art. 86/4-5).

Statutory Provision — Capital Markets Law (Law No. 6362) art. 97/3 (Measures in case of deterioration of financial condition) (unofficial translation)

“(3) The assets of capital market institutions whose activities have been temporarily suspended under paragraph one may not, from the date of the suspension decision until permission to resume activities is granted, except for transactions carried out by the Board, be transferred, pledged, given as security, made subject to an injunction or attached; all attachments and interim injunctions against them and all enforcement and bankruptcy proceedings automatically stop. Limitation and preclusive periods that can be interrupted by an enforcement step do not run.”

Because the activities of Pusula, Bulls and Alnus Yatırım were suspended under Law No. 6362 art. 97/1, to which this paragraph refers, no attachment or interim injunction may be applied against them during the suspension. Tera Yatırım was suspended under Law No. 6362 art. 96/1; the consequences of this difference for enforcement and interim measures must be assessed in each file. Temporary closure may not exceed two years (Law No. 6362 art. 97/4).

The Criminal Route: Complaints, Joining the Prosecution and Asset Measures

Offences

The CMB’s complaints rest on transaction-based market fraud: trading, placing orders or moving accounts to create a false or misleading impression of prices, supply or demand. The penalty is three to five years’ imprisonment and a judicial fine; paying twice the benefit obtained (at least TRY 500,000) to the Treasury earns a reduction for effective repentance (Law No. 6362 art. 107/1 and art. 107/3). Influencing prices by false information or rumours is information-based market fraud (Law No. 6362 art. 107/2), and trading on non-public information is insider dealing (Law No. 6362 art. 106). Using cash and instruments entrusted to an investment firm for one’s own or another’s benefit, and harming collective investment schemes through non-arm’s-length transactions with related persons, constitute aggravated breach of trust (Law No. 6362 art. 110/1-a, c). Depending on the facts, aggravated fraud (Law No. 5237 art. 158/1-h), criminal organisation (Law No. 5237 art. 220) and laundering (Law No. 5237 art. 282) may also arise; the prosecution decides the legal qualification, while the investor’s task is to set out facts and evidence concretely.

The CMB’s written application

Statutory Provision — Capital Markets Law (Law No. 6362) art. 115 (Written application and special investigation procedures) (unofficial translation)

“(1) An investigation into offences defined or referred to in this Law is conditional on a written application by the Board to the Chief Public Prosecutor’s Office. This application is a procedural precondition. (2) Where a public prosecution is brought upon the application, a copy of the indictment is notified to the Board upon its acceptance, and the Board also acquires the status of intervening party.”

For offences under Law No. 6362 the complaint should therefore be addressed to both the CMB and the public prosecutor. Which law the conduct falls under is for the prosecutor to assess; on that basis the Council of State upheld a ruling rejecting the claim that access to the prosecutor had been blocked (Council of State, 13th Chamber, E. 2021/3371, K. 2025/92). No such precondition applies to Penal Code offences. A CMB decision to take no action on a complaint is itself an administrative act that may be challenged by an annulment action (Council of State, 13th Chamber, E. 2021/88, K. 2023/4495).

Complaint, objection and joining the prosecution

During the investigation the victim and complainant may request evidence to be collected and have the file examined through counsel (Law No. 5271 art. 234), and may object to a decision not to prosecute before the criminal judgeship of peace within two weeks of notification (Law No. 5271 art. 173/1). Once a public prosecution is brought, the investor may join it:

Statutory Provision — Code of Criminal Procedure (Law No. 5271) art. 237 (Joining the public prosecution) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The victim, natural and legal persons harmed by the offence and those financially liable may join the public prosecution at any stage of the trial before the first-instance court until judgment by declaring that they are complainants. (2) No request to join may be made in appeal proceedings.”

Joining requires showing direct harm from the acts in the indictment; a fund investor should document which fund, when and in what amount units were held and how the alleged trades affected that fund. The criminal court does not, as a rule, award damages.

Seizure, trustees and making good the loss

Where there is strong suspicion based on concrete evidence that assets derive from the offence, a suspect’s real estate, accounts, receivables and company shares may be seized, on the basis of a report on the value obtained from bodies such as the CMB, BDDK or the financial intelligence unit MASAK (Law No. 5271 art. 128/1); fraud is among the offences for which this measure is available (Law No. 5271 art. 128/2). Within a limited list of offences (for example laundering) a trustee may be appointed to a company’s management (Law No. 5271 art. 133). These measures do not pay investors directly but preserve the prospect of enforcing a later damages award. In fraud and breach of trust, full compensation before prosecution reduces the sentence by up to two thirds, and before judgment by up to half; partial payment requires the victim’s consent (Law No. 5237 art. 168). Not every loss is a crime (see the decisions section); the criminal and damages routes should be pursued together.

Claim Registration and the Claim Registration Action in Bankruptcy

If the portfolio manager, a brokerage, group companies or directors are declared bankrupt, damages or restitution claims against them are pursued by registering them with the bankruptcy estate. As of 25.10.2026 no declared bankruptcy of companies in these groups was found. While a brokerage is in gradual liquidation no bankruptcy may be ordered (Law No. 6362 art. 86/4), and the CMB may request the personal bankruptcy of shareholders and directors whose responsibility has been established (Law No. 6362 art. 98).

Registration. The bankruptcy office announces the bankruptcy and calls creditors to register their claims and submit evidence within one month (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 219). The application should state the basis, amount, interest and requested rank, with receipts, statements and the MKK record attached. A creditor who gives an address for service and advances the notification costs is notified of the trustees’ decisions and the time limits for remedies run from notification (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 223/3). Late claims are accepted until the bankruptcy is closed, but the creditor bears the costs of delay and cannot share in earlier distributions (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 236).

Ranking table. The trustees prepare the ranking table within two months after the registration period and their election (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 232) and announce it; creditors whose claims are rejected are informed separately (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 234). A creditor whose claim is rejected or reduced brings a claim registration action:

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 235 (Objection to the ranking table and its effects) (unofficial translation)

“Those objecting to the ranking table must bring an action before the commercial court of the place where bankruptcy was declared within fifteen days of the announcement of the table. The provision of paragraph three of article 223 is reserved. … If the objector claims that his claim was wrongly rejected or reduced, the action is brought against the estate. If he objects to another creditor’s admitted claim or its rank, he brings the action against that creditor. … The action is heard under the simplified procedure. However, if the objection concerns not the existence or amount of the claim but only its rank, it is brought by way of complaint to the enforcement court.”

In practice: the time limit of 15 days is preclusive and runs from the last announcement or, for a creditor who advanced notification costs, from notification. The court is the commercial court of first instance where bankruptcy was declared. The defendant is the estate where one’s own claim was rejected, or the other creditor where that creditor’s claim is challenged. The burden of proof is on the claimant; if the damages claim has not yet been established by judgment, the action becomes a dispute on the merits. An objection to rank alone is brought by complaint to the enforcement court, and other acts of the trustees may be challenged by complaint within seven days of knowledge (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 16). In Court of Cassation practice mediation is not a precondition for this action; the ruling adding the mediation period to the preclusive period, where mediation had been sought, is an equitable solution (see the decisions section). The safe course is to sue within 15 days.

In the gradual liquidation of a brokerage by the ICC the liquidation rules of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law do not apply (Law No. 6362 art. 86/2); claims are determined by the ICC and the list and objection procedure of the Regulation is followed. In fund liquidation there is no bankruptcy estate; the MKK and bank reconciliation governs.

Private-Law Damages Actions

Possible defendants for loss not covered by the liquidation balance and any ICC payment:

  1. Portfolio management company: breach of the duty to protect unitholders’ interests; as mandate rules apply by analogy, the duty of care is measured by a prudent agent’s conduct (Code of Obligations art. 506).
  2. Directors: board members who culpably breach their duties are liable also to the company’s creditors (Commercial Code art. 553); several liable persons are jointly liable and may be sued together (Commercial Code art. 557). In the company’s bankruptcy, claims for the company’s loss are first asserted by the trustees (Commercial Code art. 556). Tort liability applies to acts alleged to be criminal (Code of Obligations art. 49).
  3. Custodian: liable for damage caused by failure to perform its obligations (Law No. 6362 art. 56/2).
  4. Distributing bank or brokerage: in particular where hedge funds reserved for qualified investors were sold to non-qualified investors, or risk disclosure and suitability were inadequate.
  5. Those responsible for public disclosure documents and independent auditors: for loss from false, misleading or incomplete information (Law No. 6362 art. 10, art. 32 and art. 63).

Court and mediation. Disputes arising from capital market transactions are, as a rule, commercial cases heard by the commercial court of first instance (Commercial Code art. 4/1-f). Consumer court jurisdiction is contested: the Assembly of Civil Chambers held the consumer court not competent for a leveraged trading contract expressly stated to be commercial, and a regional court finding denying consumer status because of trading volume and contract structure was upheld (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/6198, K. 2025/4684). The position of a retail investor who placed modest savings in a fund needs separate assessment; jurisdiction is a matter of public order. Mediation is a precondition for money and damages claims in the commercial court (Commercial Code art. 5/A) and, as a rule, in consumer disputes (Consumer Protection Law art. 73/A). From the application to the mediator until the final minutes, limitation is suspended and preclusive periods do not run (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A).

Limitation. In tort the period is two years from knowledge of the loss and the liable person and in any event ten years from the act; a longer criminal limitation period applies if the act is such an offence (Code of Obligations art. 72). Liability actions against directors are subject to two and five years with the same criminal exception (Commercial Code art. 560). Whether the five-year period for claims under a mandate (Code of Obligations art. 147/5) applies to the portfolio manager is debatable. Claims based on public disclosure documents are time-barred six months after the loss occurs (Law No. 6362 art. 32/6). Working to the shortest period is the safe course.

Partial action and precautionary attachment. The final amount of loss may not be known before liquidation ends. Law No. 7589 of 16.07.2026 repealed the action for an undetermined claim (Code of Civil Procedure art. 107) and allowed a one-off increase in a partial action:

Statutory Provision — Code of Civil Procedure (Law No. 6100) art. 109/4 (Partial action) (unofficial translation)

“(4) Where only part of a claim has been sued for, the subject of the claim may be increased once in the same proceedings, without being subject to the prohibition on extending the claim, until the end of the taking of evidence. In that case limitation is deemed interrupted from the date of the action also for the increased part.”

This makes it easier not to miss limitation while waiting for the liquidation outcome. If there is a risk of assets being hidden, precautionary attachment may be sought for a due money claim (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 257), bearing in mind that assets of institutions suspended under Law No. 6362 art. 97/1 cannot be attached during the suspension. Information on assets seized in the criminal file should be used in the application.

Full-Remedy Action Against the State: The CMB’s Duty of Supervision

The CMB is an independent public legal entity charged with regulating and supervising the market to ensure its reliable and stable functioning and to protect investors (Law No. 6362 art. 1 and art. 117). An investor who claims loss because supervision was not carried out properly and in time may bring a full-remedy action, open to those whose personal rights are directly harmed by administrative acts and actions (Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 2/1-b). Damages actions against Board staff for their official duties are deemed brought against the Board (Law No. 6362 art. 133/4).

Three elements are required together: fault in the service, actual loss and adequate causation. The Council of State found service fault on the part of the CMB and the BDDK for failing to prevent in time unauthorised sales to investors by a bank whose authorisation had been withdrawn and awarded damages; a dissent argued that no actual loss arose before participating in the bankruptcy liquidation. In a later file on similar facts, after investors had been paid under a special law, an administrative court ruling holding the CMB and BDDK liable was reversed for failing to take account of the new legal situation (Council of State, 13th Chamber, E. 2017/991, K. 2022/3711). By contrast, dismissals of claims by leveraged-trading investors, on the ground that the loss arose from the inherent and known risk of the transactions and causation was lacking, were upheld. What is decisive is linking the supervisory failure to concrete facts (which warning reached the CMB and when, why the concentration went unnoticed), which will largely depend on documents from the criminal and CMB examinations. The third-party crime defence did not remove fault in the 2005 decision; an informed choice of a high-risk product may, however, reduce damages for contributory fault.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 13 (Bringing a full-remedy action directly) (unofficial translation)

“1. Those whose rights have been infringed by administrative actions must, before bringing an administrative action, apply to the administration concerned to have their rights fulfilled within one year of learning of the actions by written notification or otherwise and in any event within five years of the date of the action. If these requests are rejected in whole or in part, an action may be brought within the time limit for actions from the day following notification of that decision or, if no reply is given within thirty days, from the expiry of that period.”

Steps: (1) a written prior application to the CMB, stating the act or omission, heads of loss and the amount claimed, within one year of knowledge and in any event five years of the act; (2) if there is no reply within thirty days, the request is deemed rejected; (3) an action in the administrative court within 60 days of the express or implied rejection (Law No. 2577 art. 7). If the loss results from a CMB decision, annulment and full-remedy actions may be brought together or after annulment (Law No. 2577 art. 12). The competent court is determined in the order set by Law No. 2577 art. 36: for decisions, the place of the authority that made the decision (Law No. 2577 art. 32), for actions, the place where the service was performed. The Board’s seat is stated by law to be Istanbul (Law No. 6362 art. 117/1); jurisdiction is a matter of public order. The administration will argue that no loss has arisen before liquidation ends; since the period runs from knowledge, apply in time, calculate the loss on current data and file later developments.

The Constitutional Court and the ECtHR

Once ordinary remedies are exhausted, an individual application may be made to the Constitutional Court alleging that a public act, action or omission violated a right protected by the Constitution and the European Convention on Human Rights (Law No. 6216 art. 45); the time limit is thirty days from exhaustion (Law No. 6216 art. 47/5). The rights at stake may be property, trial within a reasonable time and access to court; ordinary market risk is not shifted to the State. An individual application does not replace full-remedy or damages actions; it follows the final decision and requires a concrete violation. An application to the ECtHR may then follow, subject to the Convention’s admissibility requirements and time limit.

Common Mistakes

  1. Expecting the ICC to cover a fall in fund value: losses from price movements are excluded.
  2. Relying only on the criminal file: the criminal court does not award damages and limitation keeps running.
  3. Applying only to the prosecutor for Capital Markets Law offences: the CMB’s written application is a procedural precondition.
  4. Not advancing notification costs at registration: the action period then runs from announcement and may be missed.
  5. Waiting for liquidation to end before a full-remedy application: the one-year period runs from knowledge.
  6. Missing the six-month period for disclosure-document claims.
  7. Unsupported claims and signing releases.

Decisions of the Court of Cassation and the Council of State

Claim registration action: 15-day period, notification and mediation

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/4026, K. 2024/1590, 16.05.2024 (the Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; in the case at hand the mediation period was added to the preclusive period)

“As regulated in the article, the period for bringing an action objecting to the ranking table is 15 days. The period starts from the announcement of the ranking table. … For these creditors the period starts not from the announcement of the ranking table but from the date of this notification. … In the practice of the Court of Cassation, recourse to mediation before bringing a claim registration action is not accepted as a precondition.” (translation)

Jurisdiction: consumer courts and investment contracts concluded for commercial purposes

Court of Cassation Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/2348, K. 2019/82, 07.02.2019 (majority reasoning; leveraged trading contract, the insistence decision was upheld by majority)

“As explained above, for a transaction to be subject to consumer law the consumer must not act for commercial or professional purposes, and since the contract in dispute expressly states that it was made for commercial purposes, the consumer courts cannot be regarded as competent to resolve the dispute.” (translation)

A brokerage’s breach of contract is not always fraud

Court of Cassation Decision
Assembly of Criminal Chambers, E. 2022/330, K. 2023/75, 08.02.2023 (the Assembly’s reasoning; the objection was rejected)

“as a result of judgments of the civil courts in favour of the intervening parties, upon their bringing claims not against the defendants but against ... Menkul Kıymetler AŞ seeking both compensation of their losses and payment of the money remaining in their accounts … it must be accepted that the defendants’ acts cannot be qualified as fraudulent conduct constituting the act element of the offence of fraud and that the elements of the offence of fraud attributed to them were not established.” (translation)

Service fault: failure to supervise and take measures in time

Council of State Decision
13th Chamber, E. 2005/2625, K. 2005/5753, 02.12.2005 (decision of the Chamber as court of first instance; the CMB and BDDK were found at fault, majority on damages)

“The concept of service fault generally denotes malfunctions, deficiencies and defects in the organisation and functioning of a public service. In other words, service fault is accepted where malfunctions, irregularities, defects, disorder or deficiencies arise because administrations obliged by law to establish the organisation that will make the public service work, to provide the necessary organisation, to carry out inspections and to take measures fail to perform these obligations properly and in time.” (translation)

Causation: materialisation of a risk known to the investor

Council of State Decision
13th Chamber, E. 2019/3190, K. 2024/2309, 21.05.2024 (finding of the administrative court; leveraged trading, appeal dismissed, upheld by the Chamber)

“this loss, arising from the materialisation of the risk inherent in the transactions and known to the claimant from the outset, did not result from the services within the defendant administration’s remit not functioning or functioning late or badly, and adequate causation between the loss and the administration’s actions cannot be said to exist” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Will the Investor Compensation Center cover my loss in a liquidated fund?

As a rule, no. The ICC covers claims arising from an investment firm’s failure to deliver cash or instruments held on your behalf after a compensation decision; losses from price movements are excluded (Law No. 6362 art. 84). The fund claim is paid first from the liquidation; damages may be sought from those responsible for the remaining loss.

I received an interim payment; what should I do about the rest?

The interim payment is an advance on the final liquidation amount. Follow the final distribution and MKK reconciliation and do not sign releases. For uncovered loss, use mediation and litigation before limitation expires; a partial action may be increased once (Code of Civil Procedure art. 109/4).

Can I complain directly to the prosecutor?

Yes, but for offences under the Capital Markets Law such as market fraud the investigation depends on the CMB’s written application (Law No. 6362 art. 115). Address your complaint to both the CMB and the prosecutor. No such condition applies to Penal Code offences such as aggravated fraud or laundering.

When and where must I bring the claim registration action?

Within 15 days of the announcement of the ranking table, before the commercial court of the place of bankruptcy; if you advanced notification costs, the period runs from notification (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 235). The action is against the estate if your claim was rejected, or against the other creditor if you challenge that creditor’s claim. Mediation is not a precondition.

Can I bring a full-remedy action against the CMB, and when does time start?

Yes. First apply in writing to the CMB within one year of learning of the act and in any event within five years; if there is no reply within thirty days the request is deemed rejected, and the action is brought in the administrative court within 60 days of rejection (Law No. 2577 art. 13). Service fault, actual loss and causation must be shown concretely.

Which court hears a damages action?

As a rule the commercial court of first instance, subject to prior mediation (Commercial Code art. 5/A). The Assembly of Civil Chambers did not regard the consumer court as competent for an investment contract expressly stated to be commercial; whether a retail investor is a consumer depends on the specific contract.

Criminal, enforcement and bankruptcy and administrative proceedings concerning fund and brokerage losses fall within our Criminal Law, Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law and Administrative Law practice areas.
