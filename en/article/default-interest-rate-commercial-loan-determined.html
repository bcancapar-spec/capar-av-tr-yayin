Default Interest on Turkish Commercial Loans: Determination, Contractual Rate Plus 100%, Central Bank Rate

Short answer: On a commercial loan, the default interest rate is determined by the general loan agreement (genel kredi sözleşmesi) and by any payment schedule the borrower signed. Because interest rates are free in commercial matters (Turkish Commercial Code art. 8/1), the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu) does not apply the ceiling in art. 120 of the Turkish Code of Obligations to commercial loans (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1650, K. 2019/507, 02.05.2019). Where the agreement ties default interest to a benchmark such as the highest rate reported to the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye (TCMB) or set by the bank, plus 50% or 100%, the 11th Civil Chamber takes the lower of the rate the bank actually applied to the same type of loan on the default date and the rate it reported to the TCMB, and adds the contractual uplift to that rate (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4616, K. 2026/1587, 23.03.2026). Contractual interest runs until the default date and default interest thereafter. If the agreement sets no default rate at all, the advance rate may be claimed in commercial matters where it exceeds statutory interest, and if the contractual rate is higher, default interest cannot be lower than the contractual rate (Law No. 3095 art. 2/2-3). A surety who did not sign the payment schedule is subject not to the schedule’s rate but to the rate in the general loan agreement he or she signed (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/430, K. 2024/3891, 14.05.2024).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

On Turkish commercial loans default interest follows the contract, without the art. 120 cap, on the lower of the bank’s actual and TCMB-reported rates. The article covers Law No. 3095 art. 2 where no rate is agreed, plus-100% clauses, sureties, restructuring, BSMV, interest on interest and the Law No. 7589 amendment, with 2019–2026 Court of Cassation decisions.

Default interest (temerrüt faizi) on commercial bank loans under Turkish law: freedom of interest rates under art. 8 of the Turkish Commercial Code, non-application of the cap in art. 120 of the Turkish Code of Obligations, art. 2 of Law No. 3095, “contractual rate plus 50% or 100%” clauses, the rate reported to the Central Bank versus the rate actually applied, sureties, restructuring, BSMV, the ban on interest on interest, the Law No. 7589 amendment and Court of Cassation decisions.

Is the Default Rate on a Commercial Loan Free, and Does the Cap in Art. 120 of the Code of Obligations Apply?

On a commercial loan, the default interest rate is primarily determined by the general loan agreement between the bank and the borrowing company. The Turkish Commercial Code leaves the interest rate free in commercial matters; provisions on consumer protection are excluded from this freedom:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102), art. 8/1, 8/3 (Freedom of rate and conditions for compound interest) (unofficial translation)

“(1) In commercial matters the interest rate is determined freely. … (3) Provisions on consumer protection are reserved.”

The Turkish Code of Obligations, by contrast, provides that the annual default interest rate agreed by contract may not exceed the annual interest rate determined under the legislation by more than one hundred per cent (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 120/2). In a bank loan dispute coming from the Denizli 1st Commercial Court of First Instance, the Assembly of Civil Chambers held that this cap does not apply to commercial loans: arts. 8 and 9 of the Commercial Code are special provisions for commercial matters, and arts. 88 and 120 of the Code of Obligations cannot be applied to them. The local court had capped default interest at 20.63%, i.e. the advance rate plus 50%; the Assembly reversed that insistence decision by majority, while the dissenting opinion argued that the ceiling in art. 120 should also apply to default interest in commercial matters (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1650, K. 2019/507, 02.05.2019).

The 11th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation has likewise stated that under art. 8 of the Commercial Code the default interest rate may be freely agreed in commercial matters and that the default interest clause of the general loan agreement binds the parties (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/9135, K. 2023/655, 07.02.2023).

Which Rate Applies if the Agreement Sets No Default Rate?

If the default interest rate has not been agreed, Law No. 3095 on Statutory Interest and Default Interest applies. In commercial matters two rules operate together:

Statutory Provision — Law No. 3095 on Statutory Interest and Default Interest, art. 2/2-3 (Default interest) (unofficial translation)

“If the interest rate applied by the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye on 31 December of the previous year for short-term advances exceeds the amount stated above, default interest may be claimed at that rate in commercial matters even without an agreement. … Where the amount of default interest has not been agreed in the contract, if the amount of contractual interest is above the amount provided in the preceding paragraphs, default interest may not be less than the amount of contractual interest.”

The 11th Civil Chamber likewise states that, unless otherwise agreed, the default rate in commercial matters is determined by the advance rate (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/9135, K. 2023/655). Where the rates have been left blank, the decisive figure is the contractual rate applied by the bank. In an action for annulment of objection heard by the Denizli Commercial Court of First Instance, the Chamber set aside a regional appellate court decision that had applied arts. 88 and 120 of the Code of Obligations to an agreement with blank rates and used a default rate of 13.75%; it required the contractual rate the bank applied to the same type of commercial loan in the same period to be determined from the bank’s records and applied as the default rate as well (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/9064, K. 2023/3414, 31.05.2023).

According to the reversal decision of 27.10.2020, E. 2020/2407, K. 2020/4454, as reported in the Chamber’s affirming decision of 25.10.2023, where default interest has not been agreed or cannot be determined, the basis is not the TCMB list of weighted average rates but the average of the default rates applied by state banks to the same type of commercial loan in the same period (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/6496, K. 2023/6151, 25.10.2023; affirmed).

How Are “Contractual Rate Plus 100%” and “Highest Rate Reported to the Central Bank” Clauses Applied?

The general loan agreements in the decisions reviewed mostly define default interest by a benchmark: the highest lending rate reported to the TCMB or set by the bank plus 50%, or the contractual rate plus 100%. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, contractual rates that banks report to the TCMB but do not apply to their customers are not taken into account in determining default interest; the contractual rate applied to the loan on the date the account was closed is determined, contractual interest runs until the default date, and thereafter default interest calculated with the contractual uplift applies (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1650, K. 2019/507).

The 11th Civil Chamber describes this as its “settled and consistent practice”: the expert examines the bank’s records, on site if necessary, the rate the bank actually applied to the same type of loan on the default date is compared with the rate it reported to the TCMB, and the contractual uplift is added to the lower one. On 23.03.2026 the Chamber reversed on this ground a judgment awarding 48% default interest on the basis of the list of rates reported to the TCMB (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4616, K. 2026/1587). In a 2022 reversal the same test was expressed as the contractual rate “in favour of the debtor” (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4966, K. 2022/8843, 08.12.2022).

A numerical example: in the decision the Chamber affirmed on 30.03.2026, the contractual rate actually applied to a euro loan was 6.60%, so the default rate was found to be 13.20% with the contractual 100% uplift, and the highest rate reported to the TCMB was not used (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5089, K. 2026/1811, 30.03.2026; affirmed).

The rate actually applied is proved from the bank’s records. In the borrower’s negative declaratory action (menfi tespit), the Chamber reversed a decision that had upheld 72% default interest on a claim arising from a cashed letter of guarantee on the basis of TCMB notifications obtained through UYAP (the national judicial network); it stated that those rates are the rates declared by the bank and that a panel of experts must carry out an on-site examination of the bank’s records (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4436, K. 2026/1290, 05.03.2026). Nor may the court find the rate “excessive” and set a new rate according to market conditions: the Chamber held that an expert opinion treating the 94.5% rate claimed in the enforcement proceedings as excessive and adding 50% to the contractual rate was contrary to the contract and the legislation, and required the lowest of the rate actually applied and the rate reported to the TCMB to be used (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/8902, K. 2022/8980, 13.12.2022).

Practice is not entirely uniform: on 22.10.2025 the Chamber affirmed by majority a judgment that, according to the dissenting opinion, awarded 72% default interest on the basis of the rate reported to the TCMB (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1415, K. 2025/6433, 22.10.2025).

Which Rate Applies to a Surety Who Did Not Sign the Payment Schedule?

A surety is bound by the default interest clause in the document he or she signed. According to the regional appellate court decision affirmed by the 11th Civil Chamber on 14.05.2024, although the payment schedule of a restructuring loan stated a default rate of 39%, the sureties had not signed that schedule, so the calculation for the sureties had to follow the default interest clause of the general loan agreement; the 25.02% rate found by the expert under that clause was upheld (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/430, K. 2024/3891).

If the suretyship agreement contains its own default interest clause, that clause applies, and again the rate actually applied is used. In the decision affirmed by the Chamber on 07.11.2024, the clause in the suretyship agreement was applied because it did not make the sureties’ liability heavier than the principal debtor’s; 41%, i.e. the 20.50% contractual rate actually applied by the bank plus one hundred per cent, was used, and the 36% rate reported to the TCMB was disregarded (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6371, K. 2024/7874, 07.11.2024; affirmed).

The surety’s default date is a separate issue; in the decision affirmed by the Chamber on 30.03.2026, sureties to whom no account closure notice (hesap kat ihtarnamesi) had been served were held to be in default on the date of the enforcement proceedings, with contractual interest applying until then (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5089, K. 2026/1811). This issue is discussed in the article From Which Date Does a Surety Who Received No Acceleration Notice Owe Default Interest?

Which Rate Applies if a Restructured Loan’s Payment Schedule Is Not Followed?

The rescheduling undertaking in the case decided by the 11th Civil Chamber on 08.12.2022 provided that, if the payment schedule was not followed, the privileges granted would lapse retroactively and default interest would be applied at the rate under the general loan agreement. The Chamber stated that, since the borrowers had not complied with the payment terms, the contractual rate agreed in the undertaking (15% in that case) could no longer be taken into account. In the Chamber’s view, the more favourable to the debtor of the contractual rate the bank reported to the TCMB and the contractual rate it actually applied to the same type of loan must be determined, and default interest is found by adding the contractual 100% uplift to it (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4966, K. 2022/8843; Chamber’s reasoning for reversal).

Is BSMV Added to Default Interest, and May Interest Run on Interest?

Judgments are seen to award, together with default interest, banking and insurance transactions tax (BSMV) at 5% of the interest; the 11th Civil Chamber affirmed a judgment ordering collection of the claim with 39% default interest and 5% BSMV on the interest (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5009, K. 2025/3418, 15.05.2025). BSMV is not added automatically: in a case affirmed by the Chamber, the court of first instance left the BSMV it had calculated out of the judgment, noting that the bank had not claimed BSMV on the cash receivable in the enforcement proceedings (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6371, K. 2024/7874). The heirs of a surety who had not signed the general loan agreement and whose liability arose from the mortgage deed were also not held liable for BSMV because the mortgage deed did not cover BSMV, and the bank’s appeal was dismissed (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/6496, K. 2023/6151). Whether the mortgage limit covers interest and BSMV is the subject of a separate article.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098), art. 121/3 (Default interest on interest, annuities and gifts) (unofficial translation)

“No further default interest may be charged on default interest.”

Compound interest may not be charged when calculating statutory and default interest; the provisions of the Commercial Code are reserved (Law No. 3095 art. 3). The Commercial Code allows interest to be added to the principal and to bear interest again only in current accounts and in loan agreements that are commercial for both parties, for periods of not less than three months and between merchants; interest charged contrary to this is null and void (Turkish Commercial Code art. 8/2, 8/4). In the decision affirmed by the Chamber on 15.05.2025, the regional appellate court accepted that the bank could capitalise contractual interest accrued up to the account closure date as principal, but that no further default interest could run on the part consisting of default interest (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5009, K. 2025/3418). Running interest again from the filing date on a total formed by adding interest accrued until the filing date to the principal, however, breaches the ban on interest on interest; the Chamber therefore reversed a judgment awarding 52.80% default interest, calculated from a 26.40% contractual rate under a “contractual rate plus 100%” clause (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/2316, K. 2022/5149, 22.06.2022).

Current Position (November 2026): What Did Law No. 7589 Change?

Art. 1 of Law No. 3095, which governs statutory interest, was amended by art. 10 of Law No. 7589 of 16.07.2026, and the amendment entered into force on 31.07.2026:

Statutory Provision — Law No. 3095 on Statutory Interest and Default Interest, art. 1 (Statutory interest) (unofficial translation)

“… in cases where interest must be paid, if its amount has not been fixed by contract, payment is made annually at eighty per cent of the rediscount rate applied by the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye on 31 December of the previous year to short-term lending transactions.”

According to the Law’s table of amendments, Law No. 7589 amended only art. 1; art. 2, which governs default interest, remains unchanged. The amendment has no direct effect on commercial loans where a default rate has been agreed. Where no rate has been agreed, the general default interest in art. 2 is now tied to the rate in the new art. 1; in commercial matters the advance rate may be claimed if it is higher, and at least the contractual rate if that is higher still (Law No. 3095 arts. 1-2). The explanations in the Assembly of Civil Chambers decision of 02.05.2019 based on the then text of art. 1 are therefore no longer current; the decision’s conclusion based on art. 8/1 of the Commercial Code is unaffected by the amendment. The limited search carried out for this article did not identify a Court of Cassation decision applying the amended art. 1 to default interest on a commercial loan.

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below are decisions of the Assembly of Civil Chambers and the 11th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation dated 2019–2026. The rates mentioned in them are specific to those cases.

The interest caps in arts. 88 and 120 of the Code of Obligations do not apply to commercial loans; a rate reported to the Central Bank but not applied is not used

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1650, K. 2019/507, 02.05.2019 (reversal of the lower court’s insistence decision; by majority)

“… it must be accepted that arts. 8 and 9 of Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 are special provisions for commercial matters and must be applied in commercial matters, in other words, that the provisions of arts. 88 and 120 of Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 cannot be applied in commercial matters. … However, in the practice of the Court of Cassation it is accepted that contractual rates which banks report to the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye but do not apply to their customers are not to be taken as the basis in determining default interest …” (translation)

The contractual uplift is added to the lower of the rate actually applied and the rate reported to the Central Bank

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4616, K. 2026/1587, 23.03.2026 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; action for annulment of objection against a surety; unanimous)

“… the interest rate(s) actually applied by the claimant Bank on the default date to the same type of loan(s) subject to the enforcement proceedings being established, and, whichever of the rate(s) actually applied or reported to the TCMB is lower, the contractual clause on the default rate being applied to the lower rate so as to determine the default rate that may be claimed …” (translation)

Rates reported to the Central Bank are the bank’s own declaration; the rate actually applied is found by on-site examination of the bank’s records

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4436, K. 2026/1290, 05.03.2026 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; cashed letter of guarantee, negative declaratory action; unanimous)

“The default rate actually applied by banks is the rate applied between customers and the bank in a free competitive environment, and the rates whose printouts were obtained from the system by the Court of First Instance and the Regional Court of Appeal are rates declared by the defendant bank.” (translation)

Where the rates in the agreement are left blank, the contractual rate the bank applies to the same type of loan also serves as the default rate

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/9064, K. 2023/3414, 31.05.2023 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; unanimous)

“Under this clause, although the contractual and default rates were not specified in the agreement, under the clause on contractual interest the contractual rate applied by the bank to the same type of commercial loans in the same period must be determined by examining the bank’s records and that contractual interest must be applied. Likewise, since no default rate was specified in the clause of the agreement either, the contractual rate to be determined will also have to be applied as default interest.” (translation)

A surety who did not sign the payment schedule is liable at the default rate in the general loan agreement

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/430, K. 2024/3891, 14.05.2024 (affirmed; from the reasoning of the affirmed regional appellate court decision; unanimous)

“… although the default rate was stated as 39% in the payment schedule, the defendant sureties’ signatures did not appear beneath the payment schedule, and for the sureties the calculation had to be made under clause 12, governing default interest and its rate, of the general loan agreement with a limit of TL 600,000.00 dated 09.10.2012 signed between the claimant bank and the principal debtor company …” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the default interest rate on a commercial loan in Türkiye?

The law sets no fixed default rate for commercial loans; the rate is determined by the agreement (Turkish Commercial Code art. 8/1). For example, decisions show 39% applied to a loan with a 26% contractual rate and a 50% uplift (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5009, K. 2025/3418), and 41% applied to a loan with a 20.50% contractual rate actually applied and a 100% uplift (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6371, K. 2024/7874).

Can default interest on a commercial loan exceed the cap in art. 120 of the Code of Obligations?

Yes. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, the caps in arts. 88 and 120 of the Code of Obligations do not apply to commercial loans; the decision was taken by majority (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1650, K. 2019/507). Consumer loans fall outside this freedom (Turkish Commercial Code art. 8/3).

What happens if the loan agreement does not state a default rate?

In commercial matters the advance rate may be claimed even without an agreement if it exceeds statutory interest; if the contractual rate is higher, default interest cannot be lower than the contractual rate (Law No. 3095 art. 2/2-3). Where the rates in the agreement are left blank, the contractual rate the bank applies to the same type of loan is also applied as default interest (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/9064, K. 2023/3414).

Is a surety liable for the higher default rate in the payment schedule?

Not if the surety did not sign the payment schedule; although the schedule stated 39%, the calculation for sureties who had not signed it followed the default interest clause of the general loan agreement (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/430, K. 2024/3891).

Is BSMV added to default interest?

Judgments are seen to award BSMV at 5% of the interest together with default interest (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5009, K. 2025/3418). If the bank did not claim BSMV in the enforcement proceedings, the court does not add it to the judgment (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6371, K. 2024/7874).
