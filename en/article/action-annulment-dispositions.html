Short answer: The action for annulment of dispositions renders transfers, gifts or similar transactions made by a debtor to hide assets from a creditor ineffective only against the claimant creditor. It is brought by a creditor holding a certificate of insolvency. If the action succeeds, ownership stays with the third party, but the creditor may have the asset attached and sold up to the amount of its claim and ancillaries (EBL art. 283). The action must be brought within five years of the disposition (EBL art. 284). EBL art. 278 on gratuitous dispositions was rewritten by Law No. 7571, in force since 25.12.2025.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

What Is an Action for Annulment of Dispositions?

The action aims to render certain dispositions — actually valid ones made by the debtor before insolvency or bankruptcy — ineffective against the creditor, so that the claim can be collected by continuing forced execution over that asset. It does not seek to recover ownership from the third party; it gives the creditor the power to request attachment and sale of the asset in the third party’s hands. For this reason the Court of Cassation’s Civil General Assembly describes it as an action of a relative (in personam) nature, not one concerning the asset in rem.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 277 (Action for annulment and claimants) (unofficial translation)

“The purpose of the action for annulment is to have the dispositions set out in articles 278, 279 and 280 declared void. The following persons may bring this action: 1 – Any creditor holding a provisional or definitive certificate of insolvency; 2 – The bankruptcy administration or, in the cases set out in article 245 and article 255(3), the creditors themselves.”

According to the Civil General Assembly, the dispositions that may be annulled under EBL arts. 278-280 are not listed exhaustively; whether a disposition is annullable is for the judge to assess within the principles of those articles. The claim need not specify which article it relies on, and the court may annul on the basis of another article.

Dispositions Subject to Annulment (EBL Arts. 278, 279, 280)

Gratuitous dispositions and transactions deemed donations (Art. 278)

EBL art. 278 was entirely rewritten by Law No. 7571, adopted on 24.12.2025 and published in the Official Gazette of 25.12.2025, which entered into force on publication. Under the new text, all donations and gratuitous dispositions other than customary gifts made within one year before the provisional or definitive certificate of insolvency, an attachment record having the nature of such a certificate, or the opening of bankruptcy, are subject to annulment.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 278 (Voidness of gratuitous dispositions) (unofficial translation)

“Other than customary gifts, all donations and gratuitous dispositions made within one year before the date on which a provisional or definitive certificate of insolvency, or an attachment record having the nature of a certificate of insolvency, was drawn up or bankruptcy was opened, are subject to annulment. The following dispositions are deemed donations: a) Unless proven to have been made for consideration corresponding to their real value, dispositions between descendants and ascendants, blood relatives up to and including the third degree, the spouse even if the marriage ended within the last year, relatives by marriage up to and including the third degree, adopter and adoptee, and persons living in a common residence. b) Unless proven otherwise, contracts in which, at the time of conclusion, the debtor accepted as consideration a price very low compared with the real value of what it gave. c) Unless an appropriate consideration is proven, contracts by which the debtor established a life annuity or usufruct in its own favour or in favour of a third party, or contracts of maintenance until death.”

Three consequences stand out. First, the look-back period has been reduced to one year and is expressly linked to the date of the certificate of insolvency, the attachment record having its nature, or bankruptcy. Under the previous text the Court of Cassation applied a two-year period counted back from the date of the attachment record treated as a certificate of insolvency (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3860, K. 2025/3719, 05.03.2025). Second, treating dispositions between close persons as donations is now a rebuttable presumption, and the burden of proving consideration at real value lies on the acquirer; the scope has widened to blood and marital relatives up to the third degree, a spouse whose marriage ended within the last year, and persons living in a common residence. As recounted by the Civil General Assembly, the word “by blood” in the former text had been annulled by the Constitutional Court in 2018; the new text brings dispositions between siblings back within the scope through a rebuttable presumption. Third, contracts at a very low price and life-annuity, usufruct and maintenance contracts are likewise deemed donations unless the contrary is proven.

Law No. 7571 contains no transitional provision for EBL art. 278. Which text applies to dispositions made before the amendment and to pending cases has not yet been settled by case law. The Civil General Assembly referred to the amendment in its decision of 18.06.2026 but decided the case under EBL art. 280. In pending cases, the claim should therefore also rely on EBL art. 280 where possible.

Voidness on account of insolvency (Art. 279)

Certain pledges and unusual payments made by a debtor who does not pay its debts within one year before attachment, insolvency or bankruptcy are also void. Transferring real property in lieu of payment is a typical example.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 279 (Voidness on account of insolvency) (unofficial translation)

“The following dispositions are likewise void if made by a debtor who does not pay its debts within one year before attachment, insolvency due to lack of assets, or the opening of bankruptcy: 1 - Pledges given by the debtor to secure an existing debt, except where the debtor had previously undertaken to provide security; 2 - Payments made otherwise than in money or by customary means of payment; 3 - Payments of debts not yet due. 4. Annotations entered in the land register to strengthen personal rights. If the person who benefited from these dispositions proves that it did not know the debtor’s situation, the action for annulment shall not be heard.”

Annulment for intent to cause harm (Art. 280)

This is the provision most often relied on in practice. Transactions made by a debtor whose assets are insufficient for its debts, with intent to harm its creditors, may be annulled where this was known to the other party or there were clear indications requiring it to know. The article contains two statutory presumptions: one for close relatives and one for transfers of a commercial enterprise.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 280 (Annulment for intent to cause harm) (unofficial translation)

“All transactions made by a debtor whose assets are insufficient to meet its debts, with intent to harm its creditors, may be annulled where the debtor’s financial situation and the intent to harm were known to the other party or there are clear indications requiring that they be known. Provided that, within five years of the date of the transaction, attachment or bankruptcy proceedings must have been brought against the debtor. … If the third party is the debtor’s spouse, ascendant or descendant, or a blood or marital relative up to the third degree (inclusive), adopter or adoptee, it is presumed to have known the debtor’s situation described in the first paragraph. The third party may prove the contrary only under the last paragraph of article 279. … A person who takes over or buys all or a substantial part of a commercial enterprise or of the commercial goods at the place of business, or who acquires part of them and then occupies the place of business, is deemed to have known of the debtor’s intent to harm its creditors, and the debtor is deemed to have acted with that intent.”

The enterprise-transfer presumption can be rebutted only by proving written notice to the creditor at least three months before the transfer or announcement in the manner required by law. Because the second paragraph has been repealed, decisions refer to this rule sometimes as the third and sometimes as the last paragraph.

Conditions of the Action

In addition to the general procedural conditions, the action has special conditions, which the Civil General Assembly groups into four: a genuine claim against the debtor, enforcement proceedings that have become final, a disposition made after the debt arose, and a certificate of insolvency issued against the debtor. The same criteria appear in the settled case law of the abolished 17th Civil Chamber (17th Civil Chamber, E. 2011/1502, K. 2011/13240, 29.12.2011).

Certificate of insolvency and attachment record

A certificate of insolvency may be provisional or definitive; the definitive certificate is regulated in EBL art. 143. If no attachable assets are found, the attachment record has the effect of a certificate of insolvency; if, on the values assessed, the attachable assets are insufficient, the record serves as a provisional certificate and gives the creditor the rights in EBL art. 277 (EBL art. 105). The certificate is a condition of the action, but the defect can be cured at any stage, even on appeal; if it is missing, the court must give the claimant time. EBL art. 277 lists the bankruptcy administration as a claimant separately from the certificate requirement. In a counter-action for annulment brought by the attaching creditor against a third party’s ownership action, no certificate is required (EBL art. 97).

The debt must have arisen before the disposition

The disposition must have been made after the claim arose. The test is the date the debt arose, not the date of the enforcement proceedings. In practice the date of the loan agreement is used for loan claims and the issue date for negotiable instruments. A transfer made before the debt arose cannot, as a rule, be the subject of this action; one cannot hide assets from a creditor who does not yet exist.

The conditions must continue throughout the proceedings; if the enforcement proceedings lapse and the attachments are lifted, the legal interest may disappear.

Time Limits: Look-Back Periods and the Five-Year Time Bar

Two kinds of period must not be confused. Look-back periods determine whether a disposition falls within a ground of annulment: one year before the certificate of insolvency, attachment record or bankruptcy for EBL art. 278; one year before attachment, insolvency or bankruptcy for EBL art. 279; and, for EBL art. 280, attachment or bankruptcy proceedings brought within five years of the transaction. The period for bringing the action is five years from the disposition.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 284 (Time bar) (unofficial translation)

“The right to bring the action for annulment lapses on the expiry of five years from the date of the void disposition.”

The five-year period is a time bar examined by the court of its own motion. For real property, the date of the disposition is, as a rule, the date of the land-registry transfer.

Who Brings It and Against Whom (Procedure)

The action is brought by a creditor holding a certificate of insolvency or, in bankruptcy, by the bankruptcy administration; in the cases set out in the law the creditors themselves may also sue. It is brought against the persons who transacted with the debtor or received payment from it and their heirs; third parties acting in bad faith may also be sued, and the action does not infringe the rights of third parties in good faith (EBL art. 282). There is a compulsory joinder between the debtor and the third party who acquired the asset.

If the asset has passed to subsequent holders, the creditor may convert the action into a money claim without joining the later acquirer, or join it and seek annulment of the dispositions in the chain. For a fourth party, a gross disparity in value alone is not enough and the close-relative presumption in EBL art. 280 does not apply; its bad faith must be proven separately. If bad faith is not proven, the action is not dismissed: the debtor and the person who acquired from it are ordered to pay compensation in proportion to the real value at the date the asset left their hands, limited to the claim (12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/8185, K. 2026/3456, 13.05.2026).

Competent Court, Venue and Value in Dispute

Subject-matter jurisdiction: under the settled view of the Court of Cassation, actions for annulment of dispositions are not absolute commercial cases; being personal and directed at the debtor’s dispositions, they are heard by the civil court of first instance. Since the Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) entered into force on 01.07.2012, the relationship between civil and commercial courts is one of jurisdiction, which is a matter of public order examined of the court’s own motion (17th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/13234, K. 2018/2256, 13.03.2018). Although it is argued that the action should be treated as a relative commercial case where both parties are merchants and the transaction concerns their businesses, as at the date of this article the prevailing trend in Court of Cassation decisions favours the civil court.

Venue: even where real property is concerned, the action is personal, not in rem, so the exclusive venue of the place of the property does not apply; general venue rules govern. As a rule the court of the defendant’s domicile is competent; where there are several defendants, the action may be brought where any of them is domiciled.

Value in dispute and court fees: according to the Court of Cassation, the value in dispute is the lower of the amount of the claim on the date of the enforcement proceedings and the value of the asset; court fees and attorney’s fees are determined on that value (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/1274, K. 2021/4390, 09.07.2021).

The chamber at the Court of Cassation: these cases were long examined by the 17th Civil Chamber and, after its abolition, by the 4th Civil Chamber; decisions issued from late 2025 show the 12th Civil Chamber examining them. Precedent research should cover all three chambers.

Procedure, Precautionary Attachment, Proof and Presumptions

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 281 (Procedure in actions for annulment) (unofficial translation)

“The court hears and decides actions for annulment by summary procedure and freely assesses and resolves the related disputes in the light of the circumstances. The judge may, at the creditor’s request, order a precautionary attachment over the assets that are the subject of the annullable dispositions. Whether security is required, and its amount, is assessed and determined by the court. However, where the action concerns the value substituted for assets that have been disposed of, a precautionary attachment may not be ordered without security. If any of the defendants pays the claimant’s claim, the action is dismissed. In that case the judge, depending on the circumstances, orders each of them to pay costs or apportions the costs among them as the judge sees fit.”

Precautionary attachment: a precautionary attachment over the asset should be sought when filing or during the proceedings. It prevents further transfers that lengthen the chain and convert the action into a money claim. The court decides whether security is required, but where the action concerns the value substituted for assets disposed of, no attachment may be ordered without security. See our article on precautionary attachment.

Payment: if any defendant pays the claimant’s claim, the action is dismissed and the costs are allocated according to the circumstances.

Proof and presumptions: according to the Civil General Assembly, the “clear indications” in EBL art. 280/1 are presumptions of fact drawn from ordinary experience of life. Outside the statutory presumptions, the claimant bears the burden of proving that the third party knew of the debtor’s situation and intent; under the statutory presumptions it is enough to prove the relationship or the enterprise transfer, and the burden of proving the contrary shifts to the third party. In practice the following are treated as indications: a gross disparity between the real value established by an expert and the price in the land register, the absence of a bank record of payment, successive transfers close to the date the debt arose, the parties living at the same address or working together, and the debtor continuing to use the asset after the transfer. The court assesses the evidence freely.

Consequences of the Action (EBL Art. 283)

If the action succeeds, the creditor obtains the power to enforce its right over the asset by forced execution; for real property no correction of the register is needed and attachment and sale may be requested directly. The power is limited to the claim and ancillaries in the enforcement file and is exercised through the same file. If the asset has left the third party’s hands, the action becomes a money claim and the third party is ordered to pay compensation in cash, up to the claim, in proportion to the value substituted for the asset.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 283 (Scope of restitution) (unofficial translation)

“If the action for annulment is established, the claimant obtains the power to enforce its right by forced execution over the asset that is the subject of the action and, where the subject of the action is real property, may request its attachment and sale without any need to correct the registration in the name of the defendant third party. Where the action for annulment concerns the value substituted for assets that the third party has disposed of, the third party is ordered to pay compensation in cash in proportion to those values (not exceeding the claimant’s claim). … A third party that loses the action for annulment may, in return, claim back the thing or its price from the debtor or the bankruptcy estate.”

The third party may claim from the debtor, in the same action, the reduction its assets will suffer. A donee acting in good faith must return only what it still holds at the time of the action.

Public Receivables: Differences under Law No. 6183

For tax and similar public receivables, the annulment of dispositions is separately regulated in Law No. 6183 arts. 24-31 on the Collection Procedure of Public Receivables. These actions are also heard by the ordinary courts, with priority over other matters (Law No. 6183 art. 24). The main differences from the EBL regime are:

  • A different test replaces the certificate of insolvency: it is required that the debtor failed to declare its assets in time, declared it had none, or declared assets insufficient for the debt (Law No. 6183 art. 27).
  • Different look-back periods: for gratuitous dispositions and for pledges and unusual payments, the period covers two years before the start of the payment period and the time after it began (Law No. 6183 art. 29).
  • No time limit for transactions intended to defeat collection: transactions made to prevent collection of the public receivable are void whatever their date (Law No. 6183 art. 30).
  • The same five-year period for the action: no action may be brought after five years from the disposition (Law No. 6183 art. 26).
  • A narrower close-relative presumption: the sub-paragraph of Law No. 6183 art. 28 deeming dispositions between close relatives donations was annulled by the Constitutional Court’s decision of 22.06.2023.
  • Precautionary attachment by administrative decision: the administration may impose a precautionary attachment on the assets of the impugned disposition without a court order (Law No. 6183 art. 13).

For public receivables, the administration may also proceed directly against partners and legal representatives, as discussed in the corporate section below.

Annulment of Dispositions in Commercial Companies

Transfers of company assets to a partner or group company, share transfers made because of a partner’s personal debt, and contributing assets to a new company as capital may all qualify as “dispositions” under EBL art. 277 et seq. Which ground of annulment applies depends on the nature of the transaction.

Transfer of company assets to a partner, manager or group company

The close-relative list in EBL art. 278 is built on kinship, marriage and common residence between natural persons; it does not cover partnership or management relationships. Transfers of company assets to a partner or group company are therefore usually assessed under the “knew or should have known” test in EBL art. 280/1. In Court of Cassation practice, an organic connection (the same or related persons as partners or managers, the same address, the same line of business, shared staff and customers) is a strong indication. The 4th Civil Chamber did not find erroneous, in respect of the joined action, a judgment allowing the claim on the basis of an organic connection between the partners of the debtor and of the third-party company, although it reversed the judgment on other grounds (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/2013, K. 2021/4391, 09.07.2021). Giving assets to a partner for an existing claim, or paying a claim not yet due, should also be assessed under EBL art. 279. For the manager’s personal liability, see our article on a company manager transferring company money to a personal account.

Share transfers because of a partner’s personal debt

A partner’s transfer of its limited-company shares or joint-stock shares to a relative or another partner after the debt arose may be the subject of the action. The 4th Civil Chamber upheld a regional court decision in a case where a guarantor transferred limited-company shares after the loan agreements; the regional court had treated the attachment records in the enforcement file as certificates of insolvency under EBL art. 105 and found the conditions of the action satisfied (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/10736, K. 2025/7209, 06.05.2025; the case became devoid of subject matter because the transfer was separately set aside in the bankruptcy process). The form of the transfer and general assembly approval are separate issues, discussed in our article on share transfers in a limited company. The shares themselves can also be attached: where no share certificate has been issued in a joint-stock company, attachment is effected by notice to the company, and a transfer of attached shares is void to the extent it infringes the creditor’s rights (EBL art. 94).

Contributing assets to a new company as capital in kind

A debtor’s contribution of real property or a business to a newly formed company as capital in kind is also a disposition. Because the debtor receives shares in return, the transaction is not automatically gratuitous; but a disparity in values, a prompt transfer of the shares and management of the company by relatives are indications of intent to harm. The creditor may also have the debtor’s shares in the new company attached.

Transfer of a commercial enterprise

Where all or a substantial part of a commercial enterprise or its goods is transferred, the presumption in EBL art. 280 applies: the acquirer is deemed to have known of the intent to harm and the debtor to have acted with it. What is “substantial” depends on the structure of the business; the 12th Civil Chamber held that transferring the only service vehicle forming part of the debtor’s business to a company in a similar line of business was a transfer of a substantial part of the enterprise (see the principle box below). In addition, a person taking over assets or a business with its assets and liabilities becomes liable for the debts from notification or announcement to creditors, and the former debtor remains jointly liable for two years (Code of Obligations art. 202). This liability can be a more direct collection route, independent of annulment; see our article on transfer of a commercial enterprise.

Organic connection, commingling of assets and piercing the corporate veil

Piercing the corporate veil means holding a partner directly liable in exceptional cases where the company’s separate personality is abused to harm creditors. The Court of Cassation applies it narrowly and points first to annulment of dispositions and simulation for transactions depleting company assets. Nevertheless, the 11th Civil Chamber regarded the payment of company funds to a partner as a “partnership receivable” after a claim had been filed as commingling of assets, and reversed the judgment on the ground that the partner should be held liable for that amount (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/9060, K. 2022/9632, 29.12.2022). In groups of companies, the liability of the controlling company for unlawful use of control should also be considered (Commercial Code art. 202).

Asset-stripping through mergers and demergers

Mergers and demergers transfer assets from one company to another by universal succession, and the law protects creditors through special rules. In a merger, if creditors so request within three months after the merger becomes legally effective, the acquiring company must secure their claims (Commercial Code art. 157). In a demerger the claims of creditors who so request are secured (Commercial Code art. 175); if the company allocated the debt does not pay, the other participating companies are jointly liable on a secondary basis under the statutory conditions (Commercial Code art. 176). In schemes such as moving assets to a debt-free company before a demerger or allocating the debt to a company without means, the transfers may also be assessed under EBL art. 280.

Limited-company partners and legal representatives: the link with Law No. 6183 art. 35

For public receivables, the administration may proceed directly against partners and legal representatives for amounts that cannot be collected from the company, without an annulment action. Limited-company partners are directly liable, in proportion to their capital shares, for public receivables that cannot be collected from the company; on a share transfer, transferor and transferee are jointly liable for public receivables relating to the period before the transfer (Law No. 6183 art. 35). The liability of legal representatives, including limited-company managers, is regulated separately in Law No. 6183 repeated art. 35, under which public receivables that cannot be collected from the legal person’s assets are collected from the legal representatives’ personal assets.

This direct liability does not apply to private creditors; a commercial creditor can, as a rule, reach a partner’s personal assets only on bases such as annulment of dispositions, simulation, piercing the veil or the manager’s personal liability.

Annulment of Dispositions vs. Simulation (Heir’s Simulation)

The action for annulment of dispositions renders a genuinely made and valid disposition ineffective against the creditor and is subject to special conditions such as the certificate of insolvency and the five-year period. Simulation is where the parties make an apparent transaction they do not in fact intend; a simulated transaction is void from the outset, may be asserted against everyone and is, as a rule, not subject to a time limit. Depending on the circumstances, the creditor may rely on one of these routes or on both in the alternative. For heir’s simulation, specific to a testator hiding assets from an heir, see our article Decedent’s Simulated Transactions (Hiding Assets from Heirs).

Practical Points

For the creditor:

  • Once the proceedings are final, an attachment should be attempted at the debtor’s known addresses and the absence of attachable assets recorded; that record serves as a certificate of insolvency.
  • Land, vehicle and trade registers should be searched for transfers made after the debt arose.
  • For each disposition, the period for the action and the look-back periods should be calculated separately.
  • The claim should rely on EBL arts. 278, 279 and 280 together and request a precautionary attachment.
  • If the asset has changed hands, evidence of the subsequent acquirers’ bad faith should be prepared separately.

For the debtor and the third party: that the price matched the real value and was actually paid should be proven by bank records; after Law No. 7571, this burden is expressly on the acquirer in transactions between close persons. On the transfer of a commercial enterprise, written notice to creditors at least three months before the transfer, or a proper announcement, is the statutory way to rebut the presumption in EBL art. 280.

For companies: share transfers, payments to partners and intra-group transfers made after a debt arose should be justified by commercial books and bank records, and creditors’ rights to security in mergers and demergers should be respected.

Court of Cassation Decisions

All the decisions below were verified in full text from the official case-law database.

The purpose of the action and the certificate of insolvency as a condition of the action (curable even on appeal)

Court of Cassation Decision
17th Civil Chamber, E. 2018/4344, K. 2020/5506, 13.10.2020 (the Chamber’s reasoning)

“The purpose of the actions for annulment of dispositions regulated in articles 277 et seq. of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law is to render certain dispositions — actually valid ones made by the debtor before insolvency or bankruptcy — ineffective against the creditor as void or on the ground of ‘contravention of good-faith rules’, and thereby to secure collection of the claim by continuing forced execution over that asset. The action may be brought by a creditor holding a provisional or definitive certificate of insolvency (art. 277). This is a condition of the action, which the judge must observe directly by virtue of office. However, this deficiency may be cured at any stage of the proceedings, even at the appeal stage.” (translation)

Annulment on the basis of intent to harm and a striking difference in value; consequence: a power of forced execution limited to the claim and ancillaries

Court of Cassation Decision
17th Civil Chamber, E. 2018/2085, K. 2019/12356, 23.12.2019 (first-instance determination upheld)

“… noting that the disposition was made after the debt arose and carried the intent to harm creditors, and that there was a large difference between the sale price and the real value of the real property, the action was allowed … the disposition over the real property was annulled and the creditor was granted the power to carry out forced execution, limited to the claim and its ancillaries.” (translation)

Time must be given to the claimant to cure a missing certificate of insolvency

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2958, K. 2025/4639, 19.03.2025 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning)

“The certificate of insolvency may be produced during the proceedings and also at the Court of Cassation stage. … In that case, appropriate time should be given to the claimant’s counsel to produce the certificate of insolvency … and rendering judgment as written is not correct.” (translation)

A relative action, its special conditions and the art. 280/3 presumption for a transfer to a sibling (Civil General Assembly, 2026)

Court of Cassation Decision
Civil General Assembly, E. 2025/655, K. 2026/384, 18.06.2026 (reversal of a judgment of resistance; unanimous)

“For this reason the action for annulment is an action of a relative nature that does not concern the real property in rem and enables the creditor to collect its claim. … The special conditions of the action for annulment are that the claimant has a genuine claim against the defendant debtor, that the enforcement proceedings against the debtor have become final, that the impugned disposition was made after the debt arose, and that a certificate of insolvency has been obtained against the debtor. … For the claimant creditor to benefit from the statutory presumption, it is sufficient to prove that there is kinship of the stated degree between the debtor and the third party.” (translation)

Transferring the only service vehicle in the business is a transfer of a “substantial part of the enterprise”; the action becomes a money claim

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/3303, K. 2026/3692, 20.05.2026 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning)

“since it is understood that there was no other vehicle of this kind in the debtor’s commercial enterprise and that the debtor therefore transferred the only vehicle forming part of its enterprise to the defendant company, the disposition must be accepted, under the last paragraph of article 280 of the EBL, as a transfer of a substantial part of the commercial enterprise … [the company] should have been held liable for the price in proportion to the real value of the vehicle at the date it disposed of it (limited to the claimant’s claim and ancillaries)” (translation)

A creditor’s action for annulment based on simulation is not subject to the five-year time limit in Article 284 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Assembly of Civil Chambers)

Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers (the Assembly’s reasoning)
Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu, E. 2017/2249, K. 2021/146, T. 23.02.2021

“As is clearly understood from the subject of the statement of claim, the manner in which the material facts are described and the relief sought, the action is an action for annulment based on the legal ground of simulation as defined in Article 18 of the Code of Obligations. In that case, since there is no action brought under Articles 277 et seq. of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, there is no need to look for the conditions in those articles. Actions for annulment based on an allegation of simulation may be brought at any time.” (unofficial translation)

A creditor alleged that, shortly after an action for payment was brought against the debtor, the debtor bought a flat in the name of his spouse, and that the transaction was simulated; he sought annulment of the disposition and attachment and sale of the flat in the enforcement file. The action was brought more than five years after the disposition. The first-instance court treated it as an action for annulment of dispositions under Articles 277 et seq. of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, dismissed it as time-barred and insisted on that judgment against the reversal of the 17th Civil Chamber. The Assembly of Civil Chambers held that characterising the facts is the judge’s task, that the petition relied on simulation and that no forfeiture or limitation period applies to allegations of simulation, so the merits had to be examined; it reversed the insistence judgment by majority. The view raised in the deliberations, that the action was based on Articles 277 et seq. and that Article 283 of that Law could not be applied by analogy in a simulation action, was not adopted by the majority; the dissent defended it. The decision concerns the period of the former Code of Obligations No. 818, art. 18 (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 19), and was rendered under the former procedural code with rectification of decision still available.

Property leaving the defendant third party by court judgment: the action automatically becomes a money claim (Assembly of Civil Chambers)

Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers (the Assembly’s reasoning)
Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu, 2023/174 E., 2023/711 K., 05.07.2023

“If the third party who is a party to the action transfers the immovable in dispute while the action to set aside the disposition is pending, the action automatically becomes a claim for value; its subject becomes the value (price) replacing the asset that left the third party’s hands (Law No. 2004, art. 283/2).” — “In that case the action to set aside the disposition has automatically become a claim for value under art. 283/2 of Law No. 2004.” (unofficial translation)

In that case, while the action was pending, the person to whom the third party had promised to sell the property won an action for cancellation and registration of title and the judgment became final; although no registration had yet been made in the new owner’s name, ownership had passed by court judgment (TMK art.705). The Assembly therefore held that a judgment setting aside the first sale and ordering the sale of the property could not be enforced and that the third party should be held liable for the value of the property at the date it left his hands, and reversed the insistence judgment by majority. The dissent considered that the property could be sold once the precautionary attachment became final.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an action for annulment of dispositions?

It renders dispositions such as transfers made by the debtor to hide assets ineffective only against the claimant creditor. It does not recover ownership; it gives the creditor the power of attachment and sale over that asset.

Is a certificate of insolvency required to bring the action?

Yes; a provisional or definitive certificate is a condition of the action. An attachment record showing no attachable assets has the same nature (EBL art. 105). The defect can be cured even on appeal.

Which dispositions are subject to annulment?

Gratuitous dispositions and transactions deemed donations within one year before the certificate of insolvency or bankruptcy, certain pledges and unusual payments in the same period, and transactions made with intent to harm that the other party knew or should have known of.

Against whom do I bring the action?

Jointly against the debtor and the third party who acquired the asset (EBL art. 282). Bad-faith subsequent acquirers may be joined; if bad faith is not proven, the action becomes a money claim.

What is the time limit for the action?

The action must be brought within five years of the annullable disposition (EBL art. 284). Separately, the one-year look-back periods for the relevant ground and the requirement that proceedings be brought within five years of the transaction must be met.

Can a partner’s assets be pursued for a company debt?

As a rule, no. If company assets were moved to a partner or group company, annulment of dispositions, simulation or, exceptionally, piercing the veil may apply. For public receivables, partners and legal representatives may be directly liable under Law No. 6183 art. 35 and repeated art. 35.
