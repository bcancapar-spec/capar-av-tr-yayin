Annulling Tailor-Made Academic Job Postings in Türkiye: University Vacancies Drafted for One Candidate

Short answer: Universities may attach special conditions to an academic post that reflect scientific quality and actual service needs, but those conditions may not be narrowed to the point of describing a particular candidate. The regulations prohibit this expressly, and in the decisions of the 8th Chamber of the Council of State reviewed here, postings and appointments were annulled where the condition matched one candidate’s thesis topic or where the link to the department’s needs could not be shown concretely. Objective experience or language-of-instruction requirements that anyone can meet and document were, by contrast, upheld. The action is generally brought before the administrative court within sixty days of written notification or knowledge of the act; an annulment does not by itself result in the claimant’s appointment.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Annulment actions against academic job postings tailored to one candidate: Constitution, Law No. 2547, regulations, time limits, standing, Council of State rulings.

A guide to annulment actions against “tailor-made” academic job postings at Turkish universities and the appointments based on them: the constitutional guarantees of equality and access to public service, the appointment procedure for assistant professor, associate professor, professor, lecturer and research assistant posts under the Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547), the regulatory ban on “special conditions identifying a particular candidate”, standing, time limits, competent court, stay of execution, the 2026 appeal reform and verified decisions of the 8th Chamber of the Council of State.

What Is a Tailor-Made Job Posting?

What is popularly called a “tailor-made” or “address-delivered” posting is an announcement of a vacant academic post whose eligibility conditions are shaped around the CV of a candidate chosen in advance rather than around the post’s real needs. It is not a legal term; the regulations and the courts describe the same phenomenon as a “special condition identifying a particular candidate” or a “condition describing a person”. Typical forms are copying a candidate’s doctoral or specialty thesis title almost verbatim into the posting, combining several unrelated and very narrow research topics in one posting, or requiring experience or project work that only an in-house research assistant can show.

The legal problem with such a posting is that competition is eliminated at the application stage. Candidates who cannot meet the condition either cannot apply or are eliminated at pre-screening, and the jury evaluation no longer compares scientific merit. The dispute is therefore reviewed through the cause and purpose elements of the administrative act: is the condition based on the public interest and service needs, or was discretion used to place a specific person in the post?

Constitutional Framework: Equality and Access to Public Service

Academic posts are public posts, so job postings are subject to the constitutional provision on the right to enter public service. Its second sentence supplies the constitutional yardstick for the tailor-made posting debate: only the qualifications required by the post may be taken into account.

Statutory Provision — Constitution of the Republic of Türkiye (Law No. 2709) art. 70 (Entry into service) (unofficial translation)

“Every Turk has the right to enter public service. No criteria other than the qualifications for the office concerned shall be taken into consideration for recruitment.” (unofficial translation)

This guarantee is read together with the principle of equality before the law in Constitution art. 10, whose last paragraph requires state organs and administrative authorities to act in compliance with that principle in all their proceedings, and whose fourth paragraph prohibits privileges for any individual, family, group or class. A condition that cannot be linked to the qualifications required by the post creates an unjustified distinction between equally qualified candidates.

Universities, on the other hand, are public legal entities with scientific autonomy under Constitution art. 130, and the appointment and promotion of academic staff is regulated by law. Scientific autonomy includes deciding in which specialty a department needs a faculty member, but this is a discretion limited by the procedure and purpose set by law. The court does not decide which specialty should be recruited; it reviews whether the condition is linked to service needs and whether it closes competition around a particular candidate.

Posting and Appointment Procedure by Type of Post

Academic posts fall under two regimes. Faculty posts (assistant professor, associate professor, professor) are governed by Law No. 2547 and the Regulation on Promotion and Appointment to Faculty Membership; lecturer and research assistant posts are announced and filled under the Regulation on the Central Examination and Entrance Examinations for Appointments to Teaching Staff Posts Other Than Faculty Members (the “Central Examination Regulation”).

Assistant professor (doktor öğretim üyesi)

Assistant professor posts are announced by the rectorate; written opinions are obtained from three professors or associate professors on each candidate, the dean or director submits a proposal to the rector after the board of directors’ opinion, and the appointment is made for up to four years. Paragraph (c) provides that additional conditions may be set only with the approval of the Council of Higher Education (YÖK), exclusively to improve scientific quality, and in an objective and verifiable manner.

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 23 (Assistant professor) (unofficial translation)

“a) Vacant assistant professor posts at higher education institutions shall be announced by the rectorate. For the posts announced, the dean in faculties and the directors in other units shall designate three professors or associate professors, one being the head of that unit and one from outside that institution, and request a written opinion on each candidate. … c) Higher education institutions may, with the approval of the Council of Higher Education, set additional conditions of an objective and verifiable nature for appointment to assistant professor posts, aimed exclusively at improving scientific quality and taking into account the differences between scientific disciplines.” (unofficial translation)

The Regulation on Promotion and Appointment to Faculty Membership lays down the same announcement rule for assistant professor, associate professor and professor posts: publication in the Official Gazette and on the university’s website, with an application period of at least fifteen days. It also requires the jury to be designated within fifteen days of the application deadline and the board of directors to state reasons for its preference where there are several candidates.

Regulatory Provision — Regulation on Promotion and Appointment to Faculty Membership art. 5 (Announcement) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Vacant assistant professor posts at a university or institute of advanced technology shall be announced by the rectorate in the Official Gazette and on the website of the university or institute. The announcement shall give candidates an application period of not less than fifteen days and state the application deadline.” (unofficial translation)

Associate professor and professor

Appointment to an associate professor post first requires the title of associate professor awarded by the Inter-University Council; institutions may set additional conditions with YÖK approval and, again, exclusively to improve scientific quality (Law No. 2547 art. 24, para. (d)). For applicants to an announced associate professor post the rector designates three professors, at least one from outside the university, and appoints on the decision of the board of directors taking their reports into account. For professorships the jury consists of five professors, at least three from other universities.

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 26 (Promotion and appointment to professorship) (unofficial translation)

“In addition to these minimum conditions for promotion to professorship, universities may, with the approval of the Council of Higher Education, set additional conditions of an objective and verifiable nature, aimed exclusively at improving scientific quality and taking into account the differences between scientific disciplines. b) For promotion to professorship: 1) Professorship posts to be filled at universities or institutes of advanced technology shall be announced by the rectorate.” (unofficial translation)

Lecturer and research assistant

Candidates for lecturer posts must hold at least a master’s degree with thesis, except for vocational school specialty areas designated by YÖK (Law No. 2547 art. 31). Applicants for research assistant posts must not have reached the age of thirty-five as of 1 January of the year of the examination (Law No. 2547 art. 33, para. (a)). For these posts the procedure consists of a pre-assessment based on ALES and foreign-language scores, an entrance examination held by a jury and a final assessment; results are published at the web address given in the posting.

The clearest rule on tailor-made postings is in the Central Examination Regulation. It prohibits special conditions identifying a particular candidate, other than scientific, objective and verifiable conditions relating to the fields of graduation and the announced field, and requires even an experience condition to state the level of education after which it is counted, in a way that does not identify a candidate.

Regulatory Provision — Central Examination Regulation art. 6/5-6 (General conditions) (unofficial translation)

“(5) Higher education institutions may not include, in postings for teaching staff posts subject to this Regulation, special conditions identifying a particular candidate other than scientific, objective and verifiable conditions relating to the undergraduate or graduate fields and to the announced field. (6) In postings, the condition of having experience in the field shall state the level of education after which it is counted, in a manner that does not identify a particular candidate.” (unofficial translation)

The same Regulation requires the posting to set out the conditions together with an examination calendar covering the pre-assessment, entrance examination and result dates, and the application deadline may not be less than fifteen days from the posting. Because the calendar determines when and where results will be published, it also matters for the start of the time limit for bringing an action.

Institutional appointment criteria, additional conditions and YÖK oversight

The “appointment and promotion criteria” adopted by university senates rest on the statutory power to set additional conditions and require YÖK approval. For faculty posts, a 2021 amendment to the Regulation expressly prohibits writing thesis titles into the posting and including special conditions that identify only a particular candidate. YÖK oversight also matters in practice; in one decision reviewed here the inspection report of the Higher Education Supervisory Board was taken into account by the court.

Regulatory Provision — Regulation on Promotion and Appointment to Faculty Membership art. 3/3 (General conditions) (unofficial translation)

“The titles of candidates’ graduate or specialty theses may not be written, in whole or in part, as an application condition, nor may the posting contain special conditions identifying only a particular candidate.” (unofficial translation)

Recent Legislative Changes (2020–2026)

This article reflects the texts in force on 25 October 2026. Since a dispute is, as a rule, governed by the text in force when the act was adopted, the text applicable at the date of an older posting must be checked separately. The main changes are:

  • Law No. 7243 (15.04.2020): the master’s-with-thesis requirement for lecturer posts and the age limit of thirty-five for research assistant posts were written into the Law; the sentence on electronic access to associate professorship jury reports was redrafted.
  • Amendment to the Regulation on Promotion and Appointment (09.03.2021): writing thesis titles into postings and special conditions identifying only a particular candidate were prohibited.
  • Regulatory amendments of 06.03.2026: the paragraphs in the assistant professor, associate professor and professor sections of the Regulation on Promotion and Appointment that applied the procedure “other than the announcement requirement” to foreign academics employed under contract were repealed. On the same day a new paragraph on the selection of doctoral students in priority fields and related research assistant appointments was added to the Central Examination Regulation.
  • Law No. 7589 (adopted 16.07.2026, Official Gazette 31.07.2026): the appeal and cassation system of the Administrative Procedure Law was changed. Cassation is now available against decisions given anew by a regional administrative court after quashing the first-instance decision (Law No. 2577 art. 46/2); the cases in which the regional court may remit the file to the first-instance court were narrowed (Law No. 2577 art. 45/5). Law No. 2576 art. 7 on single-judge cases was also amended; that change applies to actions filed after the Law entered into force.

When Is a Special Condition Unlawful?

The decisions of the 8th Chamber reviewed here show that the lawfulness of a posting condition cannot be reduced to a single test; several questions are answered together. The criteria below are drawn from those decisions, and each is assessed in the specific file together with expert reports and the reasons given by the administration.

1. Does the condition describe a candidate?

The clearest unlawfulness is a condition that in fact matches one candidate’s CV. In an assistant professor posting in Early Modern History, the only other applicant was found to be a research assistant in the same department whose doctoral thesis topic matched the condition; the condition was held to identify a particular candidate and the rejection of the claimant’s application and the appointment were annulled. This finding became final after appeal and cassation (first decision below). What matters is not only the wording but who can actually meet the condition. In another file concerning an associate professor posting, the finding in the expert report relied on at first instance that “other academics also meet the condition” was not confirmed on appeal, and the posting and appointment were annulled (second decision below).

2. Has the link to service needs been shown concretely?

The condition must rest on the teaching, research and clinical needs of the unit. In a medical faculty posting requiring three unrelated and narrow research topics, it was found that the conditions derived from the appointed candidate’s unpublished specialty thesis and two multi-author studies, and that the administration had submitted no concrete information or documents showing that they were imposed for service needs; the posting and appointment were annulled. The decision was upheld on appeal and cassation (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2023/2255, K. 2024/5140, 09.10.2024). In practice the decisive question is whether the need was documented before the posting: department requests, staffing plans and the department’s teaching and clinical workload have evidential value.

3. An additional condition requiring YÖK approval, or a post-specific condition?

The Law makes additional conditions subject to YÖK approval. The courts distinguish general and objective additional criteria adopted by a university senate from a post-specific condition placed in a single posting. The latter does not require YÖK approval, but it is not exempt from review: discretion must be used in light of needs and service requirements, not by setting person-specific conditions. An objection that “YÖK approval was not obtained” therefore succeeds only against a general additional criterion; against a post-specific condition the real issue is whether it describes a person and how it relates to needs.

4. Experience, foreign language and language of instruction

Not every restrictive condition is unlawful. The Council of State upheld a lecturer posting condition of “having taught theoretical courses in the field at higher education institutions for at least two semesters”, given that lecturers are employed to teach, that anyone documenting the minimum experience could apply and that twelve candidates had applied for two posts. In that decision YÖK’s view that the condition “reduces the number of applicants even if it does not identify a person” was not followed because competition had not in fact been prevented. Similarly, a requirement of teaching experience at institutions where “the language of instruction is English” in an assistant professor posting was held at first instance to be a post-specific, quality-enhancing condition not pointing to a specific person; the appeal was dismissed and the decision was upheld by the Council of State. The common thread: a condition describing an objective, documentable qualification that is open to a broad pool of candidates is not tailor-made.

5. Publication, period and later stages

Unlawfulness may also be procedural: failure to publish in the Official Gazette or on the website, failure to observe the fifteen-day minimum application period, an examination calendar missing from the posting, a jury formed contrary to the rules, or an unreasoned preference by the board of directors between several candidates. Courts often treat the question whether a condition is tailor-made as a technical one and order an expert examination; contradictions between expert reports must be resolved. In one file the regional court, finding a supplementary report insufficient, sought the opinions of five universities and then ordered a new expert examination.

Standing and Legitimate Interest

An annulment action may be brought by those whose interests are infringed by an administrative act (Law No. 2577 art. 2/1-a). The Council of State defines this as a personal, legitimate and current interest and determines its existence according to the nature of the case. In job posting disputes this means:

  • A candidate who applied and was eliminated or not appointed: standing is not normally disputed; the candidate may challenge the rejection, the condition and the appointment of another candidate together.
  • A person unable to apply because of the condition: in the decisions reviewed, the merits were examined in an action brought by a person who could not apply because she did not meet the condition, against the posting, the examination and the appointments. Such a claimant should show that she meets all other requirements and was excluded from competition by the condition.
  • Faculty members and the head of department: in a 2026 decision the 8th Chamber held that department faculty members seeking annulment of the posting had no current, personal and legitimate interest and dismissed that part of the action for lack of standing, while upholding the judgment on the merits as regards the head of department. The dissent considered that standing should have been denied for all claimants, including the head of department (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/5928, K. 2026/896, 18.02.2026). By contrast, in an action brought by the head of a department at a medical faculty against an associate professor posting and appointment, the merits were examined and the acts were annulled (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2021/3774, K. 2023/7368, 19.12.2023). As there is no firm line, non-candidate faculty members should explain their interest concretely (duties, management responsibility, effect on teaching load).
  • Trade unions and professional bodies: none of the decisions reviewed concerned a posting challenged by such bodies; their standing is assessed under the general interest test, their statutory aims and their link to the act.

Time Limit, Subject-Matter and Territorial Jurisdiction

Time limit

As no special period applies, the time limit before administrative courts is sixty days, running from the day after written notification.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 7 (Time limit for bringing an action) (unofficial translation)

“1. Unless a different period is laid down in special laws, the time limit for bringing an action is sixty days before the Council of State and administrative courts and thirty days before tax courts. 2. These periods shall start, a) in administrative disputes, on the day following the date of written notification …” (unofficial translation)

In academic recruitment, results are often not notified individually but published on the university’s website. The Council of State upheld a judgment holding that the claimant had learned of the result at the latest on the date it was published on the university website, that the sixty-day period ran from that date either directly or via an application to the superior authority, and dismissing the late action (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/5171, K. 2024/5830, 12.11.2024). The claimant’s objections that the date of publication had become uncertain and that the act should have been notified to her did not change the outcome.

The way to suspend the period is to apply, within the time limit, to the superior authority, or failing that to the authority that adopted the act, for its withdrawal or amendment; if no reply is given within thirty days the request is deemed rejected and the period resumes (Law No. 2577 art. 11). If the posting itself is to be challenged, the most prudent course is to act within sixty days of its publication; a candidate who wishes to challenge the posting together with the appointment should take the date on which the appointment was announced or learned of and target both acts in the same petition. In the decisions reviewed, the posting, the rejection, the examination and the appointment were challenged together in one action.

Competent court

Annulment actions are heard by administrative courts (Law No. 2576 art. 5). As a general rule the competent court is the administrative court where the authority that adopted the act is located (Law No. 2577 art. 32); in actions concerning the appointment and transfer of public servants, the court of the new or former place of duty is competent (Law No. 2577 art. 33/1). In most cases both rules point to the administrative court where the university is located. Territorial jurisdiction is a matter of public order; an action filed with a court lacking jurisdiction is dismissed on that ground and the file is sent to the competent court (Law No. 2577 art. 15/1-a), which costs time.

Stay of Execution

Filing an action does not automatically suspend the posting or the appointment. A stay requires both that the act is manifestly unlawful and that its implementation would cause damage that is difficult or impossible to remedy. Since the Law does not treat appointments of public servants as acts whose effect is exhausted on implementation, a stay in such cases may be granted only after the administration’s defence has been received or the defence period has expired.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 27/2 (Stay of execution) (unofficial translation)

“2. The Council of State or administrative courts may, giving reasons, order a stay of execution after the defendant administration’s defence has been received or the period for defence has expired, where implementation of the administrative act would cause damage that is difficult or impossible to remedy and the act is manifestly unlawful, both conditions being met together. … However, administrative acts concerning the appointment, transfer, change of duty or title, and temporary or permanent assignment of public servants shall not be deemed acts whose effect is exhausted upon implementation.” (unofficial translation)

In tailor-made posting cases “manifest unlawfulness” often cannot be shown without an expert examination, so a request for a stay should be substantiated with documents showing the overlap between the condition and the appointed candidate’s thesis and publications. A decision on a stay request may be challenged once within seven days.

Appeal, Cassation and the 2026 Reform

Administrative court judgments may be appealed to the regional administrative court within thirty days of notification. Cassation before the Council of State is available only for the types of case listed in the Law. In an action against the rejection of an application for an assistant professor post and the appointment of another candidate, the 8th Chamber held that the contested act could result in removal from public service and that the case was therefore open to cassation, and quashed the regional court’s refusal of the cassation request on the ground that its judgment was final (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/2503, K. 2024/2798, 14.05.2024). The provision cited as sub-paragraph (c) at the time of that decision now appears as sub-paragraph (d) of the first paragraph.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 46 (Cassation) (unofficial translation)

“1. Final decisions of the chambers of the Council of State and decisions of regional administrative courts in the following cases may be appealed in cassation to the Council of State within thirty days of notification, notwithstanding any contrary provision in other laws: … d) Annulment actions against acts resulting in removal from a profession, public service or student status. … 2. In cases not covered by the first paragraph, decisions given anew by the regional administrative court after quashing the first-instance decision on appeal may be appealed in cassation to the Council of State within thirty days of notification. However, no cassation appeal lies, even where a new decision has been given after quashing, in the following cases and matters: a) Cases heard by a single judge in administrative and tax courts.” (unofficial translation)

The second paragraph was added by Law No. 7589. Under its transitional provision, the changes to the cassation article apply to decisions given by regional administrative courts after its entry into force (31.07.2026); earlier decisions remain subject to the former rules. Where the first-instance court upheld a posting and the regional court quashed that judgment and annulled the act (or vice versa), cassation is therefore now available irrespective of whether the case falls within one of the sub-paragraphs of the first paragraph. The same Law also narrowed the grounds for remitting a file to the first-instance court; one of them is deciding without an expert examination where one was required, although the regional court may remedy that defect itself.

Council of State Decisions

All decisions below are from the 8th Chamber of the Council of State and their full texts were read from the official case-law database. Where the Chamber upheld a judgment, the principle quoted is the finding of the first-instance or regional court as upheld; this is indicated in each note.

A condition matching a thesis topic identifies a particular candidate; annulment does not appoint the claimant

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/5773, K. 2024/5655, 05.11.2024 (finding of the first-instance court; upheld after appeal and cassation)

“the only other applicant, Dr. ..., was a research assistant in the department of Giresun University where the assistant professor post was announced, and that person’s doctoral thesis topic fell within the condition set out in the posting; … the condition identifies a particular candidate; … annulment of the unlawful administrative acts will not directly result in the claimant’s appointment, and the defendant administration may issue a new posting of an objective nature” (translation)

A post-specific condition needs no YÖK approval but must not describe a person

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2021/3774, K. 2023/7368, 19.12.2023 (finding of the regional administrative court; upheld by the Chamber)

“special, post-specific conditions imposed by universities for an announced post in view of needs and service requirements are not subject to the approval of the Council of Higher Education; administrations must use their discretion in appointments to any academic post by having regard to needs and service requirements, not by setting person-specific additional conditions … the additional condition at issue does not serve the needs of the department, is subjective, not objective, and describes a person (the intervener)” (translation)

An objective experience requirement that anyone can document is not tailor-made

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/1823, K. 2025/288, 30.01.2025 (the Chamber’s own reasoning; the regional court’s annulment was quashed by majority)

“requiring at least two semesters of experience in teaching theoretical courses in the field of the Construction Department where the post was announced is a service requirement, aims to raise academic and scientific quality and the quality of education provided to students, and the condition does not describe a person. Indeed, every candidate who meets and documents the minimum experience required in the field of the post may apply and, if the other conditions are also met, be assessed.” (translation)

Non-candidate faculty members have no interest in seeking annulment of the posting

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/5928, K. 2026/896, 18.02.2026 (the Chamber’s own reasoning; upheld with clarification, by majority)

“acts adopted in administrative law by unilateral declaration of will as final and enforceable may be challenged by annulment action only by those having a personal, legitimate and current interest in them; the existence of such an interest suffices to establish the relationship between the parties and is determined according to the nature and features of the case.” (translation)

Not appointing a candidate who meets the special condition breaches academic service requirements

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2021/3739, K. 2024/1378, 13.03.2024 (the Chamber’s own reasoning; upheld as to annulment of the appointment of the intervener who did not meet the special condition, quashed as to the non-appointment of the claimant)

“after the post is announced by the rectorate, a written opinion must be obtained on each candidate from a properly formed jury of three … the act of not appointing the claimant, who meets the special condition in the posting as well as the other requirements, is contrary to Article 23 of Law No. 2547 and to the Regulation on Promotion and Appointment to Faculty Membership and does not comply with academic service requirements” (translation)

Effects of Annulment and Compensation

Annulment removes the posting and the appointment based on it retroactively. The administration must act in accordance with the judgment without delay and in any event within thirty days of its notification (Law No. 2577 art. 28/1). However, administrative courts may not review expediency or give judgments amounting to an administrative act or removing the administration’s discretion (Law No. 2577 art. 2/2). The consequence therefore depends on the ground of annulment:

  • Tailor-made condition: annulment does not result in the claimant’s automatic appointment; the administration may issue a new, objective posting. In the first decision above the claimant objected to this reasoning, but the judgment was upheld on this point as well.
  • Appointment of a candidate not meeting the special condition: the appointment is annulled and the procedure must be completed lawfully (jury reports, board opinion and rector’s decision) for the candidates meeting the condition. The Council of State also found the non-appointment of the qualified claimant unlawful and quashed the regional court’s decision that there was “no need to rule at this stage” (last decision above).
  • Position of the appointee: annulment directly affects the appointee’s link with the post; in the files reviewed the appointees joined the proceedings as interveners on the side of the administration.

If the administration fails to comply with the judgment, an action for pecuniary and non-pecuniary damages may be brought (Law No. 2577 art. 28/3). For damage caused by an unlawful act, annulment and full-remedy actions may be brought together, or an annulment action may be brought first and a full-remedy action filed within the time limit after notification of the judgment (Law No. 2577 art. 12). The extent of the damage and causation must be proved in each case. After ordinary remedies are exhausted an individual application to the Constitutional Court is also possible, within thirty days of exhaustion (Law No. 6216 art. 47/5); which right it can be examined under depends on the common protection area of the Constitution and the European Convention on Human Rights.

Practical Points

Archive the posting on the day it is published. The full Official Gazette text, the university’s web announcement, the examination calendar and any later corrections should be kept with their dates. Web pages change, and the date on which results are published may determine when the time limit starts.

Apply if possible and document the rejection. Applying largely removes standing issues, and the rejection can itself be challenged. If the condition prevents you from applying, gather now the documents showing that you meet every other requirement (diplomas, publication list, ALES and language certificates, teaching records).

Compare the condition with the appointee’s CV. The strongest evidence of a tailor-made posting is the overlap between the condition and the appointee’s thesis title, publications, projects and position in the institution. This information should be collected, with dates, from the YÖK Thesis Centre, academic databases and open institutional sources, and it should be checked whether any other academic could meet the condition. If an expert report gets this wrong, the objection should name specific persons and works.

Ask for the administration’s justification of need. Department and faculty board decisions, staffing requests, course schedules and senate criteria can be requested by the court through an interim order. In the Council of State’s decisions, the absence of documentary proof of need was one of the grounds for annulment. Freedom-of-information requests and CİMER records help date relevant events, but since it is not always clear whether they count as an application to the superior authority suspending the time limit, the calculation of the time limit should not rely on them.

Keep the language measured. Rather than accusations against persons or institutions, the petition should show with objective data which service need the condition does not serve and who can meet it; the court reviews the cause and purpose of the act, not the intentions of individuals.

Frequently Asked Questions

May a university attach special conditions to a posting?

Yes. The Law permits objective and verifiable additional conditions with YÖK approval aimed at scientific quality, and the courts accept post-specific conditions provided they rest on service needs. What is prohibited is writing a thesis title into the posting and conditions that identify only a particular candidate.

Can I bring an action if I did not apply?

In the decisions reviewed, the merits were examined in an action brought by a person who could not apply because she did not meet the condition. You need to document that you meet all other requirements and that the condition excluded you from competition. Actions by non-candidate faculty members may be dismissed for lack of interest.

When does the time limit start?

The time limit is sixty days. Where appointment results were published on the university website, the Council of State upheld a judgment treating that date as the date of knowledge. For an action against the posting itself, the most prudent course is to file within sixty days of its publication.

If I win, will I be appointed to the post?

As a rule, no. The court cannot appoint in the administration’s place; where the condition is found tailor-made, the administration may issue a new objective posting. If the non-appointment of a qualified candidate is found unlawful, the administration must act in accordance with the judgment.

Can the appointment be suspended during the proceedings?

A stay may be ordered if manifest unlawfulness and damage difficult to remedy are shown. In appointment cases such a decision is given after the administration’s defence has been received or the defence period has expired.

Is cassation available against the regional court’s decision?

It is available in the types of case listed in the Law; the Council of State treated an action against another candidate’s appointment as open to cassation because it could result in removal from public service. In addition, regional court decisions given after 31.07.2026 that quash the first-instance judgment and rule anew can now be appealed in cassation.
