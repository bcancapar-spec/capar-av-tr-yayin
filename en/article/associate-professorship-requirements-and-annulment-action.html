Associate Professorship in Türkiye: Application Requirements and Annulment Action Against a Negative Result

Short answer: Under Turkish law, an application for associate professorship requires a doctorate or an equivalent title listed in the Law and at least 55 points in a central foreign language examination. Original publications of the number and quality set for each field by the Council of Higher Education (YÖK) on the opinion of the Interuniversity Board (ÜAK) are also required (Higher Education Law No. 2547 art. 24). Applications are, as a rule, made electronically to ÜAK in March and October; in a five-member jury, at least three members must give a favourable opinion in a detailed and reasoned report. The result and the reports are deemed served on the fifth day after they are made accessible electronically. Objections are decided by the Associate Professorship Commission; an annulment action is brought against the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board within sixty days and, according to the Council of State’s jurisdiction rulings, before the administrative courts of Ankara. Courts review the reasoning of jury reports, mostly through court-appointed experts; however, in recent regional administrative court decisions, an expert finding the candidate successful is not in itself a ground for annulment unless it is shown that the jury assessed the file contrary to academic and scientific principles.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Under Turkish law, an application for associate professorship requires a doctorate, at least 55 points in a central foreign language examination and original publications set for each field; the Interuniversity Board's jury assesses the file through detailed and reasoned reports. An annulment action against a negative result is brought against the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board within sixty days before the administrative courts of Ankara; Higher Education Law No. 2547 art. 24, the Associate Professorship Regulation and Council of State decisions.

What Is the Title of Associate Professor and Who Awards It?

In the Turkish higher education system, associate professorship (doçentlik) is the academic title that precedes professorship and a precondition for appointment to an associate professor post. The title is awarded not by the candidate’s university but by the Interuniversity Board: under Higher Education Law No. 2547 art. 24/c, the title is conferred on a candidate whom ÜAK decides has sufficient publications and work. How applications are assessed is governed by the Associate Professorship Regulation, published in the Official Gazette of 15.04.2018 and amended several times, including on 14.10.2025.

Appointment to an associate professor post is a separate process; if the additional conditions set by a university for the post include an oral examination, it is conducted by ÜAK juries (art. 24/d). For the title itself, there is currently no oral examination; the assessment is made through jury reports.

Requirements for Applying for Associate Professorship

The Law provides that applications are made at least twice a year according to the calendar set by ÜAK and lists three requirements:

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 24/a (Associate professorship and appointment) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Having obtained, after a bachelor’s degree, a doctorate, or the title of specialty in medicine, dentistry, pharmacy or veterinary medicine, or proficiency in one of the specific branches of art determined by the Council of Higher Education on the proposal of the Interuniversity Board.

(2) Having obtained at least fifty-five points in a central foreign language examination determined by the Council of Higher Education or an equivalent score in a foreign language examination whose international validity is accepted by the Council of Higher Education; where the field of associate professorship relates to a specific foreign language, taking this examination in another foreign language.

(3) Having produced original scientific publications and works of sufficient number and quality, to be determined by the Council of Higher Education on the opinion of the Interuniversity Board, taking into account the characteristics of each scientific or artistic discipline.”

The Regulation repeats the same requirements and governs the calendar and the fate of an application filed with missing documents:

Regulatory Provision — Associate Professorship Regulation art. 4/1 and art. 4/4 (Time, requirements and procedure of application) (unofficial translation)

“Unless another date is set by the Interuniversity Board, applications for associate professorship are made twice a year, starting on the twentieth day of March and October and ending at the latest at the close of working hours on the last working day of the relevant month.

… The application of a candidate who is found to have missing or incorrect diplomas, thesis, foreign language certificate or other documents required for the application and/or to have applied in breach of the procedure in the associate professorship application guide is rejected by the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board. A candidate whose application has been rejected may apply again for associate professorship in subsequent periods.”

The content of the third requirement, that is, what type of publication and how many points are required in each field, is set out not in the Law or the Regulation but in the “Associate Professorship Application Requirements” tables published on ÜAK’s website. As these tables are not in the Official Legislation Information System, they are not reproduced verbatim here; the information below relies solely on what Council of State decisions report. A 2023 Chamber decision upheld by the Council of State’s Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers (İDDK) reports that the tables at the time required, for each field, scientific activity worth at least 100 points, at least 90 of which had to be achieved after the doctorate (İDDK, E. 2023/2874, K. 2025/881). Another case concerned the requirement of 40 points from the international article item (İDDK, E. 2023/1518, K. 2025/1190). Since the tables may change, the requirements for the relevant application period should be checked in ÜAK’s current announcement.

The Application and Assessment Process

The jury. For each candidate who completes the application in time, the Associate Professorship Commission forms a jury of five full and two substitute members, having regard to the field applied for; if there are not enough faculty members in the field, the jury is formed with three full members and one substitute (Regulation art. 5/1). Only faculty members appointed as professors may sit on the jury (art. 5/3). Jury information is made accessible to the candidate together with the result (art. 5/4).

Assessment criteria. Meeting the minimum requirements is not enough on its own; the jury assesses the file as a whole:

Regulatory Provision — Associate Professorship Regulation art. 6/1 (Associate professorship assessment) (unofficial translation)

“Applications for associate professorship are assessed in terms of whether the candidate acted in accordance with the principles of scientific research and publication ethics, whether the candidate meets the minimum application requirements, the quality and originality of the works, the contribution made to the scientific field and the candidate’s competence in the field as a whole. … The fact that a candidate meets the minimum application requirements does not mean that the candidate is successful in the assessment of works.”

Jury reports alleging an ethics violation are examined by the relevant Scientific Research and Publication Ethics Commission, and reports finding that the minimum requirements are not met are examined by the Associate Professorship Commission. The application of a candidate found not to meet the minimum requirements is cancelled; the candidate may apply again in the following period after remedying the grounds of cancellation, while an application made without remedying the deficiencies is rejected (art. 6/1-a). For a candidate who meets the minimum requirements, the jury members write reports on the merits:

Regulatory Provision — Associate Professorship Regulation art. 6/2 and art. 6/8 (Associate professorship assessment) (unofficial translation)

“The associate professorship assessment jury prepares a detailed and reasoned assessment report stating whether it finds “successful” or “unsuccessful” a candidate in respect of whom it has found no violation of scientific research and publication ethics and whom it has found to meet the minimum application requirements, on the basis of the quality of the candidate’s academic work and its contribution to the scientific/artistic field.

… In five-member juries, the candidate is deemed successful if at least three members, and in three-member juries at least two members, find the candidate successful. A candidate found unsuccessful may apply again at the earliest in the third following period, counted on the basis of the application period, provided that the works and activities found lacking by the jury are completed.”

Report, result and service. The nature of the report and the notification of the result are regulated directly in the Law:

Statutory Provision — Higher Education Law (Law No. 2547) art. 24/b (Associate professorship and appointment) (unofficial translation)

“The full and substitute members of the associate professorship examination jury send to the Interuniversity Board the detailed and reasoned personal reports they prepare by assessing the candidate’s publications and work. … Jury memberships, the jury, the reports taken as the basis of assessment and the result of the application are made accessible to those concerned electronically, and this information is deemed served on those concerned on the fifth day following the date on which it is made accessible.”

Only the reports taken as the basis of assessment are made accessible to the candidate: the reports of the full jury members where the result is successful or unsuccessful, and the reports making the finding of cancellation where the application is cancelled (art. 6/7). A successful candidate is awarded the title of associate professor and issued an associate professorship certificate by decision of the Executive Board of ÜAK (art. 6/10).

Objection to a Negative Result and the Annulment Action

Objection. The Regulation entrusts the Associate Professorship Commission with deciding objections relating to the process; it sets no separate time limit for objections:

Regulatory Provision — Associate Professorship Regulation art. 8/3 (Associate Professorship Commission) (unofficial translation)

“All objections of candidates and jury members relating to the associate professorship process, and matters to be determined ex officio, are examined and decided by the Associate Professorship Commission.”

Decisions show candidates objecting to the Associate Professorship Commission and the Commission’s decision rejecting the objection being challenged together with the original act (İDDK, E. 2023/1518, K. 2025/1190). The effect of an application to the administration on the time limit for bringing an action is governed by the Administrative Procedure Law (İYUK):

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 11/1 (Application to higher authorities) (unofficial translation)

“Before an administrative action is brought, those concerned may request, within the time limit for bringing an administrative action, the higher authority or, where there is no higher authority, the authority that performed the act, to revoke, withdraw or amend the administrative act or to perform a new act. This application stops the running of the time limit for bringing an administrative action that has started to run.”

If no reply is given within thirty days, the request is deemed rejected; upon rejection the time limit runs again and the time elapsed until the application is taken into account (İYUK art. 11/2-3).

Time limit. There is no special time limit for associate professorship acts; the general time limit applies:

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 7/1 and art. 7/4 (Time limit for bringing an action) (unofficial translation)

“Unless a separate time limit is laid down in special laws, the time limit for bringing an action is sixty days before the Council of State and administrative courts and thirty days before tax courts.

… However, upon the application of these acts, those concerned may bring an action against the regulatory act or the applied act or both. The fact that the regulatory act has not been annulled does not prevent the annulment of the act based on that regulation.”

The time limit starts on the day following written notification (İYUK art. 7/2-a). Since in associate professorship matters notification is deemed made on the fifth day after the result and the reports are made accessible electronically (Law No. 2547 art. 24/b), the sixty days are counted from the day following that day. If the negative act is based directly on a provision of the Regulation or a rule in the application requirements, that rule may also be challenged together under art. 7/4.

Defendant and competent court. The rejection or cancellation of an application, or a finding that the candidate is unsuccessful, is an act of ÜAK; the action is brought against the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board. If a regulatory act such as a provision of the Regulation is also challenged, the Presidency of the Council of Higher Education is also a defendant (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2022/6771, K. 2025/3366). Jurisdiction follows the general rule of İYUK:

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 32/1 (General territorial jurisdiction in administrative actions) (unofficial translation)

“Without prejudice to the provisions on subject-matter jurisdiction, where the competent administrative court is not specified in this Law or in special laws, the competent administrative court is the administrative court of the place where the administrative authority that performed the administrative act or concluded the administrative contract in dispute is located.”

Since ÜAK is located in Ankara, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State, in jurisdiction disputes arising from actions brought in other provinces, holds the administrative courts of Ankara competent:

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2025/1273, K. 2025/6265, 26.06.2025 (determination of the competent court)

“in the action brought for the annulment of the act rejecting the associate professorship application of the client, whose application was rejected and who obtained the title of associate professor by completing the process after the 4th Administrative Law Chamber of the Ankara Regional Administrative Court, by its decision of 06/06/2023 numbered Stay Objection:2023/748, stayed the execution of the act … it has been concluded that the Ankara Administrative Court, at the place where the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board is located, is competent.”

In that case, execution was stayed in respect of an application rejected on grounds of an ethics violation and the candidate obtained the title. In the decisions cited, the decisions at the regional administrative court stage were given by the 4th Administrative Law Chamber of the Ankara Regional Administrative Court. In actions concerning individual associate professorship acts, the regional administrative court’s decision has been treated as final: the regional administrative court rejected the appeal on points of law on the ground that the matter was not among those open to appeal on points of law, and the Council of State upheld that decision (the orthopaedics decision below). Actions against regulatory acts, such as a provision of the Regulation or a decision of YÖK, were heard in the decisions cited by the 8th Chamber of the Council of State at first instance, with İDDK hearing the appeal on points of law.

After annulment, the date of the title may also be disputed: in one case the title was first awarded from 29.04.2024 and, through a second action, backdated to 16.12.2020; in the full remedy action brought for the salary differences for the intervening period, the Ankara administrative court was also held competent (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2025/5289, K. 2025/7057).

Judicial Review of Jury Reports in Court Decisions

The jury’s opinion is a scientific assessment; the court does not make it itself but refers the matter to a panel of experts consisting of faculty members in the field. The decisions show the two limits of review together.

The requirement of reasons. The Council of State has required in the expert report, too, the “detailed and reasoned” assessment that the Law requires of the jury, and quashed a judgment dismissing the action that relied on an expert report stating that the candidate was “right on the borderline” but not explaining why this was a ground for failure:

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2016/14971, K. 2020/3951, 30.09.2020 (the Chamber’s reasoning for quashing)

“it is seen that no detailed and reasoned assessment has been given as to why the fact that the claimant, with regard to the quality and quantity of the works in the associate professorship file, remained on the borderline for being assessed as successful at the stage of examination of works constituted a reason for the claimant being deemed unsuccessful. … since an expert examination containing detailed and reasoned assessments of the claimant’s academic work, capable of forming the basis of a judgment, must be carried out by the Court by forming a new panel of experts, it has been concluded that the Court’s judgment based on an expert report resting on incomplete examination is not lawful.”

The decision was given under the repealed Associate Professorship Examination Regulation and with the remedy of rectification of judgment open; it may not be final. The “detailed and reasoned” report standard on which it rests still appears today in Law No. 2547 art. 24/b and Regulation art. 6/2.

The limit of the jury’s opinion. Recent regional administrative court decisions do not consider it sufficient that an expert finds the candidate successful. In an orthopaedics case, the administrative court’s annulment judgment based on the expert report was reversed and the action dismissed, and the rejection of the appeal on points of law was upheld by the Council of State:

Council of State Decision (reasoning of the regional administrative court as reported)
Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2024/3631, K. 2024/4118, 04.07.2024 (reasoning of the regional administrative court as reported in the decision; the Council of State upheld the decision rejecting the appeal on points of law)

“that there is no allegation or finding that the jury members who expressed the opinion that the claimant was “unsuccessful” carried out an examination and assessment contrary to academic and scientific principles, that there is no information or document showing the contrary of the findings and assessments they relied on in reaching those opinions, namely that the claimant’s contribution to the scientific field was at an insufficient level, that the works in which the claimant was the principal author were limited, that the number of citations to the claimant’s works was insufficient … that the subject matter of the action is not among the matters open to appeal on points of law, and that the decision of the regional administrative court given upon appellate review is final”

In an architecture case as well, the administrative court’s annulment judgment was reversed on the same reasoning, and the regional administrative court’s decision rejecting the appeal on points of law was upheld by the Council of State (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2025/874, K. 2025/1010). On this line, the claimant must show that the jury’s specific reasons are wrong or that the jury assessed the file contrary to academic and scientific principles.

Certainty of the criterion. İDDK held unlawful, for lack of criteria, the YÖK decision that articles in “predatory/dubious” journals could not be used in applications, and also held unlawful the cancellation of the application and the rejection of the objection based on it:

Council of State Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers Decision
Council of State, Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2023/1518, K. 2025/1190, 29.05.2025 (the Assembly’s legal assessment)

“In this situation, since it is understood that the phrase “predatory/dubious journal” is a vague expression remote from objectivity, and that the measures and criteria to be taken as the basis in the associate professorship assessment to be made by jury members have not been set out in an objective, clear and understandable manner and are not foreseeable, it has been concluded that the decision of the General Assembly of Higher Education dated 07/03/2019 and numbered 2019.3.88 is contrary to the principles of legal certainty and foreseeability.”

The decision was given by majority and is final; the dissenting opinion states that the journals in which the articles were published fell within this category under ÜAK’s criterion and that the individual act was lawful.

The right of access to court and waiting periods. The provision added to the Regulation in 2021, which made a new application in the same field by a candidate found unsuccessful or whose application was cancelled dependent, if the candidate sued against that act, on the judgment becoming final, was annulled by the 8th Chamber of the Council of State, and İDDK upheld that decision:

Council of State Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers Decision (Chamber reasoning upheld)
Council of State, Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2023/2874, K. 2025/881, 21.04.2025 (Chamber reasoning reported in and upheld by the Assembly’s decision)

“that, where an action is brought against these acts, a new application for associate professorship in the same scientific/artistic field is made dependent on the judgment becoming final, and it is understood that this has a significantly deterrent effect on associate professor candidates who have brought an action by exercising their right to seek legal remedies, as regards their recourse to the courts, … that the restriction placed on the right to seek legal remedies by the contested amendment to the Regulation is not proportionate to the aim pursued”

By the same decision, the sentence providing that a candidate whose application was cancelled for failing the minimum requirements could apply only “at the earliest in the second following period” was also found contrary to the principle of proportionality, since it deprives a candidate who remedies the deficiency sooner of that right (this part by majority). By contrast, the rule that a candidate found unsuccessful in the assessment of works may apply again “at the earliest in the third following period” (art. 6/8) was considered reasonable and sufficient by the 8th Chamber of the Council of State, which found the rejection of an application based on that rule lawful. As that decision was given at first instance, by majority and with an appeal on points of law available, it may not be final (Council of State, 8th Chamber, E. 2022/6771, K. 2025/3366).

Practical Points

  • Calculate the day of service correctly. The result is deemed served on the fifth day after it is made accessible electronically; the sixty-day time limit starts on the following day.
  • Object within the time limit and keep proof. Under İYUK art. 11, an application to the administration made within the time limit for bringing an action stops the time limit; if no reply is given within thirty days, the request is deemed rejected.
  • Challenge the reasons of each report one by one. The specific reasons of each negative report (contribution to the field, originality, principal authorship, citations, quality of the journal) should be addressed separately; a report written in general terms should be challenged as falling short of the “detailed and reasoned” report standard.
  • Have the right questions put to the experts. The experts should be asked to address the jury’s reasons one by one and to explain whether the jury’s assessment complies with academic and scientific principles.
  • Bring the action in Ankara. An action brought elsewhere may turn into a jurisdiction dispute and prolong the process.
  • Plan the new application. Bringing an action does not prevent a new application; however, while an application is pending, no new application may be made in the same or a different field (art. 4/7). Of the decisions cited, the 8th Chamber decisions K. 2020/3951 and K. 2025/3366 may not be final.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the requirements for applying for associate professorship in Türkiye?

Under Law No. 2547 art. 24/a, a doctorate or an equivalent title listed in the Law, at least 55 points in a central foreign language examination (or an equivalent score in an examination accepted by YÖK) and original publications of the number and quality set for each field by YÖK on ÜAK’s opinion are required. Point tables by field are set out in the Associate Professorship Application Requirements published by ÜAK.

When are applications made, and when is the result deemed served?

Unless ÜAK sets another date, applications are made from the twentieth day of March and October until the close of working hours on the last working day of the month (Regulation art. 4/1). The result and the reports taken as the basis of assessment are deemed served on the fifth day after they are made accessible electronically (Law No. 2547 art. 24/b).

When may an unsuccessful candidate apply again?

A candidate found unsuccessful by the jury may apply again at the earliest in the third following period, provided the deficiencies are remedied (art. 6/8). A candidate whose application was cancelled for failing the minimum requirements may apply in the following period after remedying the deficiencies (art. 6/1-a).

Where and within what time limit is an action against the result brought?

The action is brought against the Presidency of the Interuniversity Board within sixty days from the day following service (İYUK art. 7). According to the Council of State’s jurisdiction rulings, the competent courts are the administrative courts of Ankara, where ÜAK is located. In the decisions cited, the regional administrative court’s appellate decision was treated as final.

Can the court change the jury’s scientific assessment?

The court does not take the jury’s place; it reviews, where necessary with experts, whether the reports are detailed and reasoned and whether the act is lawful. According to recent regional administrative court decisions, it is not enough that an expert finds the candidate successful; it must be shown that the jury’s reasons are wrong or that the jury assessed the file contrary to academic and scientific principles. An act based on a vague criterion, on the other hand, is unlawful according to İDDK.

Can a candidate who has sued apply again for associate professorship?

Yes. The regulatory provision making a new application dependent on the judgment becoming final was annulled as contrary to the right to seek legal remedies, and İDDK upheld that decision. However, a candidate whose application is still pending may not make a new application (art. 4/7).
