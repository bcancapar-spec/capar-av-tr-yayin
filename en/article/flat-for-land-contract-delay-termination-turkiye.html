Contractor Delay in Flat-for-Land Construction in Türkiye: Delay Damages, Termination, Title (TCO Art. 125)

Short answer: Under Turkish law, if the contractor misses the delivery date, the landowner as a rule either awaits performance and claims delay compensation or withdraws from the contract under arts. 123–125 of the Turkish Code of Obligations. According to the Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers, delay compensation may be claimed at least at the level of the monthly market rent even if the contract sets no amount. Because the contract includes a promise to transfer land shares, it is terminated not by unilateral notice but, unless the parties agree, by a court judgment. According to the Court of Cassation's 6th Civil Chamber, retroactive termination cannot be ordered once construction has reached 90 percent or more; in that case termination is forward-looking and the contractor receives shares in proportion to the work done.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Under Turkish law, when the contractor delays delivery the landowner may await performance and claim delay compensation at least equal to the market rent under art. 125 of the Code of Obligations, or seek termination of the contract by court judgment. The article explains the form requirement, contractual penalty, the 90 percent threshold, settlement in forward-looking termination and title annulment with Court of Cassation decisions.

The rights of the landowner under Turkish law when the contractor fails to finish the work on time under a flat-for-land (land share for construction) contract in Türkiye: granting a period, delay compensation and contractual penalty, retroactive or forward-looking withdrawal from the contract, the form requirement and title annulment (Turkish Code of Obligations arts. 123–125, 179, 473; Turkish Civil Code art. 706).

What Is a Flat-for-Land Construction Contract?

In a flat-for-land (land share for construction) contract the landowner pays no money. The contractor builds at its own expense and in return receives a certain share of the land and the independent units attached to that share. The landowner receives the flats or shops allocated to it in the contract.

The Turkish Code of Obligations does not regulate this contract separately. The Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers describes it as a “two-type, mixed” contract: on one side a contract for work, on the other a promise to sell immovable property. The rules on contracts for work and default apply to the construction side, and the form rules to the land share side.

This article explains the remedies available to the landowner when the contractor does not finish the work within the contractual period. The position of third parties who buy units from the contractor and defective delivery in turnkey projects are covered in separate articles (see Related Articles).

Form Requirement: Civil Code Art. 706 and the Notarial Deed

A contract for work is, as a rule, not subject to any form. A flat-for-land contract, however, includes the landowner's promise to transfer shares, so it falls under the form rule for contracts aimed at transferring ownership of immovable property.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Civil Code (Law No. 4721) art. 706/1 (Legal transaction)

“The validity of contracts aimed at transferring ownership of immovable property depends on their being drawn up in official form.”

Drawing up promises to sell immovable property is listed among notaries' duties in art. 60/3 of the Notaries Law. This item was amended by Law No. 7413 on 23.06.2022; the text below is the current version. The Assembly of Civil Chambers has stated that, under arts. 60/3 and 89 of the Notaries Law, promises to sell immovable property are drawn up by notaries in the form of an official notarial deed.

Statutory Provision — Notaries Law (Law No. 1512) art. 60 (General duties of notaries)

“To draw up promises to sell immovable property and, upon the request of one of the parties and payment of fees and costs, to annotate this contract in the land register through the land registry information system, and to draw up contracts for the sale of immovable property,

… To send protests, notices and warning notices,”

A contract made in simple written form is as a rule invalid. However, according to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, if the obligations have been performed fully or to a significant extent, or the land share has been transferred to the contractor, the lack of form cannot be invoked (see court decisions). In its 2020 decision the Assembly tied this result to the good faith rule (Civil Code art. 2), citing the Grand General Assembly's unification decision of 30.09.1988, E. 1987/2, K. 1988/2; the full text of that decision has not been seen.

Contractor's Default and Granting a Period

If the contractor does not deliver within the contractual period, the landowner may use the remedies in arts. 123–125 of the Turkish Code of Obligations. The first step is, as a rule, to give the contractor a reasonable period to perform.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 123 (Granting a period)

“In contracts imposing reciprocal obligations, if one party falls into default, the other may grant a reasonable period for performance of the obligation or ask the judge to grant a reasonable period.”

In some cases no period is needed, for example where the contractor has abandoned the site and its situation shows that granting a period would be ineffective, or where it is apparent from the contract that performance will not be accepted after a certain date.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 124 (Cases not requiring a period)

“No period need be granted in the following cases:

… If it appears from the debtor's situation or conduct that granting a period would be ineffective.”

There is also a rule specific to contracts for work: if it is clear by all estimates that the work will not be completed on the agreed date, the employer need not wait for the delivery date.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 473/1 (Commencement and conduct of the work)

“If the contractor does not start the work on time or delays the work contrary to the contract, or if, owing to a delay arising from a cause not attributable to the employer, it is clearly apparent by all estimates that the contractor cannot finish the work at the agreed time, the employer may withdraw from the contract without having to wait for the day fixed for delivery.”

The period may be granted by a notarial warning notice (Notaries Law art. 60/7); in the case before the Chamber in 2026 the landowners granted a 60-day period by such a notice. Stating clearly the period granted and the right to be exercised at its end makes it easier to establish the dates in later proceedings; this is the article's own inference, as the law prescribes no particular form of notice.

Delay Compensation and Contractual Penalty

When the period expires, or where no period is required, the landowner's optional rights arise. They are listed in art. 125 of the Turkish Code of Obligations.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 125 (Optional rights)

“If the defaulting debtor has not performed within the period granted, or if a period is not required, the creditor always has the right to demand performance and compensation for the delay.

The creditor may also, by immediately declaring that it waives performance and delay compensation, demand compensation for the loss arising from non-performance or withdraw from the contract.

In the event of withdrawal, the parties are released from their obligations to perform and may reclaim what they have already performed. In that case, unless the debtor proves that it was not at fault for the default, the creditor may also claim compensation for the loss suffered because the contract has become void.”

A landowner who chooses the first path waits for the building to be completed and claims delay compensation in the meantime. According to the Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers, the conditions are the contractor's default, the landowner's loss and the contractor's fault. In practice the compensation is at least the monthly market rent lost; it may be claimed even if the contract sets no amount, and if the contract sets a rent compensation, that clause applies.

Until when delay compensation runs is a separate question. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, it may as a rule be claimed, without any reservation being required, from the contractual delivery date until the unit is delivered complete and fit for use or until the landowner starts actually using it. If delivery is tied to the occupancy permit and there was no earlier actual delivery, the calculation runs until the permit date. If the landowner sells or lets the unit before taking delivery, compensation may be claimed until the date of sale or letting.

The second tool, where agreed in the contract, is the contractual penalty. Art. 179 of the Turkish Code of Obligations sets out when the penalty may be claimed together with the main obligation.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 179 (Relationship between the penalty and performance)

“If a penalty has been agreed for the case where a contract is not performed at all or not properly performed, the creditor may, unless otherwise provided by the contract, demand either performance or the penalty.

If the penalty has been agreed for the case where the obligation is not performed at the agreed time or place, the creditor may demand the penalty together with the main obligation, unless it has expressly waived this right or accepted performance without reservation.”

A penalty agreed for late delivery is the “penalty attached to performance” in the second paragraph and may as a rule be claimed together with delivery. The Assembly of Civil Chambers does not require a reservation for delay compensation, whereas for the penalty attached to performance the law treats acceptance without reservation as a ground for losing the right. Recording a reservation in the delivery report is therefore important.

Withdrawal: Retroactive and Forward-Looking Termination

Termination requires a court judgment. According to decisions of the Court of Cassation's 6th Civil Chamber, because the contract includes the transfer of land shares it cannot be terminated by a unilateral declaration; if the parties do not agree on termination, the court orders it where there is just cause. Notifying the contractor of the intention to terminate still matters; the Chamber accepted that, in a forward-looking termination, the contractual penalty may be claimed for the period between the date of default and the date of that notice.

The effect of termination depends on the level of construction. As summarised by the Assembly of Civil Chambers, the Grand General Assembly's unification decision of 25.01.1984, E. 1983/3, K. 1984/1, held that where the contractor is in default through its own fault the dispute is as a rule resolved under the rules on the debtor's default, but where the nature of the case justifies it, the court should rule under Civil Code art. 2 that the contract produces forward-looking effects. This article has not seen the full text of the unification decision; the content relies on the Assembly's summary.

  • Retroactive termination: The contract is treated as if it had never been made. The landowner reclaims land shares transferred as an advance; the contractor may, if there is lawful work, claim its value under the rules on unjust enrichment. Under the law, a contractor that cannot prove it was not at fault also compensates the landowner's loss arising from the contract becoming void (art. 125/3).
  • Forward-looking termination: The contractor receives independent units or land shares in proportion to the level reached; if this is not possible in kind, the balance is settled in money. According to the Chamber, in this case, unlike in retroactive termination, the landowner may also claim delay compensation and the penalty attached to performance.

The Court of Cassation's 6th Civil Chamber holds that retroactive termination cannot be ordered once construction has reached 90 percent or more; below that level the contractor cannot demand forward-looking termination. The Chamber has also accepted that, for a building left at 77.85 percent, the landowners could choose to terminate the contract with forward-looking effect. The conclusion drawn from these decisions is that below 90 percent the choice of which termination to seek largely lies with the landowner; this is the article's inference, and in a particular case the contract terms and the parties' claims are decisive.

Title Annulment and Registration

Where shares were transferred at the start of construction as an “advance”, termination also raises a title issue. According to the 2020 decision of the Assembly of Civil Chambers, in retroactive termination the basis of the share transferred to the contractor falls away, and the landowner may request annulment of that share and its registration in its own name. In forward-looking termination, according to the 6th Civil Chamber, the contractor returns only the share corresponding to work it has not done, if it has already received it.

The position changes if the contractor has sold the share or unit to a third party. The 2020 Assembly decision did not hear such third parties' good-faith defence, but this issue was resolved differently by the Grand General Assembly's unification decision of 16.05.2025, E. 2024/1, K. 2025/2 and is explained in a separate article: Court of Cassation: Title of a Unit Bought from the Contractor in Reliance on the Land Register Cannot Be Annulled Unless Bad Faith Is Proven.

Procedure: Actions, Proof and Time Limits

Types of action. The main actions are claims for delay compensation and contractual penalty, termination of the contract, and title annulment and registration. In the decisions examined the actions were heard by civil courts of first instance (asliye hukuk); whether a commercial court has jurisdiction depending on the parties' status, mandatory mediation and court fees are not examined in this article.

Time limits. Assembly of Civil Chambers decisions state that delay compensation may be claimed “within the limitation period”. The limitation period itself is not examined in this article.

Proof. The most disputed point in delay compensation cases is the delivery date. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, even without an occupancy permit, if the contractor alleges and proves that the unit was actually delivered and used, let or sold, compensation is calculated until that date. In the case before the Assembly in 2025, the Chamber required the delivery date to be determined separately for each unit on the basis of utility subscription records, address registration data and the evidence submitted by the contractor. In the termination decisions examined, the level of construction was determined by expert reports and, in one case, by evidence secured before the action.

Delay and Termination in Court Decisions

Form requirement and its exception. The Assembly of Civil Chambers found that the obligations under a simple written contract had been largely performed, did not hear the defence based on lack of form, and proceeded to the lost rent issue.

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2018/453, K. 2021/1702, 16.12.2021 (form requirement and its exception)

“since flat-for-land construction contracts include, on the landowner's side, an obligation to transfer ownership of the immovable to the contractor in the future (a promise aimed at transferring ownership), drawing up such contracts by a notary in official deed form is a condition of validity. Under the mandatory rule, contracts made without official form are invalid. Exceptionally, if the obligations have been performed by both sides fully or to a significant extent, or if the land share has been transferred to the contractor, the lack of form cannot be invoked.”

Delay compensation. The 2025 Assembly decision explained the conditions and scope of delay compensation on the same principles as the 2021 decision. The Assembly reversed the first-instance court's decision to persist in its earlier judgment, because the defence of actual delivery before the occupancy permit had to be examined. The decision was given by majority; the dissenting view held that in this case, where the contractor was obliged to obtain the permit, legal delivery had not taken place.

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/170, K. 2025/744, 26.11.2025 (conditions and amount of delay compensation)

“For delay compensation to be claimed, the contractor must have fallen into default in performing its obligation, the landowner's loss arising from the delay in performance must have occurred, and the contractor must be at fault in causing the loss. … Even if delay compensation is not set in the contract, the landowner may claim, within the limitation period and from the date delivery was due, compensation set at least at the level of the monthly market rent determined according to local market rates.”

The retroactive termination rule. The 2020 Assembly decision stresses that the contractor's right to the share depends on performing its obligation in accordance with the contract. The part of the decision concerning third parties who acquired shares from the contractor is not used here because, as noted above, it was later changed by a unification decision.

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2018/240, K. 2020/43, 21.01.2020 (relationship between landowner and contractor)

“Since flat-for-land construction contracts involve reciprocal obligations, for the contractor to become entitled to the title transferred to it in advance, unless the contract expressly provides otherwise, it must have performed its own obligation in accordance with the law, the contract terms and the rules of science and craft, under its duty of care. … Accordingly, if the contractor does not perform its obligation, the landowner acquires the right to request, by termination of the contract, annulment of the title transferred.”

The 90 percent threshold. In a case where the level of construction was 91.37 percent, the 6th Civil Chamber did not criticise the first-instance finding that the conditions for retroactive termination were not met, but held it a ground for reversal that the action had been dismissed without considering the landowner's request for forward-looking termination and settlement.

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/282, K. 2025/4510, 25.12.2025 (court judgment required for termination)

“The settled case law of our Chamber accepts that such contracts end when the parties agree to terminate or, where there are just causes, by a termination judgment of the court.”

In a 2026 decision the Chamber stated the threshold expressly and upheld a judgment ordering retroactive termination at a construction level of 80.60 percent.

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/2314, K. 2026/358, 03.02.2026 (the 90 percent threshold)

“where the flat-for-land construction contract has reached 90% or more, retroactive termination can no longer be ordered. … since the level of work was found to have remained below 90%, the contractor cannot demand forward-looking termination of the contract and, accordingly, the titles to the flats falling to it according to the level of work.”

Settlement in forward-looking termination. The 6th Civil Chamber accepted the landowners' request for forward-looking termination of a building left at 77.85 percent and reversed the first-instance court's judgment that ordered termination only, treating the settlement as the “subject of a separate action”. According to the Chamber, the court must specify in the judgment, in a way that leaves no doubt at enforcement, the independent units and shares that will remain with the contractor.

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/3850, K. 2026/1637, 21.04.2026 (forward-looking termination and settlement)

“In forward-looking termination, the landowner, while obliged to transfer land share in proportion to the construction carried out by the contractor, also demands payment of the losses it suffered due to the contractor's fault. … if the contractor would have received 10 independent units had it completed the work, and the work it had done when it abandoned the site amounted to 80% of the whole work, then in forward-looking termination the contractor will have become entitled to the title of 8 units.”

Currency. No Assembly decision in a different direction on delay compensation was found after the 2025 Assembly decision. The part of the 2020 decision concerning third parties has been superseded.

Where termination through the landowner’s fault has become final, the contractor may claim loss of profit

Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers (the Assembly’s reasoning)
Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu, E. 2021/874, K. 2023/118, T. 22.02.2023

“Since a construction contract in return for land share involves the transfer of a registered immovable or a share in it, it cannot be terminated by a unilateral declaration of intent; the parties’ intentions must concur on termination, or termination is possible by court decision. … In that case, as the judgment in the main action finding that the construction contract in return for land share concluded between the parties had been unjustly terminated by the landowners has become final, where the contract is terminated through the landowners’ fault, the faultless contractor may claim loss of profit from the landowners as part of the positive damage.” (unofficial translation)

In the case, the contract, made before a notary in official form, provided that the contractor could not be dismissed without force majeure. The landowners revoked the contractor’s power of attorney on the ground that the architectural project had been submitted to the municipality without consulting them, and gave notice that they would terminate the contract. The contractor sought a finding of unjust termination, its negative damage and loss of profit; the landowners counterclaimed for termination. The first-instance court found the termination unjust, awarded the expenses incurred in reliance on the contract as negative damage and rejected loss of profit. The landowners’ appeal concerning the main action was dismissed on procedural grounds because the fee was not completed despite a remittal, so the finding of unjust termination became final against them. The regional court of appeal held that the parties’ intentions to terminate had concurred through the counterclaim and that positive damage cannot be claimed upon retroactive termination, and insisted against the reversal of the (closed) 15th Civil Chamber. The Assembly, noting the final finding of unjust termination, held that the faultless contractor’s claim for loss of profit had to be examined and reversed the insistence judgment by majority on the grounds stated in the Chamber’s reversal. During deliberations, the view that loss of profit could not be claimed because the retroactive termination judgment had become final was not adopted by the majority. The Assembly did not decide the amount of the loss of profit, leaving it to the regional court of appeal. The outcome rests on this procedural situation in which the finding of unjust termination had become final; it does not mean that loss of profit may be claimed in every case where the parties’ intentions to terminate concur. For landowners, the decision shows that a dismissal and termination notice without just cause may expose them to the contractor’s claim for loss of profit.

Practical Points

  • Check the form of the contract. For a contract not drawn up as a notarial deed, the first issue is validity, that is, how far the obligations have been performed and whether any share has been transferred.
  • Calculate the delivery date and any extensions. In the contracts in the decisions examined, the period ran from the date of the building permit (12 and 18 months). In the case before the Assembly in 2025, the court added six months to the delivery period because of extraordinary events.
  • Exercise the optional right clearly. Awaiting performance and withdrawing from the contract have different consequences. The waiver of performance and delay compensation must be declared “immediately” (art. 125/2).
  • Make a reservation when taking delivery. To preserve the penalty attached to performance, it is important to record a reservation in the delivery report or a written notice (art. 179/2).
  • Document the use of the unit. Letting, sale or actual occupation determines when delay compensation ends; records showing these dates matter for both sides.
  • Have the level of construction established early. Which termination is possible depends on the level of construction; when the site is abandoned, securing evidence through the court may help; this is the article's own inference.

Frequently Asked Questions

What compensation can a landowner claim under Turkish law if the contractor delays delivery under a flat-for-land construction contract?

Under Turkish law, the landowner may, as a rule, await performance and claim compensation for the delay (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 125/1). According to the Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers, this requires the contractor's default, the landowner's loss and the contractor's fault. Even if the contract sets no amount, compensation may be claimed at least at the level of the monthly local market rent; if the contract sets a rent compensation, that clause applies. If a penalty for late delivery was also agreed, the creditor may claim it together with performance unless he expressly waived it or accepted performance without reservation (art. 179/2).

Until what date is delay compensation calculated under a flat-for-land construction contract in Türkiye?

According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, delay compensation may as a rule be claimed from the agreed delivery date until the unit is delivered complete and fit for use, or until the landowner starts actually using it. If delivery is tied to the occupancy permit and there was no earlier actual delivery, it is calculated until the permit date. If the contractor proves actual delivery, letting or sale before the permit, it is calculated until that date.

Can a landowner terminate a flat-for-land construction contract in Türkiye by unilateral notice?

According to decisions of the Court of Cassation's 6th Civil Chamber, no: because the contract includes a promise to transfer land shares, it ends only by agreement of the parties or, where there is just cause, by a court judgment of termination. The landowner's notice of termination to the contractor still matters; the Chamber used the date of that notice when calculating the contractual penalty in a forward-looking termination.

What is the difference between retroactive and forward-looking termination of a flat-for-land construction contract under Turkish law?

In retroactive termination the parties return to their position before the contract: the landowner reclaims shares transferred as an advance, and the contractor may claim the value of lawful work. In forward-looking termination the contractor receives units or shares in proportion to the work done, and the landowner may also claim delay compensation and the penalty attached to performance. The Court of Cassation's 6th Civil Chamber holds that retroactive termination cannot be ordered once construction reaches 90 percent or more; the landowner may also seek forward-looking termination at a lower level.

Is a flat-for-land construction contract in Türkiye invalid if it was not drawn up by a notary in official form?

As a rule, yes. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, because the contract includes a promise to transfer land shares, drawing it up by a notary in official form is a condition of validity (Turkish Civil Code art. 706). However, if the parties have performed their obligations fully or to a significant extent, or the land share has been transferred to the contractor, the lack of form cannot be invoked. Whether this exception applies depends on the facts of each case.
