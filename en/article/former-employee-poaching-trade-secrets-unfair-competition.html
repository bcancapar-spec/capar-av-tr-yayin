When a Former Employee Sets Up a Competing Business: Poaching, Trade Secrets, Unfair Competition in Türkiye

Short answer: Under Turkish law, in a decision upheld by the Court of Cassation, several employees resigning of their own accord and starting work at a competitor was not in itself treated as unfair competition; the decisions reviewed required proof of specific conduct contrary to good faith. Inducing customers to breach their existing contracts, inducing employees to disclose trade secrets or using unlawfully obtained trade secrets is unfair competition (Commercial Code art. 55/1-b and d). An obligation not to compete after the contract ends arises only from a written clause that complies with Code of Obligations arts. 444–445; the restriction lapses if the employer terminates without just cause or the employee terminates for a reason attributable to the employer (art. 447). According to how decisions of the Court of Cassation 9th Civil Chamber report it, the unification decision of 13.06.2025 held commercial courts of first instance competent for post-employment non-compete actions; the same Chamber held the labour court competent for a penalty claim arising from a breach committed while the employment continued. In a settlement decision of 13.04.2023, the Competition Board found an agreement between employers not to hire each other’s staff contrary to Law No. 4054 art. 4, while stating that clauses tied to a legitimate cooperation, limited to the relevant personnel and meeting the other conditions may qualify as ancillary restraints.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Poaching customers and employees and using trade secrets when a former employee sets up or joins a competing business under Turkish law: unfair competition under Commercial Code art. 55/1-b and d, the duty of loyalty, contractual non-compete clauses (Code of Obligations arts. 444–447), the competent court and the Competition Board's decision on no-poach agreements.

The legal consequences under Turkish law when a departing employee sets up a competing business or joins a competitor and takes customers, staff or trade secrets along: unfair competition (Turkish Commercial Code art. 55/1-b and d), the duty of loyalty (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 396), contractual non-compete clauses (arts. 444–447) and no-poach agreements between employers (Law No. 4054 art. 4).

When a Former Employee Becomes a Competitor: Which Rules Apply?

Complaints about lost customers and staff or the use of information may rest on three different legal grounds under Turkish law:

  • Duty of loyalty (Code of Obligations art. 396): arises by law while the employment contract continues; no separate agreement is needed.
  • Contractual non-compete clause (arts. 444–447): for the period after the contract ends, it arises only from a clause the employee has accepted in writing and is subject to statutory limits. The defendant is the former employee.
  • Unfair competition (Commercial Code art. 54 et seq.): requires no contract between the parties; it sanctions conduct contrary to good faith. The defendant may be the former employee or the competing business that hired him or her or set up a company with him or her.

Claims, limitation and mandatory mediation are covered in the article on unfair competition actions.

During Employment: Duty of Loyalty and Confidentiality (art. 396)

Competing with the employer while the employment contract continues breaches the duty of loyalty. The duty of confidentiality, by contrast, continues after the contract ends to the extent required to protect the employer’s legitimate interests; it is separate from a post-contractual non-compete obligation.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 396/3-4 (Duty of care and loyalty)

“As long as the employment relationship continues, the employee may not, in breach of the duty of loyalty, provide services to third parties for remuneration and in particular may not compete with his or her own employer.

The employee may not, while the employment relationship continues, use for his or her own benefit or disclose to others information learned in the course of work, in particular production and business secrets. To the extent necessary to protect the employer’s legitimate interests, the employee remains bound by confidentiality after the employment relationship ends.”

The Court of Cassation 9th Civil Chamber has likewise stated that the obligation not to compete while the employment contract continues is a particular form of the duty of loyalty and that no separate agreement is needed (decision below).

After Leaving: Contractual Non-Compete Clause (arts. 444–447)

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 444 (Conditions of the non-compete obligation)

“An employee with capacity to act may undertake in writing towards the employer to refrain, after the end of the contract, from competing with the employer in any manner, in particular from opening a competing business on his or her own account, working in another competing business or otherwise entering into any other kind of interest relationship with a competing business.

A non-compete clause is valid only if the employment relationship gives the employee the opportunity to obtain knowledge of the customer base or production secrets or of the employer’s business and, at the same time, the use of that knowledge would be capable of causing the employer significant damage.”

The judge may restrict an excessive non-compete clause in scope or duration (art. 445/2).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 445 (Limitation)

“The non-compete obligation may not contain restrictions as to place, time and type of business that are inappropriate in a way that unfairly endangers the employee’s economic future, and its duration may not exceed two years except in special circumstances and conditions.

The judge may limit an excessive non-compete obligation in scope or duration, freely assessing all the circumstances and equitably taking into account any counter-performance the employer may have undertaken.”

The main consequences of a breach are damages and, if agreed, a penalty; an order to stop the competing conduct is available only if expressly reserved in writing in the contract and justified by the importance of the employer’s interests infringed or threatened and by the employee’s conduct.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 446 (Consequences of breach)

“An employee who breaches the non-compete obligation must compensate all damage the employer suffers as a result.

If breach is subject to a penalty clause and the contract does not provide otherwise, the employee may release himself or herself from the non-compete obligation by paying the stipulated amount; however, the employee must compensate damage exceeding that amount.

In addition to payment of the penalty and any further damage, the employer may, provided it has expressly reserved this in writing in the contract, also demand that the breaching conduct be stopped, if the importance of its infringed or threatened interests and the employee’s conduct justify this.”

The restriction also depends on how the contract ended; that is why the reason for termination is usually the first issue in these cases.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098) art. 447 (Termination)

“The non-compete obligation ends if it is established that the employer has no real interest in maintaining it.

If the contract is terminated by the employer without just cause, or by the employee for a reason attributable to the employer, the non-compete obligation ends.”

Unfair Competition: Poaching Customers and Employees, Trade Secrets (art. 55/1-b and d)

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 55/1-b and d (Conduct contrary to good faith, commercial practices)

“b) Inducing breach or termination of a contract; in particular; … inducing customers to act in breach of contracts they have concluded with others so that he or she can conclude a contract with them personally, … seeking to obtain an advantage for oneself or others by granting or offering to third parties’ employees, agents and other auxiliaries benefits to which they are not entitled and which may induce them to act in breach of their obligations in performing their work, … inducing employees, agents or other auxiliaries to disclose or acquire the production and business secrets of their employers or principals,

… d) Unlawfully disclosing production and business secrets; in particular, a person who exploits or discloses to others information he or she has obtained secretly and without permission or otherwise learned unlawfully, and the business secrets of the producer, acts contrary to good faith.”

The law treats as unfair competition not the employee’s move to a competitor but inducing a breach or termination of contract or the disclosure of secrets; in an upheld decision, inducing termination was held contrary to good faith only where employees were enticed systematically and in a planned way. The criteria drawn from the decisions (this article’s inference):

  • Staff moving is not enough on its own. In an upheld decision, several employees resigning of their own accord, applying to and joining a competitor, the closeness of the resignation and start dates and a higher salary offer were not treated as unfair competition; proof that employees had been poached systematically and in a planned way was required. By contrast, unfair competition was found where staff known to be bound by a non-compete clause were hired and many customers were drawn away through them within a short time (see the article on unfair competition actions).
  • Loss of customers matters only with proof of enticement. Businesses in the same line of trade addressing the same customer base was seen as unavoidable; records showing customers moved to the new company and profitability data were required.
  • Links formed while still employed may be decisive. Where the employee, while still employed, formed links with a competitor and set out to exploit the former employer’s customer portfolio for that competitor, unfair competition was found and loss of profit awarded.
  • A secrecy claim must be specific. In the same decision, products that could be manufactured on any machine with a CNC lathe were found not to constitute a manufacturing secret. What the secret is and how it was used must be shown.

No-Poach (Gentlemen’s) Agreements Between Employers

The other side of the issue is employers agreeing not to hire each other’s staff. Unlike unfair competition under the Commercial Code, such agreements are assessed under Law No. 4054 and may be examined by the Turkish Competition Board.

Statutory Provision — Law No. 4054 on the Protection of Competition art. 4/1 (Agreements, concerted practices and decisions restricting competition)

“Agreements between undertakings, concerted practices and decisions and practices of associations of undertakings which have as their object or effect, or are likely to have as their effect, the prevention, distortion or restriction of competition directly or indirectly in a particular market for goods or services are unlawful and prohibited.”

The Competition Board’s decision of 13.04.2023 numbered 23-18/326-111 is an example. It was established that an undertaking providing software testing services had agreed with a customer, with the main contractor of a project on which it was a subcontractor, and with the other subcontractors on that project not to hire each other’s employees. The Board defined these agreements as follows: “No-poach agreements are agreements made by undertakings competing for labour not to employ each other’s employees.” According to the Board, the agreement need not be written, legally valid or backed by a sanction. Two distinctions stand out:

  • Narrow clauses tied to a legitimate cooperation: for project-based activities, the Board stated that “a no-poach clause applying only to the relevant personnel may be accepted as an ancillary restraint if it is in writing and certain other conditions such as proportionality, necessity and duration are also present”.
  • Clauses covering all employees: a services agreement provided that for its term and for two years afterwards neither party would offer employment to the other’s personnel without the other’s consent. The Board concluded that the clause was not limited to personnel assigned under the contract but “was imposed so as to cover all employees of both undertakings”, and did not treat it as an ancillary restraint.

The Board found that the undertaking had infringed Law No. 4054 art. 4 by entering into no-poach agreements. In the settlement interim decision, the infringement was placed in the “cartel” category for the purposes of calculating the fine; the administrative fine set at TRY 906,628.54 was reduced, as a result of active cooperation and the settlement procedure (settlement reduction of 25%), to a final TRY 407,982.84. The decision states that judicial review is available; we have not been able to establish whether it was challenged in court. Under the settlement procedure the undertaking accepts that it will not challenge the fine or the matters in the settlement text in court. A Board decision is not a court judgment.

Procedure: Competent Court, Claims and Proof

Competent court. Commercial Code art. 4/1-c treats actions arising from matters governed by arts. 444 and 447 of the Code of Obligations on non-compete obligations as commercial cases, irrespective of whether the parties are merchants.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 4/1-c (Commercial cases)

“(1) Civil actions and non-contentious matters arising from matters relating to the commercial enterprises of both parties and, irrespective of whether the parties are merchants; … c) … articles 444 and 447 on the non-compete obligation of the Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098 of 11/1/2011, … civil actions and non-contentious matters arising from the matters provided for therein shall be deemed commercial cases and commercial non-contentious matters.”

Decisions of the Court of Cassation 9th Civil Chamber report that, by decision of the Grand General Assembly for the Unification of Case Law dated 13.06.2025, E. 2023/1, K. 2025/3, commercial courts of first instance were held competent for actions for breach of the non-compete obligation under arts. 444–447 (9th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5469, K. 2025/7112). For breaches during employment, see the 9th Civil Chamber decision below. We could not access the full text of the unification decision and rely only on how the Chamber reports it.

Claims and defendants. For breach of a non-compete clause, damages or a penalty may be claimed from the former employee and, if expressly reserved in writing in the contract and the conditions of art. 446/3 are met, an order to stop the conduct (art. 446). Unfair competition claims may also be directed against the competing business. If both grounds are relied on, it is helpful to set out the non-compete and unfair competition allegations separately in the statement of claim (this article’s suggestion).

Proof. In the decisions reviewed, the claimant employer was expected to produce specific evidence: correspondence showing enticement, company formation and trade registry announcement dates, social security records, customer-level sales data and expert comparison of the commercial books.

Case Law

The three decisions of the 11th Civil Chamber below are general affirmations; the quotations are from the reasoning of the court whose decision was upheld.

Court of Cassation Decision (reasoning of the first-instance court, upheld)
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/3135, K. 2025/7794, 25.12.2025 (staff moving; the regional court’s dismissal on the merits was upheld)

“for inducing termination of contracts to be contrary to good faith within the meaning of art. 54 of the Commercial Code, the condition that the claimant’s employees were enticed systematically and in a planned way must be met; several staff members working at one of the firms active in the same field resigning of their own accord and then applying to and starting work at the defendant company cannot be accepted as unfair competition”

As there was no written non-compete agreement, art. 444 was not applied. The regional court added that hiring employees by offering a higher salary could not be treated as evidence that trade secrets had been obtained; the expert did not find the customers listed by the claimant in the defendant’s records.

Court of Cassation Decision (reasoning of the first-instance court, upheld)
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/5673, K. 2024/4368, 28.05.2024 (customer portfolio; partial acceptance after reversal upheld)

“the products manufactured by the defendant company were not capable of constituting a manufacturing secret, and this did not fall within the scope of the non-compete obligation … owing to the links the defendant formed with the defendant … Ltd. while still working at the claimant company, there was unfair competition aimed at exploiting the claimant company’s customer portfolio for the benefit of the defendant company”

The defendant, who had worked as quality assurance officer, became a partner in a company in the same field about seven months after leaving. The court found unfair competition, ordered it to stop and awarded loss of profit calculated by an expert. The employment contract contained a five-year post-employment non-compete clause with no geographical limit; the first-instance court found the partnership contrary both to that clause (art. 444) and to Commercial Code art. 54. The events predate the entry into force of Law No. 6098. Earlier in the same file the Chamber had reversed a decision declining jurisdiction in favour of the labour court.

Court of Cassation Decision (reasoning of the first-instance court; appeal dismissed on the merits, regional court decision upheld)
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/6600, K. 2023/1707, 20.03.2023 (just-cause termination and end of the non-compete obligation; the regional court’s dismissal on the merits was upheld)

“although it is established that after leaving the defendant set up a company active in the same sector and, according to witness statements, also moved the claimant company’s customer portfolio to the company he set up, since the defendant left the claimant’s workplace by terminating the contract with just cause, the non-compete obligation ended by operation of law”

That the employee had terminated with just cause had been established by a final labour court judgment. Former Code of Obligations No. 818 applied; the first-instance court noted that art. 447/2 of the current Code is to the same effect. Since the action was based on the non-compete obligation, the decisions did not separately examine moving the customer portfolio under the heads of art. 55 (this article’s inference).

Court of Cassation Decision
9th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/3969, K. 2026/3579, 21.04.2026 (the Chamber’s own reasoning; reversal on jurisdiction)

“The employee’s obligation not to compete while the contract continues is a particular form of the duty of loyalty, and an employee who breaches it breaches the duty of loyalty. … The Unification Decision holds that the … court of first instance is competent in the event of breach of the non-compete conditions occurring after the employment relationship between the parties has ended.”

A penalty was claimed on the allegation that, while employed, the employee passed the firm’s drawings to a company set up by former employees. The Chamber reversed the first-instance decision declining jurisdiction, holding that the labour court was competent because the breach occurred during employment. The court’s name is anonymised in the text; in the same Chamber’s decision E. 2025/5469, which reports the unification decision, it reads “commercial courts of first instance”.

Note on later case law: In the limited search carried out for this article among Court of Cassation decisions since 2019, no decision was found departing from the approach that staff moving is not by itself unfair competition. On jurisdiction, as some earlier decisions treated the labour court as competent, care is needed when relying on decisions predating the unification decision of 13.06.2025. The search was limited to decisions published in the official database.

Practical Points

  • Draft the non-compete clause in writing and proportionately. The clause should be used for positions that genuinely have access to the customer base or confidential information.
  • Assess the reason for termination at the outset. A finding of just-cause termination in the employee’s labour claim may determine the outcome of the non-compete case.
  • Choose the right legal ground and defendant. A non-compete clause binds only the former employee; the competing business can be pursued only under unfair competition rules. Whether the breach occurred during or after employment may change the competent court.
  • Do not enter into staff agreements with competitors. Understandings not to hire each other’s staff may be examined under Law No. 4054 art. 4 even if oral or informal. If a clause tied to a legitimate project is needed, under the criteria in the Board’s decision it must be limited to the relevant personnel, written, proportionate, necessary and time-limited; even then, its treatment as an ancillary restraint is not certain.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it unfair competition under Turkish law for a former employee to set up a company in the same sector?

The decisions reviewed did not treat this as unfair competition in itself. In an upheld decision, employees who left of their own accord starting work at a competitor for a higher salary was seen as part of the freedom to work; unfair competition required proof of specific conduct such as inducing customers to breach their contracts, systematically poaching employees or unlawfully using trade secrets (Commercial Code art. 55/1-b and d). In one case where the employee, while still employed, set out to exploit the former employer’s customer portfolio for a competitor, unfair competition was found.

Under Turkish law, can a former employee take customers to a new company if the employment contract has no non-compete clause?

Without a written non-compete clause, a former employee is as a rule free to compete after the contract ends; customers moving of their own choice does not by itself lead to a sanction. However, inducing customers to breach their contracts with the former employer or using unlawfully obtained information may be unfair competition (Commercial Code art. 55/1-b and d). The duty of confidentiality also continues after the contract to the extent necessary (Code of Obligations art. 396).

When is an employee non-compete clause invalid or terminated under Turkish law?

An employee non-compete clause is not valid if it is not in writing, if the employee cannot obtain knowledge of the customer base, production secrets or the employer’s business, or if the use of that knowledge could not cause the employer significant harm (Code of Obligations art. 444). An excessive restriction may be narrowed by the judge; the duration may not exceed two years save in special circumstances (art. 445). The restriction ends if the employer has no real interest left or if the contract is terminated by the employer without just cause or by the employee for a reason attributable to the employer (art. 447).

Which court hears an action against a former employee for breach of a non-compete clause in Türkiye?

According to decisions of the Court of Cassation 9th Civil Chamber, the unification decision of 13.06.2025 held commercial courts of first instance competent for breaches of the non-compete obligation occurring after the employment relationship has ended; Commercial Code art. 4/1-c also treats these actions as commercial cases. For a penalty claim arising from a breach while the employment continued, the same Chamber held the labour court competent.

Are agreements between employers not to hire each other’s staff (no-poach or gentlemen’s agreements) lawful in Türkiye?

The Turkish Competition Board has found agreements between competing employers not to employ each other’s staff contrary to Law No. 4054 art. 4 and, in its decision of 13.04.2023, fined an undertaking; the agreement need not be in writing. According to the Board, in project-based activities a clause limited to the relevant personnel may qualify as an ancillary restraint if it is in writing and also meets conditions such as proportionality, necessity and duration. A Board decision is not a court judgment.
