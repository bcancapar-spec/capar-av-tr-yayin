Expropriation Price Determination and Registration in Türkiye

Short answer: Under Turkish law, the action for determination of the expropriation price and registration is the action the administration brings before the civil court of first instance (asliye hukuk mahkemesi) where the immovable is located when the purchase (negotiation) talks fail (Expropriation Law art. 10). The court first invites the parties to agree on the price; if they do not, it carries out a site inspection, a panel of at least three experts determines, under the criteria of art. 11, the value of the immovable on the day the administration filed its documents with the court, and the judge fixes a fair and equitable price. Once the administration deposits that price in a bank within fifteen days (extendable once), the court orders registration of the immovable in the administration’s name and payment of the price to the owner; the registration ruling is final, and appeal and cassation concern the price only. In 2026 the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation held by majority that, after the Constitutional Court annulled the interest provision, the price bears the highest interest rate applied to public receivables from the date of the action to the date of judgment.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

What Is the Action for Determination of the Expropriation Price and Registration?

If the administration cannot agree with the owner on the price of an immovable it wishes to expropriate, it has the court fix the price and, in the same action, asks for the immovable to be registered in its own name. Under Turkish law this action rests on art. 10 of the Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942). The general framework of expropriation is explained in Expropriation and the Owner’s Rights; this article focuses on the action itself, step by step.

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 10(1) (Determination of the expropriation price by the court and registration of the immovable in the name of the administration) (unofficial translation)

“Where the expropriation cannot be carried out by the purchase procedure, the administration shall apply to the civil court of first instance of the place where the immovable is located by a petition to which it attaches the information and documents collected under article 7, the price determination it had made under article 8 and the other information and documents on the matter, and shall request the determination of the expropriation price of the immovable and a decision for its registration in the name of the administration against payment of that price in advance or, if the expropriation was made under the second paragraph of article 3, in instalments.”

The administration brings the action and the owner is the defendant. In this action the owner argues that the price does not reflect the real value of the immovable; the unlawfulness of the expropriation measure itself is argued not here but in an annulment action before the administrative courts. If the administration has taken the immovable without expropriation, the route is different: the owner brings the price action (De Facto Expropriation Claims in Türkiye). Cassation review is carried out by the 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Cases Heard by the Court of Cassation 5th Civil Chamber).

Conditions of the Action and the Parties

Failure of the purchase procedure. The first sentence of the article sets the precondition of the action: the expropriation must not have been achievable by the purchase procedure. The Assembly of Civil Chambers likewise states that art. 8 of the Law makes the purchase procedure the primary route for immovables registered in the land register and that the art. 10 action is brought when that procedure fails (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2023/1024, K. 2024/600, 04.12.2024). The petition is therefore accompanied by the information and documents collected under art. 7 and the price determination made under art. 8.

An expropriation measure that still stands. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, the annulment of an urgent expropriation decision by the administrative courts does not end the action as long as the expropriation decision stands (details in this case note).

The parties. The claimant is the expropriating administration and the defendant is the registered owner. If, while the action is pending, it emerges that the true owner is someone else or that the registered owner had died earlier, the action continues with the true owner or the heirs joined (art. 14(5)). In shared or joint ownership, each co-owner may challenge the expropriation measure alone (art. 14(3)).

Procedure: Court, Summons, Time-Limits, Fees and Evidence

Competent court and summons

The action is heard by the civil court of first instance where the immovable is located (art. 10(1)). The court sets a hearing date no later than thirty days after the administration’s application and, attaching copies of the petition and documents, summons the owner by an annotated summons, or by public notice where the address cannot be found. A summary of the expropriation and the documents is published at least once each in a newspaper and an online news site of the place where the immovable is located and in a newspaper published throughout Türkiye. The content of the summons is listed in the Law:

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 10(3) (Summons and notice) (unofficial translation)

“In the annotated summons to be sent by the court directly to the owner or in the notification to be made by public notice;

… f) that, if those who bring an annulment action against the expropriation measure before the administrative courts within the period provided in article 14 do not document … that they have brought an action, the expropriation measure will become final and the immovable will be registered in the name of the expropriating administration on the basis of the expropriation price determined by the court,

… h) that they must notify the court in writing, within ten days of service, of all their defences and evidence concerning the matter and the value of the immovable,

shall be stated.”

The summons starts two separate periods for the owner: ten days for defences and evidence on value, and thirty days for an action against the expropriation measure itself.

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 14(1), (3) (Right of action) (unofficial translation)

“Within thirty days of the date of service made by the court under article 10 or, for those who could not be served, of the date of the notice published by the court in a newspaper in lieu of service, the owner of the immovable subject to expropriation may bring an annulment action against the expropriation measure before the administrative courts and a correction action against material errors before the civil courts.

… In joint ownership or shared ownership, the co-owners have an individual right of action.”

If an owner who has brought an annulment action before the administrative courts does not document it, the expropriation measure becomes final and the immovable is registered in the administration’s name on the basis of the price determined (art. 10(3)(f)). For the general rules of the annulment action: The Annulment Action in Administrative Jurisdiction.

Settlement hearing, site inspection and the expert report

The first hearing is a settlement session. If the parties agree, the agreed amount is treated as the expropriation price and is deposited in a bank in the right-holder’s name, in advance and in cash (art. 10(8)). If they do not, the proceedings move to the site inspection and expert stage; the village or neighbourhood headman (muhtar) is also summoned to the inspection and his or her statement is taken (art. 10(6)):

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 10(5)-(7) (Settlement, site inspection and report) (unofficial translation)

“At the hearing to be held on the day set by the court, the judge shall invite the parties to agree on the price of the immovable. …

If the parties fail to agree on the price at the hearing held by the court, the judge shall, setting a site inspection within ten days at the latest and a hearing date thirty days later, carry out a site inspection to determine the value of the immovable through the experts listed in article 15 and in the presence of all those concerned. …

The experts, also taking into account the statements of the parties and other persons concerned, shall submit to the court within fifteen days their reports stating the value of the immovable in accordance with the principles in article 11. The court shall serve this report on the parties without waiting for the hearing date. … At this hearing the parties’ objections, if any, to the expert reports shall be heard and the experts’ responses to those objections shall be taken.”

Court fees and costs

The travel allowances of the court panel, the fees of the experts and the headman, land registry fees and other costs required by the Law are borne by the administration:

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 29 (Payment of costs; as amended by Law No. 7584) (unofficial translation)

“The travel allowances of the court panel under article 10, the fees of the experts appointed by the court under article 15 and of the headman heard at the site inspection, to be assessed by the court, as well as land registry fees, … and other costs required by this Law shall be paid by the administration.”

As to court fees, the 5th Civil Chamber stated in an earlier reversal that, by the nature of the action, a fixed (not proportional) fee is to be ordered in price determination and registration actions and that no fee is collected from a claimant administration that is exempt from fees, and it then upheld the judgment given in line with that reversal (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/11838, K. 2025/6813, 05.05.2025). Whether the administration is exempt from fees is determined separately in each case.

Evidence

Value is determined through the site inspection and expert examination. The parties submit comparable sales, the tax declaration, official valuations and crop and yield documents within the ten-day period. The judge is not bound by a single report: where necessary, he or she appoints a new expert panel and fixes the price drawing on the reports (art. 10(8)).

How Is the Price Determined? The Expert Panel and the Criteria of Art. 11

Value is determined as of the day the administration filed its documents with the court, that is, the date of the action:

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 15(2), (5) (Experts) (unofficial translation)

“It is mandatory to form an expert panel of at least three persons according to the type and nature of the place subject to expropriation.

… In the determination of value by the experts, the day on which the documents were filed with the court by the administration shall be taken as the basis.”

One member of the panel must hold a master’s degree or doctorate in real estate development or be chosen from among persons authorised to value real estate under the Capital Markets Law (art. 15(2)). The method depends on the nature of the immovable, and the report must answer each element separately:

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 11 (Principles for determining the expropriation price) (unofficial translation)

“… f) for land, the net income the immovable or resource would yield according to its … location and conditions if used as it is,

g) for building plots, the sale value according to comparable sales without a special purpose before the day of expropriation,

h) for buildings, … the official unit prices, building cost calculations and depreciation,

… taking these as the basis, they shall determine the value of the immovable on the basis of a reasoned valuation report complying with the valuation standards adopted by the Capital Markets Board, stating the answers to all these elements separately in their report and taking into account the statements of those concerned.

In determining the value of the immovable, increases in value to be caused by the zoning and service undertaking requiring the expropriation, and the profit it would yield according to future forms of use, shall not be taken into account.”

For details such as the capitalisation rate and objective value increase, see Expropriation and the Owner’s Rights; for the review of the price against final awards in the same expropriation, see this case note.

Deposit of the Price in a Bank and Registration

Once the price is fixed, the outcome of the action depends on the administration depositing it in a bank. Registration cannot be ordered until a receipt is produced showing that the price has been deposited or blocked in the right-holder’s name:

Statutory Provision — Expropriation Law (Law No. 2942) art. 10(8) (as amended by Law No. 7139) (unofficial translation)

“If the parties fail to agree on the price, the judge shall, where necessary, appoint a new expert panel to complete its work within fifteen days, and the judge shall determine a fair and equitable expropriation price, drawing on the report or reports and statements of the parties and the experts. … If the parties fail to agree, the administration shall be given fifteen days to deposit the price determined by the judge as the expropriation price … in the bank account to be designated by the court and to produce the receipt showing that it has been deposited. … Where necessary, this period may be extended once by the court. … The registration ruling is final; the parties’ rights of appeal or cassation concerning the price are reserved. If the expropriation price that becomes final as a result of the appeal or cassation review is less than the amount paid to the right-holder in advance and in cash, the difference shall be claimed from the person concerned. …”

Once the receipt is produced, the court orders registration and payment of the price to the right-holder; the decision is notified to the land registry and the bank. Only the price is argued on appeal; if the price is reduced by a higher court, the owner repays the difference, but no interest is charged for the period between the payment and the service of the repayment letter (art. 10(8)). If the right-holder cannot be identified, the price is kept in a three-month time deposit to earn interest.

The Action in the Case Law

Net income for land: production costs cannot be less than one third of gross income

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2023/1024, K. 2024/600, 04.12.2024 (the Assembly’s reasoning; unofficial translation)

“Therefore, the value of land is found by dividing by the capitalisation rate the net income obtained by deducting the production costs of the rotation crops from the gross income calculated according to the crop rotation actually cultivable on the immovable. … In this context, according to the settled practice of the Court of Cassation, when the expropriation price is determined, having regard to the conditions under which agriculture is carried out in Türkiye and the principles of economic farming, production costs should not be less than 1/3 of gross income.”

In this case concerning a dedicated orchard, the regional court of appeal had resisted, stating that for similar immovables in the same expropriation plan the 5th Civil Chamber had accepted costs of one seventh of gross income. The Assembly, holding that the Chamber’s reversal, which secures uniform application, should have been followed, unanimously set aside the resistance decision. This outcome shows that consistency with files from the same area does not replace the established cost standard.

Depositing the price: fifteen days twice and a properly set peremptory period

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/13280, K. 2025/9652, 25.06.2025 (the Chamber’s ground for reversal; unofficial translation)

“Where the price determined cannot be blocked within the 15-day period given for depositing the expropriation price determined by the court, a second 15-day period must be given; dismissing the action because the price was not blocked within a period given only once, contrary to article 10 of Law No. 2942, requires reversal.”

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/587, K. 2025/568, 24.09.2025 (the Assembly’s ground for reversal on different reasoning; unofficial translation)

“In this context, for a peremptory period to produce legal effect, the interim decision setting it must be made in accordance with the law and case law. … Accordingly, before the court gives the claimant administration a peremptory period to deposit the difference in the expropriation price, the amounts already deposited in the expropriation file must be established beyond doubt (where necessary by asking the bank), and only then should the subsequent procedural steps be completed.”

Read together, the two decisions mean that the action may be dismissed if the price is not deposited, but only if the fifteen-day period has been given a second time and the interim decision setting the peremptory period was properly made. In the case before the Assembly, the 5th Civil Chamber had taken into account, on grounds of procedural economy, a late deposit made at the appeal stage, whereas the Assembly resolved the dispute on the different ground that the peremptory period was procedurally defective. In a recent decision the Chamber stated that dismissal is correct where the price was not deposited despite properly given periods and that, faced with a deposit made after the judgment, dismissal stands if the defendant owner has made no statement asking for the merits to be examined (5th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/16894, K. 2026/2253, 12.02.2026). The owner’s statement is therefore decisive when the deposit is late.

Interest: the highest rate after the Constitutional Court’s annulment

Art. 10(9), which provided for statutory interest on the price in actions not concluded within four months, was annulled by the Constitutional Court’s decision of 05.04.2023, E. 2022/83, K. 2023/69 (Official Gazette, 01.08.2023). The 5th Civil Chamber, on the ground that annulment decisions do not have retroactive effect, applied statutory interest starting four months after the date of the action in actions brought before the annulment. The Assembly of Civil Chambers did not adopt this approach:

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2025/113, K. 2026/225, 08.04.2026 (resistance decision upheld as to interest; by majority; unofficial translation)

“In the light of these explanations, it is clear that annulment decisions of the Constitutional Court given through concrete review must be applied to pending files that have not become res judicata. … Accordingly, the regional court of appeal’s resistance decision, reasoning that, assessing the matter in line with the annulment decision of the Constitutional Court and applying, in place of the annulled statutory provision, the last paragraph of article 46 of the Constitution as the higher norm, the highest interest rate applied to public receivables must be applied for the period from the date of the action to the date of judgment without the need for a request by the defendant, is correct.”

In the same decision the Assembly also held unanimously that the official data of the District Directorate of Agriculture and Forestry for the valuation year must be used in the net income calculation. After the Assembly’s decision, a decision of the 5th Civil Chamber that we examined reached the same result in an action brought before the annulment:

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/3121, K. 2026/12089, 25.06.2026 (judgment upheld as corrected, in an action brought in 2021; unofficial translation)

“Whereas the highest interest rate provided for public receivables should have been applied to the expropriation price from the date of the action to the date of judgment pursuant to the last paragraph of article 46 of the Constitution, ruling as written requires reversal.”

During the Assembly’s deliberations, the view was put forward that, since the defendant had not requested interest other than statutory interest, statutory interest should apply under the principle that the court is bound by the parties’ requests; the majority did not adopt it. The line is new; in pending files it matters that the interest request is drafted with this development in mind.

Practical Points

  • The ten-day period: Within ten days of service of the summons, defences and evidence on value (comparable sales, crop and yield documents, information on buildings and trees) are notified in writing (art. 10(3)(h)).
  • Two separate routes: Objections to the price are raised in this action, while the unlawfulness of the expropriation measure is raised before the administrative courts within thirty days; if the annulment action is not documented, the measure becomes final (art. 10(3)(f), art. 14(1)).
  • Site inspection and report: It should be checked that every element affecting value is recorded in the inspection minutes, that the report is reasoned and that official data and the cost standard are correctly applied in the net income calculation.
  • Valuation date: Value is assessed as of the day the administration filed its documents with the court (art. 15(5)); the Assembly of Civil Chambers requires the official data of the valuation year to be used in the net income calculation.
  • Deposit period: The action cannot be dismissed without the fifteen-day period being given a second time or on the basis of a procedurally defective peremptory period; where the deposit is made after the judgment, the owner’s statement on whether the merits should be examined affects the outcome.
  • Interest: The Assembly of Civil Chambers held that the highest rate applies without a request; however, since the decision was given by majority, stating the interest request expressly in the petitions is a prudent course.
  • Appeal: The registration ruling is final; appeal and cassation concern the price only. If the price is reduced by a higher court, the difference is claimed back (art. 10(8)).

Frequently Asked Questions

Who brings the action for determination of the expropriation price and registration, and which court hears it?

The expropriating administration brings it when no agreement is reached under the purchase procedure. The action is heard by the civil court of first instance where the immovable is located; cassation review is carried out by the 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation.

What time-limits does an owner who receives the summons have?

The owner notifies the court in writing of defences and evidence on the value of the immovable within ten days of service. The period for an annulment action before the administrative courts against the expropriation measure, and for a correction action before the civil courts for material errors, is thirty days from service or notice.

As of which date and how is the expropriation price determined?

Value is determined by an expert panel of at least three persons as of the day the administration filed its documents with the court. Net income is used for land, comparable sales for building plots, and official unit prices and depreciation for buildings; increases in value caused by the project requiring the expropriation are not taken into account.

What happens if the administration does not deposit the price in a bank?

The administration is given fifteen days, which may be extended once. If the price is not deposited despite properly given periods, the action may be dismissed; dismissal directly at the end of the first period, however, has been treated by the Court of Cassation as a ground for reversal.

Does the expropriation price bear interest?

In 2023 the Constitutional Court annulled the provision on statutory interest after four months. In its decision of 08.04.2026 the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation held by majority that the annulment also applies to actions not yet final and that the price bears the highest interest rate applied to public receivables from the date of the action to the date of judgment.

Can the registration ruling be challenged, and what happens if the price is reduced on appeal?

The registration ruling is final; appeal and cassation concern the price only. If the price that becomes final on appeal is less than the amount paid to the owner, the difference is claimed from the owner; however, no interest is charged for the period between the date of payment and the date of service of the repayment letter.
