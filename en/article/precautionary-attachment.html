Precautionary Attachment

Short answer: Under Turkish law, precautionary attachment is a provisional measure ordered by a court over the debtor’s property and rights. It secures the collection of a due monetary claim that is not secured by a pledge. For a debt not yet due, it may be sought only where the debtor has no fixed domicile or is attempting to conceal assets or abscond (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Code (EBC) Art. 257). The creditor must present evidence satisfying the court of the claim and, where necessary, of the ground for attachment, and must as a rule provide security; no security is required where the claim is based on a judgment (EBC Art. 259). The order lapses automatically unless the creditor applies to the enforcement office for its enforcement within ten days of the order (EBC Art. 261), and a creditor who obtained attachment before suing or starting enforcement must file an enforcement request or bring an action within seven days of the attachment being carried out (EBC Art. 264). A debtor against whom the order was made without a hearing may object within seven days to its grounds, the court’s jurisdiction and the security; the decision on the objection may be appealed to the regional court of appeal, whose decision is final (EBC Art. 265).

What Is Precautionary Attachment?

Precautionary attachment is a provisional protective measure by which, before any final enforcement proceeding or judgment exists, a court order places an advance attachment over the debtor's movable or immovable property and rights — whether held by the debtor or by a third party — in order to secure the collection of the creditor's monetary claim. The institution is governed by Articles 257 to 268 of Enforcement and Bankruptcy Code No. 2004 (“EBC”). Its purpose is to prevent the debtor from disposing of assets and thereby defeating the creditor's claim before enforcement or litigation can run its course.

Requirements

Precautionary attachment may be sought only where the claim is not secured by a pledge and, depending on the nature of the claim, the further statutory conditions are met.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 257 (unofficial translation)

“A creditor of a monetary debt that is due and not secured by a pledge may have the debtor's movable and immovable property and claims and other rights, whether held by the debtor or by a third party, precautionarily attached. For a debt not yet due, precautionary attachment may be sought only in the following cases: 1 – where the debtor has no fixed domicile; 2 – where the debtor, in order to escape its obligations, prepares to conceal or remove its property or to abscond, or absconds, or engages in fraudulent transactions to that end impairing the creditor's rights. Where precautionary attachment is imposed on this basis, the debt becomes due only as against that debtor.”

Two distinct situations therefore arise: (i) for a due, unsecured monetary claim, precautionary attachment may be sought directly; (ii) for a claim not yet due, it may be sought only where the debtor has no fixed domicile or is attempting to abscond or conceal assets in order to escape its obligations. In the latter case, imposing precautionary attachment accelerates the debt only as against that particular debtor; the debt remains not yet due as against other debtors or sureties.

Procedure: Application to Court, the Competent Court, and Security

Precautionary attachment is ordered by the court having jurisdiction under Article 50 EBC (as a rule, the court that would hear the main action, or of the place of enforcement). The court is free to decide whether or not to hear both parties; in practice, the application is most often examined and decided upon ex parte, without hearing the debtor.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 258 (unofficial translation)

“Precautionary attachment is ordered by the court having jurisdiction under Article 50. The creditor is obliged to produce evidence sufficient to satisfy the court as to the claim and, where relevant, the grounds for attachment. The court is free to decide whether or not to hear both parties. (…) A decision rejecting a request for precautionary attachment shall be reasoned, and an appeal (istinaf) lies against it. The party against whom precautionary attachment has been ordered in that party's presence may likewise appeal. The regional court of appeal shall examine such appeals as a priority, and its decision is final.”

Granting the creditor's application depends on the creditor producing evidence sufficient to satisfy the court of the existence of the claim and, for claims not yet due, of the ground for attachment; conclusive proof is not required, and a showing of strong probability (prima facie proof) suffices. An appeal also lies against a decision rejecting the application.

Obtaining precautionary attachment is, as a rule, conditioned on the creditor providing security.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 259 (unofficial translation)

“A creditor who requests precautionary attachment is liable for all damages thereby suffered by the debtor or a third party should the attachment prove unwarranted, and is obliged to provide the security specified in Article 96 of the Code of Civil Procedure. However, no security is required where the claim is founded on a judgment. Where the claim is founded on a document having the effect of a judgment, the court shall determine, in its discretion, whether security is required. An action for damages may be brought even before the court that ordered the precautionary attachment.”

The purpose of the security is to guarantee compensation for any damage suffered by the debtor or a third party should the attachment prove unwarranted; under Court of Cassation case law, precautionary attachment may not be ordered without security except where an exception is expressly provided by statute (see the Court of Cassation decisions below).

Enforcement of the Order and the Ten-Day Period

A precautionary attachment order obtained from the court is not self-executing; the creditor must request its enforcement from the enforcement office within a specified period.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 261 (unofficial translation)

“The creditor is obliged, within ten days of the date the precautionary attachment order was rendered, to request its enforcement from the enforcement office within the judicial district of the court that rendered it. Failing this, the precautionary attachment order lapses automatically. Precautionary attachment orders are enforced in accordance with the provisions of Articles 79 to 99 governing the manner of attachment. (…) Complaints relating to the enforcement of a precautionary attachment are lodged with the enforcement court to which the enforcing enforcement office is attached.”

This ten-day period is a peremptory (forfeiture) period observed by the authorities of their own motion; if it is allowed to lapse, the precautionary attachment order falls away automatically, with no further act required of the enforcement office or the court. Where the enforcement request is made in time, the attachment proceeds under the general attachment procedure set out in Articles 79-99 EBC (declaration of assets, attachment record, valuation, and the like).

The Steps Completing the Attachment: the Seven-Day and One-Month Periods

Precautionary attachment is not, by itself, a permanent measure; the creditor must, within specified periods, take further steps converting the attachment into a final attachment or founding it on a court decision. Failing this, the measure lapses automatically.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 264 (unofficial translation)

“A creditor who has obtained precautionary attachment before bringing an action or commencing enforcement proceedings is obliged, within seven days of the attachment being carried out (or, if carried out in the debtor's absence, of service of the attachment record), either to file a request to commence enforcement proceedings (attachment or bankruptcy) or to bring an action. Where enforcement proceedings are commenced and the debtor objects to the payment order, that objection is immediately served on the creditor. The creditor is obliged, within seven days of such service, either to request the enforcement court to set aside the objection or to bring an action before the court. Should the enforcement court reject the request to set aside the objection, the creditor must bring an action within seven days of the pronouncement or service of that decision. (…) Where the precautionary attachment was imposed while an action on the claim was pending, or where the creditor has brought an action before the court under the first paragraph, the creditor is obliged to file a request to commence enforcement proceedings within one month of service of the judgment on the merits. Should the creditor allow these periods to lapse, or abandon the action or the enforcement request, or should the enforcement request lapse through expiry of the statutory periods, or should the case file be struck off the docket and not revived within one month, or should the creditor be unsuccessful in the action, the precautionary attachment becomes void (…) Where the debtor does not object to the payment order within the period allowed, or the objection is finally set aside by the enforcement court or annulled by the court, the precautionary attachment is automatically converted into a final attachment.”

The Article governs three distinct time frames commonly encountered in practice: (i) where precautionary attachment was obtained before an action or enforcement proceedings, the creditor must, within seven days of the attachment being carried out (or of service, if carried out in the debtor's absence), file a request to commence enforcement proceedings or bring an action; (ii) where the debtor objects in proceedings that have been commenced, the creditor must, within seven days of service of that objection, request that it be set aside or bring an action to annul it; should the enforcement court reject the request to set aside the objection, a further action must be brought within seven days of that decision's pronouncement or service; (iii) where precautionary attachment was imposed during a pending action on the underlying claim, the creditor must file a request to commence enforcement proceedings within one month of service of the judgment on the merits. Allowing any of these periods to lapse, abandoning the action or enforcement request, or being unsuccessful in the action, causes the precautionary attachment to become void automatically; conversely, where the debtor fails to object in time, or the objection is finally set aside or annulled, the precautionary attachment is automatically converted into a final (executory) attachment.

Where property under precautionary attachment is subsequently attached by another creditor before the precautionary attachment converts into a final attachment, the precautionary-attachment creditor automatically and provisionally participates in that attachment; however, where a precautionary or final attachment predates a pledge, no attachment — including one for a public claim — may participate in that earlier attachment, and precautionary attachment confers no separate priority right on its holder.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 268 (unofficial translation)

“Where property precautionarily attached under Article 261 is attached by another creditor under this Code or another statute before the precautionary attachment converts into a final attachment, the precautionary-attachment creditor automatically and provisionally participates in that attachment under the conditions of Article 100. Where a precautionary or final attachment predates a pledge, no attachment, including one for a public claim, may participate in the attachment predating the pledge. (…) Precautionary attachment confers no other priority right.”

Objection to the Order and Further Appeal

A debtor against whom precautionary attachment was ordered without being heard may object, within a specified period, to the grounds on which the attachment rests, to the court's jurisdiction, and to the security; the same right is granted to third parties whose interests are affected.

Applicable Statutory Provision — EBC Art. 265 (unofficial translation)

“A debtor against whom precautionary attachment was ordered without being heard may object before the court that rendered the order to the grounds on which the attachment rests, to the court's jurisdiction, and to the security, within seven days — where the debtor was present, from the carrying out of the attachment; otherwise, from service of the attachment record. Third parties whose interests are affected may likewise object to the grounds on which the precautionary attachment rests, or to the security, within seven days of learning of it. The court examines the objection strictly by reference to the grounds raised and either grants or rejects it. The objecting party is obliged to append to its petition all documents relied upon. Upon objection, the court, having summoned both parties and heard those who appear, may amend or set aside its order if it finds the objection well-founded; however, if neither party appears, the matter is decided on the basis of the case file. (…) An appeal (istinaf) lies against the decision rendered upon objection. The regional court of appeal examines such an appeal as a priority, and its decision is final. An appeal does not suspend enforcement of the precautionary attachment order.”

Judicial review on objection is narrow: the court examines only the three grounds specified (the grounds for the attachment, the court's jurisdiction, and the security); no adjudication of the merits of the underlying claim takes place at the precautionary-attachment stage. An appeal lies against the decision on objection, and the regional court of appeal's decision on that appeal is final; the Law expressly fixed this finality by the 2020 amendment under Law No. 7251 — in the earlier period (2003-2020), the same finality rule applied, under the then-current text, to review by the Court of Cassation on further appeal (temyiz) (see the Assembly of Civil Chambers decision below). An appeal does not automatically suspend enforcement of the precautionary attachment order.

Court of Cassation Decisions

Finality of the decision on objection and the impossibility of a lower court's insistence decision

Court of Cassation Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, File No. 2017/46, Decision No. 2018/119, 07.02.2018

“This being so, the reversal decision rendered by the Special Chamber, upon the debtor's further appeal against the lower court's decision rejecting the debtor's objection to the decision granting the creditor's request for precautionary attachment, is final under Article 265 of Enforcement and Bankruptcy Code No. 2004. It is not procedurally possible for the local court to render a decision of insistence against this final reversal decision.”

This decision applied the text of Article 265 as amended by Law No. 4949 of 17 July 2003 and then in force, which provided for review by further appeal (temyiz). Following the amendment by Law No. 7251 of 22 July 2020, current law provides that the decision on objection is challenged not by further appeal to the Court of Cassation but by appeal (istinaf) to the regional court of appeal, whose decision on that appeal is final; the principle conveyed here — that the decision on objection is final, with the attendant procedural consequences — applies equally under the current appellate system.

Automatic lapse of precautionary attachment where the ten-day enforcement period is missed

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, File No. 2017/8019, Decision No. 2017/15050, 04.12.2017

“Accordingly, the creditor is obliged, within ten days of the date the precautionary attachment order was rendered, to apply to the competent enforcement office to request enforcement of the precautionary attachment order. If the creditor fails to request enforcement of the precautionary attachment order, within ten days of the date it was rendered, from the competent enforcement office or from the enforcement office where a pending enforcement proceeding exists, the precautionary attachment order lapses automatically. A creditor who has obtained a precautionary attachment order without first bringing enforcement (or bankruptcy) proceedings against the debtor, and has thereby had the debtor's property precautionarily attached, is obliged to file a request to commence enforcement proceedings (by attachment or bankruptcy) against the debtor within seven days. Failing to file such a request, the precautionary attachment becomes void automatically.”

The principle that precautionary attachment may not be ordered without security, and a bank's own letter of guarantee

Court of Cassation Decision
19th Civil Chamber, File No. 2011/10535, Decision No. 2011/12851, 19.10.2011

“Under this provision, as a rule, it is mandatory for the party requesting precautionary attachment to provide security. Precautionary attachment may not be ordered without security. (…) Although, as a rule, precautionary attachment may not be ordered without security, where statutes contain exceptional provisions dispensing with security, precautionary attachment may be ordered without security, limited to the purpose and scope of those exceptional provisions. (…) For these reasons, precautionary attachment may not be ordered without security except under the exceptions expressly recognised by statute.”

The decision further clarifies that a bank may not rely, as security for precautionary attachment, on a letter of guarantee it has itself issued in respect of its own debt, since doing so would defeat the security's protective function.

Failure to request setting-aside of the objection or to bring an action within seven days voids only the precautionary attachment, while the enforcement proceeding remains pending

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, File No. 2015/29893, Decision No. 2016/752, 14.01.2016

“Where the creditor fails, within seven days, to request the enforcement court to set aside the objection, or fails to bring an action to annul the objection, only the precautionary attachment becomes void (Art. 264(IV)); the enforcement proceeding, however, remains pending notwithstanding the precautionary attachment becoming void.”

The decision underscores that, under Article 264(IV) EBC, missing the period results only in the precautionary attachment becoming void, while the enforcement proceeding (now without the security of precautionary attachment) continues; the creditor may still, within separate periods, request that the objection be set aside or bring an action to annul it in order to continue the pending proceeding.

The narrow scope of the objection review, and setting aside precautionary attachment does not warrant staying the pending enforcement proceeding

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, File No. 2013/11030, Decision No. 2013/15987, 18.09.2013

“Under Article 265, governing objection to a precautionary attachment order, ‘A debtor against whom precautionary attachment was ordered without being heard may object before the court that rendered the order to the grounds on which the attachment rests, to the court's jurisdiction, and to the security, within seven days — where the debtor was present, from the carrying out of the attachment; otherwise, from service of the attachment record. The court examines the objection strictly by reference to the grounds raised and either grants or rejects it.’ Having regard to these statutory provisions, no provision has been made for staying a pending enforcement proceeding following the enforcement of a precautionary attachment order and an objection thereto.”

The decision shows that, even where an objection to precautionary attachment results in the attachment being set aside, this does not by itself justify staying a pending enforcement proceeding absent a separate legal basis; the objection review is confined to the grounds, jurisdiction, and security issues listed in Article 265 EBC.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is precautionary attachment, and under what conditions may it be sought?

Precautionary attachment is a provisional measure by which, on the creditor's application, the court places an advance attachment over the debtor's property and rights to secure collection of a monetary claim that is due and not secured by a pledge. For a claim not yet due, it may be sought only where the debtor has no fixed domicile or is attempting to abscond or conceal assets in order to escape its obligations.

Is providing security mandatory to obtain a precautionary attachment order?

As a rule, yes; under Article 259 EBC the creditor must provide security to cover any damages that may arise should the attachment prove unwarranted. However, no security is required where the claim is founded on a judgment; where it is founded on a document having the effect of a judgment, the court decides, in its discretion, whether security is needed.

Within what period must a precautionary attachment order be enforced after it is obtained?

Under Article 261 EBC, the creditor must, within ten days of the date the order was rendered, request its enforcement from the enforcement office within the judicial district of the court that rendered it. If this period lapses, the precautionary attachment order falls away automatically.

Is there a time limit for bringing an action or commencing enforcement proceedings after precautionary attachment has been carried out?

Yes. Under Article 264 EBC, a creditor who obtained precautionary attachment before bringing an action or commencing enforcement proceedings must, within seven days of the attachment being carried out (or, if carried out in the debtor's absence, of service of the attachment record), file a request to commence enforcement proceedings or bring an action; failing this, the precautionary attachment becomes void.

How may a debtor object to a precautionary attachment order made against them?

Under Article 265 EBC, a debtor against whom precautionary attachment was ordered without being heard may object, within seven days — from the carrying out of the attachment where the debtor was present, or otherwise from service of the attachment record — to the grounds on which the attachment rests, to the court's jurisdiction, and to the security, before the court that rendered the order. Third parties whose interests are affected may likewise object within seven days of learning of it.

Can the decision rendered upon an objection to precautionary attachment be further appealed?

Yes; under Article 265 EBC, an appeal (istinaf) lies against the decision rendered on objection. The regional court of appeal examines such an appeal as a priority, and its decision is final; the appeal does not suspend enforcement of the precautionary attachment order.
