Bank Debits During a Turkish Concordat Moratorium: Set-Off, Netting and Account Blocks (EBL Art. 294)

Short answer: As a rule, no: the temporary moratorium (geçici mühlet) produces the effects of the definitive moratorium, and no enforcement proceedings may be taken against the debtor company during the moratorium. The bank’s right of set-off (takas) is subject to arts. 200–201 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (İİK), applied by reference to the date on which the temporary moratorium is announced (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law arts. 288/1, 294). Transfers, EFT payments and POS proceeds reaching the company’s account after the announcement therefore cannot be set off against the loan; set-off against a balance held before the announcement is possible only if the bank’s loan claim is also due (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 139/1). Unless the project provides otherwise, interest stops running on an unsecured bank claim from the date of the temporary moratorium (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4330, K. 2026/1114, 30.03.2026). A secured bank may pursue enforcement by way of realisation of the pledge during the moratorium, but may not take protective custody measures or sell the pledged asset (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 295/1). The proceeds of customer cheques transferred to the bank by assignment endorsement and collected during the moratorium may, however, be applied to the loan if the loan claim arose before the moratorium and had fallen due (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1755, K. 2025/2890, 09.09.2025).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

In a Turkish concordat, a bank cannot set off against its loan any money reaching the company account after the temporary moratorium is announced; set-off against an earlier balance requires a due loan claim (Enforcement Law arts. 294, 200). The article covers account blocks, customer cheques, interest, secured banks, recovery of unlawful debits and collection after approval, with 2022–2026 case law.

Bank debits during a concordat moratorium in Türkiye: effects of the temporary and definitive moratorium (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 294), set-off and netting of incoming funds against a loan, account blocks, customer cheques, interest, secured and unsecured bank creditors, recovery of debits made in breach of protective measures, and decisions of the 6th, 11th and 12th Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation.

How Does a Concordat Moratorium Limit the Bank’s Ability to Collect?

On a request for concordat (konkordato), if the required documents are complete the court immediately grants a temporary moratorium and takes all measures it considers necessary to preserve the debtor’s assets (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 287/1). The temporary moratorium produces the effects of the definitive moratorium (kesin mühlet); the decision is announced and also notified to the Banks Association of Türkiye and the Participation Banks Association of Türkiye (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 288/1-2). For creditors, the main consequence of the moratorium is the stay of enforcement:

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 294/1 (Consequences of the definitive moratorium for creditors) (unofficial translation)

“During the moratorium no enforcement proceedings, including proceedings under Law No. 6183 of 21/7/1953 on the Collection Procedure of Public Receivables, may be taken against the debtor, and proceedings already commenced are stayed; interim injunctions and precautionary attachment orders are not executed; and limitation and forfeiture periods that can be interrupted by an enforcement step do not run.”

Secured claims aside, the bank therefore cannot start enforcement proceedings for its loan claim during the moratorium, and proceedings already started are stayed. Concordat courts may also give concrete form to this stay through measures that address banks directly: in cases that reached the Court of Cassation, the court had ordered the lifting of blocks placed on funds reaching the account after the moratorium (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3590, K. 2025/918, 06.03.2025) and had stayed all enforcement steps by way of interim injunction (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3470, K. 2024/9880, 26.11.2024). The debtor company itself cannot create a pledge from the date of the moratorium without the court’s permission, failing which the transaction is void (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 297/2); granting the bank a pledge over deposits during the moratorium falls within this rule.

Can the Bank Set Off Incoming Funds Against the Loan?

When the bank “applies” (mahsup) money in the account to the loan, it sets off the company’s loan debt against its own obligation to repay the deposit. In a concordat, set-off is tied to the bankruptcy rules:

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 294/4 (Consequences of the definitive moratorium for creditors) (unofficial translation)

“Set-off is subject to Articles 200 and 201 of this Law. In applying these articles, the date of announcement of the temporary moratorium is taken as the basis.”

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 200 (Set-off) (unofficial translation)

“A creditor may set off its claim against the bankrupt’s claim against it. Set-off is not permitted in the following cases. … 2 – If the bankrupt’s creditor becomes a debtor of the bankrupt or of the estate after the bankruptcy is opened;”

In a concordat the date of announcement of the temporary moratorium takes the place of the “opening of bankruptcy”; the bank cannot set off against its loan claim any amount it comes to owe the company after that date. Transfers and EFT payments reaching the account after the announcement, POS (merchant) proceeds, and the proceeds of bills handed to the bank by collection endorsement and collected after the announcement fall into this category. For a balance held in the account before the announcement, art. 200 does not prohibit set-off; but set-off requires both debts to be due (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 139/1). The 6th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation likewise found no breach of the moratorium in applying cheque proceeds to a loan, relying on the fact that the loan claim had arisen and fallen due before the temporary moratorium (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1755, K. 2025/2890, 09.09.2025).

Under a general loan agreement the claim arises when the agreement is signed and the loan is drawn; according to the 6th Civil Chamber, even if the bank has accelerated the account (hesap kat) during the moratorium it cannot demand unconditional payment under the general rules and must pursue the claim within the concordat rules (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4330, K. 2026/1114, 30.03.2026). In the limited search carried out for this article, no Court of Cassation decision directly examining set-off of amounts reaching a deposit or POS account after the moratorium was found; the conclusion above rests on the statutory text and the cheque decisions.

Can the Proceeds of Customer Cheques Endorsed to the Bank Be Applied to the Loan?

When post-dated customer cheques endorsed to the bank against a loan are collected during the moratorium, it is disputed to whom the proceeds belong. In 2022 the 6th Civil Chamber held that the transfer of a post-dated cheque endorsed before the announcement and falling due after it was void under art. 294/6 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, and that the cheque proceeds had to be transferred to the account under the commissioner’s control (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/1251, K. 2022/1999, 11.04.2022). The judgment allowing the claim after that reversal was upheld with the enforcement denial compensation (icra inkâr tazminatı) removed (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1824, K. 2023/2068, 29.05.2023), and the same view was repeated in 2024 (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3042, K. 2024/4246, 21.11.2024).

In 2025 the Chamber adopted different reasoning: by assignment endorsement (temlik cirosu) all rights under the cheque pass to the bank before the concordat request; the endorsement is not an assignment of a future claim, because the claim under the cheque has already arisen and post-dating the cheque does not mean that the claim arises on that date. If the loan claim arose and fell due before the temporary moratorium, applying the collected cheque proceeds to the loan does not breach the moratorium. If, however, the company proves that the cheques, though formally endorsed by assignment, were in fact given as security for the loan by a disguised pledge endorsement (gizli rehin cirosu), the collected proceeds must be returned to the commissioner (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1755, K. 2025/2890, 09.09.2025). In 2026 the Chamber also upheld, in two cases and in one of them with a correction, judgments that deducted cheque proceeds collected during the concordat process from the bank’s claim (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/673, K. 2026/1020, 25.03.2026; Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/824, K. 2026/1170, 01.04.2026). The decisive factor is the real purpose of the endorsement; in the 2022 decision that purpose was established from the cheque deposit slips and the parties’ statements.

Can the Bank Charge Interest During the Moratorium and Debit It from the Account?

Unless the approved project provides otherwise, interest stops running on all claims not secured by a pledge from the date of the definitive moratorium (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 294/3). According to the 6th Civil Chamber, since the temporary moratorium also produces the effects of the definitive moratorium, interest on unsecured claims stops from the date of the temporary moratorium; if the account was accelerated after the temporary moratorium the debtor is not in default, and contractual interest on overdue instalments is calculated only up to the date of the temporary moratorium (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2336, K. 2024/2845, 23.09.2024; Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4330, K. 2026/1114, 30.03.2026). Accruing interest on an unsecured claim during the moratorium and debiting it from the company’s account breaches this rule.

The result is the same where the claim is secured not by the debtor company’s property but by a mortgage over a third party’s real property. In a negative declaratory action brought by a company alleging that the bank had debited interest from its account despite the temporary moratorium measures, the 6th Civil Chamber treated that claim as an unsecured concordat claim, held that no interest runs on it, and reversed the judgment in the company’s favour. A bank that voted against the concordat may assert its interest claim only against the third party jointly liable for the debt; for that reason the dismissal of the claim of the company that was a joint and several surety was found correct (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/426, K. 2023/488, 09.02.2023; Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 303/1). The Chamber maintained the same view by majority in a decision of 15.05.2024; the dissent argued that this settled practice should be reconsidered after Law No. 7101 (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/1131, K. 2024/1531, 15.05.2024).

Does It Make a Difference if the Bank Holds a Pledge?

A secured bank is in a different position from an unsecured creditor during the moratorium:

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 295/1 (Consequences of the definitive moratorium for secured creditors) (unofficial translation)

“During the moratorium, proceedings by way of realisation of the pledge may be commenced, or proceedings already commenced may be continued, for claims secured by a pledge; however, no protective custody measures may be taken on account of such proceedings and the sale of the pledged asset may not be carried out.”

If under the project the pledged asset will not be used by the business, will lose value or is costly to preserve, the court may permit its sale (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 295/2). The 12th Civil Chamber upheld a decision that found nothing irregular in mortgage enforcement proceedings started against a company in the concordat process, relying on art. 295 and on the absence of any contrary measure in the approval decision (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/9874, K. 2023/2656, 13.04.2023).

General loan agreements contain clauses granting the bank rights of pledge, retention, set-off and netting over deposits. In a case upheld by the 11th Civil Chamber, the bank had blocked the deposit of a company under temporary moratorium, relying on the contractual pledge right in respect of a loan for which the company was a surety, and had not paid the money despite the commissioner’s written request. The regional court of appeal found the deposit claim liquid and the bank’s objection unjustified; since the block was lifted during the proceedings it held that there was no need to rule on the merits, but ordered the bank to pay twenty per cent enforcement denial compensation (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3323, K. 2024/5376, 01.07.2024; upheld). In the limited search carried out for this article, no Court of Cassation decision was found that expressly resolves whether a contractual pledge over deposits may be enforced by the bank on its own initiative during the moratorium.

How Is an Unlawful Debit or Account Block Reversed?

The first step is to inform the commissioner and the concordat court; at the commissioner’s request the court may send the bank a written order (müzekkere) to lift the block and return the amount. That order is neither a judgment nor a document within art. 68 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law; in proceedings without judgment based on it, the bank’s objection cannot be overcome by a request for removal of objection (itirazın kaldırılması). The 12th Civil Chamber reversed a removal-of-objection decision on this ground in a case where the bank had blocked TRY 1,126,934.79 reaching the account, citing its letter-of-guarantee risk (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3470, K. 2024/9880, 26.11.2024).

The route used against the bank is enforcement proceedings without judgment for the deposit or the collected amount and, if the bank objects, an action for annulment of objection (itirazın iptali davası); applying to a mediator before a commercial action for annulment of objection is a condition of action (Turkish Commercial Code art. 5/A). If the bank lifts the block and transfers the amount to the account under the commissioner’s supervision before the action is filed, there is no longer a legal interest in the action (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3590, K. 2025/918, 06.03.2025); if the block is lifted during the proceedings, enforcement denial compensation and costs may be imposed on the bank (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3323, K. 2024/5376). In an action for return of cheque proceeds, by contrast, enforcement denial compensation was removed because the dispute concerned the method of collection rather than the existence of the claim (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1824, K. 2023/2068).

A person who acts in breach of an interim injunction is punished, on a complaint filed within six months of learning of the breach, with disciplinary imprisonment of up to six months; if the main action has not yet been filed, the competent court is the court that granted the injunction, and the penalty lapses if the breach ends (Code of Civil Procedure arts. 398/1, 398/8). This route is aimed at punishing the person in breach, not at recovering the money.

How Does the Bank Collect After the Concordat Is Approved?

The concordat becomes binding upon the approval decision and is mandatory for all claims arising before the concordat request; privileged claims, secured creditors’ claims up to the value of the pledge and public receivables are excluded from this rule (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 308/c). According to the 6th Civil Chamber, a creditor cannot assert a claim subject to the concordat outside the concordat rules; for the rejected part it files an action within one month of the announcement of the approval decision, and the amount accepted is paid within the framework of the project (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4330, K. 2026/1114, 30.03.2026; Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 308/b). Attachments imposed in proceedings started before the temporary moratorium and not yet realised also lapse once the concordat becomes binding (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 308/ç).

The picture is different for a secured bank: the 12th Civil Chamber accepted that a bank which had not signed an agreement, and for which the approval decision contained neither a payment plan nor a stay of enforcement, could pursue mortgage enforcement after approval (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/10694, K. 2022/4236, 30.03.2022). New loans granted with the commissioner’s permission after the temporary moratorium are not subject to the concordat terms and may be enforced, in case of default, even during the moratorium (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 308/c-4). Postponement of the return of assets under financial leasing is governed by a separate regime (Return of leased assets in a concordat).

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below are decisions of the 6th and 12th Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation dated 2023–2026. The view taken in the 2022 and 2024 decisions on customer cheques has not been included in the boxes because it changed with the 2025 decision.

A bank claim secured by a third party’s pledge is an unsecured claim in the concordat and does not bear interest

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/426, K. 2023/488, 09.02.2023 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; negative declaratory action; unanimous)

“In concordat practice, the legislator has not subjected secured creditors and unsecured creditors to the same treatment and has prevented secured creditors from being included in the unsecured-claim quorum. The natural consequence of this is that such a claim is accepted as an unsecured claim subject to the concordat and that no interest runs on it.” (translation)

Proceeds collected during the moratorium on cheques proved to have been given by disguised pledge endorsement must be returned to the commissioner

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1755, K. 2025/2890, 09.09.2025 (upheld; Chamber’s reasoning; on the facts the endorsement was treated as an assignment endorsement; unanimous)

“However, if the party requesting the concordat proves that, although the cheques were formally transferred by assignment endorsement, they were transferred to the bank by a disguised pledge endorsement as security for the loan, the collected proceeds of the post-dated cheques must be returned to the commissioner in accordance with the purpose of the concordat and the principle of equal treatment of creditors.” (translation)

A bank claim subject to the concordat cannot be asserted outside the concordat rules

Court of Cassation Decision
6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4330, K. 2026/1114, 30.03.2026 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; disputed-claim action under art. 308/b; unanimous)

“Under this provision, since the concordat is mandatory for all claims other than those listed in the third paragraph of Article 308/c of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, the creditor has no possibility of asserting its claim subject to the concordat outside the concordat provisions.” (translation)

A written order sent to the bank by the concordat court cannot ground a removal of objection

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3470, K. 2024/9880, 26.11.2024 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; removal of objection; unanimous)

“In the present case, the written order of the Commercial Court on which the general attachment proceedings without judgment were based is not a document having the nature of a judgment, nor does it have the nature of … specified in Article 68 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law.” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a bank apply money reaching the account of a company in concordat to its loan?

Not money reaching the account after the announcement of the temporary moratorium; in a concordat set-off is subject to arts. 200–201 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, applied by reference to the announcement date (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 294/4). Set-off against money held in the account before the announcement is possible if the bank’s loan claim is also due (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 139/1).

Can a bank block a company’s account during a concordat moratorium?

During the moratorium no enforcement proceedings may be taken against the debtor and interim injunctions and precautionary attachment orders are not executed (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 294/1). Concordat courts may order, as a protective measure, the lifting of blocks on funds reaching the account after the moratorium; in a decision upheld by the 11th Civil Chamber, the objection of a bank that withheld a deposit through a block based on a contractual pledge was found unjustified (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3323, K. 2024/5376).

Is interest payable to the bank during the concordat process?

Unless the project provides otherwise, no interest runs on an unsecured bank claim from the date of the temporary moratorium (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2336, K. 2024/2845). The 6th Civil Chamber also treats a claim secured only by a mortgage over a third party’s real property as an unsecured claim (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/426, K. 2023/488).

Can the bank apply the proceeds of customer cheques collected during the moratorium to the loan?

According to the 2025 decision of the 6th Civil Chamber it can, if the cheques were transferred by assignment endorsement and the loan claim arose and fell due before the temporary moratorium. If the company proves that the cheques were given as security by a disguised pledge endorsement, the collected proceeds must be returned to the commissioner (Court of Cassation, 6th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1755, K. 2025/2890).

How is money debited by the bank in breach of concordat measures recovered?

Return is first requested through the commissioner and the concordat court. If the bank does not pay, enforcement proceedings without judgment are started and, if the bank objects, an action for annulment of objection is filed after mediation (Turkish Commercial Code art. 5/A). The court’s written order to the bank is not a sufficient document for removal of objection (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/3470, K. 2024/9880).

Set-off, account block, interest and cheque disputes with banks during the concordat process, and actions for recovery, fall within the firm’s Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law and Commercial and Corporate Law practice areas.
