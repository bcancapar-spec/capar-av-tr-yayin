Acquiring Turkish Citizenship after Birth: Routes, Conditions and Challenging a Refusal

Short answer: Under Turkish law, citizenship may be acquired after birth through the general route (five years of uninterrupted residence in Türkiye counted back from the application date, plus the other conditions in art. 11). It may also be acquired on the basis of a marriage to a Turkish citizen that has lasted at least three years (art. 16), through the exceptional route by decision of the President (art. 12), by re-acquisition (arts. 13–14) and by adoption (art. 17). Meeting the conditions does not give the applicant an absolute right (art. 10/1). The Council of State decisions read for this article accept that the administration has wide discretion; even so, the refusal was annulled in some cases, while in others a negative assessment by the security services was treated as sufficient. An annulment action may be brought before the administrative court within sixty days from the day after written notification of the Ministry’s refusal (Administrative Procedure Law art. 7). Because administrative courts may not review expediency (Administrative Procedure Law art. 2/2), an annulment does not make the applicant a citizen; according to a 2025 decision of the 10th Chamber of the Council of State, which may not yet be final, the administration reassesses the application and may issue a new favourable or unfavourable act.

In brief

Under Turkish law, citizenship may be acquired after birth through the general route (five years of uninterrupted residence), marriage (at least three years), the exceptional route, re-acquisition and adoption; meeting the conditions does not give an absolute right. The article explains the conditions in Law No. 5901, the application procedure in Türkiye, the sixty-day time limit, the competent court and how the Council of State and the Constitutional Court review refusals.

How a foreign national can acquire Turkish citizenship after birth under Turkish law, how the application is processed, and how a refusal can be challenged before the administrative courts in Türkiye.

What Is Acquisition of Turkish Citizenship after Birth?

Acquisition after birth means that a person who is not a citizen by birth becomes a Turkish citizen by a decision of the competent authority taken on application, or through adoption. The legal basis under Turkish law is the Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) and its implementing Regulation. Renouncing Turkish citizenship with permission and the Blue Card are outside the scope of this article; they are covered in The Blue Card and Dual Citizenship.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 10/1 (Acquisition of Turkish citizenship by decision of the competent authority)

“(1) A foreigner who wishes to acquire Turkish citizenship may acquire it by decision of the competent authority if he or she meets the conditions set out in this Law. However, meeting the required conditions does not give the person an absolute right to acquire citizenship.”

Under the second paragraph, added in 2017, the basic principles and rules to be applied in acquisition after birth are determined by the Ministry after obtaining the views of the relevant institutions (art. 10/2). In the decisions read for this article, the refusals were issued by the General Directorate of Population and Citizenship Affairs of the Ministry of Interior.

The General Route: Conditions and Five Years of Residence

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 11/1 (Conditions required for application)

“(1) Foreigners who wish to acquire Turkish citizenship are required;

a) to be of full age and to have capacity of discernment under their own national law or, if stateless, under Turkish law,

b) to have resided in Türkiye without interruption for five years counted back from the date of application,

c) to confirm by their conduct that they have decided to settle in Türkiye,

ç) not to have a disease that poses a danger to public health,

d) to be of good moral character,

e) to be able to speak Turkish adequately,

f) to have an income or occupation in Türkiye sufficient to provide for themselves and their dependants,

g) not to be in a situation that constitutes an obstacle in terms of national security and public order.”

The second paragraph of the article was repealed in 2017.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 15 (Residence and calculation of periods)

“(1) For a foreigner, residence means living in Türkiye in accordance with Turkish laws. A foreigner applying to acquire Turkish citizenship may be outside Türkiye for a total not exceeding twelve months within the period of residence required for the application. Periods spent outside Türkiye are counted within the periods of residence provided for in this Law.”

An example: a person who spent a total of ten months abroad in the last five years is within the limit, and those ten months count towards residence. A person who spent a total of fourteen months abroad exceeds the limit; on this article’s reading, the residence condition is not met in that case. Which types of permit count towards the period is not examined in this article.

Acquisition through Marriage

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 16/1-2 (Acquisition of Turkish citizenship through marriage)

“(1) Marriage to a Turkish citizen does not directly confer Turkish citizenship. However, foreigners who have been married to a Turkish citizen for at least three years and whose marriage continues may apply to acquire Turkish citizenship. Applicants are required;

a) to live in family unity,

b) not to engage in any activity incompatible with the marital union,

c) not to be in a situation that constitutes an obstacle in terms of national security and public order.

(2) If the marriage ends after the application because of the death of the Turkish spouse, the condition in sub-paragraph (a) of the first paragraph is not required.”

The five-year residence condition of the general route is not listed in this article. If the marriage is later declared null, a foreigner who entered into it in good faith keeps the citizenship acquired (art. 16/3).

According to the provisions of the Regulation as set out in the 2025 decision of the 10th Chamber of the Council of State, the provincial police directorate investigates family unity, and the commission interviews the spouses separately and together to establish whether the marriage is genuine (Regulation arts. 28–29). According to the same account, where the Turkish spouse acquired citizenship later, the duration of the marriage is counted from the date on which that spouse became a citizen (Regulation art. 25/3).

Failure to submit a valid residence permit can end the application before the merits are reached. In a case examined by the Constitutional Court, the applicant held a temporary protection identity document and a humanitarian residence permit; the application through marriage was removed from processing before a decision, under the Regulation, because no valid residence permit had been submitted. The administrative court dismissed the action and the Constitutional Court found the individual application inadmissible (Constitutional Court (Second Section), App. No. 2020/34394, 02.10.2024).

The Exceptional Route and Investment

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 12/1 (Exceptional cases in the acquisition of Turkish citizenship)

“(1) Provided that they are not in a situation that constitutes an obstacle in terms of national security and public order, the following foreigners may acquire Turkish citizenship by decision of the President.

… b) … Those who obtain a residence permit under sub-paragraph (j) of the first paragraph of article 31 of the Law on Foreigners and International Protection (Law No. 6458 of 4/4/2013), foreigners holding a Turquoise Card, and their foreign spouse and the minor or dependent foreign children of the holder and of the spouse.

c) Persons whose naturalisation is considered necessary.

d) Persons accepted as immigrants.”

Sub-paragraph (a) concerns persons who bring industrial facilities or have rendered outstanding service, and for whom the relevant ministries have made a reasoned proposal. The deciding authority is the President; the Ministry carries out the procedure (art. 19/2). Applications from persons who are in a situation that constitutes an obstacle are refused by the Ministry (art. 12/2). No period of residence is listed in the article.

What is commonly called citizenship by investment rests on sub-paragraph (b). The amounts are not in the Law; they are set by the Regulation.

Regulatory Provision — Regulation on the Implementation of the Turkish Citizenship Law arts. 20/2-b and 20/10 (Exceptional acquisition of Turkish citizenship)

“… A foreigner who meets any one of the following conditions may acquire Turkish citizenship by decision of the President under sub-paragraph (b) of the first paragraph of article 12 of the Law:

… who is found by the Ministry of Environment, Urbanisation and Climate Change to have purchased immovable property for which condominium ownership or a condominium easement has been established, or land with a building on it, worth at least 400,000 US dollars or the equivalent in foreign currency, on condition that an annotation is entered in the land registry that it will not be sold for three years; or to have been promised the sale of immovable property for which condominium ownership or a condominium easement has been established, by a contract drawn up before a notary, with at least 400,000 US dollars or the equivalent in foreign currency paid in advance and on condition that an undertaking that it will not be transferred or cancelled for three years is annotated in the land registry.

… The foreign currency amounts referred to in sub-paragraphs (b), (ç), (d), (e) and (f) of the second paragraph are sold, before the transaction, to a bank operating in Türkiye and by that bank to the Central Bank.”

According to the text of the Regulation as retrieved on 14 November 2026, the other options are: a fixed capital investment of at least 500,000 US dollars; creating employment for at least 50 people; and placing at least 500,000 US dollars, to be kept for three years, in a bank deposit, in government debt instruments, in units of a real estate or venture capital investment fund, or in the private pension system (art. 20/2). The amount for immovable property was raised from 250,000 to 400,000 by Presidential Decision No. 5554, published in the Official Gazette No. 31834 of 13 May 2022. These are provisions of a regulation and have been amended by Presidential decisions in the past; the current text should be checked before applying.

Re-acquisition and Adoption

Those who lost Turkish citizenship by obtaining permission to renounce, and those who lost it in connection with a parent and did not exercise the right of option within the period in article 21 of the Law, may re-acquire citizenship by decision of the Ministry regardless of the length of their residence in Türkiye, provided that there is no obstacle in terms of national security (art. 13). Those who were deprived of citizenship under article 29 of the Law may re-acquire it by decision of the President, and those who lost it under article 34 by decision of the Ministry; on this route the absence of an obstacle in terms of national security and three years of residence in Türkiye are required (art. 14).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 17 (Acquisition of Turkish citizenship through adoption)

“(1) A minor adopted by a Turkish citizen may acquire Turkish citizenship as of the date of the decision, provided that he or she is not in a situation that constitutes an obstacle in terms of national security and public order.”

Where to Apply, the Commission and the Decision

Inside Türkiye the application is made to the governorship of the place of residence, and abroad to the Turkish foreign missions; it is made in person or through a power of attorney relating to the exercise of this right (art. 37). For applications under the general route and through marriage, the provincial citizenship application review commission establishes whether the conditions are met (art. 18).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 19/1 (Procedure and principles in acquisition by decision of the competent authority)

“(1) A citizenship file is drawn up for those foreigners wishing to acquire Turkish citizenship by decision of the competent authority who meet the conditions required for application, and it is sent to the Ministry for a decision. Following the examination and investigation carried out by the Ministry, those whose situation is found suitable may acquire Turkish citizenship by decision of the Ministry; the requests of those not found suitable are refused by the Ministry.”

On this article’s reading, the commission’s opinion does not bind the Ministry; the provision leaves the final decision to the Ministry. The decision is notified to the person concerned and to the authority that received the application (art. 41). A decision granting citizenship takes effect from its date and does not affect the citizenship of the spouse; children in the applicant’s custody acquire citizenship if the other spouse consents (art. 20/1-2).

Challenging a Refusal: Court and Time Limits

A refusal is an administrative act and may be challenged by an annulment action.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 7/1 (Time limit for bringing an action)

“Where no separate period is provided in the special laws, the time limit for bringing an action is sixty days before the Council of State and the administrative courts and thirty days before the tax courts.”

The period starts on the day after the date of written notification (art. 7/2-a). The articles of Law No. 5901 retrieved for this article contain no separate time limit for bringing an action.

Competent court. An action against the Ministry’s refusal is brought before the administrative court. Since no court is designated in a special law, the court with territorial jurisdiction is the administrative court of the place where the authority that issued the act is located (Administrative Procedure Law art. 32/1). The actions underlying the two Constitutional Court decisions read for this article were heard by administrative courts in Ankara. Where the contested act is a decision of the President, the Council of State is the court of first instance (Council of State Law (Law No. 2575) art. 24/1-a); the 10th Chamber recalled this in a case concerning the cancellation of citizenship, in a final decision rejecting the petition that it gave as a court of first instance (Council of State, 10th Chamber, E. 2025/1055, K. 2025/1863, 27.03.2025). A 2025 case in which the applicant challenged both the refusal and a provision of the Regulation was also heard by the Council of State at first instance; on this article’s reading, the basis is art. 24/1-b of Law No. 2575.

Appeals. The decision of the administrative court may be appealed to the regional administrative court (istinaf) within thirty days of notification (Administrative Procedure Law art. 45/1). The cases open to further appeal before the Council of State (temyiz) are listed in art. 46/1; refusal of a citizenship application is not separately mentioned there. On this article’s reading, the regional court’s decision is therefore final as a rule (art. 45/6). However, under art. 46/2, added by Law No. 7589 of 16 July 2026, where the regional administrative court sets aside the first-instance decision and gives a new decision, that decision may be appealed to the Council of State within thirty days, subject to the listed exceptions.

Limits of review.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 2/2 (Types of administrative action and limits of administrative jurisdiction)

“Administrative jurisdiction is limited to reviewing the lawfulness of administrative actions and acts. Administrative courts may not review expediency, and may not give judicial decisions that restrict the performance of the executive function in accordance with the forms and principles laid down in laws and Presidential Decrees, that have the nature of an administrative action or act, or that remove the discretionary power of the administration.”

Refusal of Citizenship in Case Law

Council of State Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers Decision
Council of State, Assembly of Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2022/2372, K. 2023/1014, 17.05.2023 (the Assembly’s reasoning for reversal; by majority)

“Once the examination and investigation have been carried out as to whether a foreigner applying to acquire Turkish citizenship through marriage meets the conditions in article 16 of Law No. 5901, the State has wide discretion as to whether the request will be accepted, because naturalisation falls within its sovereign rights.”

In that case the administrative court had annulled the refusal, holding that it could not be issued without examining the content of the intelligence service’s letter and without substantiating the allegations, and had then insisted on its decision. The Assembly reversed by majority, finding the service’s negative assessment sufficient; the decision stated that a request for rectification could be made. In a similar case the 10th Chamber also reversed, by majority, an annulment that the administrative court had based on the finding that there was nothing beyond an “abstract allegation” (Council of State, 10th Chamber, E. 2016/13184, K. 2021/68, 18.01.2021).

On the other hand, the 10th Chamber upheld a judgment annulling a refusal that was based on events before the marriage.

Council of State Decision (reasoning of the administrative court, upheld)
Council of State, 10th Chamber, E. 2016/2766, K. 2020/4436, 03.11.2020 (upheld; by majority)

“… the contested act, which refused the citizenship request on the ground that the claimant had engaged in activities incompatible with family unity because of measures taken against the claimant before 21/04/2009, the date of the marriage, is not legally sound …”

When the administration argued at the appeal stage that the applicant had divorced, the Chamber reversed the annulment so that this point could be investigated.

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 10th Chamber, E. 2016/15683, K. 2020/4435, 03.11.2020 (the Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; unanimous on the outcome, by majority on the reasoning)

“There is no doubt that, in the acquisition of Turkish citizenship through marriage, the conditions of living in family unity and of not engaging in any activity incompatible with the marital union must be present together.”

In the most recent decision, the decision deferring the pronouncement of the judgment against the applicant had been set aside at the end of the supervision period and the criminal case had been discontinued; the refusal nevertheless relied on that decision. The Chamber dismissed the request concerning the relevant provision of the Regulation and annulled the refusal. The decision is open to appeal and may not yet be final.

Council of State Decision
Council of State, 10th Chamber, E. 2023/5620, K. 2025/4552, 15.10.2025 (given by the Chamber as a court of first instance; may not yet be final)

“… it is clear that taking as the basis for administrative acts a decision deferring the pronouncement of the judgment, which was given before the discontinuance decision and legally ceased to exist as a result of it, would amount to a violation of the presumption of innocence. … it is beyond doubt that the annulment of the contested individual act on the ground stated above will not result in the claimant being directly granted Turkish citizenship, and that the defendant administration may make a fresh assessment in respect of the other matters required by the legislation and within its discretion and, as a result, issue a new act that may be favourable or unfavourable.”

In a case examined by the Constitutional Court, an application under the general route had been refused on the ground that the “good moral character” condition was not met because of a relationship outside marriage, and the administrative court had dismissed the action. The Court noted that no assessment had been made of the fact that the applicant had long been lawfully resident in Türkiye or of whether the applicant had engaged in any activity disturbing public order; it found a violation and ordered a retrial.

Constitutional Court Decision
Constitutional Court (First Section), App. No. 2018/31431, 03.03.2022 (individual application; violation of the right to respect for private life, § 41)

“On that basis, the decision-making authorities are expected – also taking into account the diversity of ways of life that results from the changes and developments occurring in the structure of society over time – to explain, with relevant and sufficient reasons and according to the particular features of the case, the drawbacks that would arise in terms of public order if the person were admitted to citizenship …”

This decision does not make every refusal unconstitutional. In its 2024 decision mentioned above, the Court repeated that meeting the conditions does not give a right to have the request accepted, and stated that an examination from the angle of private life may be required where the request is refused for a reason relating to private life or where the refusal affects private and family life.

Cancellation of the Acquisition Decision

Statutory Provision — Turkish Citizenship Law (Law No. 5901) art. 31 (Cancellation of Turkish citizenship)

“(1) If the decision granting Turkish citizenship came about as a result of a false statement by the person concerned or of his or her concealment of important matters forming the basis for the acquisition of citizenship, it is cancelled by the authority that gave the decision.”

In the decision of 27.03.2025 cited above, citizenship acquired through the exceptional route had been cancelled by a decision of the President, and the action was brought directly before the Council of State. The Chamber also stated that the request for an order re-registering the citizenship could not be met, because a court cannot give a decision that has the nature of an administrative act.

Practical Points

  • Keep separate acts separate. The 10th Chamber of the Council of State held that a decision cancelling citizenship and the implied refusal of a freedom-of-information request asking for the reason for the cancellation cannot be challenged in the same petition.
  • Follow the reason for refusal through the file. In the cases read, the refusal cited the relevant sub-paragraph of the Law, and the content of negative reports by the National Intelligence Organisation or the police was debated during the proceedings.
  • Old criminal records. According to art. 72/5 of the Regulation as set out in the decision, persons sentenced to more than six months’ imprisonment, other than for negligent offences, cannot acquire Turkish citizenship even if the sentence was suspended, time-barred, subject to deferral of the pronouncement of the judgment, converted to a fine or pardoned. In its 2025 decision, which may not yet be final, the 10th Chamber of the Council of State dismissed the request to annul this provision, but annulled the refusal in a case where the deferral decision had ceased to exist on discontinuance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many years of residence are required to apply for Turkish citizenship under Turkish law?

Under Turkish law, the general route requires five years of uninterrupted residence in Türkiye counted back from the date of application (Law No. 5901 art. 11/1-b). Within that period the applicant may be abroad for a total not exceeding twelve months (art. 15). For an application through marriage the test is not a period of residence but a marriage that has lasted at least three years (art. 16). Meeting the conditions does not by itself give a right to citizenship (art. 10/1).

When and on what conditions can a foreigner married to a Turkish citizen apply for Turkish citizenship through marriage?

Under Turkish law, marriage does not directly confer citizenship. A foreigner who has been married to a Turkish citizen for at least three years and whose marriage continues may apply; the applicant must live in family unity, must not engage in any activity incompatible with the marital union and must not be in a situation that constitutes an obstacle in terms of national security and public order (Law No. 5901 art. 16/1). Even where the conditions are met, the administration has discretion (art. 10/1).

Is the amount required for Turkish citizenship by investment written in the Turkish Citizenship Law?

No. Under Turkish law, the Law only provides for the exceptional route and the decision of the President (Law No. 5901 art. 12/1-b); the amounts are in article 20 of the Regulation on the Implementation of the Turkish Citizenship Law. According to the text of the Regulation as retrieved on 14 November 2026, the purchase of, or a notarised promise-of-sale contract for, immovable property requires at least 400,000 US dollars and a three-year annotation in the land registry, and the other listed investment options require at least 500,000 US dollars or the creation of employment for at least 50 people. These are provisions of a regulation and have been amended by Presidential decisions in the past; the current text should be checked before applying.

If an application for Turkish citizenship is refused, in which court and within how many days can an action be brought in Türkiye?

Under Turkish law, an annulment action against the Ministry’s refusal may be brought before the administrative court within sixty days from the day after written notification (Administrative Procedure Law art. 7). As there is no special rule, the court with territorial jurisdiction is the administrative court of the place where the authority that issued the act is located (art. 32). Where the contested act is a decision of the President, the Council of State is the court of first instance (Law No. 2575 art. 24/1-a). The administrative court’s decision may be appealed to the regional administrative court within thirty days of notification (art. 45).

If a court annuls the refusal of a Turkish citizenship application, does the applicant automatically become a Turkish citizen?

No. Under Turkish law, administrative courts may not review expediency and may not give decisions that remove the administration’s discretion (Administrative Procedure Law art. 2/2). In its decision of 15.10.2025, which may not yet be final, the 10th Chamber of the Council of State stated that annulling the refusal does not result in the applicant being directly granted citizenship, and that the administration may make a fresh assessment in respect of the other matters required by the legislation and issue a new act that may be favourable or unfavourable.

Can Turkish citizenship acquired after birth be cancelled later under Turkish law?

Yes. Under Turkish law, if the decision granting citizenship resulted from a false statement by the person concerned or from the concealment of important matters forming the basis for the acquisition, it is cancelled by the authority that gave the decision (Law No. 5901 art. 31). Where the cancellation is made by a decision of the President, the action is heard at first instance by the Council of State.
