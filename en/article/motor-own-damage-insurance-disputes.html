What Is Kasko Insurance? Scope of Cover and Burden of Proof

An insurance contract is one by which the insurer undertakes, against a premium, to indemnify the loss caused to a person’s pecuniary interest by the occurrence of the risk (Commercial Code art. 1401). Kasko covers damage to the insured vehicle itself and is described in Court of Cassation decisions as a type of property insurance; the insurer indemnifies the loss suffered by the insured (Commercial Code art. 1459). Damage caused to the other vehicle is the subject of liability insurance: under a voluntary liability (ihtiyari mali mesuliyet) clause added to a kasko policy, the insurer covers the property damage exceeding the limits of compulsory traffic insurance up to the limits stated in the policy (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10666, K. 2024/8342).

The scope of cover is determined by the policy and the General Conditions of Motor Vehicle (Kasko) Insurance. According to article A/1 of the General Conditions as set out by the 4th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, accidents such as the impact of an object on the vehicle or of the vehicle on an object, overturning, falling or rolling, caused by sudden external events beyond the will of the insured or the driver while the vehicle is moving or stationary, malicious or mischievous acts of third parties, damage caused by persons lacking capacity to act, and property damage resulting from fire, theft or attempted theft are within the cover (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/11242, K. 2022/15072).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 1409 (Scope of insurance) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The insurer is liable for the loss or the sum arising from the occurrence of the risk provided for in the contract. (2) The burden of proving that any or some of the risks provided for in the contract fall outside the insurance cover lies with the insurer.”

The rule on the burden of proof is clear: the insurer must prove that the risk falls outside the cover. The 4th Civil Chamber holds that, if the insurer alleges that the event occurred not as notified by the insured but as the insurer claims, that manner of occurrence must be one of the exclusions listed in article A.5 of the General Conditions; however, if the insured intentionally breaches the duty of correct notification or, clearly contrary to good faith, notifies an excluded event as if it were covered, the burden of proof shifts to the insured (same decision).

Exclusions: Intent, Allegations of Bad Faith and Alcohol

Intent: Unless otherwise agreed, the insurer must indemnify losses resulting from the negligence of the policyholder, the insured and the persons for whose acts they are legally responsible. Where, however, the policyholder, the insured or, in order to obtain the indemnity, persons for whose acts they are legally responsible intentionally cause the risk to occur, the insurer is released from the indemnity and does not return the premiums received (Commercial Code art. 1429).

An allegation of intent or bad faith cannot remain abstract. The 4th Civil Chamber reversed an Appeal Arbitration Panel decision that had rejected a claim on the ground that the driver had deliberately caused the accident in bad faith, because, against a course of events established by the police accident report, the insurer relied only on an abstract loss-investigation report and there was no concrete evidence that the burden of proof had shifted (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/11242, K. 2022/15072).

Driving under the influence of alcohol: Article A.5.5 of the General Conditions, as quoted in a Court of Cassation decision, excludes losses occurring while the vehicle is driven by persons who have taken narcotic substances or more alcohol than the amount prohibited under the Highway Traffic Law. According to the 4th Civil Chamber, however, the driver’s being under the influence of alcohol is not sufficient in itself; the accident must have occurred exclusively as a result of the alcohol, and the burden of proving this lies with the insurer. The Chamber stated that no exclusivity report is required for a driver who is wholly at fault and has 1.00 promille or more of alcohol, whereas the loss remains within the cover where the driver is not at fault or not wholly at fault, and reversed a decision rejecting the claim in a case where the insured driver had 1.44 promille of alcohol and was secondarily at fault while the other driver was primarily at fault (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/668, K. 2024/9713).

The Policy and the Duties of Disclosure and Notification

The policy: The policy sets out the parties’ rights, the provisions on default and the general and any special conditions, and must be easy to read. Provisions of the policy that differ from the proposal form to the detriment of the policyholder, the insured or the beneficiary are invalid; unless the law provides otherwise, amendments to the general conditions in their favour apply immediately and directly (Commercial Code art. 1425).

Duty of disclosure: When the contract is concluded, the policyholder must disclose to the insurer all material facts that he knows or ought to know; matters asked by the insurer in writing or orally are deemed material until the contrary is proven (Commercial Code art. 1435). If a material fact has not been disclosed or has been misstated, the insurer may withdraw from the contract within the statutory period or request an additional premium. If, after the risk has occurred, it emerges that the duty of disclosure was breached through negligence, and the breach could affect the amount of the indemnity or the occurrence of the risk, the indemnity is reduced according to the degree of negligence; in the case of intent, the insurer’s obligation lapses if there is a connection between the breach and the risk, and otherwise the insurer pays in proportion to the premium paid and the premium that should have been paid (Commercial Code art. 1439).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 1446 (Notification when the risk occurs) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The policyholder shall notify the insurer without delay upon learning that the risk has occurred. (2) If failure to notify, or late notification of, the occurrence of the risk has caused an increase in the indemnity or sum payable, the indemnity or sum shall be reduced according to the gravity of the fault. (3) If the insurer has actually learned of the occurrence of the risk earlier, it may not rely on the second paragraph.”

Late notification does not in itself extinguish the indemnity; it leads to a reduction according to the gravity of the fault only if it increased the indemnity. The policyholder must also provide within a reasonable time the information and documents needed to establish the risk and the extent of the indemnity and allow the insurer to investigate; if a breach of this duty increases the amount payable, the indemnity is reduced according to the gravity of the fault (Commercial Code art. 1447).

Calculating the Indemnity: Repair, Total Loss, Salvage and Diminished Value

The insurer pays the actual loss: if the vehicle can be repaired economically, the indemnity is the cost of repair; if repair is uneconomic, the vehicle is treated as a total loss and its market value on the date of the risk is taken as the basis. According to clause 3.3.2.2 of the General Conditions dated 01.04.2013, as set out in a recourse file upheld by the Court of Cassation, a vehicle is deemed a total loss if the repair costs exceed its value on the date of the risk and an expert report establishes that it can no longer be repaired; a vehicle whose full value has been paid becomes the insurer’s property if the insurer so requests, and if, with the insured’s consent, the vehicle is left to the insured in its damaged state, the difference between its market value and its damaged value may be paid. In the same file, the reversal judgment of the (abolished) 17th Civil Chamber stated that, in applying this clause, the market value on the date of the accident must be compared with the repair costs and that the insurer must pay the actual loss (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/6579, K. 2024/1418).

Date of the market value and the release form: In a file concerning a claim for the total-loss difference, the 4th Civil Chamber held it incorrect to decide on the basis of a market-value report relying on 2022 listing data although the accident occurred in 2021, and required the market value to be determined as of the accident date and the adequacy of the payment to be examined as of the payment date. In the same file the Appeal Arbitration Panel had accepted that a release form signed by the insured with a reservation of further rights released the insurer only for the amount paid, and the Chamber rejected the appeal against that finding (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/16346, K. 2025/1344).

Diminished value: Whether the loss in value remaining after repair can be claimed under a kasko policy depends on the policy conditions. In a file where the special conditions of a kasko policy with a voluntary liability clause expressly excluded all diminished value, the 4th Civil Chamber held that diminished value was not the insurer’s responsibility and reversed the decision awarding it (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10666, K. 2024/8342). Claims for diminished value against the operator and the compulsory traffic insurer of the vehicle that caused the accident are discussed in our article on vehicle diminished-value compensation.

Payment of the Indemnity, Advance Payment and Default

The indemnity falls due once the documents have been delivered to the insurer and its investigations are complete, and in any event forty-five days after notification; if the investigations cannot be completed within three months, the insurer pays an advance of at least fifty per cent. Once the debt is due, the insurer is in default without the need for a notice.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 1427 (Payment of the indemnity) (unofficial translation)

“(2) The insurance indemnity or sum falls due after the occurrence of the risk and the delivery to the insurer of the documents relating to the risk, once the insurer’s investigations concerning its performance are completed, and in any event forty-five days after the notification to be made under article 1446. For personal insurance this period is fifteen days. If the examination is delayed owing to a fault not attributable to the insurer, the period does not run. (3) If the investigations cannot be completed within three months from the notification to be made under article 1446, the insurer shall pay as an advance, to be set off against the indemnity or sum, at least fifty per cent of the amount of loss or sum to be determined promptly by agreement of the parties or, failing agreement, according to the result of a preliminary expert examination ordered by the court. (4) Once the debt falls due, the insurer is in default without the need for a notice. (5) Contractual provisions releasing the insurer from the obligation to pay default interest are invalid.”

The 4th Civil Chamber found it correct, in determining the date of default, to take into account that under Commercial Code art. 1427/2 and clause B.3.3.4.1 of the General Conditions in force on the accident date the insurer was obliged to pay at the latest by the end of the 45th day after the notification of the loss (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10666, K. 2024/8342).

The Insurer’s Subrogation and Recourse

A kasko insurer that pays the indemnity is subrogated to the insured’s rights against those liable for the loss up to the amount it has paid, and may continue any action or enforcement proceedings already initiated against them from where they stand. An insured who infringes the rights passed to the insurer is liable to the insurer; if only part of the loss has been indemnified, the insured retains his right of recourse against those liable for the remainder.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 1472 (Subrogation) (unofficial translation)

“(1) When the insurer pays the insurance indemnity, it is legally subrogated to the insured. If the insured has a right of action against those liable for the loss that occurred, this right passes to the insurer up to the amount it has indemnified. If an action or enforcement proceedings have been initiated against those liable, the insurer may, by proving the payment made to its insured, continue the action or proceedings from where they stand under the rule of subrogation, without the consent of the court or of the other party. (2) If the insured acts in a way that infringes the rights passed to the insurer under the first paragraph, he is liable to the insurer. If the insurer has compensated the loss in part, the insured retains his right of recourse against those liable for the remainder.”

In a recourse action the insurer cannot claim more from the liable party than the right the insured had. In the recourse file mentioned above, upheld by the Court of Cassation, an amount up to the policy limit had been collected from the other vehicle’s traffic insurer; once it was established that the vehicle did not meet the total-loss conditions and that the loss would have remained at the determined repair cost, the kasko insurer’s remaining recourse claim was calculated on the basis of the repair cost (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/6579, K. 2024/1418).

Limitation

Claims arising from the insurance contract are time-barred two years from the date on which the claim fell due, and claims for the insurance indemnity are in any event time-barred six years from the date on which the risk occurred; the due date is determined under Commercial Code art. 1427.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 1420 (Limitation) (unofficial translation)

“(1) All claims arising from the insurance contract are time-barred two years from the date on which the claim fell due and, subject to article 1482, claims relating to the insurance indemnity and the sum insured are in any event time-barred six years from the date on which the risk occurred. (2) Provisions in other laws are reserved.”

In a file concerning a kasko indemnity for a stolen vehicle, the 4th Civil Chamber stated that the two-year period of Commercial Code art. 1420 applies to claims based on a kasko contract and that the extended criminal limitation period does not apply; the regional court of appeal had likewise held that the claim could not be assessed under Highway Traffic Law art. 109, which governs the extended limitation period (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/14634, K. 2023/9076).

The Insurance Arbitration Commission, the Competent Court and Mediation

An insured in dispute with the insurer may, as an alternative to the courts, apply to the Insurance Arbitration Commission. This requires a prior application to the insurer and documentary proof that the request was wholly or partly rejected; failure of the insurer to reply in writing within fifteen working days is also sufficient. No application may be made to the Commission for disputes that have already been brought before a court or a consumer arbitration committee.

Statutory Provision — Insurance Law (Law No. 5684) art. 30 (Arbitration in insurance; paragraphs 1, 13 and 14) (unofficial translation)

“(1) An Insurance Arbitration Commission is established within the Association for the resolution of disputes arising from the insurance contract between the policyholder or persons benefiting from the insurance contract and the party assuming the risk, or between persons entitled to benefit from the Account and the Account.… A person in dispute with an institution that is a member of the insurance arbitration system may use the arbitration procedure even if there is no special provision in the contract in dispute.… (13) In order to apply to the Commission, the person in dispute with the insurance institution must have made the necessary applications to the insurance institution concerning the event giving rise to the dispute and must document that the request was wholly or partly rejected. Failure of the insurance institution to respond in writing within fifteen working days from the date of application is also sufficient for applying to the Commission. (14) No application may be made to the Commission concerning disputes that have been brought before a court or before a Consumer Arbitration Committee under the Consumer Protection Law.”

The possibility of objecting to and appealing against arbitral awards depends on monetary thresholds laid down in the Law, which may be updated (Insurance Law art. 30/12, Insurance Law art. 30/22); many of the 4th Civil Chamber decisions cited in this article were rendered on appeals against decisions of the Appeal Arbitration Panel. The attorney’s fee awarded against a party whose request is wholly or partly rejected is one fifth of the fee under the Minimum Attorney Fee Tariff (Insurance Law art. 30/17); the 4th Civil Chamber stated that this fraction applies for both parties to the fee for cases before courts of first instance, without falling below the fixed fee (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10666, K. 2024/8342).

Competent court and mediation: For a consumer who insures a privately used vehicle under a kasko policy, the contract is a consumer transaction and the consumer court has jurisdiction over the claim for indemnity; jurisdiction is a matter of public order (Consumer Protection Law art. 73; 4th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/7105, K. 2023/11538). Actions brought by consumers before consumer courts are exempt from court fees (Consumer Protection Law art. 73). For consumer disputes below the threshold set by the Law and increased each year by the revaluation rate, applying to a consumer arbitration committee is mandatory; this rule does not prevent recourse to alternative dispute resolution bodies under the relevant legislation (Consumer Protection Law art. 68). For disputes heard by consumer courts, applying to a mediator before suing is a precondition to suit, save for exceptions such as matters within the competence of consumer arbitration committees (Consumer Protection Law art. 73/A). If the insured is not a consumer, actions arising from matters regulated in the Turkish Commercial Code are commercial cases regardless of whether the parties are merchants, so the dispute is heard by the commercial court of first instance, and for claims for a sum of money applying to a mediator before suing is a precondition to suit (Commercial Code art. 4, Commercial Code art. 5, Commercial Code art. 5/A).

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below have been verified against their full texts in the official case-law database. The decisions in this article were rendered by the 4th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation in 2022–2025; the text of one of them states that the 17th Civil Chamber, which had earlier reversed the judgment in the same file, had been abolished (4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/6579, K. 2024/1418).

The insurer must prove an exclusion; an allegation of bad faith must be proven by concrete evidence, not an abstract report

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/11242, K. 2022/15072, 21.11.2022 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning; the Appeal Arbitration Panel decision was reversed)

“…as a rule, the allegation that the risk falls outside the cover must be proven by the insurer.… The insurance company must prove, not by abstract allegations but by concrete evidence, that the accident was caused in bad faith, that the damage is not within the cover, that the event occurred not as notified by the insured but as they allege, and that this manner of occurrence is one of the excluded cases. There is no concrete evidence in the file that the burden of proof has shifted.” (translation)

Alcohol in kasko: for the loss to be excluded, the accident must have occurred exclusively as a result of the alcohol

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/668, K. 2024/9713, 15.10.2024 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning; the Appeal Arbitration Panel decision was reversed)

“…the loss caused by drivers who are 100% at fault and have 1.00 promille or more of alcohol, because they are unable to drive the vehicle safely, shall be deemed outside the insurance cover without requiring an exclusivity report. Where the driver of the vehicle insured under kasko is not at fault or not wholly at fault, a loss caused because not only the driver’s alcohol but also other causes contributed to the occurrence of the traffic accident shall be deemed within the kasko cover. Accordingly, for the damage to fall outside the cover, it is not sufficient that the driver was merely under the influence of alcohol; the accident must have occurred exclusively under the influence of the alcohol. Moreover, in such a case the burden of proving that the damage falls outside the cover lies with the insurer under Article 1409 of the Turkish Commercial Code.” (translation)

For a total-loss indemnity, the market value is determined as of the accident date

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/16346, K. 2025/1344, 30.01.2025 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning; the Appeal Arbitration Panel decision was reversed)

“Although the accident date is 2021, it was not correct to decide on the basis of a report determining the market value for 2022. In that case, the market value of the vehicle must be determined as of 01.09.2021, the date of the accident, and it must be established whether the payment made by the defendant ... was sufficient as of the payment date and whether the claimant has any remaining claim.” (translation)

Diminished value expressly excluded in the policy cannot be claimed; the indemnity must be paid at the latest by the end of the 45th day after notification

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/10666, K. 2024/8342, 30.09.2024 (the Chamber’s reasoning; the Appeal Arbitration Panel decision was reversed as to diminished value and the attorney’s fee)

“…that there is no error in taking into account, in determining the date of default, that under Article 1427/2 of the TCC and clause B.3.3.4.1 of the General Conditions of Kasko Insurance in force on the accident date the insurer was obliged to pay the indemnity at the latest by the end of the 45th day after the notification of the loss, counted from the date of the claimant’s application to the defendant before suing… The claimant’s claim for diminished value is not within the kasko insurance cover under the special conditions of the policy. Examination of the full text of the policy in the file shows that it contains an express provision that all diminished value is excluded. Accordingly, under the policy conditions the defendant is not liable for diminished value.” (translation)

The two-year limitation period applies to kasko claims; the extended criminal limitation period does not

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/14634, K. 2023/9076, 12.09.2023 (decision upholding the judgment; the Chamber’s reasoning for upholding)

“…the expiry of the 2-year limitation period in Article 1420 of Law No. 6102, since the claimant’s claim is based on a kasko insurance policy, which is a type of property insurance, and the inapplicability of the extended criminal limitation period to a claim for indemnity based on a kasko insurance contract…” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

What losses does kasko insurance cover?

Cover is determined by the policy and the General Conditions of Kasko Insurance. Under article A/1 of the General Conditions as set out in Court of Cassation decisions, property damage to the vehicle caused by accidents such as impact, collision, overturning, falling or rolling, by malicious acts of third parties, and by fire, theft or attempted theft is covered. The insurer must prove that a loss falls outside the cover (Commercial Code art. 1409).

Does kasko pay if the driver was under the influence of alcohol?

Alcohol alone is not enough. According to the 4th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, for the loss to be excluded the accident must have occurred exclusively as a result of the alcohol, and the insurer must prove this; no exclusivity report is required for a driver who is wholly at fault and has 1.00 promille or more of alcohol. In a case where the other driver was primarily at fault, the loss was held to be covered.

When must the kasko insurer pay the indemnity?

The indemnity falls due once the documents have been delivered and the investigations completed, and in any event forty-five days after the notification of the loss; if the investigations are not completed within three months, the insurer pays an advance of at least fifty per cent. Once the indemnity is due, the insurer is in default without the need for a notice (Commercial Code art. 1427).

Does kasko cover the diminished value of my vehicle?

This depends on the policy conditions. The Court of Cassation held, in a file where the special conditions of the policy expressly excluded all diminished value, that the insurer was not liable for it. Diminished value may separately be claimed from the operator and the compulsory traffic insurer of the vehicle that caused the accident.

Where should a kasko dispute be taken?

A written application must first be made to the insurer. If the request is rejected or not answered within fifteen working days, an application may be made to the Insurance Arbitration Commission (Insurance Law art. 30). The courts are also available: the consumer court has jurisdiction if the insured is a consumer, and otherwise the commercial court of first instance; the rules on mediation before suing and, for consumer disputes below the set threshold, on the consumer arbitration committee must be observed.

What is the limitation period for a kasko indemnity?

Claims arising from the insurance contract are time-barred two years from the date on which the claim fell due, and claims for the indemnity are in any event time-barred six years from the date on which the risk occurred (Commercial Code art. 1420). The Court of Cassation holds that the extended criminal limitation period does not apply to kasko claims.
