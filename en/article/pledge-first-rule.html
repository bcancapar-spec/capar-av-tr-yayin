Pledge-First Rule in Turkish Enforcement Law: Can a Mortgagee Bank Go Straight to Attachment? (EBL Art. 45)

Short answer: On a mortgage-secured loan, a bank must as a rule first enforce by way of realisation of the mortgage; it cannot start general attachment or bankruptcy proceedings directly against the debtor (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 45/1). The Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay Hukuk Genel Kurulu) treats this rule as a matter of public order; annulment of attachment proceedings started in breach of it may be sought by complaint without any time limit (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2021/997, K. 2023/801, 13.09.2023). The bank may resort to attachment for the part of the claim not covered by the pledge or exceeding the mortgage limit; if it holds a promissory note or cheque it may choose bills-of-exchange enforcement, but it cannot run two sets of proceedings for the same claim at the same time. A joint and several surety whose suretyship debt is not secured by the mortgage may be pursued before the pledge is realised, provided that the debtor is in default and a demand has gone unanswered, or the debtor is manifestly insolvent (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 586/1).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

A bank holding a mortgage must first enforce by realising it; attachment brought directly against the debtor breaches Turkish Enforcement Law art. 45/1. The article explains the public-order nature of the rule, the complaint without time limit, pledge shortfall and claims above the mortgage limit, duplicate proceedings, the bills-of-exchange exception and sureties, with 2023–2025 Court of Cassation decisions.

The pledge-first rule (önce rehne müracaat) under Article 45 of the Turkish Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law: annulment of general attachment proceedings on a mortgage-secured bank loan, complaint without time limit, pledge shortfall, claims exceeding the mortgage limit, duplicate proceedings, the bills-of-exchange exception, sureties and Court of Cassation decisions.

What Is the Pledge-First Rule?

The pledge-first rule (önce rehne müracaat) means that a creditor whose claim is secured by a pledge or mortgage (ipotek) may, as a rule, enforce only by way of realisation of the pledge (rehnin paraya çevrilmesi). Even if the debtor is a company subject to bankruptcy, the creditor cannot start general attachment (genel haciz) or bankruptcy proceedings without first resorting to the pledge.

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 45/1 (Receivables secured by pledge and mortgage) (unofficial translation)

“Even if the debtor of a receivable secured by a pledge is a person subject to bankruptcy, the creditor may only pursue enforcement by way of realisation of the pledge. However, if the amount of the pledge is insufficient to pay the debt, the creditor may pursue the remaining receivable by way of bankruptcy or attachment.”

For bank loans, this means that a bank holding a mortgage cannot go directly, by general attachment, after the debtor’s bank accounts, vehicles or immovables other than the mortgaged one. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, even where the pledged asset belongs to a third party, the creditor must as a rule first enforce by way of realisation of the pledge. In the same decision the Assembly explains the two purposes of the rule: to prevent the debtor’s assets from being unnecessarily reduced by the attachment of assets other than the pledge, and to preserve the ability of the debtor’s other creditors to resort to those assets. For this reason the rule is regarded as mandatory and as a matter of public order (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2021/997, K. 2023/801, 13.09.2023). The parties cannot agree otherwise; such an agreement is void (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/300, K. 2025/571, 24.09.2025).

How Are Attachment Proceedings in Breach of the Rule Challenged, and Is There a Time Limit?

If general attachment proceedings are started directly for a mortgage-secured claim, the debtor applies to the enforcement court by way of complaint (şikâyet) and seeks annulment of the proceedings. As a rule, a complaint must be filed within seven days of learning of the act; however, a complaint may be filed at any time where a right has not been fulfilled (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 16). The 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation treats an allegation that the proceedings breach art. 45 as a complaint under art. 16 and, because it concerns public order, does not subject it to a time limit. In the same decision the Chamber stated that it now also examines an allegation of duplicate proceedings in non-judgment enforcement not as an objection to the debt but as a complaint to the enforcement court without time limit (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1109, K. 2023/7900, 28.11.2023).

The availability of a complaint without time limit does not mean the debtor is protected without taking any step. According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, if the debtor objects to the payment order and the creditor brings an action for annulment of the objection (itirazın iptali davası), the judge may apply art. 45/1 of its own motion; a debtor who neither objects to the proceedings nor files a complaint, however, cannot benefit from this protection. In the same decision it was held that dismissing the whole action on art. 45 grounds, on the creditor’s appeal alone and in favour of a debtor who had not appealed the partial acceptance, breached the prohibition of reformatio in peius (aleyhe hüküm kurma yasağı) (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/300, K. 2025/571, 24.09.2025).

What Can the Bank Do if the Pledge Is Insufficient or the Mortgage Limit Is Exceeded?

Under the second sentence of art. 45/1, if the amount of the pledge is insufficient to pay the debt, the creditor may pursue the remaining claim by attachment or bankruptcy. The ordinary route is to obtain a certificate within the proceedings for realisation of the pledge:

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 150/f (Provisional pledge shortfall certificate) (unofficial translation)

“If, according to the value assessed and finalised after the creditor’s request for sale, it is understood that the pledged asset will not cover the receivable, a provisional pledge shortfall certificate is issued to the creditor, upon request, for the outstanding amount.”

On the basis of this certificate the creditor may request attachment of the debtor’s other assets (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 150/f). If the pledge cannot be sold, or the sale price does not cover the claim, a pledge shortfall certificate (rehin açığı belgesi) is issued for the remaining claim; this certificate has the character of an instrument containing an acknowledgement of debt. If attachment is requested within one year from the auction day where no sale took place, or from the date the sale became final, no new enforcement order or payment order needs to be served (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 152).

In bank lending, mortgages are usually maximum-amount mortgages (üst sınır ipoteği). According to the 12th Civil Chamber, the part of the claim secured by the total of the maximum-amount mortgages may only be enforced by realisation of the pledge; general attachment is possible for the part exceeding the limits, and general attachment proceedings are annulled up to the total of the limits (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3362, K. 2024/476, 17.01.2024). The 11th Civil Chamber likewise upheld a regional appellate decision finding no breach of art. 45 in general attachment proceedings in which the enforcement request held the principal debtor liable only for the part exceeding the mortgage limit (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3313, K. 2024/5522, 03.07.2024; affirmed).

Some decisions of the 12th Civil Chamber state, with reference to the Assembly of Civil Chambers decision of 18.04.2001, 2001/12-354 E., 2001/367 K., that where it is evident that the claim cannot be covered by the pledge, general attachment proceedings may be brought against the principal debtor subject to a “without double recovery” reservation. This criterion is applied narrowly: in a case where a mortgage of TRY 4,000,000 exceeded an enforced claim of about TRY 766,000, the Chamber held that the general attachment proceedings had to be annulled (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1086, K. 2023/9163, 21.12.2023).

If the Bank Starts Both Attachment and Mortgage Proceedings, Which One Is Annulled?

Banks sometimes start non-judgment general attachment proceedings for a loan claim and then mortgage proceedings for the same claim “without double recovery” (tahsilde tekerrür olmamak kaydıyla). According to the Assembly of Civil Chambers, because resorting to the pledge first is a statutory obligation, an allegation of duplication can only be raised against proceedings other than realisation of the pledge; mortgage proceedings cannot be annulled for duplication even if they were started second (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2021/997, K. 2023/801, 13.09.2023). What must be annulled is the general attachment proceedings started in breach of art. 45/1; the 12th Civil Chamber has stressed that this result does not depend on which proceedings came first (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1086, K. 2023/9163, 21.12.2023).

Proceedings start when the enforcement request is submitted to the enforcement office. In a case where the general attachment request was recorded in the National Judiciary Informatics System (UYAP) on 20.10.2023 and the mortgage request on 23.10.2023, the Chamber found that the first proceedings were the general attachment proceedings and held that they had to be annulled (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1307, K. 2025/2865, 07.04.2025).

Can the Bank Go Straight to Bills-of-Exchange Enforcement if It Holds a Promissory Note or Cheque?

Yes. Article 45/3 reserves art. 167 on bills of exchange, promissory notes and cheques (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 45/3):

Statutory Provision — Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004, art. 167/1 (Conditions for accepting the proceedings) (unofficial translation)

“A creditor whose receivable is based on a cheque, bill of exchange or promissory note may, even if the receivable is secured by a pledge, pursue enforcement by way of attachment under the special procedures of this chapter or, if the debtor is subject to bankruptcy, by way of bankruptcy.”

This exception gives a right of choice, not a right to run both proceedings together. According to the decision of the Grand General Assembly of the Court of Cassation on the unification of case law (içtihadı birleştirme kararı) of 20.01.2023, E. 2021/2, K. 2023/1, as cited in the Assembly of Civil Chambers decision, enforcement for a claim that is secured by a pledge and also tied to a bill of exchange cannot be pursued simultaneously and regardless of order both by realisation of the pledge and by bills-of-exchange attachment, even with a “without double recovery” reservation. Noting that unification decisions are binding under art. 45 of the Court of Cassation Law No. 2797, the Assembly held that mortgage proceedings started for the same claim while promissory-note enforcement was pending had to be annulled (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2022/1039, K. 2023/856, 27.09.2023).

In 2026 the 11th Civil Chamber upheld a regional appellate decision, based on the same principle, which approved the dismissal of an action for annulment of objection concerning mortgage proceedings started later by a bank that had exercised its choice in favour of bills-of-exchange enforcement (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5804, K. 2026/2655, 06.05.2026; affirmed). Where no bills-of-exchange proceedings are brought, the exception does not apply and the creditor has no right of choice (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2021/997, K. 2023/801, 13.09.2023).

Are There Other Exceptions to the Rule?

Article 45 contains two further exceptions. For pledge-secured claims arising from housing finance as defined in art. 38/A of Capital Markets Law No. 2499, to which the article refers, and for pledge-secured claims of the Housing Development Administration (Toplu Konut İdaresi Başkanlığı), the creditor may enforce by realisation of the pledge or resort directly to attachment (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 45/2). For mortgage-secured interest and annual instalment claims, realisation of the pledge, attachment or bankruptcy may be pursued according to the creditor’s choice and the debtor’s status (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 45/4). A limited search for this article did not identify a recent Court of Cassation decision on the application of art. 45/4 to bank loan claims.

Does the Rule Apply to a Surety, or to a Surety Who Also Gave a Mortgage?

The pledge-first rule was laid down for the principal debtor and, as a rule, does not apply to sureties. Where the mortgage secures only the debts of the borrower, the 12th Civil Chamber does not regard general attachment proceedings against a joint and several surety (müteselsil kefil) as breaching art. 45 once the conditions of art. 586 of the Turkish Code of Obligations are met (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/7479, K. 2021/7971, 27.09.2021; affirmed).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, art. 586/1 (Joint and several suretyship) (unofficial translation)

“Where the surety has agreed to assume the obligation as a joint and several surety or by any expression to that effect, the creditor may pursue the surety without first pursuing the debtor or realising an immovable pledge. However, this requires that the debtor be in default of performance and that a notice has remained without result, or that the debtor be manifestly insolvent.”

Where the claim is secured by a possessory pledge over movables or by a pledge over receivables, the joint and several surety cannot be pursued before the pledge is realised, save for the statutory exceptions (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 586/2). An ordinary surety (adi kefil), where the claim was also secured by a pledge before or at the time of the suretyship, may require that the claim first be satisfied from the pledged asset, except where the debtor has been declared bankrupt or granted a concordat moratorium (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 585/2).

The result changes if the surety gave a mortgage that also secures his or her own suretyship debt. The 12th Civil Chamber holds that where, according to the mortgage deed (ipotek akit tablosu), the mortgage also covers the debts arising from the mortgagor’s suretyship, the surety too must first be pursued by realisation of the pledge, while general attachment may be brought against the surety for the part of the claim not covered by the mortgage (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4387, K. 2025/6585, 22.10.2025; Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/1307, K. 2025/2865, 07.04.2025). The enforcement route against a surety is therefore determined by the scope of security stated in the mortgage deed.

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below are decisions of the Assembly of Civil Chambers and the 12th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation dated 2023–2025. As the full text of the unification decision of 20.01.2023 could not be found in the official case-law database, that decision is reported only as cited in the Assembly of Civil Chambers decisions.

Mortgage proceedings cannot be annulled for duplication even if started second

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2021/997, K. 2023/801, 13.09.2023 (reversal of the lower court’s insistence decision; unanimous)

“Since, under the first paragraph of Article 45 of Law No. 2004, a pledgee whose receivable is secured by a pledge must (as a rule) first pursue enforcement by way of realisation of the pledge, an allegation of duplication can only be raised in respect of proceedings other than those conducted by way of realisation of the pledge. Accordingly, even if the second proceedings brought against the debtor are proceedings by way of realisation of the mortgage, they cannot be annulled on grounds of duplication.” (translation)

In that case the bank started non-judgment general attachment proceedings on 20.11.2018 and, on 05.12.2018, judgment-type mortgage realisation proceedings with a “without double recovery” reservation; the insistence decision annulling the mortgage proceedings was reversed.

A creditor who also holds a bill of exchange must choose one route; a “without double recovery” reservation does not save the second proceedings

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2022/1039, K. 2023/856, 27.09.2023 (reversal of the regional appellate court’s insistence decision; unanimous)

“Accordingly, once the creditor has chosen one of these enforcement routes and commenced proceedings, it may not resort to the other route for the same receivable while those proceedings are pending, even with a ‘without double recovery’ reservation.” (translation)

A debtor who neither objects nor files a complaint cannot rely on the pledge-first rule

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/300, K. 2025/571, 24.09.2025 (reversal of the regional appellate court’s insistence decision; action for annulment of objection; unanimous)

“If the debtor files a complaint, the complaint will not be subject to a time limit owing to the public order the provision seeks to protect; and if the debtor objects to the payment order and the matter is brought before the court by an action for annulment of the objection so that the stayed proceedings may continue, the judge may take this prohibition into account of its own motion even if the debtor has not raised it; however, if the debtor does not object to, or file a complaint against, proceedings commenced in breach of the pledge-first requirement, it cannot benefit from the protection of this norm.” (translation)

With maximum-amount mortgages, general attachment is available only for the part of the claim exceeding the limits

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3362, K. 2024/476, 17.01.2024 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; unanimous)

“Under art. 45/1 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, for the part of this receivable covered by the pledge, enforcement may only be pursued by way of realisation of the pledge. Enforcement by way of general attachment may not be pursued. However, since the mortgages given to secure the same receivable are maximum-amount mortgages, enforcement by way of general attachment may be pursued for the part of the receivable exceeding the total of the maximum-amount mortgages and not secured by mortgage.” (translation)

Where the suretyship debt is also secured by the mortgage, the pledge-first rule applies to the surety too

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4387, K. 2025/6585, 22.10.2025 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; unanimous)

“In that case, where the joint and several sureties of the loan agreement have given a mortgage as security for their own suretyship, under the Chamber’s case law they, like the principal debtor, must first be pursued by way of realisation of the pledge provided for in Article 45 of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law.” (translation)

In that case the mortgage deed was found to cover also the debts arising from the mortgagor’s suretyship and guarantee agreements, and it was held that the general attachment proceedings against the surety had to be annulled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a bank holding a mortgage attach the company’s accounts or vehicles?

As a rule, no. For a mortgage-secured loan claim the bank must first enforce against the borrower by realisation of the mortgage (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 45/1). Attachment is available for the part of the claim not covered by the pledge or exceeding the mortgage limits (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/3362, K. 2024/476); a bank holding a promissory note or cheque may choose bills-of-exchange enforcement instead (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 167/1).

Within how many days must a complaint against proceedings breaching the pledge-first rule be filed?

The Court of Cassation treats this complaint as a matter of public order and does not subject it to a time limit (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/1109, K. 2023/7900). However, a debtor who neither objects to the proceedings nor files a complaint cannot benefit from the rule’s protection (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2024/300, K. 2025/571). The existence and scope of any mortgage should therefore be examined as soon as the payment order is served.

If the mortgage does not cover the debt, how does the bank pursue the balance?

If the finalised valuation does not cover the claim, the bank may obtain a provisional pledge shortfall certificate and request attachment of the debtor’s other assets (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 150/f). If the sale price is insufficient, a pledge shortfall certificate is issued; if attachment is requested within one year, no new enforcement order or payment order needs to be served (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 152).

Can a bank bring both promissory-note enforcement and mortgage enforcement for the same loan?

Not at the same time. Under the unification decision of 20.01.2023, E. 2021/2, K. 2023/1, as cited by the Assembly of Civil Chambers, two sets of proceedings cannot be brought simultaneously for the same claim even with a “without double recovery” reservation; the creditor chooses one route, and mortgage proceedings started while bills-of-exchange enforcement is pending are annulled as duplicate (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2022/1039, K. 2023/856).

I am a surety for the loan and also gave a mortgage; can the bank pursue me directly by attachment?

It depends on the mortgage deed. If the mortgage secures only the company’s debts, the bank may pursue you as a surety by general attachment under the conditions of art. 586 of the Turkish Code of Obligations (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/7479, K. 2021/7971). If the mortgage also covers debts arising from your suretyship, the mortgage must be realised first; attachment is available for the part not covered by the mortgage (Court of Cassation, 12th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/4387, K. 2025/6585).
