Turkish Joint-Stock Company General Assembly: How It Is Convened, the Call, Agenda and Quorums

In brief

In a Turkish joint-stock company the general assembly is, as a rule, called by the board of directors at least two weeks in advance, excluding the days of the announcement and of the meeting, by an announcement on the company website and in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette, and whoever calls the meeting sets the agenda. Unless a heavier quorum applies, it meets with at least one quarter of the capital and decides by a majority of the votes present; amendments to the articles of association as a rule require half of the capital, or one third at a second meeting. A defect in the call is a ground for annulment within three months if it affected the resolution, while resolutions of a general assembly convened without authority may be non-existent unless all shareholders attend without objection.

Convening the general assembly of a Turkish joint-stock company: the competent body, the minority’s right to have a meeting called, the agenda, the form and timing of the call, the general assembly without a call, meeting and decision quorums, quorums for amending the articles of association, electronic general assemblies and the consequences of a defective call; TCC arts. 409-421, TCC arts. 445-447 and Court of Cassation decisions.

The board of directors does not convene the general assembly, or a shareholder learns afterwards that the articles of association were amended at a meeting whose announcement he never saw: how, and with what quorum, must the general assembly meet, and what happens to the resolution if the rules are breached?

When and by Whom Is the General Assembly Called?

The ordinary general assembly meets within three months from the end of each financial year and resolves on matters such as the election of the company’s organs, the financial statements, the use of profit and the discharge of the board; an extraordinary general assembly is called whenever necessary (TCC art. 409). The power to call belongs to the board of directors, even if its term has expired; if the board is permanently unable to meet, cannot reach a quorum or does not exist, a single shareholder may call the meeting with the court’s permission (TCC art. 410). The resolution to call is, as a rule, adopted by a majority of the members present at a board meeting attended by a majority of all members (TCC art. 390/1). A finding of non-existence concerning a general assembly convened on the basis of a call resolution adopted by two members, where it could not be proven that the third member had been invited, was upheld by majority by the Court of Cassation (Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2180, K. 2025/3118).

Can a Minority Have a Meeting Called or Add Agenda Items?

Yes. Shareholders holding at least one tenth of the capital, or one twentieth in publicly held companies, may ask the board, through a notary and stating the reasons and the agenda, to call the general assembly or to place items on the agenda; a request for agenda items must reach the board before the fee for the announcement in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette is paid. If a request for a meeting is accepted, the general assembly is called so as to be held within forty-five days at the latest (TCC art. 411). If the request is rejected or not answered positively within seven business days, the commercial court of first instance may order the meeting and appoint a trustee to set the agenda; its decision is final (TCC art. 412). The agenda is set by whoever calls the meeting, and a matter not on the agenda cannot be resolved except under statutory exceptions (TCC art. 413). For details, see our articles on minority rights and on the agenda rule.

How and How Far in Advance Must the Call Be Made?

The announcement is made on the company website and in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette; the period is at least two weeks, excluding the days of the announcement and of the meeting, and registered shareholders are also sent a registered letter with return receipt (TCC art. 414/1):

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 414 (Form of the call) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The general assembly shall be called to meeting by an announcement published in the manner specified in the articles of association, on the company’s website and in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette. This call shall be made at least two weeks before the meeting date, excluding the days of the announcement and of the meeting. Shareholders registered in the share ledger and shareholders who have previously notified the company of their addresses by submitting share certificates or documents proving shareholding shall be informed by registered letter with return receipt of the meeting date, the agenda and the newspapers in which the announcement has appeared or will appear. …”

Is It Different in a Limited Company?

In a limited company the general assembly is called by the managers at least fifteen days before the meeting; the articles may extend this period or shorten it to ten days (TCC art. 617/1-2). The joint-stock company rules on the call, the minority’s right to call and to make proposals, the agenda and the general assembly without a call apply by analogy (TCC art. 617/3), as do the rules on nullity and annulment (TCC art. 622).

Can the General Assembly Meet Without a Call?

Yes. The holders or representatives of all shares may, unless one of them objects, meet as a general assembly without observing the call procedure and adopt resolutions as long as the meeting quorum exists; items may be added to the agenda only unanimously (TCC art. 416). According to the Court of Cassation, all shareholders must be present, no one may object and the resolution must be adopted with the participation of all partners, all at the same time; an objection must be raised before the agenda is taken up, and a shareholder who leaves the meeting removes its capacity to decide. Because all partners attended without objection and stayed until the end, resolutions of a meeting called by board members without the power to call were not treated as non-existent (Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/6698, K. 2025/4673). Where, however, a shareholder had objected through a notary before the meeting to a call based on an invalid board resolution, the finding of non-existence was upheld by majority, the dissent considering that the conditions of a general assembly without a call were met (Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2180, K. 2025/3118).

What Are the Meeting and Decision Quorums?

As a rule, the meeting quorum is at least one quarter of the capital and must be maintained throughout the meeting; no quorum is required at a second meeting, and resolutions are adopted by a majority of the votes present (TCC art. 418):

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 418 (Meeting and decision quorum) (unofficial translation)

“(1) General assemblies shall meet with the presence of the holders or representatives of shares corresponding to at least one quarter of the capital, except where this Code or the articles of association provide for a heavier quorum. This quorum must be maintained throughout the meeting. If the said quorum is not reached at the first meeting, no quorum is required for the second meeting to be held. (2) Resolutions shall be adopted by a majority of the votes present at the meeting.”

Votes of shareholders deprived of voting rights are excluded from the decision quorum: board members may not vote the shares they own on resolutions discharging the board (TCC art. 436/2), and approval of the balance sheet results in a discharge unless the resolution states otherwise (TCC art. 424). The Court of Cassation held that board members are also deprived of voting rights in such an implied discharge and treated a resolution that lacked the quorum once their votes were excluded as non-existent (Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/295, K. 2024/4130).

Which Quorums Apply to Amendments to the Articles of Association?

The quorum depends on the type of amendment; the articles cannot lower the general quorums or provide for a relative majority (TCC art. 421/1):

(Secondary) obligations to cover balance-sheet losses, moving the registered office abroadUnanimity of the entire capital; the same at subsequent meetingsTCC art. 421/2, TCC art. 421/4
Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 421 (Meeting and decision quorums for amendments to the articles of association) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Unless otherwise provided in the Code or the articles of association, resolutions amending the articles of association shall be adopted by a majority of the votes present at a general assembly at which at least half of the company’s capital is represented. If the meeting quorum is not achieved at the first meeting, a second meeting may be held within one month at the latest. The meeting quorum for the second meeting is the representation of at least one third of the company’s capital. Provisions of the articles of association that lower the quorums laid down in this paragraph or provide for a relative majority are invalid. (2) The following resolutions amending the articles of association shall be adopted unanimously by the holders or representatives of the shares constituting the entire capital: a) Resolutions imposing obligations and secondary obligations to cover balance-sheet losses. b) Resolutions on moving the company’s registered office abroad. (3) The following resolutions amending the articles of association shall be adopted with the affirmative votes of the holders or representatives of shares constituting at least seventy-five per cent of the capital: a) A complete change of the company’s field of activity. b) The creation of privileged shares. c) Restriction of the transfer of registered shares. (4) If the quorums set out in paragraphs two and three are not reached at the first meeting, the same quorum is required at subsequent meetings. …”

Can the General Assembly Be Held Electronically?

Yes. In a joint-stock company, attending the general assembly, making proposals, expressing opinions and voting electronically produce all the legal consequences of physical attendance and voting; the details are set by a Ministry regulation, and the system is mandatory for companies whose shares are listed on the stock exchange (TCC art. 1527/5).

How Does a Defective Call or a Missing Quorum Affect the Resolution?

  • Annulment: An action to annul a resolution contrary to the law, the articles or the principle of good faith is brought within three months from the resolution before the commercial court of first instance at the company’s seat (TCC art. 445). A shareholder alleging that the call was not made properly or that the agenda was not duly announced may sue even without attending the meeting, but must also allege that the irregularity affected the adoption of the resolution (TCC art. 446/1-b). In a limited company case to which the joint-stock company call rules applied by analogy, the Court of Cassation held that the failure to announce in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette was not a ground of nullity; and because the shareholders knew of the meeting in advance, attended it and had their dissent recorded, it found that the defect did not affect the outcome and was not a ground for annulment either (Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/9529, K. 2018/5070).
  • Nullity: In particular, resolutions that restrict or remove the shareholder’s inalienable rights such as attendance, minimum voting and the right to sue, that restrict the rights to information and inspection beyond what the law permits, or that undermine the company’s fundamental structure or breach the capital protection rules are void (TCC art. 447).
  • Non-existence: Resolutions of a meeting convened by an invalid call that does not meet the conditions of a general assembly without a call, and a resolution lacking the quorum once the votes of disqualified shareholders are excluded, may be treated as non-existent (Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2180, K. 2025/3118; Court of Cassation 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/295, K. 2024/4130). For the procedure, see our article on annulment of general assembly resolutions.

What Should Be Done, Step by Step?

  1. Resolution to call: All board members must be invited; the resolution on the call and the agenda must be adopted with the required quorum and be in writing and signed (TCC art. 390).
  2. Announcement: The website and Trade Registry Gazette announcements and the registered letters must be planned around the two-week period.
  3. Meeting: The meeting quorum must be monitored at the opening and throughout, votes of disqualified shareholders must be deducted from the decision quorum, and the heavier quorums must be checked separately for amendments of the articles.
  4. Shareholder: If no meeting is called, a request is made through a notary; if no positive answer is received within seven business days, the court may be asked to order the meeting.
  5. Objection and action: An objection to a defective or unauthorised call should be made in writing before the meeting and recorded in the minutes before the agenda is taken up; an annulment action must be filed within three months from the resolution (TCC art. 445).

Case Study: Is a Resolution Valid If the Announcement Was Incomplete?

Hypothetical case: In a joint-stock company whose shareholders are A (fifty per cent), B (thirty per cent) and C (twenty per cent), the board meets with two members, without inviting its member C, and calls the general assembly; the announcement is published in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette but not on the company website. C objects through a notary before the meeting; the general assembly completely changes the field of activity with the votes of A and B.

Analysis: A call resolution adopted without inviting a member and a pre-meeting notarial objection resemble the facts of the finding of non-existence upheld by majority by the Court of Cassation; had C attended without objection and stayed until the end, a general assembly without a call (TCC art. 416) would have been at issue. The missing website announcement is not sufficient on its own for annulment; its effect on the resolution must also be alleged (TCC art. 446/1-b). The seventy-five per cent affirmative vote was reached, as A and B together hold eighty per cent (TCC art. 421/3). C may primarily seek a declaration of non-existence and, failing that, annulment.

Court of Cassation Decisions

A defect in the announcement is not a ground for annulment if it did not affect the outcome

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/9529, K. 2018/5070, 17.07.2018 (the Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; limited company, joint-stock company call rules applied by analogy)

“although the meeting date and agenda were not announced in the trade registry gazette as required by Article 414 of the TCC, this is not a ground of nullity; and since the claimants knew of the meeting date and agenda beforehand, attended the meeting and had their dissent recorded in the minutes, the defect did not affect the outcome either and therefore cannot be regarded as a ground for annulment under Article 446 of the TCC.” (translation)

Where a call based on an invalid board resolution was objected to in advance, the finding of non-existence was upheld

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2180, K. 2025/3118, 06.05.2025 (the Chamber’s reasoning for upholding; joint-stock company; by majority, the dissent considering that the conditions of a general assembly without a call were met)

“in particular, since the board resolution was adopted without complying with the procedure in Article 390 of TCC No. 6102 and is non-existent, and since the claimant objected to the general assembly meeting through a notary and may claim non-existence, it was necessary to uphold the regional court of appeal’s decision, which is in accordance with procedure and law.” (translation)

The conditions of a general assembly without a call must be met at the same time

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/6698, K. 2025/4673, 30.06.2025 (the Chamber’s own reasoning; joint-stock company; upheld unanimously)

“Under Article 416 of TCC No. 6102, for a general assembly without a call to exist, the conditions that all shareholders or their representatives are present at the meeting, that no shareholder objects to the meeting and that, at the final stage, the resolution is adopted with the participation of all partners must be fulfilled at the same time. … The objection must be made before the agenda is taken up.” (translation)

If the quorum is not reached once disqualified votes are excluded, the resolution is non-existent

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/295, K. 2024/4130, 21.05.2024 (the Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; joint-stock company; balance-sheet approval operating as an implied discharge)

“it must be accepted that board members are also deprived of voting rights in these resolutions that result in an implied discharge. … it is clear that, once the votes of the said persons are excluded, the decision quorum is not reached and the resolution is non-existent.” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the meeting quorum for the general assembly of a joint-stock company?

Unless a heavier quorum applies, it is at least one quarter of the capital; the quorum must be maintained throughout the meeting, no quorum is required at a second meeting, and resolutions are adopted by a majority of the votes present (TCC art. 418).

What quorum is required to amend the articles of association?

As a rule, a majority of the votes present at a meeting where at least half of the capital is represented; one third suffices at a second meeting. Seventy-five per cent affirmative votes are required for decisions such as a complete change of the field of activity, and unanimity for moving the registered office abroad (TCC art. 421).

How many days before the meeting must the call be made?

At least two weeks, excluding the days of the announcement and of the meeting; the announcement is published on the company website and in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette, and registered shareholders receive a registered letter (TCC art. 414/1).

Is a resolution annulled if the call was defective?

Yes, if the defect affected the adoption of the resolution; a shareholder may bring an annulment action within three months from the resolution even without attending the meeting (TCC art. 445, TCC art. 446/1-b).

Are resolutions of a general assembly convened without authority valid?

If all shareholders attend, do not object before the agenda is taken up and do not leave, the conditions of a general assembly without a call are met (TCC art. 416). Otherwise, especially where an objection was made before the meeting, the resolutions may be treated as non-existent.

Assessment: The same defect in the call may lead to annulment in one case and to non-existence in another; the articles of association, the board’s resolution book, the announcements, the list of attendees and the minutes should therefore be examined together.
