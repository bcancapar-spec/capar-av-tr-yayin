Which Court Hears Commercial and Consumer Cases in Türkiye: Muğla, Manisa, Uşak, Afyonkarahisar, Kütahya

Short answer: Under Turkish law, where there is no commercial or consumer court, the action is filed with the civil court of first instance, which hears it “in the capacity of a commercial court” or “in the capacity of a consumer court”. Decisions No. 1235 (commercial) and No. 1236 (consumer) of the First Chamber of the Council of Judges and Prosecutors dated 25.11.2021 set the rule for which numbered civil court hears these cases. According to official sources, in the provincial centres Uşak and Kütahya have neither a commercial nor a consumer court; Muğla and Manisa have a commercial court but no consumer court; Afyonkarahisar has a consumer court but no commercial court. Where there is no commercial court, failing to state the capacity does not require a decision of lack of jurisdiction (Turkish Commercial Code art. 5(4)).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Under Turkish law, where there is no commercial or consumer court, the civil court of first instance hears commercial and consumer cases in the relevant capacity, and Council of Judges and Prosecutors Decisions No. 1235 and 1236 set the rule for which numbered civil court hears these cases. This article sets out the position in central Muğla, Manisa, Uşak, Afyonkarahisar and Kütahya, jurisdiction objections, mediation and the arbitration committee threshold, with official sources.

Which Court Hears the Case Where There Is No Commercial or Consumer Court?

The general court for civil cases is the civil court of first instance. Statute assigns commercial cases to the commercial court and consumer disputes to the consumer court, but these courts do not exist everywhere. Which courts exist in a province is shown by establishment decisions and the courthouse's structure, not by the statute.

Statutory Provision — Code of Civil Procedure (Law No. 6100) art. 2 (Jurisdiction of civil courts of first instance)

“(1) Regardless of the value or amount in dispute, the competent court in actions concerning property rights and personal rights is the civil court of first instance, unless otherwise provided.”

For commercial cases, the “other provision” is article 5 of the Turkish Commercial Code. It gives jurisdiction to the commercial court, but only “if there is a commercial court in a place”, and it states expressly that in a judicial district without a commercial court the civil court continues with the case.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 5 (Courts hearing commercial cases and non-contentious matters)

“(1) Unless otherwise provided, and regardless of the value or amount in dispute, the commercial court of first instance has jurisdiction over all commercial cases and commercial non-contentious matters.

… (2) If there is a commercial court of first instance in a place, commercial cases under article 4 that fall within the jurisdiction of the civil court of first instance, and other matters to be heard by the commercial court under special provisions, are heard by the commercial court of first instance.

… The relationship between the commercial court of first instance and the civil court of first instance and other civil courts is one of subject-matter jurisdiction, and the procedural rules on jurisdiction apply.

… In a commercial case within a judicial district where there is no commercial court of first instance, the fact that the jurisdiction rule was not invoked does not require a decision of lack of jurisdiction; the civil court of first instance continues with the case.”

For consumer cases, the jurisdiction rule is in article 73 of the Consumer Protection Law. That article contains no separate rule for places without a consumer court; the practice of the civil court hearing such cases “in the capacity of a consumer court” rests on Council decisions and Court of Cassation case law (see the 3rd Civil Chamber decision below).

Statutory Provision — Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 73 (Consumer courts)

“(1) Consumer courts have jurisdiction over actions concerning disputes that may arise from consumer transactions and practices directed at consumers.

… (5) Consumer actions may also be brought before the consumer court of the place where the consumer is domiciled.”

Which numbered court hears the case? Article 5(5) of Law No. 5235 provides that, for specialisation, the Council may determine the allocation of work among chambers and that these decisions are published in the Official Gazette. On this basis the First Chamber of the Council of Judges and Prosecutors gave two decisions on 25.11.2021, published in Official Gazette No. 31675 of 30 November 2021:

  • Decision No. 1235 (commercial): where no commercial court has been established or the district is not attached to one, commercial cases go to civil court No. 3 where there are three civil courts, No. 4 where there are four or five, and Nos. 5 and 6 where there are six or more.
  • Decision No. 1236 (consumer): where no consumer court has been established or the district is not attached to one, consumer cases go to civil court No. 4 where there are four or more civil courts. The decision names no number for places with three or fewer civil courts.

Both decisions apply to new cases filed from 15.12.2021; pending cases were not transferred, and the designated courts continued to receive work from general allocation according to case numbers.

Which Cases Are Commercial and Which Are Consumer Cases?

To find the right court, first determine the nature of the case. A commercial case is defined in article 4 of the Turkish Commercial Code.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 4(1) (Commercial cases: general)

“(1) Civil actions and non-contentious matters arising from matters relating to the commercial enterprises of both parties, and, regardless of whether the parties are merchants;

… civil actions and non-contentious matters arising from the matters provided for [in the listed provisions] are deemed commercial cases and commercial non-contentious matters.”

A commercial case is therefore either a dispute between two merchants relating to both parties' commercial enterprises (for example, a claim for the price of goods between two companies) or a case deemed commercial by statute regardless of whether the parties are merchants (companies, negotiable instruments, intellectual property, banking and so on). According to the 20th Civil Chamber decision below, if one party is not a merchant or the dispute does not concern a commercial enterprise, there is no commercial case unless statute specifically says so.

A consumer case arises from a consumer transaction or a practice directed at consumers. According to the 3rd Civil Chamber, regulation by Law No. 6502 alone is not enough; one party must be a consumer. A bank's action to set aside an objection against a personal-loan debtor is therefore a consumer case.

The lines on court numbers apply the rule in Council decisions No. 1235 and No. 1236 to today's number of civil courts; no later Council decision amending those decisions could be identified for this article.

No consumer court is listed; consumer cases are heard by the civil court in the capacity of a consumer court. Because the page lists two civil courts, the numbering rule of Decision No. 1236 (four or more courts) does not apply in central Muğla.

Applying the rule of Decision No. 1236 to today's six courts points to court No. 4 for consumer cases. Because the number of courts has changed, confirming the allocation in Uşak is particularly important.
  • Afyonkarahisar (centre): The courthouse's civil courts page lists a Consumer Court; consumer cases are filed there. There is no commercial court; commercial cases are heard by the civil court in the capacity of a commercial court. Applying the rule of Decision No. 1235 (courts Nos. 5 and 6 where there are six or more) to today's six courts points to courts Nos. 5 and 6; this should be confirmed with the allocation office.

Applying the rules of Decisions No. 1235 and No. 1236 to the four courts listed in the report points to court No. 4 for both types of case; this should be confirmed with the allocation office.

Appellate court: Denizli for Muğla and Uşak, İzmir for Manisa, Konya for Afyonkarahisar and Bursa Regional Court of Appeal for Kütahya. For details see Denizli Regional Court of Appeal under Turkish Law: Jurisdiction, Appeal Procedure and Time Limits.

Subject-Matter Jurisdiction, Objections and Their Consequences

The jurisdiction of courts is regulated only by statute, and jurisdiction rules are matters of public order (Code of Civil Procedure art. 1). The court having jurisdiction is a procedural precondition (art. 114(1)(c)); the court examines it of its own motion and the parties may raise it throughout the proceedings. Since the 2012 amendment by Law No. 6335, the relationship between the commercial court and the civil court is one of subject-matter jurisdiction (Turkish Commercial Code art. 5(3)). Locally this means:

  • Where there is a commercial court (as in central Muğla and Manisa), a commercial case filed with the civil court results in a decision of lack of jurisdiction. The reverse also holds.
  • In a district with no commercial court (as in central Uşak, Afyonkarahisar and Kütahya), filing a commercial case with the civil court without calling it a “commercial court” is not a ground for lack of jurisdiction; the civil court continues (art. 5(4)). The court must, however, hear the case in the capacity of a commercial court.
  • In consumer cases, where a consumer court exists (as in central Afyonkarahisar), a consumer case filed with the general court ends in a decision of lack of jurisdiction; where there is none, the civil court hears the case in the capacity of a consumer court.

A decision of lack of jurisdiction does not send the case on automatically: if neither party requests transfer within two weeks, running from service if the decision is final when given, from finality if no appeal was lodged, or from service of the rejection of an appeal, the action is deemed not to have been filed (Code of Civil Procedure art. 20(1)). Capacity also affects court fees: article 73(2) of Law No. 6502 exempts actions brought by consumers before consumer courts from fees. If the court does not treat the case as a consumer case and hears it under general rules, completion of the fees will arise; the Assembly of Civil Chambers decision below deals with that situation.

Mandatory Mediation and the Consumer Arbitration Committee Threshold

Knowing the court is not enough; in some cases a mediator or the consumer arbitration committee must be approached before filing. In commercial cases concerning a sum of money, mediation is a procedural precondition.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 5/A (Mediation as a procedural precondition)

“(1) In commercial cases specified in article 4 of this Code and in other laws, in actions for receivables, compensation, setting aside an objection, negative declaratory relief and restitution whose subject is a sum of money, having applied to a mediator before filing the action is a procedural precondition.”

This requirement applies even where the case will be heard in Uşak or Kütahya by the civil court in the capacity of a commercial court. In consumer disputes there are two separate gateways.

Statutory Provision — Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 68 (Application)

“Without prejudice to the parties' rights under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, application to consumer arbitration committees is mandatory in disputes whose value is below thirty thousand Turkish liras. No application may be made to consumer arbitration committees for disputes above these values.

… (4) The monetary limits in this article apply from the beginning of each calendar year, increased by the revaluation rate determined and announced for that year under repeated article 298 of the Tax Procedure Law No. 213 of 4/1/1961.”

The thirty thousand Turkish lira in the statute is the base amount introduced by the 2022 amendment and rises each year by the revaluation rate; the current amount for the year of application should be checked separately. Below the threshold the application goes to the consumer arbitration committee, or the district governor's office where there is no committee (art. 68(3)); above it, mediation must as a rule come first.

Statutory Provision — Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 73/A (Mediation as a procedural precondition)

“(1) In disputes heard by consumer courts, having applied to a mediator before filing the action is a procedural precondition. However, the provisions on mediation as a procedural precondition do not apply to the following:

a) Disputes within the remit of the consumer arbitration committee

b) Objections against consumer arbitration committee decisions”

The article also excludes the actions in articles 73(6) and 74 and consumer disputes arising from rights in rem over immovable property. The statute sets the requirement for “disputes heard by consumer courts”; since in Manisa, Muğla, Uşak and Kütahya the same dispute is heard by the civil court in the capacity of a consumer court, satisfying the requirement before filing is the safe course. No decision on whether the requirement applies where the civil court hears the case in the capacity of a consumer court was examined for this article. For the committee procedure see Applying to the Consumer Arbitration Committee.

Case Law

A jurisdictional relationship with a single exception. The 20th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, designating the competent court after both a civil and a commercial court had declined jurisdiction in an action to set aside an objection, explained the nature of the relationship after the 2012 amendment and the place of art. 5(4):

Court of Cassation Decision
20th Civil Chamber, E. 2019/2552, K. 2019/4093, 17.06.2019 (decision designating the competent court; the Chamber's own reasoning)

“Accordingly, unlike under the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6762 and the version of the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 before the amendment by Law No. 6335, the legal relationship between the commercial court of first instance and the civil court of first instance and other civil courts is not one of division of work but one of jurisdiction. Therefore, in cases that the commercial court must hear, the civil court cannot be regarded as having jurisdiction. … The only exception to this rule is regulated in art. 5(4) of the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 as amended by article 2 of Law No. 6335. Under this provision, in places with no separate commercial court within the judicial district, the fact that the jurisdiction rule was not invoked in actions filed with civil courts does not require a decision of lack of jurisdiction.”

On the facts the Chamber designated the commercial court; the text of art. 5(3) and 5(4) it applied is unchanged today.

Omitting “in the capacity of a commercial court”. The Court of Cassation's 23rd Civil Chamber took two different approaches to the civil court's failure to state that it heard the case as a commercial court. In a claim between two merchants under a service procurement contract, the Chamber listed this omission as a separate ground alongside a ground on the start of interest:

Court of Cassation Decision
23rd Civil Chamber, E. 2016/4548, K. 2019/190, 28.01.2019 (ground 2 of the reversal)

“In places with no commercial court of first instance, the case must be heard in the capacity of a commercial court of first instance. Accordingly, overlooking this point when the case should have been heard in the capacity of a commercial court was not found correct.”

In an action to set aside an objection brought by a cooperative against a member for unpaid dues, however, the same Chamber did not treat the same omission as a ground for reversal; it merely criticised it and upheld the judgment:

Court of Cassation Decision
23rd Civil Chamber, E. 2016/660, K. 2018/2416, 28.03.2018 (decision upholding the judgment; criticism)

“While, under article 99 of the Cooperatives Law No. 1163, these cases should be heard in the capacity of a commercial court of first instance in places with no commercial court, the fact that the case was not heard in the capacity of a commercial court was not made a ground for reversal but called for criticism.”

So the omission did not produce the same result in every file; in the first, reversal also rested on another ground. Both decisions were given in files from before the regional appeal system. The safe course is to request expressly, in the petition and at the preliminary examination, that the case be heard in the capacity of a commercial court.

Consumer court capacity and lack of jurisdiction. In an action by a bank against a personal-loan debtor to set aside an objection, the Court of Cassation's 3rd Civil Chamber held that the court was wrong to decline jurisdiction after a reversal:

Court of Cassation Decision
3rd Civil Chamber, E. 2020/6877, K. 2021/8931, 23.09.2021 (reversal; the Chamber's own reasoning)

“By decision No. 141 of 26.03.2014 of the Council of Judges and Prosecutors, it was decided that in places with no separate consumer court, cases and matters within the jurisdiction of consumer courts would continue to be heard by the civil courts of that place, with judicial districts the same as those of the civil court. In the present case, since it is understood that there is no separate consumer court within the court's judicial district, the court should have heard the case in the capacity of a consumer court without giving a decision of lack of jurisdiction as regulated in article 20 et seq. of the Code of Civil Procedure; giving a decision of lack of jurisdiction is contrary to procedure and law and requires reversal.”

The decision relies on the Council's general 2014 decision; Decision No. 1236, which specifies the numbered chamber, was given about two months later and applies to actions filed from 15.12.2021.

Capacity and court fees. The Assembly of Civil Chambers examined court fees as a preliminary issue in a claim arising from a vehicle sale. The action had been filed in the capacity of a consumer court and no fee was paid, but the court heard it as a general court:

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2018/749, K. 2020/995, 02.12.2020 (insistence decision reversed on procedure; the Assembly's own reasoning)

“In the present case, although the statement of claim was drafted with the note ‘in the capacity of a consumer court’ and accordingly no fee at all was paid, the case was heard and decided as the court of general jurisdiction.”

The Assembly found it contrary to article 30 of the Fees Law to give judgment without granting time to complete the missing fees, and reversed without examining the merits. The file was appealed under the former Code No. 1086; the decision matters in showing that capacity changes the fee outcome.

Practical Points

  • State the capacity expressly in the petition. In central Uşak, Afyonkarahisar and Kütahya a commercial case is addressed to the “Civil Court of First Instance (in the capacity of a Commercial Court)”; in central Muğla, Manisa, Uşak and Kütahya a consumer case uses “(in the capacity of a Consumer Court)”. Article 5(4) prevents a decision of lack of jurisdiction, but omission of the capacity from the judgment has been one of the reversal grounds in one Court of Cassation decision.
  • Confirm the number with the allocation office. When the number of civil courts changes, the designated chamber may change.
  • Do not skip the gateway. In commercial money claims mediation, and in consumer disputes the arbitration committee or mediation depending on the amount, should, as the safe course, be completed before filing; the committee threshold changes every year.
  • Check district courthouses separately. In districts such as Bodrum, Fethiye, Akhisar, Salihli or Tavşanlı, the position must be verified separately from that courthouse's structure and jurisdiction decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a commercial court in Uşak in Türkiye, and where is a commercial case filed in Uşak?

There is no commercial court in central Uşak. A commercial case is filed with the Uşak civil court of first instance, which hears it in the capacity of a commercial court. Applying the rule of Council of Judges and Prosecutors Decision No. 1235 to today's six civil courts, courts Nos. 5 and 6 hear these cases; since the decision named court No. 4 for Uşak at the time, the number should be confirmed with the allocation office.

Is there a consumer court in Kütahya in Türkiye, and where is a consumer case filed in Kütahya?

According to the Kütahya Courthouse's 2025 activity report, there is no consumer court in the provincial centre. A consumer case is filed with the Kütahya civil court of first instance, which hears it in the capacity of a consumer court. Applying the rule of Council of Judges and Prosecutors Decision No. 1236 to today's four courts points to court No. 4; the number should be confirmed with the courthouse's allocation office. Depending on the amount, the consumer arbitration committee or mediation may have to come first.

Which court hears consumer cases in Manisa and Muğla in Türkiye?

The provincial centres of Manisa and Muğla have a commercial court but no consumer court. Consumer cases are heard by the civil court of first instance in the capacity of a consumer court. In central Manisa, applying the rule of Decision No. 1236 to today's five courts points to court No. 4, to be confirmed with the courthouse's allocation office; in central Muğla two civil courts are listed, so the decision's numbering rule does not apply.

Is there a commercial court in Afyonkarahisar in Türkiye, and which court hears commercial cases in Afyonkarahisar?

Central Afyonkarahisar has a consumer court but no commercial court. Commercial cases are heard by the civil court of first instance in the capacity of a commercial court; applying the rule of Council of Judges and Prosecutors Decision No. 1235 to today's six courts points to courts Nos. 5 and 6; the number should be confirmed with the courthouse's allocation office. Consumer cases are filed with the Afyonkarahisar Consumer Court.

Under Turkish law, where there is no commercial court, is a commercial case filed with the civil court without stating its capacity dismissed for lack of jurisdiction?

No. Under article 5(4) of the Turkish Commercial Code, in a judicial district without a commercial court the fact that the jurisdiction rule was not invoked does not require a decision of lack of jurisdiction; the civil court continues with the case. The court must, however, hear the case in the capacity of a commercial court; the Court of Cassation listed omitting this capacity among the reversal grounds in one decision and merely criticised it in another.
