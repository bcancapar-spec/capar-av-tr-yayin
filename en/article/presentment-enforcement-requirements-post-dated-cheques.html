Presentment and Enforcement of Post-Dated Cheques in Türkiye: Early Presentment Ban until 31/12/2028

Short answer: A post-dated cheque is a cheque bearing an issue date later than the day on which it was actually handed over. Under the general rule a cheque is payable at sight and a cheque presented before the date written on it is payable on the day of presentment (Commercial Code art. 795); however, this rule does not apply until 31/12/2028: until that date, presenting a cheque to the bank before the issue date written on it is invalid (Law No. 5941, provisional art. 3/5). No legal action may be brought on a cheque that was presented early and not paid; enforcement requires the cheque to be presented to the bank within the statutory presentment period calculated from the date written on it and to be certified as dishonoured (Law No. 5941 art. 3/8). The presentment period starts on the day after the issue date written on the cheque, not on the day it was actually delivered (Commercial Code art. 796).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Until 31/12/2028 a post-dated cheque cannot be presented to the bank before its written date; early presentment is invalid, and enforcing an unpaid cheque requires presenting it again within the period calculated from its written date and having it certified as dishonoured. What Is a Post-Dated Cheque?

The law designs the cheque as a means of payment: it is payable at sight and any provision to the contrary is deemed unwritten (Commercial Code art. 795). Writing a “maturity date” on a cheque therefore has no legal effect; what actually turns a cheque into a deferred-payment instrument is that the issue date, one of its mandatory elements (Commercial Code art. 780), is written as a date later than the actual date of delivery. Such cheques are called “post-dated” or “forward-dated” cheques in legal writing and in practice.

The Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation has noted that, already under the repealed Law No. 6762, it was accepted that a cheque may bear a date later than its real issue date but must nonetheless be paid on the day it is presented, and has stressed that for a post-dated cheque the presentment periods run from the date written on it, not from the day it actually entered circulation (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1638, K. 2020/763). Today the special rules on post-dated cheques are found in the Cheque Law No. 5941. The general presentment periods, certification of dishonour and rights of recourse are explained in our article on cheque presentment periods and the legal effect of a cheque; this article concentrates on issues specific to post-dated cheques.

The Ban on Early Presentment and the History of Its Deadline

Under the text in force, “until 31/12/2028, presentment of a cheque to the drawee bank for payment before the issue date written on it is invalid” (Law No. 5941, provisional art. 3/5). This provision suspends, for the transitional period, the rule in Commercial Code art. 795 that a cheque presented early is payable on the day of presentment. Consequently, even if a cheque whose written date has not yet arrived is handed to the bank or sent through clearing for payment, this does not count as a valid presentment; even if there are no funds, no valid certification of dishonour can be based on such a presentment.

The deadline of the transitional ban has been extended many times since 2009. Since most changes were made through separate omnibus laws, one has to check which text was in force on the date the cheque was presented:

The second row of the table has mattered in practice: the Assembly of Civil Chambers held valid an early presentment made on 19.01.2012, between 31/12/2011, when the first transitional provision expired, and 03.02.2012, when Law No. 6273 entered into force, because no provision banning presentment was in force at that time and the general rule applied (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1638, K. 2020/763). By the same logic, unless a new extension is made beyond 31/12/2028, Commercial Code art. 795 will again apply in full to presentments made after that date. Since the deadline has been amended so often, for long-dated cheques the text in force on the presentment date should be checked separately.

Can an Early-Presented Cheque Be Enforced?

No. The Cheque Law regulates the consequence of early presentment separately and expressly:

Statutory Provision — Cheque Law (Law No. 5941) art. 3/8 (Condition for legal action on a post-dated cheque) (unofficial translation)

“(8) Where the funds for a cheque presented before the issue date written on it are not paid in whole or in part pursuant to article 707 of the Turkish Commercial Code, no legal action may be brought in respect of that cheque. For legal action to be brought in respect of a cheque bearing a future issue date, the cheque must be presented to the bank within the statutory presentment period calculated from the issue date written on it and must be subjected to the ‘dishonoured’ procedure.”

The reference in the provision is to the “payment at sight” rule of the repealed Law No. 6762, whose current counterpart is Commercial Code art. 795. The two sentences must be read together: the first sentence states that no “legal action” may be based on a cheque not paid upon early presentment. The second sentence, however, does not close the holder’s path: if the cheque is presented again within the statutory presentment period calculated from its written date and certified as dishonoured, enforcement is possible. The Court of Cassation accepts that a second presentment after an early one must also fall within the period calculated from the written date, and that a presentment made after that period does not save the enforcement (second decision below).

Effect on enforcement proceedings: In bill-enforcement proceedings started after an early presentment, the debtor may file a complaint with the enforcement court within five days of service of the payment order (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 168); in a matter brought before it by a timely complaint or objection, the enforcement court may of its own motion take into account that the creditor has no right of enforcement under the law of negotiable instruments and annul the proceedings (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 170/a). The Court of Cassation has held that proceedings based on a cheque presented to the bank two days before its written date must be annulled in this way (first decision below). The debtor’s remedies and the unjust enrichment claim for a cheque not presented in time are the subject of a separate article.

The bank’s per-leaf liability: The drawee bank must pay the holder up to the amount set by law for an unfunded cheque; however, this obligation arises only for “each cheque leaf presented in time” (Law No. 5941 art. 3/3). Since early presentment is invalid, this payment cannot be claimed either. The amount is set every January by the Central Bank of the Republic of Türkiye; by the Central Bank Communiqué published in the Official Gazette of 29.01.2026, the main amount has been TRY 16,350 since 30.01.2026 (for cheques for which the transitional provision of the same communiqué provides a separate amount, that amount applies). The Assembly of Civil Chambers also required, for the bank to pay this amount, that the holder request it and leave the original cheque with the bank (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1638, K. 2020/763). Moreover, if the cheque is not presented within five years of the printing date written on it, the bank’s liability for that amount ends (Law No. 5941 art. 3/9); this limit should not be overlooked for very long-dated cheques.

Precautionary attachment: A claim arising from a cheque whose written date has not arrived is not yet due. For a debt that has not fallen due, precautionary attachment may be requested only in the cases listed in the law, such as where the debtor has no fixed domicile or is preparing to hide or smuggle away assets or to flee (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 257). The Court of Cassation reversed a precautionary attachment granted on post-dated cheques without proof of these grounds (fifth decision below). The general framework is in our article on precautionary attachment.

Criminal consequences: The offence relating to bad cheques is committed by causing a cheque to be certified as dishonoured “upon presentment within the statutory presentment period calculated from the issue date written on it” (Law No. 5941 art. 5). A procedure carried out upon early presentment does not meet this condition. The Court of Cassation likewise requires, for a penalty to be imposed, first the existence of an unfunded cheque presented to the bank for payment within the presentment period, and a complaint lodged within the three-month and one-year preclusive periods of Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 347 (19th Criminal Chamber, E. 2018/3249, K. 2019/5808). For details, see our article on bad cheques and criminal liability.

How Is the Presentment Period Computed from the Date on the Cheque?

A cheque must be presented within ten days if payable at the place of issue and within one month if payable elsewhere; these periods “start on the day following the issue date written on the cheque” (Commercial Code art. 796). For a post-dated cheque this means that the period starts not on the day the cheque changed hands, but on the day after the date written on it. According to the Court of Cassation, where the issue date is written as a day later than the real date, the presentment period is deemed extended (fourth decision below). Three further rules govern the computation:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 817 (Computation of periods) (unofficial translation)

“(1) In computing the periods set out in this Part of the Law, the day on which they begin is not counted.”

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 816 (Holidays) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Presentment and protest of a cheque may only be made on a business day. (2) Where the last day of the period fixed by law for acts relating to a cheque, and in particular for presentment and protest or an equivalent certification, falls on a Sunday or another holiday, the period is extended to include the first business day that follows. Intervening holidays are included in the computation of the period.”

The practical consequences are: (1) for the ten-day period, the last day is found by adding ten days to the date written on the cheque, because the starting day (the written date) is not counted. (2) Sundays and holidays falling within the period are counted; only if the last day falls on a Sunday or holiday is the period extended to the next business day. (3) A cheque dated on a Sunday cannot be presented on that day, because presentment can only be made on a business day. Since the law mentions only “Sunday” and “another holiday” for the extension of the last day, where the last day falls on a Saturday the safest course is to present the cheque on the preceding business day.

The Commercial Code does not specify how a one-month period is counted. Under the general rule in Code of Obligations art. 92, a period fixed in months ends on the day of the last month corresponding to the starting day; if there is no such day in the last month, the last day of that month applies. Which period applies is determined by comparing the place of issue and the place of payment; the Court of Cassation applies the ten-day period where the place of issue and the location of the drawee branch are the same (second decision below). Writing the place of issue clearly is therefore also important: since the presentment period is determined by the place of issue, the Court of Cassation has treated a place of issue written in abbreviated form, leaving doubt as to which place it denotes, as a defect that deprives the instrument of its cheque character (12th Civil Chamber, E. 2018/6, K. 2018/1357). If the date on the cheque has been corrected and the correction has not been initialled by the drawer, the correction is deemed non-existent and the presentment period is calculated from the date before the correction (12th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/19503, K. 2017/11471).

QR-Coded Cheques: What to Check Before Accepting a Post-Dated Cheque

By the amendment made by Law No. 6728, the serial number given by the bank and the QR code were added to the mandatory elements of a cheque (Commercial Code art. 780). For cheques printed by a foreign bank, the absence of these two elements does not affect validity (Commercial Code art. 781). Since a post-dated cheque makes the creditor dependent on the drawer’s future ability to pay, the information provided by the QR code is particularly valuable for such cheques:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 780/2 (Data accessible through the QR code) (unofficial translation)

“(2) Cheque creditors may access, through the QR code, data relating to the cheque account holder and the drawers of the cheque they hold. Through the QR code, … d) the number and amount of cheques of the account holder not yet presented to banks, e) the number and amount of cheques issued and delivered to banks, … i) the number and amounts of cheques certified as ‘dishonoured’ in the last five years and still unpaid, … are made available to third parties without the consent of the account holder or the endorser.”

The system providing access to this data is set up by the Risk Centre of the Banks Association of Türkiye (Commercial Code art. 780). In particular, the item “number and amount of cheques not presented to banks” shows how much post-dated cheque debt the drawer has in the market, so it should always be checked before accepting a cheque. The Cheque Law also imposes a registration obligation on the payee and sets out an important consequence of that registration for corporate drawers:

Statutory Provision — Cheque Law (Law No. 5941) art. 3/10 (Registration of QR-coded cheques) (unofficial translation)

“(10) The payee in whose favour a QR-coded cheque is issued registers the cheque received in the system referred to in the third paragraph of article 780 of the Turkish Commercial Code. Changes in the representatives of the legal entity drawing the cheque after the date on which the QR-coded cheque was registered in the system do not remove the liability of the legal entity holding the cheque account.”

This provision addresses a real risk of post-dated cheques. If the company’s authorised signatory changes after a post-dated cheque has been drawn on behalf of the company, it may be argued that, as of the date on the cheque, the signature belongs to an unauthorised person. In a case examined by the Court of Cassation, the creditor argued that the cheques had been signed by the company’s former representative before the dates written on them; the Chamber stated that this claim could only be proven by a document bearing the joint signatures of the parties and that no such document had been submitted (fourth decision below). A payee who registers the cheque in the system on the day of receipt can instead rely on the law that changes of representatives after registration do not remove the liability of the account-holding company. According to the amendment table of Law No. 5941, this rule entered into force on 31.12.2017.

Clearing Houses, Electronic Presentment and Presentment at a Branch

In practice, post-dated cheques are mostly handed to the holder’s own bank for collection through clearing. By law, presentment of a cheque to a clearing house is equivalent to presentment for payment (Commercial Code art. 798). A cheque may also be presented to a branch other than the one where the account is held; in that case it is paid after the funds are checked (Law No. 5941 art. 3/1). For electronic clearing the Cheque Law also provides:

Statutory Provision — Cheque Law (Law No. 5941) art. 8/3-4 (Electronic clearing) (unofficial translation)

“(3) Processing of cheques, within the principles set out in the regulation, by sending only the cheque data electronically to the drawee bank through interbank clearing houses without physical presentment is equivalent to presentment to a clearing house under article 710 of the Turkish Commercial Code No. 6762. (4) No partial payment, including the liability amount set out in the third paragraph of article 3, is made for cheques presented through clearing houses. This does not remove the drawee bank’s obligation to pay the liability amount. However, where it is established that a cheque presented through clearing houses lacks sufficient funds in the account, the partial funds in the account are blocked by the drawee bank in favour of the presenting holder for fifteen days.”

Since presentment through clearing is also equivalent to presentment for payment, the ban on early presentment produces the same result for this route: if the cheque is sent to clearing before its written date, the outcome is no different from early presentment at a branch. If there are no funds, non-payment may also be certified by a dated declaration of the clearing house (Commercial Code art. 808). The Assembly of Civil Chambers recalled that presentment at a branch and to a clearing house is valid, and treated the bank’s endorsement, showing payment of the per-leaf liability amount and containing the presentment date, as a dated declaration evidencing timely presentment and lack of funds (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2025/651, K. 2026/371).

Closure of the cheque account by the drawer does not make a post-dated cheque worthless either. By law, after a cheque account is closed, “cheques presented within the statutory presentment period according to the issue date written on them are subjected to the dishonoured procedure” (Law No. 5941 art. 2/10). Even if the account has been closed, the holder should present the cheque in time according to its written date and have the certification made.

Post-Dated Cheques Issued Abroad

A cheque issued in a country other than the country of payment must be presented within one month if the places of issue and payment are on the same continent and within three months if they are on different continents; a cheque issued in a European country and payable in a country with a Mediterranean coast, and vice versa, is deemed issued on the same continent (Commercial Code art. 796). The law also indicates which law applies to a cheque payable in Türkiye:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 822 (Law of the place of payment) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The following matters are determined by the law of the country in which the cheque is payable: a) whether the cheque must be payable at sight or may also be drawn payable at a fixed period after sight, and the effects of writing on the cheque a date later than the real date of issue; b) the presentment period. …”

Accordingly, for a post-dated cheque payable at a bank in Türkiye, even if issued abroad, the effects of a later date and the presentment period are determined by Turkish law; the ban on early presentment valid until 31/12/2028 and the condition for enforcement should therefore also be taken into account for such cheques payable in Türkiye. Where the calendars of the places of issue and payment differ, a conversion is made:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 797 (Difference of calendars) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Where a cheque is drawn between two places having different calendars, the day of issue is converted into the corresponding day of the calendar of the place of payment.”

Where the amount of the cheque is expressed in a currency that is not current at the place of payment, the rate and currency of payment are separately regulated (Commercial Code art. 802). Since the absence of a serial number and QR code does not affect the validity of cheques printed by foreign banks (Commercial Code art. 781), no QR query can be made for such cheques; the drawer’s ability to pay must be investigated by other means.

Common Mistakes in Practice

  • Handing the cheque to the bank or to clearing before its written date and forgetting to present it again in time after it is dishonoured. Early presentment is invalid; for enforcement the cheque must be presented again within the period calculated from its written date.
  • Counting the period from the day the cheque was actually delivered. The period starts on the day after the written date; having received the cheque months earlier does not shorten the period, and the later the written date, the later the period runs.
  • Applying a one-month period instead of ten days. If the places of issue and payment are the same, the period is ten days; the place of issue and the drawee branch should be checked when accepting the cheque.
  • Miscalculating the last day. Intervening holidays are counted; the period is extended only if the last day falls on a Sunday or holiday. Where the last day is a Saturday, it is safer to present earlier.
  • Having the dishonour procedure carried out incompletely. The procedure is carried out at the holder’s request; if the holder refuses to sign, the dishonour procedure is not carried out (Law No. 5941 art. 3/4). It should be checked that the endorsement states the presentment date.
  • Ignoring an uninitialled date correction. An uninitialled correction may be disregarded and the period then runs from the original date.
  • Skipping the QR query and registration. The payee’s registration obligation also protects against changes of the company’s authorised signatories (Law No. 5941 art. 3/10).
  • Requesting precautionary attachment directly on a cheque whose date has not arrived. For a claim not yet due, the special grounds listed in Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 257 must be proven.
  • Filing a criminal complaint based on dishonour upon early presentment. The bad-cheque offence requires presentment within the statutory period calculated from the written date; the complaint is also subject to the periods in Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 347.

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below have been verified in full text from the official case-law database. Some older commercial decisions belong to the 19th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, which heard such cases at the time and was later abolished; recent decisions on enforcement proceedings based on negotiable instruments are given by the 12th Civil Chamber.

No bill enforcement on a cheque presented before its written date; the proceedings must be annulled

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2019/14009, K. 2020/5837, 29.06.2020 (the Chamber’s own reasoning; the Regional Court of Appeal’s decision was set aside and the first-instance decision reversed. At the time of the decision the deadline in the transitional provision was 31.12.2020.)

“In the present case, it is seen that the cheque subject to enforcement, bearing the issue date 25/03/2018, was presented to the drawee bank … on 23/03/2018, before its issue date. Since, under provisional article 3/5 of Law No. 5941 in force on the date of presentment, the presentment of the cheque is invalid, no enforcement can be brought on this cheque.” (translation)

Early presentment is invalid; a later presentment must also fall within the period calculated from the written date

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/6165, K. 2016/8542, 23.03.2016 (the Chamber’s reasoning for reversal)

“In the present case, since the place of issue of the cheque underlying the enforcement is ... and the drawee bank is also located in ..., the presentment period is ten days. The cheque was issued on 31.03.2015 and was presented on 30.03.2015 and 05.05.2015; as regards the presentment of 05.05.2015, the cheque was presented after the ten-day period had expired. … Under this statutory provision, the presentment made on 30.03.2015, before the issue date of 31.03.2015 written on the cheque underlying the enforcement, is invalid.” (translation)

The presentment period runs from the date written on the cheque, not from the actual date of delivery

Court of Cassation Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/1638, K. 2020/763, 14.10.2020 (general statement of the Assembly; the dispute concerned the bank’s per-leaf liability amount. While treating the presentment as valid, the Assembly reversed the decision of resistance by majority because the original cheques had not been left with the bank.)

“For a post-dated cheque, the starting date of the statutory presentment periods is the date shown on the cheque as the issue date. The actual date of creation cannot be the starting point for calculating the presentment period. As also accepted in legal scholarship, the issue date is not the date on which the instrument actually entered circulation but the date written on it.” (translation)

A later date extends the presentment period; a claim that the cheque was signed earlier must be proven by a jointly signed document

Court of Cassation Decision
12th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/5627, K. 2022/12095, 22.11.2022 (the Chamber’s reasoning; the decision was reversed because compensation should have been awarded to the debtor)

“The issue date, which is one of the essential elements of a cheque, may also be written as a date later than the real issue date. In that case the presentment period is deemed extended (Fırat Öztan, Law of Negotiable Instruments, p. 1054). Although the creditor claimed that the cheque was drawn post-dated, this can only be accepted if proven by a document bearing the joint signatures of the parties.” (translation)

Precautionary attachment for a post-dated cheque whose date has not arrived may be requested only on the special grounds listed in the law

Court of Cassation Decision
19th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/5643, K. 2015/9733, 01.07.2015 (the Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; at the time of the decision the deadline in the transitional provision was 31.12.2017)

“In the present case, rejecting the objection to the request for precautionary attachment on an erroneous reasoning, although the claim had not fallen due before the issue dates written on the cheques subject to the attachment and no evidence was submitted that the grounds in Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law article 257/2 existed, was not considered correct and required reversal.” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a post-dated cheque be handed to the bank before its written date?

Even if handed over, it has no legal effect. Until 31/12/2028, presentment of a cheque to the bank for payment before the issue date written on it is invalid (Law No. 5941, provisional art. 3/5). Since presentment through clearing is equivalent to presentment for payment, the same result applies to a cheque sent to clearing.

Can enforcement proceedings be brought on a cheque presented early and dishonoured?

No. No legal action may be brought on a cheque presented before its written date and not paid. Enforcement requires the cheque to be presented again within the statutory presentment period calculated from its written date and certified as dishonoured (Law No. 5941 art. 3/8). Otherwise the bill enforcement is annulled by the enforcement court upon the debtor’s timely complaint or objection.

From which date does the presentment period of a post-dated cheque start?

It starts not on the day the cheque was actually delivered but on the day after the issue date written on it (Commercial Code art. 796). It is ten days for a cheque payable at the same place and one month for a cheque payable elsewhere; the starting day is not counted (Commercial Code art. 817).

What happens if the last day of the presentment period falls on a Sunday or a public holiday?

The period is extended to include the first business day that follows; intervening holidays are counted in the period (Commercial Code art. 816). Since presentment can only be made on a business day, a cheque dated on a Sunday cannot be presented on that day.

What is the presentment period of a cheque issued abroad and payable in Türkiye?

It is one month if the places of issue and payment are on the same continent and three months if they are on different continents; a cheque issued in a European country and payable in a country with a Mediterranean coast is deemed issued on the same continent (Commercial Code art. 796). The effects of a later date and the presentment period are determined by the law of the country of payment, i.e. Turkish law (Commercial Code art. 822).

Why is the QR code important when accepting a post-dated cheque?

A QR query reveals the number and amount of the drawer’s cheques not yet presented to banks and its dishonoured cheques in the last five years (Commercial Code art. 780). Registration of the cheque in the system by the payee also ensures that changes of company representatives after registration do not remove the liability of the account-holding company (Law No. 5941 art. 3/10).
