Mining Licence Cancellation in Türkiye: Forfeited Security and Fines (Law No. 3213 Arts. 10, 13, 17, 24)

Short answer: Under Turkish law, an action against administrative fines imposed under Mining Law No. 3213 is brought before the administrative court within thirty days of notification, and bringing the action does not stop enforcement and collection (art. 13). Since the Law does not appear to set a separate time limit for the cancellation of a licence, the general sixty-day limit in Law No. 2577 art. 7 applies as a rule; this is the article’s own inference. Today the security deposit is recorded as revenue mainly for exploration licences (art. 17) and through the fine in provisional art. 21 for past security deposit debts. The Council of State has found unlawful a licence cancellation made before the end of June and a fine imposed for a security deposit not yet due; the Constitutional Court has stated that the art. 24 fine cannot be applied where there is no fault in failing to obtain permits.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Under Turkish law, an action against an administrative fine imposed under the Mining Law must be brought before the administrative court within thirty days of notification, and the action does not stop collection; for the cancellation of a licence, on this article’s own inference, the general sixty-day limit applies as a rule. The article explains the grounds for cancellation and fines, forfeiture of the security deposit and the competent court with Council of State and Constitutional Court decisions.

Under Turkish law, the administrative court route against the cancellation of a mining licence, the forfeiture of the security deposit and administrative fines imposed under Mining Law No. 3213: which court, which time limit, and the criteria emerging from Council of State and Constitutional Court decisions.

What Are Licence Cancellation, Forfeiture of the Security Deposit and the Administrative Fine?

Under Turkish law, the right to explore for and operate a mine depends on a licence granted by the General Directorate of Mining and Petroleum Affairs (the General Directorate). Mining Law No. 3213 ties this right to a series of obligations: paying the licence fee, submitting reports on time, obtaining the necessary permits and declaring production correctly. If an obligation is not met, the Law provides for three kinds of consequence.

Cancellation of the licence is the most severe consequence, ending the right. Recording the security deposit as revenue means that the security deposited by the licence holder is not returned but booked as revenue. An administrative fine is a monetary sanction attached to specific acts. All three may arise from the same event; for example, a single act may impose a fine and also warn that the licence will be cancelled if payment is not made.

The security deposit system changed substantially in 2015. As set out in the Constitutional Court decision cited below, before Law No. 6592, under art. 24 the security deposit of licences that did not obtain the permits within three years was recorded as revenue and the licence cancelled; after the amendment an administrative fine was provided for instead. The Council of State decision of 02.04.2024 also states that Law No. 6592 abolished the security deposit and fee practice and introduced the licence fee. Today, in the text of the Law, recording the security deposit as revenue appears mainly in art. 17 on exploration licences.

Main Grounds for Licence Cancellation and Administrative Fines

Non-payment of the licence fee

The licence and rehabilitation fees are paid each year by the end of January. If they are not paid, there is a second period:

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) art. 13/1 (Licence fee, rehabilitation fee, fines and other sanctions)

If the licence and rehabilitation fees are not paid in full by the end of January, the unpaid portion must be paid by the last day of June of that year, increased at the rate of the late-payment surcharge calculated under Article 51 of Law No. 6183; otherwise the licence shall be cancelled. No separate notification or notice shall be given to the licence holder concerning payment of the licence and rehabilitation fees.

This text has changed over time. As the Council of State decisions record, before 28.02.2019 an administrative fine was imposed for an unpaid fee and three months were granted; Law No. 7164 introduced payment of “twice” the amount by the end of June; the current text, as amended by Law No. 7257 (25.11.2020), provides for an increase at the late-payment surcharge rate. In a dispute, it is therefore necessary first to determine which year’s fee and which text apply.

Forfeiture of the security deposit during exploration

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) art. 17 (Exploration activity)

If the activity reports are not submitted on time or the minimum activities set out in the regulation are not carried out, at the end of the relevant period, and if the deficiencies in the reports are not remedied within the one-month period to be granted by the General Directorate, at the end of the period granted, the security deposit shall be recorded as revenue and the exploration licence cancelled.

…Where the minimum activities have been carried out but it is established that the exploration activities were not conducted in accordance with the project, the amount of the security deposit corresponding to the annual investment not realised shall be recorded as revenue. The part of the security deposit corresponding to the investment realised shall be returned to the licence holder on request. If the investment programme is realised below fifty per cent for two consecutive years, the security deposit given shall be recorded as revenue and the licence cancelled.

These two paragraphs were amended by Law No. 7554 (19.07.2025). For security deposit debts dating from before 2015 there is a transitional provision:

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) provisional art. 21 (Completion of the licence fee)

For transactions in which, on the date this article entered into force, the security deposit should have been recorded as revenue but could not be, or in which revenue-recording procedures were started but not completed, an administrative fine equal to the security deposit revenue not applied under this Law shall be imposed.

Untrue declarations and unjust acquisition of rights

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) art. 10 (Declaration procedure)

Licence holders who, by making untrue or misleading declarations, prevent the application of the provisions of the Law and/or unjustly acquire rights shall be given an administrative fine of TRY 50,000. On a second breach of this paragraph, the previous fine shall be applied doubled.

…If it is established that the provisions of the article have been breached three times within three years of the first finding, the licence shall be cancelled.

The same article lists acts such as producing minerals outside the licence group, producing without permission, and producing or shipping without an operating permit or without the art. 7 permits, treats them as “unjust acquisition of rights” and states that rights acquired in this way shall be taken back.

Failure to obtain permits in time and production without a licence

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) art. 24/11 (Operating licence and operation of the mine)

After the licence holder submits to the General Directorate, within three years of the operating licence entering into force, the environmental impact assessment decision, the ownership permit, the workplace opening and operating licence and the permits relating to areas entered in the General Directorate’s records that must be obtained under Article 7, the operating permit shall be issued.

…For licences whose obligations are not fulfilled in time, an administrative fine of TRY 50,000 shall be imposed each year. If the operating permit cannot be obtained by the end of the operating licence period because of the permits referred to in this paragraph, the licence period shall not be extended.

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) art. 12/5 (Production and shipment)

If it is established that production has been carried out without a licence or within a licence area belonging to another person, the activities shall be stopped and the mineral produced shall be seized by the local administration. Those who commit this act shall be given, in addition to the State royalty payable, an administrative fine of five times the pit-head sale price of all mineral produced within the scope of this paragraph that has been seized and/or can no longer be seized.

The amounts in these texts are the amounts stated in the Law. Under art. 13/2, administrative fines applied under the Law are increased each year by the revaluation rate determined under the Tax Procedure Law; the current amount to be applied should be checked separately.

Competent Court and Time Limit for Bringing an Action

The route against administrative fines imposed under the Mining Law is the administrative courts, and the time limit is special:

Statutory Provision — Mining Law (Law No. 3213) art. 13 (Licence fee, rehabilitation fee, fines and other sanctions)

Administrative fines imposed under this Law shall be paid into the account of the accounting unit of the General Directorate within one month of notification. An action against administrative fines may be brought before the administrative courts within thirty days. Recourse to judicial remedies against the administrative fines imposed does not stop enforcement and collection.

As recorded in the Council of State decision of 05.02.2026, before this paragraph was amended by Law No. 7164 it provided that the Misdemeanours Law No. 5326 would apply to Mining Law fines imposed by the Ministry, local administrative authorities and special provincial administrations. Since 28.02.2019 the text expressly designates the administrative court as the route.

Cancellation of a licence, by contrast, is a separate administrative act. No special time limit for actions against licence cancellation was found in the articles of Law No. 3213 examined for this article. In that case the general rule applies:

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 7/1 (Time limit for bringing an action)

The time limit for bringing an action, where no separate time limit is laid down in special laws, is sixty days before the Council of State and the administrative courts and thirty days before the tax courts.

Accordingly, the sixty-day limit applies as a rule to an action against licence cancellation; this is the article’s own inference. Where a fine and a cancellation warning, or a cancellation, are combined in the same act, the thirty-day limit may be applied in practice; the Council of State decision below applied the thirty-day limit to such an act. To avoid missing the deadline, it is safer to rely on the shorter limit.

Statutory Provision — Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 32/1 (General jurisdiction in administrative actions)

Without prejudice to the provisions on jurisdiction, where the competent administrative court is not specified in this Law or in special laws, the competent administrative court is the administrative court of the place where the administrative authority that carried out the administrative act or concluded the administrative contract in dispute is located.

The competent court is determined by the location of the authority that made the act. It is necessary to check whether the fine was imposed by the General Directorate, or by a governorship or its investment monitoring and coordination directorate.

The Issue in Council of State and Constitutional Court Decisions

The Council of State decisions below were given by the 8th Chamber of the Council of State. The decisions were given “as final”.

No fine for a security deposit not yet due

Council of State Decision
8th Chamber, E. 2021/6125, K. 2024/2041, 02.04.2024 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; provisional art. 21 fine)

“It is also clear that, as of the date the amendment entered into force (18/02/2015), the payment date for the 2015 environmental compliance security deposit in dispute (the end of June 2015) had not yet arrived. Furthermore, after the said statutory amendment there is no provision of law stating that, where the environmental compliance security deposit for 2015 is not paid, the security deposit shall be recorded as revenue as an administrative sanction.”

In this case a fine equal to the security deposit revenue had been imposed under provisional art. 21 for the 2015 environmental compliance security deposit. The Chamber recalled the principle of legality in administrative sanctions and reversed the regional administrative court’s judgment rejecting the appeal against the dismissal of the action, stating that on the date the transitional provision entered into force the payment date for that security deposit had not arrived, so there was no situation requiring the security deposit to be recorded as revenue. The decision shows that whether the act underlying the fine falls within the transitional provision must be examined date by date.

No cancellation before the end of June

Council of State Decision
8th Chamber, E. 2021/2419, K. 2025/1497, 27.02.2025 (Chamber’s reasoning in upholding with explanation)

“It has been concluded that the act in dispute cancelling the mining operating licence under Article 13 of Mining Law No. 3213 before the period granted had expired, when the possibility of paying the operating licence fees by the last day of June had been provided, is not lawful.”

In this decision the administration had granted three months for the 2019 fee under the old text and then cancelled the licence before the end of June. The Chamber did not find it unlawful to grant a period under the old text, but concluded that cancellation could not take place before the possibility of payment by the end of June under the new text had expired. The text referred to in the decision is the “twice the amount” procedure of the Law No. 7164 period; the current text provides for an increase at the late-payment surcharge rate.

Failure to obtain permits does not remove the fee debt

Council of State Decision (reasoning of the first-instance court upheld)
8th Chamber, E. 2021/4722, K. 2024/1794, 22.03.2024 (the Chamber upheld the first-instance judgment with general reasoning)

“since it is clear that the failure to obtain the permits required under the said Law does not prevent payment of the licence fee and that the absence of those permits does not remove the claimant’s obligation to pay the licence fee, the claimant’s arguments to that effect were not accepted”

In this decision the reasoning belongs not to the Chamber but to the first-instance court whose judgment was upheld. The claimant argued that it could not obtain an operating permit because of the mining ban in olive grove areas and was therefore without fault; the first-instance court held that failure to obtain permits did not remove the obligation to pay the licence fee, and the Chamber upheld the judgment. This criterion is limited to cancellation for non-payment of the licence fee; as regards the art. 24 fine, the Constitutional Court’s assessment of fault is also important.

The thirty-day special limit and the judicial recess

Council of State Decision
8th Chamber, E. 2020/6177, K. 2023/6613, 30.11.2023 (Chamber’s reasoning for reversal; time limit)

“Paragraph 3 of Article 13 of Mining Law No. 3213, as amended by Law No. 7164, expressly provides that an action may be brought before the administrative courts within thirty days against administrative fines arising from the Mining Law, and thus a special time limit for bringing an action has been laid down.”

In this case the act contained both an administrative fine and a warning that the licence would be cancelled if the fee was not paid within three months. The regional administrative court had relied on the sixty-day limit and dismissed the action as time-barred. The Chamber instead applied the thirty-day special limit; it took the fifth day after electronic notification reached the registered e-mail (KEP) address as the date of notification and, because the limit expired during the judicial recess, took account of the seven-day extension and the weekend. The action was therefore deemed brought in time and the judgment was reversed. The act itself also stated the thirty-day period for applying to the court.

Competence of the authority imposing the fine

Council of State Decision
8th Chamber, E. 2023/31, K. 2026/615, 05.02.2026 (Chamber’s decision upholding on different reasoning)

“Since it cannot be said that this power and responsibility ceased to exist as a result of the amendment to Article 13 of the Mining Law, it is understood that there is no unlawfulness in the act in dispute as regards competence.”

A fine had been imposed under art. 12 by a governorship’s investment monitoring and coordination directorate on the ground that basalt had been taken without permission from pasture land. The administrative court annulled the fine on the ground that the reports and the calculation of the quantity were insufficient; the regional administrative court found the outcome correct, stating that the General Directorate was competent to impose the fine. The Chamber held that the competence of local administrative authorities continued and that there was no unlawfulness as regards competence, but upheld the annulment as correct “in its result”. The decision does not separately examine the first-instance court’s assessment of the evidence; the reasoning on insufficient evidence should therefore not be read as the Chamber’s own criterion.

Constitutional Court: no art. 24 fine without fault

Constitutional Court Decision (norm review)
Constitutional Court, E. 2024/162, K. 2025/17, 16.01.2025 (objection dismissed; reasoning on the principle of fault)

“In this context, where the time limits laid down in the Law are exceeded without any fault attributable to the person, the administrative fine provided for by the rule cannot be applied.”

On referral by an administrative court, the Constitutional Court did not find the annual administrative fine in art. 24/11 unconstitutional and dismissed the objection by majority. The reasoning stressed that if permit procedures cannot be completed without fault on the part of the licence holder, this will be taken into account by the administration and the courts. The dissenting opinion argued that the rule should be annulled, noting that the permits are interdependent, sequential procedures and that there is no force majeure exception. The first two sentences of the paragraph under review were repealed by Law No. 7554 (19.07.2025); the fine sentence remains unchanged in the current text.

Practical Points

  • Count the time limit from notification. For an administrative fine the limit is thirty days. For notification by registered e-mail (KEP), the day on which notification is deemed made and the judicial recess extension must be calculated separately, as in the 8th Chamber’s decision of 30.11.2023.
  • Rely on the shorter limit for combined acts. If the fine, the payment warning and the cancellation appear in the same letter, bringing the action within thirty days reduces the risk; this is a precaution, not a fixed rule.
  • Collection does not stop. Bringing an action does not by itself stop enforcement and collection of the fine (art. 13). A separate decision of the court is needed for collection to stop.
  • Determine which text applies. Arts. 13, 17 and 24 have been amended several times (Laws No. 6592, 7164, 7257 and 7554). The text in force on the date of the act and the text in force on the date of the administrative decision may lead to different results; in its decision of 27.02.2025 the 8th Chamber assessed the payment period under the text in force on the date of the act and the period to be awaited before cancellation under the new text.
  • Document fault and permit procedures. For the art. 24 fine, the file should show when permit applications were made and how long the administration took to respond. For cancellation due to non-payment of the licence fee, in the first-instance judgment upheld by the 8th Chamber’s decision of 22.03.2024, failure to obtain permits was not accepted as a defence on its own.
  • Check competence. Whether the authority imposing the fine is competent under the Law is a separate ground for annulment; in its decision of 05.02.2026 the 8th Chamber accepted the competence of local administrative authorities under art. 12.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the time limit under Turkish law for suing against an administrative fine imposed under the Mining Law?

Under Mining Law No. 3213 art. 13, an action against these fines may be brought before the administrative court within thirty days of notification. This special limit replaces the general sixty-day limit in Law No. 2577 art. 7. The day on which electronic notification is deemed made and extensions such as the judicial recess must be calculated separately; the 8th Chamber of the Council of State made this calculation in detail in one decision.

Does suing against a Mining Law administrative fine in Türkiye stop collection of the fine?

No. Law No. 3213 art. 13 expressly states that recourse to judicial remedies against administrative fines does not stop enforcement and collection. The fine is payable within one month of notification. Bringing an action alone is not enough to stop collection; a separate decision of the court is needed. This article does not examine the conditions for a stay of execution.

Within what time limit and before which court is a mining licence cancellation challenged in Türkiye?

Cancellation of a licence is an administrative act and is challenged by an action for annulment before the administrative court. Since no time limit specific to licence cancellation was found in the Mining Law articles examined for this article, the general sixty-day limit in Law No. 2577 art. 7 applies as a rule; this is the article’s own inference. If the act also contains an administrative fine, it is safer to rely on the thirty-day limit. The competent court is, as a rule, the administrative court where the authority that made the act is located (Law No. 2577 art. 32).

Under Turkish law, is a mining licence cancelled immediately if the licence fee is not paid by the end of January?

As a rule, no. Under the current text of Law No. 3213 art. 13, the portion unpaid at the end of January may be paid by the last day of June of that year, increased at the late-payment surcharge rate; if this is not done, the licence is cancelled without separate notice. In its decision of 27.02.2025, the 8th Chamber of the Council of State upheld a judgment finding a cancellation made before the end of June unlawful.

When is a mining licence security deposit recorded as revenue under Turkish law today?

Law No. 6592 (2015) abolished the security deposit and fee practice and introduced the licence fee; the current art. 13 also provides for a rehabilitation fee. Today Law No. 3213 art. 17 provides for the security deposit to be recorded as revenue for exploration licences where activity reports are not submitted, minimum activities are not carried out or the investment is not realised. For past security deposits that could not be recorded as revenue, provisional art. 21 provides for an administrative fine equal to that security deposit revenue.

Is a fine imposed under the Turkish Mining Law if the permits required for the operating permit are not obtained in time?

Under Law No. 3213 art. 24, if permits such as the environmental impact assessment decision, the ownership permit and the workplace opening and operating licence are not submitted within three years of the operating licence entering into force, an administrative fine is imposed each year. In its decision of 16.01.2025 the Constitutional Court did not find the rule unconstitutional, but stated that the fine cannot be applied where no fault can be attributed to the licence holder for the delay.
