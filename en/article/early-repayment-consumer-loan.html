Early Repayment of a Consumer Loan in Türkiye: Interest and Cost Reductions, Compensation Cap, Restructuring

Short answer: Under Turkish law, the consumer may pay one or more instalments not yet due or repay the entire credit debt early, and the lender must make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early (Consumer Protection Law art. 27). The reduction is calculated on the basis of the date on which the consumer makes the payment, and that date cannot be changed to the consumer’s detriment. The Law provides for early repayment compensation only for housing loans: where the interest rate is fixed and the contract so provides, the compensation may not exceed one per cent of the amount repaid early on loans with a remaining term of up to thirty-six months and two per cent on loans with a longer remaining term, and it may not be charged at all on variable-rate loans (Consumer Protection Law art. 37/2). This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Early repayment of consumer loans under the Turkish Consumer Protection Law (arts. 27 and 37): the lender’s duty to reduce interest and other cost elements when all or part of a loan is repaid early, the payment date as the basis of the calculation, a free new repayment schedule after an interim payment, refunds on loans with interest collected in advance, the one and two per cent cap on early repayment compensation for fixed-rate housing loans and the ban on such compensation for variable-rate loans, why restructuring (refinancing) is not early repayment, early repayment within the withdrawal period, credit-linked insurance and the recovery of amounts overcharged, with decisions of the Assembly of Civil Chambers and the 11th and 13th Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation.

A guide to the early repayment of consumer loans under Turkish law: the lender’s duty to reduce interest and other cost elements when all or part of a personal, vehicle or housing loan is repaid early (Consumer Protection Law art. 27), a new repayment schedule after a partial early repayment, refunds on loans with interest collected in advance, the one and two per cent cap on early repayment compensation for fixed-rate housing loans and the ban on such compensation for variable-rate loans (Consumer Protection Law art. 37), why restructuring is not early repayment, early repayment within the withdrawal period, credit-linked insurance and dispute resolution, with decisions of the Assembly of Civil Chambers and the chambers of the Court of Cassation.

The Right to Repay a Consumer Loan Early (Consumer Protection Law art. 27)

A consumer credit agreement is an agreement under which the lender grants, or promises to grant, credit to the consumer in the form of a deferred payment, a loan or a similar financial accommodation in return for interest or a similar benefit (Consumer Protection Law art. 22/1). Under the general rule of the law of obligations, a debtor may perform before the due date but, unless the law, the contract or custom provides otherwise, may not make a deduction for performing early (Code of Obligations art. 96). For consumer loans the Law makes an exception to this rule: the consumer may pay instalments not yet due or the whole debt early, and the lender must make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early.

Statutory Provision — Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 27 (Early repayment) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The consumer may pay one or more instalments that have not yet fallen due or may repay the entire credit debt early. In such cases the lender is obliged to make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early.”

The details are laid down in the Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation issued under the Law. The Regulation defines early repayment as the payment, before the due date, of the whole debt or of any amount not less than one instalment; its early repayment rules also apply to instalment cash advances but not to credit card and overdraft agreements, while housing finance agreements are governed by a separate regulation (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation arts. 2 and 4/1-d). The lender must state the right to repay early and its duty to reduce the cost in the pre-contractual information form and in the agreement (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation arts. 6/1-m and 11/1-n).

The Court of Cassation has stated that the early repayment reduction for consumer loans is governed by a mandatory statutory provision and the secondary legislation based on it, that the parties cannot agree otherwise and that a contrary term would be absolutely null and void under Code of Obligations art. 27 (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/12491, K. 2018/5907; see the box below).

How Is the Reduction Calculated? Payment Date and Interest Collected in Advance

The reduction is calculated on the basis of the date on which the consumer makes the payment, and that date may not be changed to the consumer’s detriment. For loans where all or part of the interest is collected in advance, the lender must set up a system to make the necessary reduction at the contractual interest rate.

Regulatory Provision — Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 15 (Early repayment) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The consumer may pay one or more instalments that have not yet fallen due or may repay all or part of the credit debt early. In such cases the lender is obliged to make the necessary reduction in accordance with articles 16 and 17, including where all or part of the interest has been collected in advance. (2) Where the consumer pays one or more instalments not yet due or repays all or part of the credit debt before its due date, the date on which the consumer makes the payment is taken as the basis for calculating the early repayment reduction. This date may not be changed to the consumer’s detriment. (3) For loans where all or part of the interest is collected from the consumer in advance, the lender is obliged to set up a system to make the necessary reduction at the contractual interest rate if the consumer repays early. In that case the matters set out in Annex 3 are taken as the basis.”

If the whole loan is repaid on an instalment date, the instalment then due is collected together with the principal contained in the remaining instalments and any interest, taxes, charges and similar statutory liabilities not yet collected by that date. If repayment takes place between two instalments, the interest accrued on the principal from the last instalment paid until the payment date is added. For loans with interest collected in advance, interest is refunded at the contractual rate on the basis of the date and amount of the early repayment (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 16). Under this method, no interest for the period after the payment date is collected from the consumer.

Partial Early Repayment (Interim Payment) and the New Repayment Schedule

A partial payment of not less than one instalment is an interim payment. In that case the interest accrued on the remaining principal since the previous instalment date and the related statutory liabilities are collected, the rest is deducted from the principal and, as a rule, a new instalment amount is set without changing the number of instalments or the payment dates. At the consumer’s request a payment holiday may be set, and a different schedule may be agreed by the parties.

Regulatory Provision — Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 17 (Interim payment) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Payment by the consumer, before its due date, of any amount not less than one instalment in the repayment schedule is regarded as an interim payment. (2) Where the consumer makes an interim payment, the interest calculated on the remaining principal at the contractual interest rate for the number of days elapsed since the previous instalment date, together with the taxes, charges and similar statutory liabilities calculated on that interest, is collected from the consumer. The remainder is deducted from the principal debt. In that case a new instalment amount and a new repayment schedule are drawn up without changing the number of remaining instalments and the payment dates. … (5) The new repayment schedule is given to the consumer in writing or on a durable medium without any fee being charged under any name. (6) The burden of proof regarding the consumer’s requests under this article lies with the lender.”

The fact that the new repayment schedule must be provided free of charge and that the burden of proof regarding the consumer’s requests lies with the lender are two decisive safeguards in disputes about interim payments.

Early Repayment Fees on General Consumer Loans

Law No. 6502 provides for early repayment compensation only for housing finance (Consumer Protection Law art. 37/2). Neither Consumer Protection Law art. 27, which governs general consumer loans, nor the Regulation’s early repayment provisions provide for an early repayment fee or compensation; the lender is subject only to a duty to reduce the cost. The Regulation also requires that information on every fee to be charged to the consumer be given as an annex to the agreement, with the burden of proving this on the lender (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 29/3).

Under the repealed Law No. 4077, the 13th Civil Chamber stressed that the provision on early repayment of consumer loans set out the lender’s duty to reduce the cost but contained no rule allowing an early repayment commission, and reversed a judgment on the ground that the “early closure” charge taken on a personal loan had to be assessed within that framework (13th Civil Chamber, E. 2014/46241, K. 2015/10022).

Early Repayment Compensation on Housing Loans: The One and Two Per Cent Cap

For housing loans too, the consumer may pay part of the instalments or the whole debt early, and the housing finance institution must make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early (Consumer Protection Law art. 37/1). Unlike general consumer loans, however, the Law allows early repayment compensation under certain conditions:

  • The interest rate must be fixed and the compensation must be provided for in the agreement.
  • The compensation may not exceed one per cent of the amount repaid early, calculated after the interest reduction, for loans with a remaining term not exceeding thirty-six months, and two per cent for loans with a longer remaining term.
  • If the interest rate is variable, no early repayment compensation may be claimed.
Statutory Provision — Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 37 (Early repayment in housing finance) (unofficial translation)

“(1) The consumer may pay one or more instalments that have not yet fallen due or may repay the entire housing finance debt early. In such cases the housing finance institution is obliged to make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early. (2) Where the interest rate is fixed, the housing finance institution may, if this is provided for in the agreement, claim early repayment compensation from the consumer when one or more payments are made before their due date. The early repayment compensation may not exceed one per cent of the amount repaid early by the consumer to the housing finance institution, calculated after making the necessary interest reduction, for loans whose remaining term does not exceed thirty-six months, and two per cent for loans whose remaining term exceeds thirty-six months. Where the rates are variable, no early repayment compensation may be claimed from the consumer.”

The Housing Finance Agreements Regulation adds that the compensation is calculated on the principal repaid early and may in no case exceed the total reduction to be granted to the consumer; the new repayment schedule drawn up after an early repayment is also provided free of charge.

Regulatory Provision — Housing Finance Agreements Regulation art. 11 (Early repayment) (unofficial translation)

“… (3) The new repayment schedule is given to the consumer in writing or on a durable medium without any fee being charged under any name. (4) Where the interest rate is fixed, the housing finance institution may, if this is provided for in the agreement, claim early repayment compensation from the consumer where the consumer pays, before its due date, the whole amount owed or any amount not less than one instalment. The early repayment compensation may not exceed one per cent of the principal repaid early by the consumer to the housing finance institution for loans whose remaining term does not exceed thirty-six months, and two per cent for loans whose remaining term exceeds thirty-six months. The amount of early repayment compensation may in no case exceed the total reduction to be granted to the consumer. Where the rates are variable, no early repayment compensation may be claimed from the consumer.”

The Assembly of Civil Chambers has likewise stated that Consumer Protection Law art. 37 permits an early repayment fee and sets its ceiling, and that such a fee may be charged only if the credit agreement so provides, subject to review for unfair terms (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/410, K. 2020/189). Under the repealed Law No. 4077 it was also accepted that an early closure fee provided for in the agreement and not exceeding the statutory cap on a fixed-rate housing loan could not be ordered to be refunded (13th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/15248, K. 2015/26426); Law No. 6502 reset that cap at one or two per cent depending on the remaining term.

Restructuring (Refinancing) Is Not Early Repayment

When interest rates fall, the consumer may pay off the loan from his or her own resources or with a loan from another bank, or may agree with the same bank on a change in the interest rate, term, type of interest or currency. The first two routes are early repayment; the last is refinancing, which the Regulation treats as a separate institution. In refinancing, the consumer’s approval in writing or on a durable medium is obtained for the changes, and comparative information on the changes is given before that approval.

Regulatory Provision — Housing Finance Agreements Regulation art. 12 (Refinancing) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Refinancing occurs where, by agreement between the housing finance institution and the consumer, at least one of the following takes place in the housing finance agreement: a) a change in the credit interest rate, b) a change in the term of the credit, c) a change in the type of interest specified in the housing finance agreement, ç) where more than one housing finance agreement has been concluded on the security of the same dwelling, their consolidation into a single housing finance agreement, d) a change in the currency of the credit. (2) For changes to be made in the housing finance agreement in the context of refinancing, the consumer’s approval is obtained in writing or on a durable medium. Before the approval is obtained, the housing finance institution gives the consumer, on paper or on a durable medium, comparative information setting out the changes made to the housing finance agreement for refinancing purposes, in at least twelve-point type, in comprehensible language, and in a clear, plain and legible manner.”

In a decision concerning the period of the repealed Law No. 4077, the Assembly of Civil Chambers held that the early repayment rules apply where the consumer pays off the debt from his or her own means or with a loan taken from another bank, whereas refinancing agreed with the same bank is a concept distinct from early repayment, so that the bank cannot claim an early repayment fee in that case. The bank may claim certain costs only if the consumer has been informed in accordance with the legislation, the parties have reached agreement and the consumer has given express written approval; in a dispute, the bank bears the burden of proving that the costs deducted reflect reality (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/583, K. 2020/137). In the same vein, it has been accepted that restructuring a fixed-rate housing loan with the same bank because interest rates fell is not early repayment and that amounts collected as a restructuring fee or commission are unlawful (13th Civil Chamber, E. 2014/46241, K. 2015/10022).

Early Repayment within the Withdrawal Period

The consumer may withdraw from the credit agreement within fourteen days without giving reasons and without paying a penalty (Consumer Protection Law art. 24/1). Since the 2022 amendment, if the entire credit debt is repaid early within the withdrawal period, the withdrawal rules apply without any separate notice being required (Consumer Protection Law art. 24/2). The consumer then pays the principal and the interest accrued at the contractual rate from the date the credit was drawn until the date of repayment; nothing may be claimed beyond this and the costs paid to a public body or to third parties (Consumer Protection Law art. 24/3). The withdrawal period starts on the date the agreement is concluded or, if a copy of the agreement is provided later, on the date the copy is received (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 24/2).

Regulatory Provision — Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 24 (Right of withdrawal) (unofficial translation)

“… (3) … The lender must prove that the consumer was informed of the right of withdrawal. It is sufficient that the notice of withdrawal is directed to the lender in writing or on a durable medium within the withdrawal period. If the entire credit debt is repaid early within the withdrawal period, the provisions of this article apply without any notice being required. … (5) Interest is calculated at the contractual interest rate. No charge may be claimed from the consumer other than the contractual interest so calculated and the fees paid to a public body or institution or to third parties. Any other fee collected from the consumer is refunded to the consumer within seven days of the date on which the consumer repays the principal and the accrued interest to the lender.”

Where goods or services were bought with a linked credit, the linked credit agreement also terminates without any obligation to pay compensation or a penalty if the consumer withdraws from the sales contract and the notice is also directed to the lender within the withdrawal period (Consumer Protection Law art. 30/3).

What Happens to Credit-Linked Insurance

Credit-linked insurance may not be taken out without the consumer’s express request in writing or on a durable medium (Consumer Protection Law art. 29/1). For credit-linked sum insurance, the insurance contract is terminated where the credit debt is repaid before the agreed term or the structure of the debt changes; the policy may continue on its existing terms, or be adjusted to the new debt structure, only if the consumer is separately informed by the lender and gives express consent at the time of these transactions (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 26/2).

The Difference from Commercial Loans

These protective rules apply to loans to which a consumer, that is, a person acting for non-commercial and non-professional purposes, is a party (Consumer Protection Law art. 3/1-k). For commercial loans, the Assembly of Civil Chambers accepted that early closure may be made subject to the bank’s consent, but that the bank is bound by good faith and the prohibition of abuse of rights in setting and applying the early closure fee provided for in the agreement (Civil Code art. 2), referring for comparison to the statutory caps on housing loans (Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/410, K. 2020/189). The 11th Civil Chamber likewise emphasised that for commercial loans a reduction for early performance is, as a rule, assessed under Code of Obligations art. 96 and the contract, whereas consumer loans are governed by mandatory provisions (11th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/12491, K. 2018/5907).

Recovering Amounts Overcharged

Where no reduction was made on early repayment, an early repayment fee was charged contrary to the Law, or an unfounded commission was deducted on restructuring, the consumer may claim the refund of the amount overcharged. For disputes below the monetary threshold set by the Law, application to a consumer arbitration committee is mandatory (Consumer Protection Law art. 68/1); since the threshold is increased each calendar year by the revaluation rate (Consumer Protection Law art. 68/4), the amount applicable on the date of application should be checked. Disputes above the threshold are heard by the consumer courts (Consumer Protection Law art. 73/1), and applying to a mediator before bringing the action is a procedural prerequisite (Consumer Protection Law art. 73/A); the action may also be brought in the consumer court of the consumer’s place of residence (Consumer Protection Law art. 73/5). The lender must state these avenues in a fixed-term credit agreement (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 11/1-o). The actions for recovering money paid are discussed in our article Negative-Declaratory and Recovery Actions.

Court of Cassation Decisions

The decisions below have been verified against their full texts in the official case-law database. They were rendered by the competent chambers and the Assembly of Civil Chambers under the division of work in force at the time; those concerning the period of the repealed Law No. 4077 and those concerning commercial loans are flagged in the note.

The law provides no early repayment commission for personal loans; the early closure charge taken must be examined

Court of Cassation Decision
13th Civil Chamber, E. 2014/46241, K. 2015/10022, 31.03.2015 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning; concerns the period of the repealed Law No. 4077)

“Moreover, article 10/3 of Law No. 4077, which governs early repayment of consumer loans, while stating that in the event of early repayment the lender is obliged to make the necessary reduction in interest and commission in proportion to the amount paid, contains no provision that an early repayment commission will be charged. … That being so, whereas the court should have ruled after assessing, together, the aforementioned law and the provisions of the contract in question with regard to the “early closure” charge which the claimant alleges was unjustly taken from him, deciding as stated is contrary to procedure and law and is a ground for reversal.” (translation)

The early repayment reduction for consumer loans is mandatory; a contrary term is absolutely null and void

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/12491, K. 2018/5907, 02.10.2018 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning; the dispute concerns a commercial loan and the finding on consumer loans is made by way of comparison)

“In the case of early performance, where, as provided in Laws No. 4077 and 6502 on consumer loans, a reduction and its conditions are laid down by a mandatory statutory provision and the secondary legislation issued on the basis of that provision, there is no doubt that the contracting parties must comply with that legislation, that they cannot make a contrary arrangement by contract on this point, and that any such arrangement would be absolutely null and void under article 27 of the Code of Obligations.” (translation)

On housing loans an early repayment fee depends on a contractual provision and is capped by law

Court of Cassation Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/410, K. 2020/189, 20.02.2020 (the Assembly’s own reasoning; the dispute concerns a commercial loan and the explanation on consumer loans is given for comparison; the judgment of resistance was reversed)

“Accordingly, article 37 of Consumer Protection Law No. 6502 (article 10/B of Consumer Protection Law No. 4077) permits an early repayment fee to be charged and sets its ceiling. Under this provision, in housing finance loans the consumer is granted the right to repay the credit debt in full or one or more instalments early, and the housing finance institution (bank) is obliged to make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early. In addition, the charging of an early closure fee is made conditional on the credit agreement between the consumer and the housing finance institution containing a provision to that effect, without prejudice to the review mechanisms for unfair terms, and the housing finance institution is permitted to charge an early closure fee at a certain rate (1% or 2%) depending on the remaining term of the amount repaid early.” (translation)

On a fixed-rate housing loan, an early closure fee within the contractual cap is not unlawful

Court of Cassation Decision
13th Civil Chamber, E. 2015/15248, K. 2015/26426, 07.09.2015 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning; concerns the period of the repealed Law No. 4077; Law No. 6502 reset the cap according to the remaining term)

“Accordingly, given the “interest” type of the fixed-rate housing finance agreement between the parties and article 13 of the agreement between the parties, there is nothing unlawful in collecting a 2% early closure fee from the claimant in the event of early closure of the credit debt. … For the reasons explained, ordering the refund of TRY 2,063.61 collected from the claimant as an early closure fee is contrary to procedure and law and requires reversal” (translation)

Refinancing is not early repayment; costs may be claimed only with information, express approval and proof

Court of Cassation Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/583, K. 2020/137, 13.02.2020 (the Assembly’s own reasoning; the Chamber’s reversal was adopted and the judgment of resistance reversed; concerns the period of the repealed Law No. 4077)

“All these explanations show that, technically, early repayment and refinancing are different concepts, and that banks have no possibility of claiming an early repayment fee … in refinancing agreements directly under article 10/B of Law No. 4077. … Although no early repayment fee may be claimed under paragraph 14 of article 10/B of the Law in refinancing agreements, which are subject to the same rules as housing loan agreements, the bank, being a merchant, may claim certain of its costs. For this, however, the consumer must be informed in accordance with the legislation, the parties must reach mutual agreement on the new conditions stated in the information, and the consumer’s express written approval must be obtained; and, if a dispute arises, the bank must discharge the burden of proving whether the amounts deducted as costs reflect reality.” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

If I repay my personal loan early, must the bank reduce the interest?

Yes. The consumer may pay instalments not yet due or the whole debt early, and the lender must make the necessary reduction in all interest and other cost elements in proportion to the amount repaid early (Consumer Protection Law art. 27). The reduction is calculated on the basis of the payment date, which may not be changed to the consumer’s detriment (Consumer Credit Agreements Regulation art. 15/2).

Can an early closure fee be charged on a personal or vehicle loan?

The Law provides for early repayment compensation only for housing loans (Consumer Protection Law art. 37/2); Consumer Protection Law art. 27, which governs general consumer loans, provides for no such compensation and regulates only the duty to reduce the cost. Under the repealed Law No. 4077, the Court of Cassation also stressed that there was no provision allowing an early repayment commission on consumer loans.

How much early repayment compensation can the bank charge when I pay off my housing loan?

It may be charged only on fixed-rate loans and only if the agreement provides for it. It may not exceed one per cent of the amount repaid early for loans with a remaining term of up to thirty-six months, and two per cent for longer remaining terms; it may not be charged on variable-rate loans (Consumer Protection Law art. 37/2). Under the Regulation, it also may not exceed the total reduction granted to the consumer.

Do I pay early repayment compensation if I restructure my loan with the same bank?

In a decision concerning the period of the repealed Law No. 4077, the Assembly of Civil Chambers held that refinancing agreed with the same bank is not early repayment and that no early repayment fee may therefore be claimed; the bank may claim certain costs only if the consumer is informed, gives express written approval and the bank proves that the costs reflect reality. Paying off the loan with a loan from another bank, by contrast, is early repayment.

What happens if I pay off the loan within 14 days of taking it out?

There is a fourteen-day right of withdrawal from a consumer credit agreement. If the whole credit debt is repaid early within that period, the withdrawal rules apply without any separate notice; the consumer pays the principal and the contractual interest for the period of use, and nothing may be claimed beyond the costs paid to public bodies or third parties (Consumer Protection Law art. 24).

How can I recover an amount the bank overcharged?

For disputes below the statutory monetary threshold, application to a consumer arbitration committee is mandatory; for disputes above the threshold an action may be brought in the consumer court after applying to a mediator (Consumer Protection Law art. 68 and Consumer Protection Law art. 73/A). As the threshold is updated every year, the amount applicable on the date of application should be checked.
