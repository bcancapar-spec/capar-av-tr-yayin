Aggravated Injury by Result (Turkish Criminal Code Art. 87): Bone Fractures, Danger to Life, Higher Sentences

Short answer: Under Turkish law, where intentional injury causes a bone fracture or dislocation, the penalty determined under Criminal Code art. 86 is increased by up to one half according to the effect of the fracture on vital functions (art. 87/3). The rate is set not by the statute but by the degree stated in the forensic medical report (mild 1, moderate 2-3, severe 4-5-6) and by the principle of proportionality: the Criminal General Assembly found a 1/4 increase reasonable for a moderate (2) fracture, while the 1st Criminal Chamber considered a 1/3 increase excessive for the same degree. Where the injury endangers life, leaves a permanent facial scar or permanently weakens the function of an organ, the penalty is doubled and may not fall below the statutory minimum (art. 87/1). Where a fracture and danger to life occur together, only the increase for the most serious result is applied. Judgments based on reports that do not state the degree of the fracture in figures, or that leave doubt as to causation, are being reversed for incomplete investigation.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Under Turkish law, Criminal Code art. 87 on intentional injury resulting in a bone fracture, danger to life, a permanent facial scar or death: one- and two-fold increases, increases of up to one half according to the degree of the fracture and proportionality, statutory minimums, a single increase where there are several results and review of the forensic medical report, with decisions of the Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly and 1st Criminal Chamber.

Aggravated intentional injury under Turkish Criminal Code art. 87 where the injury results in a bone fracture, danger to life, a permanent facial scar, loss of organ function or death: increase rates, minimum penalties, the order in which the sentence is calculated, the forensic medical report and the Court of Cassation’s review of reports, with decisions of the Criminal General Assembly and the 1st Criminal Chamber.

What Is Aggravated Injury by Result?

Article 87 of the Turkish Criminal Code governs cases in which intentional injury produces a serious and often permanent result in the victim’s body. It does not create a separate offence: the offender is first sentenced under Criminal Code art. 86 and that penalty is then increased according to the result. Each paragraph of the article therefore refers back to art. 86 with the words “the penalty determined under the preceding article”. The basic offence, injuries curable by simple medical intervention, the complaint requirement and the qualified forms (with a weapon, against a spouse, etc.) are discussed in a separate article: Intentional Injury under Turkish Law (Criminal Code Art. 86): Minor and Aggravated Forms, Complaint, Settlement and Penalty.

The article has four tiers: results that double the penalty (paragraph 1) or triple it (paragraph 2); an increase of up to one half for bone fractures and dislocations (paragraph 3); and separate penalty ranges where death results (paragraph 4). These results rest on medical findings; the Criminal General Assembly stresses, for example, that the effect of a bone fracture on vital functions requires special and technical knowledge, cannot be resolved with the judge’s general legal knowledge and can be determined only through a medical report (Criminal General Assembly, E. 2016/727, K. 2020/146, 03.03.2020).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 86/1 (Intentional injury) (unofficial translation)

“(1) A person who intentionally causes bodily pain to another or impairs another’s health or perceptive ability shall be sentenced to imprisonment from one year and six months to three years.”

Results Requiring a Twofold or Threefold Penalty (art. 87/1-2)

If one of the five results listed in paragraph 1 occurs, the penalty determined under art. 86 is increased by one fold (doubled): permanent weakening of one of the senses or organs, permanent difficulty in speech, a permanent facial scar, a condition endangering life (in practice “danger to life”, hayati tehlike) and premature birth of a pregnant woman’s child. For the more serious results in paragraph 2, namely an incurable illness or vegetative state, loss of the function of a sense or organ, loss of the ability to speak or to have children, permanent alteration of the face and miscarriage, the penalty is increased by two folds (tripled).

The last sentence of each paragraph sets a minimum. Under the current text, the penalty under paragraph 1 may not be less than four years in cases falling under art. 86/1 and six years in cases falling under art. 86/3 (for example, injury with a weapon); under paragraph 2 the minimums are six and nine years. The Criminal General Assembly treats this sentence not as a separate ground of increase but as a minimum that determines the penalty for the sub-paragraph applied (Criminal General Assembly, E. 2025/347, K. 2026/11, 07.01.2026).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 87/1-2 (Aggravated injury by result) (unofficial translation)

“(1) If the act of intentional injury causes the victim:

a) Permanent weakening of the function of one of his senses or organs,

b) Permanent difficulty in speech,

c) A permanent scar on the face,

d) A condition endangering his life,

e) Where committed against a pregnant woman, the premature birth of her child,

the penalty determined under the preceding article shall be increased by one fold. However, the penalty to be imposed may not be less than four years in cases falling under the first paragraph and six years in cases falling under the third paragraph.

… (2) If the act of intentional injury causes the victim:

a) An illness with no prospect of recovery or a vegetative state,

b) Loss of the function of one of his senses or organs,

c) Loss of the ability to speak or to have children,

d) Permanent alteration of the face,

e) Where committed against a pregnant woman, the miscarriage of her child,

the penalty determined under the preceding article shall be increased by two folds. However, the penalty to be imposed may not be less than six years in cases falling under the first paragraph and nine years in cases falling under the third paragraph.”

Danger to life. A condition endangering life is a medical finding on the seriousness of the injury and is stated separately in the forensic report; for instance, in a case examined by the Criminal General Assembly, the Forensic Medicine Institute’s report showed separately that the victim’s life had been endangered, that the fracture to his head was of severe (4) degree and that a permanent facial scar had formed (Criminal General Assembly, E. 2021/351, K. 2022/299, 26.04.2022). If the offender intended not to injure but to kill the victim, the provisions on intentional killing rather than art. 87 come into play; the General Assembly states this expressly for cases where death occurs.

Permanent facial scar. A permanent facial scar is likewise established by a report and often occurs together with a fracture. The 1st Criminal Chamber held, in a case involving a permanent facial scar and a moderate (3) fracture, that the basic penalty should have been set above the minimum because more than one qualifying circumstance was present, but did not treat this as a ground for reversal since there was no appeal against the defendant (1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2025/7769, K. 2026/808, 09.02.2026).

Bone Fractures and Dislocations: Degree and Increase Rate (art. 87/3)

Paragraph 3 governs bone fractures and dislocations as a separate result. The maximum increase is one half; the rate is determined “according to the effect of the fracture or dislocation on vital functions”. The present wording of the paragraph was introduced by Law No. 5560; according to the Criminal General Assembly, the earlier text provided a separate penalty of one to six years for bone fractures (E. 2016/727, K. 2020/146).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 87/3 (Bone fracture or dislocation) (unofficial translation)

“Where intentional injury causes a bone fracture or dislocation in the body, the penalty determined under the preceding article shall be increased by up to one half according to the effect of the fracture or dislocation on vital functions.”

The degree system. The statute does not list fracture degrees. Under the settled forensic medical practice described by the Criminal General Assembly, fractures are classified as mild (1), moderate (2-3) and severe (4-5-6); the difference between, say, a linear nasal fracture and an open comminuted fracture of the thigh bone is therefore left to the report. This classification is described here solely on the basis of the decisions’ account of it.

Rate and proportionality. The statute sets no fixed rate for each degree; the court chooses a rate of up to one half according to the degree shown in the report and the principle of proportionality in Criminal Code art. 3. Bearing in mind that the penalty may be increased by up to one half (6/12) for the most severe fracture, the Criminal General Assembly found a 1/4 (3/12) increase reasonable for a moderate (2) fracture. The 1st Criminal Chamber, on the other hand, considered a 1/3 increase for a moderate (2) fracture contrary to proportionality and indicated a rate of 1/6 (1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2024/3983, K. 2025/9709, 29.12.2025). Read together, the two decisions show that rates of 1/6 and 1/4 have been accepted for a moderate (2) fracture while 1/3 was found excessive; the Criminal General Assembly referred to the one-half rate in relation to the most severe fracture.

Death Resulting from Intentional Injury (art. 87/4)

Where death results from the injury, the current text provides for imprisonment from ten to fourteen years in cases falling under art. 86/1 and from fourteen to eighteen years in cases falling under art. 86/3. The Criminal General Assembly regards this paragraph as a specific application of the rule on offences aggravated by result in Criminal Code art. 23 and requires four conditions together: intent to injure, an injury within art. 86/1 (or together with art. 86/3), a causal link between the act and the death, and at least negligence on the offender’s part as to the death. Article 87/4 does not apply where death follows an injury curable by simple medical intervention (art. 86/2) (E. 2021/351, K. 2022/299).

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 87/4 (Death) (unofficial translation)

“(4) If death occurs as a result of intentional injury, imprisonment from ten to fourteen years shall be imposed in cases falling under the first paragraph of the preceding article, and from fourteen to eighteen years in cases falling under its third paragraph.”

Causation is also often a question of forensic medicine. In the same decision the victim died of a pneumonia-related condition about five and a half years after the injury; the death certificate and an expert report did not link the death to the injury, whereas an expert board of the Forensic Medicine Institute accepted the causal link. Because there was doubt as to causation, the General Assembly unanimously reversed the judgment rendered without a report from the High Board of Forensic Medicine.

Calculating the Sentence: Basic Penalty, Order of Increases and Minimum

The judge sets the basic penalty between the minimum and maximum of art. 86/1 according to criteria such as the manner in which the offence was committed, the instrument used, the seriousness of the harm or danger and the gravity of fault (Criminal Code art. 61/1); increases are then applied for the qualified form under art. 86/3 (e.g. a weapon) and for the result under art. 87. Unjust provocation and discretionary reduction are applied to that penalty in the order laid down in art. 61/5. Unjust provocation and self-defence are covered in a separate article: Unjust Provocation and Self-Defence in Intentional Injury (Criminal Code Arts. 29 and 25).

More than one result. Where the same injury causes both a fracture and danger to life, two separate increases are not applied. The 1st Criminal Chamber held that the offender is liable for the ground of increase producing the most serious result, that a further increase under art. 87/3 after applying art. 87/1-d is unlawful, and that the other result is to be taken into account by setting the basic penalty above the minimum (1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2026/2145, K. 2026/3266, 27.04.2026). In the same decision the Chamber recalculated the sentence itself: a basic penalty of 2 years under art. 86/1, increased by one half to 3 years under art. 86/3-e because a stick was used, increased by one fold to 6 years under art. 87/1-d for danger to life, and reduced to 5 years by the discretionary reduction (art. 62, 1/6). This calculation follows the text in force on the date of the offence.

Minimum and basic penalty. The last sentences of art. 87/1 and 87/2 set a floor for the penalty reached after the increase. Setting the basic penalty at the maximum may, however, fail the proportionality review: the 1st Criminal Chamber found a 3-year basic penalty disproportionate for a moderate (2) fracture (E. 2024/3983, K. 2025/9709).

Procedure: Forensic Medical Report, Additional Defence and Amendments

Content of the report. The Court of Cassation requires the report to answer every criterion in arts. 86 and 87: simple medical intervention, danger to life, the degree of the fracture, a permanent facial scar, and weakening or loss of the function of a sense or organ. A judgment based on a report that did not state the degree of the fracture in figures was reversed on the ground that a new report should have been obtained from the Forensic Medicine Branch Directorate with all treatment records, films and X-rays (1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2023/5066, K. 2024/930, 13.02.2024).

Conflicting or doubtful reports. Where reports reach different conclusions, the court is expected to obtain further examination that establishes the material truth beyond hesitation. The Criminal General Assembly cites art. 16 of Presidential Decree No. 4: the High Boards of Forensic Medicine examine reports of expert boards and expert departments that the court does not find sufficiently convincing in scope, as well as conflicts among them and between them and the board reports of other health institutions. In the case at hand the General Assembly held that the conflict between the death certificate and expert report on the one hand and the expert board report on the other did not in itself justify recourse to the High Board, but that a High Board report was required because of the doubt as to causation (E. 2021/351, K. 2022/299).

Additional defence. Where the indictment cites art. 87/1-d but not the last sentence setting the minimum, the defendant need not be given a separate opportunity for additional defence before that sentence is applied; the Criminal General Assembly regarded it as a minimum that does not change the legal characterisation of the offence. The decision was adopted by majority, with three members dissenting. The 1st Criminal Chamber, whose reversal was set aside, had relied on the Criminal General Assembly’s decision of 25.04.2017, E. 2015/1167, K. 2017/247, in holding that additional defence was required (E. 2025/347, K. 2026/11). The point is therefore contested. The General Assembly distinguished circumstances requiring an increased penalty that first emerge at the hearing; applying a result under art. 87 that was not cited in the indictment at all (e.g. a fracture revealed later by a report) was not the subject of this decision.

Legislative amendment. Under the current text the minimum under art. 86/1 is one year and six months and the minimums in art. 87/1 are four and six years; in the former text of art. 86/1 cited in the Criminal General Assembly’s 2020 decision, the minimum was one year. These limits were increased by Law No. 7550, published in the Official Gazette of 4 June 2025 and in force on the same day: the minimum under art. 86/1 was raised from one year to one year and six months, the minimums in the last sentence of art. 87/1 from three and five years to four and six years, and those in the last sentence of art. 87/2 from five and eight years to six and nine years. The same Law replaced the eight-to-twelve-year range in art. 87/4 for cases falling under art. 86/1 with a ten-to-fourteen-year range and raised the minimum for cases falling under art. 86/3 from twelve to fourteen years. The figures in the decisions reflect the text in force on the date of the offence; where the laws differ, the law more favourable to the offender applies (Criminal Code art. 7/2).

Court and HAGB. In the decisions examined, cases under art. 87/1 and 87/3 were heard by criminal courts of first instance and the art. 87/4 case by an assize court (ağır ceza mahkemesi). Because of the high minimums, deferral of the announcement of the verdict (HAGB) may not be available in many cases; in a case involving a permanent facial scar and a fracture, the 1st Criminal Chamber noted that the length of the final sentence left no legal possibility for it (E. 2025/7769, K. 2026/808).

Court of Cassation Decisions

The degree of the fracture depends on the report, the increase rate on proportionality

Court of Cassation Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2016/727, K. 2020/146, 03.03.2020 (reasoning of the Criminal General Assembly; the Chief Public Prosecutor’s objection was unanimously dismissed)

“… such an assessment can be made only through a medical report that may be prepared in line with settled forensic medical practice based on scientific data and classifying bone fractures as mild (1), moderate (2-3) and severe (4-5-6). … bearing in mind that the penalty may be increased by up to one half (6/12) for a bone fracture of the most severe degree, it must be accepted that the 1/4 (3/12) increase applied for a bone fracture of moderate (2) degree is reasonable and that the principle of proportionality laid down in art. 3 of the Criminal Code has not been violated.” (translation)

For a moderate (2) fracture, a 1/3 increase and a basic penalty at the maximum are excessive

Court of Cassation Decision
1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2024/3983, K. 2025/9709, 29.12.2025 (the Chamber’s grounds for reversal; unanimous)

“… the imposition of an excessive penalty by setting the basic penalty at 3 years in a manner disproportionate to the defendant’s act, … the imposition of an excessive penalty by increasing the defendant’s penalty by (1/3) instead of (1/6), contrary to the principle of proportionality under art. 3 of Law No. 5237, although the forensic examination report of the participating victim stated that the effect of the bone fracture on vital functions was of moderate (2) degree, …” (translation)

Where a fracture and danger to life coincide, only the increase for the most serious result applies

Court of Cassation Decision
1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2026/2145, K. 2026/3266, 27.04.2026 (reversal in the interest of law; the Chamber’s reasoning, unanimous, sentence redetermined by the Chamber)

“… since in aggravated injury by result where more than one result arises from the same act the convict must be held liable for the ground of increase producing the most serious result, applying art. 87/3 of Law No. 5237 as well, instead of confining the application to art. 87/1-d of Law No. 5237, in respect of the convict for acts of injury causing a bone fracture and danger to life, is contrary to the Law …” (translation)

The last sentence of art. 87/1 is a minimum, not a ground of increase (majority decision)

Court of Cassation Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2025/347, K. 2026/11, 07.01.2026 (reasoning of the majority of the Criminal General Assembly; the objection was upheld, with three members dissenting)

“Art. 87/1-last sentence of the Criminal Code, which does not change the characterisation of the offence in line with the provision cited against the defendant and is applied solely to determine the penalty, is not a circumstance requiring an increased penalty that first emerged at the hearing either. … It is not possible to regard that provision, which provides for no change in the elements of the offence, as a provision of increase.” (translation)

A report that does not state the degree of the fracture in figures is insufficient

Court of Cassation Decision
1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2023/5066, K. 2024/930, 13.02.2024 (the Chamber’s grounds for reversal; unanimous)

“… it being understood that the report stated that the injury caused a bone fracture but did not state in figures to what degree this fracture affected the participating victim’s … functions, … the rendering of judgment on the basis of an insufficient report through incomplete investigation, without regard to the need to obtain a report covering all the criteria laid down in arts. 86 and 87 of Criminal Code No. 5237 and to determine the defendant’s legal position accordingly, …” (translation)

Conditions for liability for death; art. 87/4 does not apply to injuries curable by simple medical intervention

Court of Cassation Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2021/351, K. 2022/299, 26.04.2022 (reasoning of the Criminal General Assembly; the objection was unanimously upheld and the judgment reversed for incomplete investigation)

“In conclusion, for art. 87(4) of the Criminal Code concerning the death of the victim as a result of intentional injury to apply, the following conditions must be met together: a- the offender acted with intent to injure, b- the victim was injured within the scope of art. 86(1) of the Criminal Code, or the act of injury within art. 86(1) was committed in breach of the third paragraph as well, c- the victim died in such a way that there is a causal link with the offender’s act, d- the offender bears fault, at least in the form of negligence, as to the resulting death. … Article 87(4) shall not apply to injuries that can be cured by simple medical intervention, which fall under the second paragraph of that article.” (translation)

Practical Points

  • Scope of the report: Check whether the report separately answers each question: danger to life, simple medical intervention, degree of the fracture (in figures), permanent facial scar and the function of senses or organs; a missing answer is a ground for requesting a new report.
  • Medical records and conflicts: All treatment records, films and X-rays should be sent to the forensic medicine unit; if reports reach different conclusions, the opinion of an expert board or the High Board may be requested.
  • Increase rate: Review whether the art. 87/3 rate is proportionate to the degree of the fracture and whether the basic penalty is reasoned; high rates for moderate fractures have led to reversals.
  • More than one result: If there is both a fracture and danger to life (or a permanent facial scar), check whether two separate art. 87 increases were applied; the correct approach is a single increase for the most serious result.
  • Calculation and minimum: Check separately the order of increases, the minimums in art. 87/1 and 87/2, and the comparison between the law in force on the date of the offence and the current law (Criminal Code art. 7/2); if the act was committed before 4 June 2025, the comparison is made with the text as it stood before the amendment by Law No. 7550.
  • Death: Where death occurs long after the injury, causation should be clarified with medical records; if there is doubt, a High Board report may be needed.
  • For the victim: Obtaining a final report and attending follow-up examinations matters for a complete finding of scarring, loss of function and fracture degree.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a bone fracture increase the penalty for intentional injury?

Under Criminal Code art. 87/3 the penalty is increased by up to one half according to the effect of the fracture on vital functions. The Criminal General Assembly found a 1/4 increase reasonable for a moderate (2) fracture, while the 1st Criminal Chamber considered a 1/3 increase excessive for the same degree.

Who determines the degree of a bone fracture?

The degree is determined by the forensic medical report: mild (1), moderate (2-3), severe (4-5-6). If the report does not state the degree in figures, the Court of Cassation reverses judgments rendered without obtaining a new report for incomplete investigation.

If there is both a bone fracture and danger to life, is the penalty increased twice?

No. According to the 1st Criminal Chamber, the offender is liable for the ground of increase producing the most serious result; once art. 87/1-d is applied for danger to life, no further increase under art. 87/3 may be made. The fracture is instead taken into account by setting the basic penalty above the minimum.

What do the four- and six-year minimums in Criminal Code art. 87/1 mean?

The penalty reached after the one-fold increase may not be less than four years in cases under art. 86/1 and six years in cases under art. 86/3 (current text). According to the Criminal General Assembly, this sentence is a minimum rather than a separate ground of increase; if the indictment cites art. 87/1-d, the absence of the last sentence does not require additional defence. The decision was adopted by majority. These minimums were raised from three and five years by Law No. 7550, in force since 4 June 2025; for offences committed before that date, the more favourable law is identified by comparison.

If the injured person later dies, under which provision is the offender liable?

Criminal Code art. 87/4 applies if the offender acted with intent to injure, the injury went beyond what simple medical intervention can cure, there is a causal link between the act and the death, and the offender acted at least negligently as to the death. Article 87/4 does not apply to injuries curable by simple medical intervention; if the offender intended death, the provisions on intentional killing come into play.
