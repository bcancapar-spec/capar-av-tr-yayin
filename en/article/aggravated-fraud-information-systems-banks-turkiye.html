Aggravated Fraud via IT Systems and Banks in Türkiye: Penalty and the Line with Theft (TCK Art. 158/1-f)

Short answer: Under Turkish law, fraud committed by using information systems, banks or credit institutions as instruments is an aggravated form under art. 158/1-f of the Turkish Criminal Code. The Code provides for three to ten years’ imprisonment; under this sub-paragraph the minimum term is four years and the judicial fine may not be less than twice the benefit obtained. As a rule, the sub-paragraph applies where a person is deceived through the system and parts with assets by a consent vitiated by deceit; where money is taken through the system without anyone being deceived, the Court of Cassation may treat the act as theft through information systems (art. 142/2-e), and where another person’s card is used without consent, as misuse of a bank card (art. 245). In cases where a password is obtained by telephone and the offender then transfers the money, two decisions of the Court of Cassation’s 2nd Criminal Chamber point in different directions and the files have been sent to the Criminal General Assembly.

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

What Is Aggravated Fraud?

The basic form of fraud is in art. 157 of the Turkish Criminal Code: deceiving a person by fraudulent conduct and obtaining a benefit to the detriment of that person or another. The Code punishes certain ways of committing the offence more severely; these are the aggravated forms listed in art. 158. This article focuses on the two forms most often met in practice: using information systems as an instrument, and using banks or credit institutions as an instrument. Both are regulated in the same sub-paragraph, art. 158/1-f.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 157 (Fraud)

“(1) A person who deceives another by fraudulent conduct and thereby obtains a benefit for himself or another to the detriment of that person or another shall be sentenced to imprisonment from one to five years and a judicial fine of up to five thousand days.”

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 158/1-f, 3 and 4 (Aggravated fraud)

“(1) Where the offence of fraud is committed; … f) by using information systems, banks or credit institutions as an instrument, … imprisonment from three to ten years and a judicial fine of up to five thousand days shall be imposed. … However, in the cases listed in sub-paragraphs (e), (f), (j), (k) and (l), the minimum term of imprisonment may not be less than four years and the judicial fine may not be less than twice the benefit obtained from the offence.

… (3) … Where the offences in this article and in article 157 are committed jointly by three or more persons, the penalty shall be increased by one half; where they are committed within the activities of an organisation formed to commit offences, the penalty shall be doubled.

… (4) … Where participation in the offences in this article and in article 157 is limited to the act of giving to another person, in order to obtain an unjust benefit for oneself or another, payment instruments such as one’s own or another’s bank or credit card, or the essential information or means enabling the use of an account held with a bank, intermediary institution, payment service provider or crypto-asset service provider, the penalty shall be reduced by one half.”

The difference between simple and aggravated fraud is not only the length of the sentence. While the basic offence carries one to five years’ imprisonment, the minimum rises to four years under sub-paragraph (f); the judicial fine may not be less than twice the benefit obtained from the offence. If the act is committed jointly by three or more persons, the penalty is increased by one half; if committed within the activities of an organisation, it is doubled (art. 158/3).

These limits have changed over time. As recounted in a 2021 decision of the Criminal General Assembly, Law No. 6763 of 24.11.2016 raised the range for sub-paragraph (f) from “two to seven years” to “three to ten years”, and the minimum from three to four years. For older acts, the text in force at the time of the offence and the more favourable provision are assessed separately.

Using Information Systems as an Instrument

An information system is a system that collects data and subjects it to automatic processing. However, the mere fact that a computer, telephone or the internet was used in a fraud may not on its own be enough for the sub-paragraph to apply. The Criminal General Assembly stresses that the system itself cannot be deceived; the sub-paragraph applies where a human being is deceived by using the system as an instrument.

Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2018/51, K. 2021/68, 02.03.2021 (reasoning of the General Assembly)

“Since one cannot speak of deceiving an information system, this sub-paragraph can apply only where a human being is deceived, that is defrauded, by using this system as an instrument. Otherwise, that is, where no person is deceived by entering the system but a benefit is obtained by making use of the system, an information-system offence or the offence of theft by using an information system will arise.”

The facts were that the accused posted a computer advertisement on an online second-hand sales website, persuaded the complainant who called after seeing the advertisement to transfer the price to a bank account, and did not send the item. The first-instance court classified the act as “taking advantage of the facility provided by press and broadcasting means” (art. 158/1-g); the General Assembly held that both sub-paragraphs (f) and (g) were fulfilled and that judgment should be given under (f), which carries the heavier penalty.

Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2018/51, K. 2021/68, 02.03.2021 (the General Assembly’s conclusion on the facts)

“since it is understood that the accused committed the offence both by using the information system as an instrument and by taking advantage of the facility provided by the press and broadcasting means … in that case, under the last sentence of the same paragraph, it must be accepted that judgment should be given for the aggravated form carrying the heavier penalty, namely ‘fraud by using information systems as an instrument’.”

In the same file, the deputy president and one member of the (since abolished) 23rd Criminal Chamber, which had given the reversal, proposed narrower criteria in their dissent, such as the offender not facing the victim and the victim acting in reliance on the system. Without discussing those criteria separately, the General Assembly unanimously concluded that sub-paragraph (f) should apply on the facts and reversed the judgment. The decision was given under the text before Law No. 6763; its reasoning on classification does not conflict with the current text, but the sentencing limits have changed. In our search we did not find a later General Assembly decision reaching a different result in online-advertisement cases.

Using Banks or Credit Institutions as an Instrument

The second form in the sub-paragraph is using banks or credit institutions as an instrument. The legislative reasoning quoted in the General Assembly decision above states that using information systems or “banks or credit institutions, each of which is an institution of trust” as an instrument considerably facilitates the commission of the offence. The Code also treats, in sub-paragraph (l), an offender presenting himself as a public official or as an employee of a bank, insurance or credit institution as a separate aggravated form; there too the minimum term is four years.

In each case it is debated separately whether the deceit truly used the bank or the information system as an instrument. This article does not rely on a decision of the Criminal General Assembly on whether the mere fact that money was sent through a bank is enough on its own to trigger this sub-paragraph.

Fraud, Theft or Bank Card Offence?

The key question in property offences committed with information and banking tools is classification. The same event may be fraud, theft through information systems or misuse of a bank card, depending on how the victim’s will comes into play. The sentencing ranges also differ.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 142/2-e (Aggravated theft)

“(2) Where the offence is committed; … e) by using information systems, … imprisonment from five to ten years shall be imposed.”

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 245/1 and 5 (Misuse of bank or credit cards)

“(1) A person who, having obtained or holding by any means a bank or credit card belonging to another, obtains a benefit for himself or another by using it or having it used without the consent of the cardholder or of the person to whom the card should be delivered, shall be punished with imprisonment from three to six years and a judicial fine of up to five thousand days.

… (5) … The provisions of this Code on effective repentance for offences against property shall apply to acts falling under the first paragraph.”

In a 2025 decision, the Criminal General Assembly explained the test that separates fraud from theft through consent: in fraud the property is handed over with the victim’s consent, albeit consent vitiated by deceit; in theft there is no consent.

Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2024/500, K. 2025/485, 19.11.2025 (reasoning of the General Assembly)

“In fraud the victim consents to handing over the property, but this consent is vitiated because deceit was used. Where the fraudulent conduct did not lead to a consensual handover, even a temporary one, and the property was obtained by being taken from where it was through a physical act of the offender such as sleight of hand or special skill, the act will constitute not fraud but theft.”

In that case, a person helping the victim at an ATM inserted the victim’s card and had the victim enter the PIN; while they moved to the neighbouring machine, the accused took the money from the machine where the transaction had been made. The General Assembly classified the act not as robbery, fraud, a bank card offence or theft through information systems, but as simple theft (art. 141/1), and discontinued the proceedings because the limitation period had expired. The decision also states that no bank card offence arose because the cardholder was at the ATM and possession of the card had not ended.

In a 2026 decision, the Criminal General Assembly considered money withdrawn with a card left in an ATM while the account was still open. The General Assembly accepted that the victim’s actual control over the card ended when it was forgotten, and treated art. 245/1 as the special provision in relation to theft through information systems. The decision was given by majority; four members considered that the act did not constitute the bank card offence.

Court of Cassation Criminal General Assembly Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2024/199, K. 2026/28, 14.01.2026 (General Assembly decision given by majority)

“Since the actual control/possession over the bank card of a victim who leaves the ATM having forgotten the card in the machine and leaving the account open comes to an end, it must be accepted that the act of the accused, who obtained a benefit by withdrawing money from the victim’s account using without consent both the information system and the victim’s bank card that he had obtained, constitutes the offence of misuse of a bank card regulated in art. 245/1 of the Turkish Criminal Code, which is a special provision in relation to the offence of theft (art. 142/2-e of the Turkish Criminal Code).”

Obtaining passwords by telephone: an open debate. In a common scenario, the offender presents himself as a bank officer, asks the victim for a password on the pretext of a “refund”, and then transfers the money in the account himself. In its decision of 07.10.2025, the Court of Cassation’s 2nd Criminal Chamber held that the victim had been deceived only into giving banking details, not into transferring money, and that the money was transferred without the victim’s knowledge; it treated the act as theft through information systems under art. 142/2-e, dismissed the Chief Public Prosecutor’s objection and sent the file to the Criminal General Assembly.

Court of Cassation Decision
2nd Criminal Chamber, E. 2025/10542, K. 2025/17419, 07.10.2025 (objection dismissed; file sent to the Criminal General Assembly)

“In this situation, the complainant was led into error and deceit for the purpose of obtaining banking details. The complainant did not fall into this trap by being deceived by the accused’s deceit into transferring money to anyone.”

In its decision of 02.02.2026, the same chamber, in a very similar case, did not uphold the regional court of appeal’s resistance decision classifying the act as theft through information systems and sent the file to the Criminal General Assembly. The text of the decision itself is short; that the chamber treated the act as fraud under art. 158/1-f appears from the dissenting opinion of two members.

Court of Cassation Decision (dissenting opinion)
2nd Criminal Chamber, E. 2022/9201, K. 2026/1369, 02.02.2026 (majority view as recounted in the dissenting opinion)

“The majority of our Chamber decided to send the file to the Criminal General Assembly, stating that the resistance decision was wrong and that the act constitutes the offence of fraud.”

These two decisions show that classification in password-by-telephone cases is not yet settled. In our search of the official case-law database at the time of writing, we found no decision of the Criminal General Assembly in these files. The outcome matters: art. 158/1-f carries three to ten years’ imprisonment plus a judicial fine, art. 142/2-e five to ten years’ imprisonment; effective repentance operates through art. 168 in both.

Handing Over a Card or Account Details: Art. 158/4

Law No. 7589 of 16.07.2026 added a fourth paragraph to art. 158. Under it, if a person’s participation in fraud is limited to giving another person, in order to obtain an unjust benefit, payment instruments such as one’s own or another’s bank or credit card, or the essential information or means enabling the use of an account held with a bank, intermediary institution, payment service provider or crypto-asset service provider, the penalty is reduced by one half.

The paragraph does not create a new offence; it requires participation and the aim of an unjust benefit. That cases where the account is used without the holder’s knowledge and will fall outside the question of participation is this article’s inference. For the debate on intent in account-renting cases and on effective repentance, see our article on renting out a bank account (IBAN). Because the reduction is a favourable provision, whether it applies to acts committed before the paragraph entered into force may be debated; this is this article’s inference, and our search found no Court of Cassation decision applying this paragraph.

Effective Repentance and the Competent Court

Effective repentance also applies to aggravated fraud under art. 168. The reduction depends on the loss being fully made good by returning the property in kind or by compensation, and on the stage at which this happens.

Statutory Provision — Turkish Criminal Code (Law No. 5237) art. 168/1, 2 and 4 (Effective repentance)

“(1) Where, after the offences of theft, damage to property, breach of trust, fraud, fraudulent bankruptcy, negligent bankruptcy … are completed but before prosecution has begun on that account, the perpetrator, instigator or aider personally shows remorse and fully makes good the victim’s loss by returning the property in kind or by compensation, the penalty to be imposed shall be reduced by up to two thirds.

(2) Where effective repentance is shown after prosecution has begun but before judgment is given, the penalty to be imposed shall be reduced by up to one half.

… (4) In the case of partial return or compensation, the consent of the victim shall additionally be required for the provisions on effective repentance to apply.”

The details of effective repentance (full compensation, partial payment, the start of prosecution) are discussed in our article on effective repentance in theft; art. 168 is the same text for fraud. For misuse of bank cards, art. 245/5 refers to the same provisions.

Competent court. The current text of art. 12 of Law No. 5235, in listing the jurisdiction of the assize court (ağır ceza mahkemesi), does not mention aggravated fraud separately; apart from the listed offences, the assize court hears offences carrying more than ten years’ imprisonment. Provisional art. 7, added by Law No. 7571 of 24.12.2025, accepts that jurisdiction has changed and lays down a transitional rule.

Statutory Provision — Law No. 5235 art. 12 (Jurisdiction of the assize court)

“Without prejudice to cases where laws separately confer jurisdiction, the assize courts have jurisdiction over cases brought for the offences of robbery (art. 148) … and over cases and matters concerning offences requiring aggravated life imprisonment, life imprisonment and imprisonment of more than ten years.”

Statutory Provision — Law No. 5235 provisional art. 7

“In cases pending before assize courts or in files under appeal or cassation review on the date this article enters into force, no decision of lack of jurisdiction or reversal may be given on the ground that the jurisdiction of the court hearing aggravated fraud (art. 158) offences has changed by the Law introducing this article. These cases shall continue to be heard under the jurisdiction rules in force before this article entered into force until they are concluded by a final judgment.”

From these two provisions it follows that, as a rule, the criminal court of first instance (asliye ceza mahkemesi) has jurisdiction over aggravated fraud cases filed after provisional art. 7 entered into force, while jurisdiction is assessed separately where the act requires more than ten years’ imprisonment, for example because of the increases in art. 158/3; this is this article’s inference. Cases pending before the assize court, or under appeal or cassation review, on the date provisional art. 7 entered into force continue under the previous jurisdiction rules until a final judgment.

Points to Watch in Practice

  • Separate the victim’s will step by step: Did the victim send the money himself, or only give a password or information? The Court of Cassation’s test is whether the property left the victim with consent vitiated by deceit or without consent.
  • The outcome in password-by-telephone cases is uncertain: the 2nd Criminal Chamber’s 2025 and 2026 decisions point in different directions; the Criminal General Assembly’s decision should be followed.
  • Who holds the card is decisive: if the cardholder is at the ATM, art. 245 may not arise; where the card was forgotten and left the holder’s control, the General Assembly applied art. 245/1.
  • An online advertisement may not always be enough on its own: the General Assembly held that sub-paragraph (f) should apply in a case committed through an advertisement and a telephone call; but whether the system was truly an instrument of the deceit is debated on the facts.
  • The position of a person who hands over a card or account details: if participation is limited to this act, the penalty is reduced by one half under art. 158/4; whether intent exists is debated separately.
  • Timing of compensation: full compensation before prosecution begins allows a reduction of up to two thirds, after prosecution begins but before judgment up to one half; partial payment requires the victim’s consent.
  • Check the relevant dates separately: for the sentencing limits (Law No. 6763, 2016) and art. 158/4 (Law No. 7589, 2026), the text in force on the date of the offence and the more favourable provision; for the competent court, the stage the case had reached under provisional art. 7 added by Law No. 7571.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the penalty for aggravated fraud under art. 158/1-f of the Turkish Criminal Code?

For aggravated fraud committed by using information systems, banks or credit institutions as instruments, Turkish law provides for three to ten years’ imprisonment and a judicial fine; under this sub-paragraph the minimum term is four years and the judicial fine may not be less than twice the benefit obtained. If the act is committed by three or more persons the penalty is increased by one half, and if within the activities of an organisation it is doubled. Reductions such as effective repentance and art. 158/4 may also apply.

Is fraud committed through an online advertisement aggravated fraud under art. 158/1-f of the Turkish Criminal Code?

In a 2021 decision, the Court of Cassation’s Criminal General Assembly held, in a case where a complainant who saw an online sales advertisement was persuaded by telephone and transferred money to a bank account, that both the use of information systems as an instrument and the use of press and broadcasting means were present, and held that sub-paragraph (f), which carries the heavier penalty, should apply. Whether the system was used as an instrument of the deceit is assessed separately in each case.

Under Turkish law, is a person who poses as a bank officer, obtains a password and transfers the money punished for fraud or for theft through information systems?

Court of Cassation practice on this point is not settled. In its decision of 07.10.2025, the 2nd Criminal Chamber held that the victim was deceived only into giving information and that the money was transferred without the victim’s knowledge, and treated the act as theft through information systems (art. 142/2-e of the Turkish Criminal Code). In its decision of 02.02.2026, given by majority, the same chamber treated a similar case as aggravated fraud. Both files have been sent to the Criminal General Assembly.

Under Turkish law, is withdrawing money from an ATM with another person’s bank card aggravated fraud?

As a rule, no. Where a benefit is obtained by using another person’s card without consent and without anyone being deceived, the offence of misuse of bank cards under art. 245/1 of the Turkish Criminal Code arises. In its decision of 14.01.2026, given by majority, the Criminal General Assembly treated a withdrawal with a card forgotten in an ATM as this offence. Where the cardholder is at the ATM and possession of the card continues, the General Assembly stated that the bank card offence does not arise.

Under Turkish law, how much does paying back the loss reduce the penalty for aggravated fraud?

Under art. 168 of the Turkish Criminal Code, if the loss is fully made good by returning the property in kind or by compensation before prosecution begins, the penalty is reduced by up to two thirds; if it is made good after prosecution begins but before judgment, by up to one half. In the case of partial payment, the victim’s consent is additionally required for effective repentance to apply. The court sets the reduction within these limits.
