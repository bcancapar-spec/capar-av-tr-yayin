Zoning Readjustment under Art. 18 of Law No. 3194 in Türkiye: Contribution Share, Objection, Annulment Action

Short answer: Under Turkish law, a municipality (outside municipal and adjacent areas, the governorship) may merge land and plots within the zoning boundary without the owners’ consent, re-parcel them according to the zoning plan and deduct up to forty-five per cent of the pre-readjustment area as the readjustment contribution share (DOP) (Zoning Law art. 18). As a rule, DOP is taken from a parcel only once, according to the Council of State’s 6th Chamber, the same rate applies to everyone in the same readjustment area, and the new parcel is allocated first where the old parcel was located. Under the Law, the parcellation plan is posted for one month and becomes final at the end of that period (art. 19); the Regulation sets the posting period at thirty days and provides for objections to be assessed by the administration. The Council of State’s 6th Chamber held, in a parcellation where an objection made during the posting period went unanswered, that the action must be brought in the administrative court within sixty days after the reply period running from the day after the last day of the posting (sixty days under the text in force at the time of the decision; thirty days for applications made since 14/7/2021).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Under article 18 of the Zoning Law (Law No. 3194), a municipality in Türkiye may merge and re-parcel land and plots without the owners’ consent and may deduct up to forty-five per cent of the pre-readjustment area as the readjustment contribution share. The article explains the scope and limits of that share, the allocation of parcels, posting and objection, the time limit for an annulment action and reversal after annulment, with decisions of the Council of State.

What an owner in Türkiye whose property enters a zoning readjustment (land and plot readjustment under article 18 of the Zoning Law, Law No. 3194) needs to know: the scope of the readjustment contribution share and its forty-five per cent limit, where the new parcel will be allocated, posting and objection, the time limit for an annulment action and the return to the original parcels after annulment.

What Is a Zoning Readjustment (Article 18 Readjustment)?

A zoning plan shows how an area is to be used; but cadastral parcels often do not match the roads, blocks and building areas the plan provides for. A zoning readjustment, known in practice as an “article 18 readjustment” or “parcellation”, removes this mismatch: all parcels in the readjustment area are pooled, divided into blocks and parcels conforming to the zoning plan and redistributed to the owners. Its legal basis is article 18 of the Zoning Law (Law No. 3194).

Statutory Provision — Zoning Law (Law No. 3194) art. 18(1) (Preparation of parcellation plans)

“Municipalities are authorised, without seeking the consent of the owners or other right holders, to merge built or unbuilt plots and land within the zoning boundary with one another, with surplus road areas and with places belonging to public institutions or municipalities, to divide them anew into blocks or parcels conforming to the zoning plan, to distribute them to the right holders on the basis of sole, shared or condominium ownership, and to have the registration carried out ex officio. If those places are outside municipal and adjacent areas, the powers stated above are exercised by the governorship.”

The owner’s consent is not required; the land registry records are rearranged ex officio according to the new parcels. The Law treats it as the “principle” that the municipality or governorship prepare and approve parcellation plans within five years of the zoning plans becoming final, and holds those administrations responsible for expropriation work arising from a failure to do so. The details are set out in the Regulation on Land and Plot Readjustment issued under the Law; the Regulation was significantly updated on posting, objection and reversal by an amendment published in the Official Gazette of 11/9/2025.

Readjustment Contribution Share: Scope, Rate and Limits

The readjustment contribution share is the area deducted free of charge from every parcel to create public service areas such as roads, parks and school sites in the readjustment area and in return for the increase in value created by the readjustment. The Law sets its upper limit expressly.

Statutory Provision — Zoning Law (Law No. 3194) art. 18(2)

“During the distribution of land and plots subjected to readjustment by municipalities or governorships, a sufficient area may be deducted from their surface areas as the "readjustment contribution share" for establishing the public service areas needed for the population of the readjustment area to carry on their urban activities and in return for the increases in value resulting from the readjustment. However, the readjustment contribution shares to be taken under this article may not exceed forty-five per cent of the pre-readjustment surface areas of the land and plots subjected to readjustment.”

The areas that may be provided from DOP are listed in the third paragraph of the Law: roads, squares, parks, car parks, children’s gardens, green areas, places of worship and police stations, education and health facility sites, market places, neighbourhood sports areas, municipal service areas, social and cultural facility areas and the like. The first sentence of that paragraph was last amended by Law No. 7534 of 5/12/2024, which added uses such as public nurseries and day-care centres, technical infrastructure and transformer areas, and bus terminal areas. DOP may not be used for purposes other than facilities related to these services.

If the area that must be set aside for public services exceeds forty-five per cent, or if there are areas that could not be transferred to the public because DOP cannot be deducted a second time in a parcellation already registered, those areas are transferred to the public by the methods in article 13 of the Law; where they are to be expropriated, the parts of the parcels in the readjustment that fall on those areas are expropriated by the administration concerned. If part of a parcel is also to be expropriated, DOP is set aside from the area remaining after expropriation.

Statutory Provision — Zoning Law (Law No. 3194) art. 18 (No DOP more than once; additional deduction)

“Under the provisions of this article, it is the principle that the readjustment contribution share is not taken more than once from any parcel. However, where building conditions and population increase by any kind of zoning plan decision, an additional readjustment contribution share deduction may be made from the parcels with the increase, provided that their value after the readjustment is not less than their previous value, to top up the readjustment contribution share rate of the first readjustment to 45%, taking into account the cessions or deductions made in earlier zoning readjustments.”

Under the Law, DOP is also taken from parcels registered by subdivision and merger without a parcellation plan, in the amount needed to top up the proportion previously ceded for public service areas to the DOP rate of the new parcellation. Where DOP cannot be taken because of existing buildings, the amount of the share may be converted into money; under the sentence added by Law No. 7534, that sum is paid to the other parcel owners who had to bear an extra DOP deduction. A note is entered in the declarations section of the land register that no transaction may be made until the sum is paid.

Allocation of the New Parcels

The area remaining after DOP is given to the owner as a new zoning parcel; its location is governed by a paragraph added to the Law in 2019 by Law No. 7181.

Statutory Provision — Zoning Law (Law No. 3194) art. 18 (Allocation)

“Parcels to be given to property owners as a result of the readjustment are given first from where the property included in the readjustment is located and, if that is not possible, from the nearest equivalent area.”

Under the same article, shared plots and land may be made separate with the consent of the shareholders or on the basis of actual use, provided the minimum parcel size is met; where the implementation zoning plan contains no provision, no parcel may be created below the minimum parcel size set in the regulation. If the area allocated to an owner is not large enough to form a zoning parcel on its own, shared allocation may be unavoidable. Trees and buildings on land set aside for public services are removed by the municipality or governorship upon payment of their value or salvage value.

Posting, Objection and Finality

The objection procedure is found in the Regulation, not in the Law.

Statutory Provision — Zoning Law (Law No. 3194) art. 19(1) (Preparation and registration of parcellation plans)

“Parcellation plans are prepared according to zoning plans and enter into force after approval by the municipal council committee within municipal and adjacent areas and by the provincial administrative board outside them. These plans are posted at the relevant administration for one month. They are also announced by customary means. They become final at the end of this period.”

According to the note in the legislation text, the first paragraph of article 26 of the Regulation was annulled by the Council of State’s 6th Chamber’s decision of 13/5/2024, E.2020/5904, K.2024/2887, and was re-enacted by the amendment of 11/9/2025. Under the new text, the parcellation plan is posted for thirty days at the municipality or governorship, published on the website and entered into the PARSİD system; owners of the properties included are notified via e-Government. The sheets, the parcel list and the distribution schedule are posted; if an owner applies, information on the plan is provided.

Regulatory Provision — Regulation on Land and Plot Readjustment art. 26(2) (Announcement of parcellation plans)

“If there is no objection within the thirty-day posting period, the parcellation plans become final. If there is an objection, the objections are assessed by the relevant administration. If the objections are not found appropriate, the parcellation plans become final by a decision/approval of the relevant administration stating the reasons.”

If an objection is upheld, the plan is prepared again and the posting process restarts; however, re-posting is not needed if the owners affected by the change are notified with a ten-day objection period or give their consent. Objections may also be made through PARSİD (art. 26(4)). The Law says “one month” and the Regulation “thirty days”; where the calculation is in doubt, it is safer to rely on the shorter one. This is the article’s own inference.

Annulment Action: Time Limit and Parties

A parcellation is an administrative act directly affecting the owner’s property, and its annulment is sought in the administrative court. Under article 7 of the Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577), as cited in the Council of State decision discussed below, the time limit for bringing an action before administrative courts is sixty days where special laws do not provide a different period. Under article 11 of the same Law, an application to the administration before bringing an action suspends the running period; if no reply is given, the request is deemed rejected and the period starts running again. In the text quoted in the decision, this reply period was sixty days; Law No. 7331 of 8/7/2021 reduced it to thirty days. For applications made before 14/7/2021 the former period applies (Law No. 2577 provisional art. 10).

In a parcellation posted in 2017 and objected to during the posting period, the Council of State’s 6th Chamber calculated the period under article 11: under the text then in force, if no reply is given within sixty days, the action must be brought within the second sixty-day period following the last day of the posting. Since the reply period is thirty days for objections made after 14/7/2021, the same approach suggests that the action must be brought within sixty days after a thirty-day reply period running from the day after the last day of the posting; this is the article’s own inference. A rejection letter notified later does not start a new period. The search made for this article found no decision departing from this approach. The decision reviewed, however, concerns a posting process before the 11/9/2025 amendment of the Regulation. As the new text states that, if objections are rejected, the plan becomes final by an administrative decision “stating the reasons”, whether notification of that decision affects the period was not addressed in the decisions reviewed; the period should be counted from the earliest possible starting point. This, too, is the article’s own inference.

The defendant is the administration that adopted and approved the parcellation. The Council of State’s 6th Chamber stated that where a parcellation was adopted by a district municipality committee and approved by a metropolitan municipality committee, the metropolitan municipality must also be made a party. In the decisions reviewed, the courts ordered on-site inspections, and the DOP calculation, the readjustment boundary and the location of the allocation were examined by technical report.

Reversal after an Annulment Decision

When a parcellation is annulled, the administration carries out a new readjustment to comply with the judgment. At this stage “reversal”, that is, returning to the original (root) parcels as they stood before the annulled readjustment, comes into play.

Regulatory Provision — Regulation on Land and Plot Readjustment art. 37(2) (Parcellation plans annulled by court decision)

“If the parcellation plan, the committee decision approving it or the approval of the relevant institution is annulled by a court decision, reversal operations must be carried out and the root parcels as they stood before the parcellation plan must be restored. However, if dispositions such as the sale of newly created properties, building permits, construction servitude or condominium have been made on the zoning parcels created by the annulled parcellation plan in the readjustment area, it is mandatory to prepare a new parcellation plan at the same time as the reversal operations, taking into account those dispositions and the court’s grounds for annulment.”

For cases where returning to the root parcels is legally or physically impossible, Law No. 7221 added a special paragraph to the Law in 2020, which was amended by Law No. 7534 of 5/12/2024; its current text therefore differs from the text quoted in the Council of State decision below; the decision quotes the earlier text, which referred “first” to allocation of a site with the right holder’s consent.

Statutory Provision — Zoning Law (Law No. 3194) art. 18 (Readjustments annulled by court decision)

“Where zoning readjustments made under this article are annulled by final court decisions and … it is determined by the approving authority of the administrations competent to approve parcellation plans that it is not possible to return to the pre-readjustment root parcels through reversal operations, a new zoning readjustment aimed at complying with the court decisions may first be carried out on the registered zoning parcels.”

If that too is not possible, under the same paragraph a suitable site in the readjustment area is allocated with the right holder’s consent; failing agreement, the market value after deduction of DOP is paid, taking into account the location in the root parcel.

Zoning Readjustment in Council of State Decisions

In the parcellation decisions reviewed, appeals on points of law were examined by the Council of State’s 6th Chamber and insistence decisions by the Plenary Session of Administrative Law Chambers. The decisions below were taken in full text from the official case-law database, and all four were given “as final”.

Council of State Decision
6th Chamber, E. 2023/1197, K. 2025/658, 13.02.2025 (the Chamber’s grounds for reversal: different DOP rates in the same area)

“The net zoning parcel area is obtained as a result of the deduction made by applying this rate equally to all parcels within the readjustment boundary. … The parcels from which no DOP was deducted in the parcellation benefited from the increase in value brought by the higher building conditions and additional amenity areas introduced by the zoning plan, but were exempted from the part of the DOP rate corresponding to those parcels, which is the counterpart of that increase in value.”

In that case a new parcellation had been made under a plan with increased building conditions; a DOP of 44.80% was deducted from some parcels while no deduction at all was made from parcels that had already been through parcellation. Since an additional DOP deduction was available, the Chamber found the use of different rates contrary to the principle of equality and reversed the regional administrative court’s decision dismissing the appeal against the judgment rejecting the action. The decision also notes that the earlier parcellation was made before the upper limit of DOP was raised from 40% to 45% (the upper limit was raised from forty to forty-five per cent by Law No. 7181 of 4/7/2019).

Council of State Decision
6th Chamber, E. 2020/7773, K. 2023/3710, 12.04.2023 (the Chamber’s own reasoning: objection during posting and time limit; under the pre-2021 text of art. 11)

“Since an objection was made to the parcellation plan in dispute within the posting period, it is clear that the time limit for bringing an action must be determined under article 11 of the Administrative Procedure Law. Accordingly, since the objection was rejected by not being answered within sixty days, the action must be brought within the second sixty days following the last day of the announcement.”

In that file the regional administrative court had calculated the period from the date of the objection, that is, the date the act became fully known, and had dismissed the appeal with different reasoning. The Chamber upheld the dismissal for being out of time on its own reasoning, starting the period from the last day of the posting. The first-instance court had stated that a zoning status certificate obtained later could not revive the time limit; the dismissal of the claim concerning that certificate was also upheld.

Council of State Plenary Session of Administrative Law Chambers Decision
Plenary Session of Administrative Law Chambers, E. 2022/3034, K. 2023/729, 10.04.2023 (the Plenary’s grounds for reversal: allocation at the original location)

“that the zoning parcels to be created by the readjustment must be allocated, as far as possible, to the old cadastral parcels in the same place or nearby, but that, where this is not possible for a technical or legal reason, allocation may also be made to the property owner from another place, … it is understood that giving a share from parcels created in another block where there is no necessity, and creating and distributing zoning parcels in favour of certain parcel owners, would be contrary to the legislation.”

The Plenary reversed the regional administrative court’s insistence decision by majority. The decision applied the “as far as possible from the same place” allocation rule of the old Regulation in force on the date of the act; today the same principle is found in article 18 of the Law. The Plenary listed the reasons that may prevent allocation at the original location with examples such as falling on a public service area (legal necessity), not being large enough to form an independent parcel (physical necessity) and more than one right holder falling on the same parcel (technical necessity).

Council of State Decision
6th Chamber, E. 2022/9072, K. 2023/7963, 07.11.2023 (the Chamber’s grounds for reversal: reversal to root parcels)

“Court decisions annulling a parcellation on grounds specific to the parcel in dispute, such as turning a solely owned or lightly shared ownership structure into a shared one, increasing the number of shareholders and/or allocating a zoning parcel from a place not equivalent to the cadastral parcel, do not make it mandatory in the new parcellation to carry out reversal and implement on the basis of the cadastral parcel. … in a parcellation to be made after an annulment decision given where circumstances constituting a general ground of annulment of the parcellation exist, reversal must always be applied.”

The Chamber found it unlawful to make a new parcellation on the zoning parcels of earlier readjustments annulled on a general ground, and reversed the part of the judgment dismissing the action concerning the parcellation. The text of article 18 quoted in the decision differs from the current text: the first sentence of the third paragraph and the reversal paragraph were amended in 2024 by Law No. 7534.

Practical Points

  • Follow the posting. Watch the e-Government notification and the municipality’s posting; document the start and end dates of the posting. Time limits depend on these dates.
  • Examine the distribution schedule. The schedule shows the area of the root parcel, the DOP rate applied, any additional DOP or conversion into money, and the location of the new parcel allocated. Check whether different rates were applied in the same area.
  • Make the objection specific. Object specifically to the rate, the location, the sharing or the readjustment boundary; keep the date of the objection.
  • Count the time limit from the earliest date. If an objection was made during posting, under the 6th Chamber’s approach a reply period (thirty days for applications made after 14/7/2021, Administrative Procedure Law art. 11) runs from the day after the last day of posting, followed by a sixty-day period for the action. Waiting for a later rejection letter or a zoning status certificate may not save the time limit.
  • Identify the defendants correctly. If the administrations that adopted and approved the parcellation are different (such as a district and a metropolitan municipality), consider the position of both.
  • Follow the new readjustment after a final annulment. If the annulment rests on a general ground, check whether reversal was carried out and, if not, whether this was determined by a reasoned technical report (Regulation art. 37(6)).

Frequently Asked Questions

Under Turkish law, how much land can be deducted as the readjustment contribution share (DOP) in a zoning readjustment under article 18 of the Zoning Law?

Under article 18(2) of the Zoning Law (Law No. 3194), the readjustment contribution share may not exceed forty-five per cent of the pre-readjustment area of the land and plots included in the readjustment. As a rule, the share is taken from a parcel only once; where a plan decision increases building conditions and population, the rate of the first readjustment may be topped up to 45% provided that the parcel’s value does not fall. If public service areas exceed that limit, the excess is transferred to the public by the methods in article 13 of the Law, for example by expropriation.

How is an objection made during the posting period against a parcellation plan (article 18 readjustment) in Türkiye?

Article 19 of the Zoning Law states that a parcellation plan is posted at the relevant administration for one month and becomes final at the end of that period; the objection procedure is set out in the Regulation on Land and Plot Readjustment. Under article 26 of the Regulation, amended on 11/9/2025, the posting period is thirty days, owners are notified via e-Government and objections may also be filed through the PARSİD system. The administration assesses the objection; if it is rejected, the plan becomes final by a reasoned administrative decision.

Under Turkish law, when must an action for annulment be brought against a zoning readjustment (parcellation act)?

The time limit for an action before an administrative court in Türkiye is sixty days unless a special law provides otherwise (Administrative Procedure Law art. 7). In a 2017 posting, the Council of State’s 6th Chamber held that where an objection is made during the posting period and the administration does not reply within the sixty-day period then in force, the action must be brought within the second sixty-day period following the last day of the posting, and that a later rejection letter does not start a new period. Since 14/7/2021 the reply period under article 11 has been thirty days; the effect of the 2025 amendment to the Regulation was not addressed in the decisions reviewed. The period should therefore be counted from the earliest possible starting point; this is the article’s own inference.

In a zoning readjustment in Türkiye, must the new parcel be allocated where the old parcel was located?

As a rule, yes. Under article 18 of the Zoning Law, parcels are allocated first from where the property was located and, if that is not possible, from the nearest equivalent area. The Council of State’s Plenary Session of Administrative Law Chambers found it contrary to the legislation to allocate a share in another block where no legal, physical or technical necessity prevented allocation at the original location. Where, for example, the parcel falls on a public service area or is too small to form a building parcel on its own, allocation elsewhere or as a shared parcel is possible.

Under Turkish law, if a court annuls a zoning readjustment, is the land returned to the original parcels?

Under article 37 of the Regulation on Land and Plot Readjustment, if the parcellation plan or its approval is annulled, as a rule a reversal is carried out and the original (root) parcels are restored; where the annulment concerns only a specific parcel, a correction may be made for the parcels concerned. The Council of State’s 6th Chamber stated that reversal must be applied where there is a general ground of annulment. If returning to the root parcels is legally or physically impossible, article 18 of the Law provides for a new readjustment on the registered zoning parcels, failing which allocation of a suitable site or payment of the market value.
