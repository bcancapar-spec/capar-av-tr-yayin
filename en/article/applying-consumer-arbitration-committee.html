Applying to the Consumer Arbitration Committee

Short answer: Under Turkish law, application to the consumer arbitration committee is mandatory for disputes below the monetary threshold set in Law No. 6502, which is increased at the start of each calendar year by the revaluation rate; disputes above the threshold cannot be brought before the committee (Law No. 6502 art. 68). The application may be made to the committee at the consumer’s place of residence or where the consumer transaction took place, or, where there is no committee, to the district governorship. The Court of Cassation has held that applying to the committee in a dispute within the threshold is a condition of action that the consumer court must examine of its own motion. The committee’s decisions bind the parties and are enforced under the rules on the enforcement of judgments; the parties may object to the consumer court within two weeks of notification, and the objection does not by itself stay enforcement (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3). The consumer court’s decision on the objection is final (Law No. 6502 art. 70/5).

What Is the Consumer Arbitration Committee?

Consumer arbitration committees are established by the Ministry of Trade to resolve disputes that may arise from consumer transactions and practices directed at consumers; their territorial jurisdiction and the division of work among them are also determined by the Ministry (Law No. 6502 art. 66/1). The establishment of the committees, the application procedure, objections to their decisions, consumer courts and mediation as a condition of action are governed by Law No. 6502 on Consumer Protection (“the Law”). Application to the committee is mandatory for disputes below the monetary threshold, and the committee’s decisions bind the parties (Law No. 6502 art. 68/1 and Law No. 6502 art. 70/1).

Statutory Provision — Law on Consumer Protection (Law No. 6502) art. 66/1 (Establishment and remit) (unofficial translation)

“The Ministry is charged with establishing consumer arbitration committees to resolve disputes that may arise from consumer transactions and practices directed at consumers. The territorial jurisdiction of, and the division of work among, consumer arbitration committees are determined by the Ministry.”

The committee is chaired, in provinces, by the provincial director of trade and, in districts, by the district governor (kaymakam), or by an official designated by either. Together with the chair, it consists of five members: a municipal staff member designated by the mayor, a member designated by the bar association from among its members, a member designated by the relevant chamber of commerce or chamber of artisans depending on the seller, and a member chosen by consumer organisations from among themselves (Law No. 6502 art. 66/2).

An example of the Ministry’s determination of territorial jurisdiction is the 2018 restructuring. As described by the 3rd Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation, the Ministry reorganised the committees’ territorial jurisdiction with effect from 01.08.2018; in provinces without metropolitan-municipality status, the provincial consumer arbitration committee was empowered to decide applications made to it and to the district governorates in that province (Court of Cassation 3rd Civil Chamber, E. 2021/3990, K. 2021/7144).

Requirements for Application and the Monetary Threshold

Without prejudice to the parties’ rights under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, application to the consumer arbitration committee is mandatory for disputes valued below the monetary threshold set by the Law, and no application can be made to the committee for disputes above that threshold (Law No. 6502 art. 68/1). Disputes above the threshold are heard by the consumer court; in such actions, save for the exceptions listed in the Law, applying to a mediator before filing suit is a condition of action (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A/1).

Statutory Provision — Law on Consumer Protection (Law No. 6502) art. 68 (Application) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Without prejudice to the parties’ rights under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, application to consumer arbitration committees is mandatory for disputes valued below thirty thousand Turkish Lira. No application can be made to consumer arbitration committees for disputes above these values. (2) Consumer arbitration committees must accept the applications made to them for appropriate action. … (4) The monetary thresholds specified in this article are applied, with effect from the beginning of each calendar year, as increased by the revaluation rate determined and announced for that year under repeated article 298 of Tax Procedure Law No. 213 of 4/1/1961. Fractions of one thousand Turkish Lira are disregarded in calculating these increases. … (5) This article does not prevent consumers from applying to alternative dispute resolution bodies under the relevant legislation.”

The amount of thirty thousand Turkish Lira is set by Law No. 6502 art. 68/1 as amended by Law No. 7392 of 24.3.2022; under Law No. 6502 art. 68/4, this amount is applied as increased by the revaluation rate with effect from the beginning of each calendar year. The amount for the current year therefore changes every year and should be confirmed from official sources before applying. This article does not state the amount for the current year.

The Court of Cassation treated application to the committee in a dispute within the threshold as a condition of action to be examined by the consumer court of its own motion, and held that an action for annulment of an objection brought without such application had to be dismissed on procedural grounds without examining the merits (Court of Cassation 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/9334, K. 2016/14097). The mandatory application rule leaves the parties’ rights under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law unaffected, and the article does not prevent consumers from applying to alternative dispute resolution bodies under the relevant legislation (Law No. 6502 art. 68/5).

Application Procedure and the Competent Committee

An application may be made to the consumer arbitration committee of the place where the consumer is domiciled or where the consumer transaction took place. In places without a committee, the application may be made to the district governorate, which forwards it, for appropriate action, to the competent consumer arbitration committee designated by the Ministry (Law No. 6502 art. 68/3).

Statutory Provision — Law on Consumer Protection (Law No. 6502) art. 68/3 (Application) (unofficial translation)

“Applications may be made to the consumer arbitration committee of the place where the consumer is domiciled or where the consumer transaction took place. In places without a consumer arbitration committee, applications may be made to the district governorate. Such applications are forwarded by the district governorates, for appropriate action, to the competent consumer arbitration committee designated by the Ministry.”

Consumer arbitration committees must accept the applications made to them for appropriate action (Law No. 6502 art. 68/2). Stating clearly the subject of the dispute, the amount claimed and the supporting documents (such as invoices, contracts and correspondence) makes the committee’s examination easier.

Where the committee’s competence is disputed, the 13th Civil Chamber, in a decision reversing a judgment in the interest of the law, stated that annulling a committee decision for lack of competence does not rest on the exclusive-venue rule in Code of Civil Procedure art. 114/ç, and held that, in a file containing no information or document showing that the objection to competence had been raised before the committee and where the objection had been raised before the court, the court should have examined the merits (Court of Cassation 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2019/4481, K. 2019/13157).

Binding Effect, Attorney’s Fees and Enforcement of the Decision

Decisions of the consumer arbitration committee bind the parties and are enforced in accordance with the provisions of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law on the enforcement of judgments (Law No. 6502 art. 70/1-2). An objection to the decision does not automatically suspend enforcement (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3).

Statutory Provision — Law on Consumer Protection (Law No. 6502) art. 70/1-2 (Decision and objection to the decision) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Decisions of the consumer arbitration committee bind the parties. Consumer arbitration committees may not order the payment of attorney’s fees. (2) … Decisions of the consumer arbitration committee are enforced in accordance with the provisions of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law on the enforcement of judgments.”

The committees may not order the payment of attorney’s fees (Law No. 6502 art. 70/1). By contrast, in an objection action brought against a committee decision rendered in favour of the consumer, if the decision is annulled, an attorney’s fee calculated on the proportional scale of the minimum attorney fee tariff is awarded against the consumer; however, where the annulment results from information or a document that was available but was not submitted to the committee and was then submitted to the consumer court, no costs or attorney’s fees may be awarded against the consumer (Law No. 6502 art. 70/6).

Objecting to the Decision, the Time Limit and the Competent Court

The parties may object to the committee’s decision, within two weeks of service, before the consumer court of the place where the consumer arbitration committee or the consumer’s domicile is located. The objection does not suspend enforcement of the decision; however, upon request, the judge may suspend enforcement by way of an interim measure (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3).

Statutory Provision — Law on Consumer Protection (Law No. 6502) art. 70/3 and 5 (Decision and objection to the decision) (unofficial translation)

“(3) The parties may object to decisions of the consumer arbitration committee, within two weeks of service, before the consumer court of the place where the consumer arbitration committee or the consumer’s domicile is located. An objection does not suspend enforcement of the consumer arbitration committee’s decision. However, upon request, the judge may suspend enforcement of the consumer arbitration committee’s decision by way of an interim measure. … (5) The decision rendered by the consumer court upon an objection to decisions of the consumer arbitration committee is final.”

Consumer courts have jurisdiction over actions concerning disputes arising from consumer transactions and practices directed at consumers (Law No. 6502 art. 73/1). Venue in an objection action is not exclusive: the 5th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation held that, where the court had declined venue of its own motion but the defendant had not objected to venue, the dispute had to be decided by the consumer court in which the action was first filed, relying on Code of Civil Procedure art. 19/4, under which, in actions where venue is not exclusive, the court in which the action was filed becomes competent if the defendant does not raise a venue objection in time (Court of Cassation 5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5656, K. 2024/9689).

The decision of the consumer court on an objection is final (Law No. 6502 art. 70/5); the Court of Cassation likewise held that no appeal lies against such a decision (Court of Cassation 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2017/154, K. 2017/521). The provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply to disputes within the committee’s remit or to objections to committee decisions, so no separate application to a mediator is required for an objection action (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A/1).

Court of Cassation Decisions

Application to the committee is a condition of action for disputes within the threshold

Court of Cassation Decision
13th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/9334, K. 2016/14097, 01.06.2016 (the Chamber’s reversal reasoning)

“Under this article, application to district consumer arbitration committees is mandatory for disputes valued below two thousand Turkish Lira … This is a condition of action, to be taken into account by the consumer courts of their own motion. … An action for annulment of an objection may not be brought before the consumer court without applying to the consumer arbitration committee. This being so, while the court should have dismissed the claim on procedural grounds for want of a condition of action without entering into the merits of the dispute … rendering judgment by entering into the merits of the case is contrary to procedure and law and requires reversal.” (translation)

The monetary thresholds applied in the decision (TRY 2,000/3,000) belong to the text in force on the date of the action; under the current text of Law No. 6502 art. 68/1 the threshold is thirty thousand Turkish Lira, increased every year under Law No. 6502 art. 68/4. The mandatory application rule remains in the current text.

The consumer court’s decision on an objection is final

Court of Cassation Decision
13th Civil Chamber, E. 2017/154, K. 2017/521, 24.01.2017 (the Chamber’s reasoning; the appeal petition was rejected)

“Article 70/5 of the same Law states that “the decision to be rendered by the consumer court upon an objection to decisions of the consumer arbitration committee is final”. It appears from the file that, by reason of the amount in dispute, the dispute fell within the remit of the consumer arbitration committee and that, under article 70/5 of the aforementioned Law, the judgment rendered by the consumer court upon an objection to the consumer arbitration committee’s decision is final; therefore no appeal lies against a final court decision.” (translation)

Annulment for the committee’s lack of competence does not rest on an exclusive-venue rule

Court of Cassation Decision
13th Civil Chamber, E. 2019/4481, K. 2019/13157, 25.12.2019 (the Chamber’s reasoning; the judgment was reversed in the interest of the law, without effect on the outcome)

“In the present case, the court annulled the decision of the İnönü Consumer Arbitration Committee on the ground that it lacked competence. This is not a decision rendered on the basis of the exclusive-venue rule set out in sub-paragraph (ç) of article 114 of the Code of Civil Procedure. Moreover, the file contains no information or document showing that the plaintiff raised the objection to the committee’s lack of competence before that same committee; it is understood that the objection to competence was raised before the court. While the court should have examined the merits of the case and decided accordingly, deciding as it did is contrary to procedure and law, and the Chief Public Prosecutor’s request for reversal in the interest of the law must be granted.” (translation)

The provincial committee’s competence after the 2018 restructuring

Court of Cassation Decision
3rd Civil Chamber, E. 2021/3990, K. 2021/7144, 23.06.2021 (the Chamber’s reasoning; the judgment was reversed in the interest of the law, without effect on the outcome)

“… in the Province of Zonguldak, which does not have metropolitan-municipality status, the authority to decide the application made by the defendant consumer on 18/12/2018 belongs to the Zonguldak Provincial Consumer Arbitration Committee. This being so, while the court should have examined the merits of the case and rendered judgment according to the outcome, having regard to the provincial consumer arbitration committee’s competence to decide the defendant consumer’s application, rendering judgment as it did through a mistaken assessment and incomplete examination is contrary to procedure and law, and the Ministry of Justice’s well-founded request for reversal in the interest of the law must be granted.” (translation)

The decision shows that, after the Ministry of Trade’s 2018 restructuring, the provincial consumer arbitration committee in a province without metropolitan-municipality status was competent to decide applications made to the district governorates in that province as well, and that failure to take this into account was a ground for reversal in the interest of the law.

Venue in an objection action is not exclusive (determination of the competent court)

Court of Cassation Decision
5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5656, K. 2024/9689, 09.12.2024 (the Chamber’s reasoning; final decision determining the competent court)

“It appears from the file that venue is not exclusive in an action seeking annulment of a consumer arbitration committee’s decision, that the action was filed in the Konya 2nd Consumer Court, that the court declined venue of its own motion, but that the defendant did not raise a venue objection; the dispute must therefore be resolved by the Konya 2nd Consumer Court, being the court where the action was first filed.” (translation)

The decision is a determination of the competent court in a venue dispute between the Konya and Mersin consumer courts, rendered under Code of Civil Procedure arts. 21-22 and Law No. 5235 art. 36/3. The text of Law No. 6502 art. 70/3 quoted in the decision gives the objection period as “fifteen days”; the current text of the article in the Official Legislation Information System gives it as “two weeks” (see the statutory box above).

Mandatory mediation before consumer court actions was held constitutional; the obligation is limited to applying to a mediator

Constitutional Court Decision (constitutional review)
Anayasa Mahkemesi, E. 2020/73, K. 2023/181, T. 26.10.2023

“…this obligation is limited to applying to mediation, and mediation is a process over whose functioning and outcome the will of the parties prevails. The parties may end the process whenever they wish and are free to decide whether or not to reach an agreement at its end. If no agreement is reached, recourse to the courts for the resolution of the dispute is possible. … An action dismissed on procedural grounds for want of the precondition because mediation was not sought may also be brought again once that deficiency has been remedied.” (unofficial translation)

In an action for annulment brought by members of parliament, the Court reviewed the words “…is a precondition to suit.” in the first sentence of Article 73/A(1) of Law No. 6502, added by Article 59 of Law No. 7251, under Articles 13 and 36 of the Constitution. It held that the requirement to apply to a mediator before suing in the consumer courts has a statutory basis and a legitimate aim, and that the resulting restriction on the right of access to a court is proportionate. In assessing proportionality it took into account that mediation must be completed within four weeks at most, including in compelling cases; that limitation periods are suspended and time limits for forfeiture do not run from the application until the final minutes (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A(9) and (15)); that the rule making a party who fails to attend the first meeting liable for the costs of the later action does not apply against the consumer (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A(2)); that in the listed cases the mediation fee payable by the consumer is met from the Ministry of Justice budget (art. 73/A(3)); and that there are exceptions, such as disputes within the jurisdiction of the consumer arbitration committee and objections to its decisions. The request to annul these words was dismissed unanimously. A separate provision reviewed in the same decision, unrelated to mediation (the words on the “overriding interest worthy of protection” of persons concerned, permitting closed hearings under Code of Civil Procedure art. 28(2)), was annulled by majority. The decision was published in the Official Gazette of 22.12.2023, No. 32407, and concerns the text of Article 73/A as introduced by Law No. 7251.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is application to the consumer arbitration committee mandatory?

Without prejudice to the parties’ rights under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, application to the consumer arbitration committee is mandatory for disputes valued below the monetary threshold set by the Law and increased every year by the revaluation rate (Law No. 6502 art. 68/1 and 68/4); the Court of Cassation treated this requirement as a condition of action to be examined by the consumer court of its own motion (Court of Cassation 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/9334, K. 2016/14097). No application can be made to the committee for disputes above the threshold; in such disputes, applying to a mediator before filing suit in the consumer court is, as a rule, a condition of action (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A). The current year’s amount should be confirmed from official sources before applying.

Where, and within what time limit, must an objection to the committee’s decision be filed?

The parties may object, within two weeks of service of the decision, before the consumer court of the place where the consumer arbitration committee or the consumer’s domicile is located (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3). The Court of Cassation has accepted that venue in an objection action is not exclusive (Court of Cassation 5th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/5656, K. 2024/9689).

Does filing an objection automatically suspend enforcement of the decision?

No. An objection does not suspend enforcement of the consumer arbitration committee’s decision; however, upon request, the judge may suspend enforcement by way of an interim measure (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3).

Can a consumer arbitration committee decision be enforced directly?

Yes. Consumer arbitration committee decisions are enforced in accordance with the provisions of the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law on the enforcement of judgments (Law No. 6502 art. 70/2); an objection to the decision does not automatically suspend enforcement either (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3).

Can the consumer court’s decision on an objection be challenged further?

No. The decision of the consumer court on an objection is final (Law No. 6502 art. 70/5). The Court of Cassation likewise held that no appeal lies against such a decision (Court of Cassation 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2017/154, K. 2017/521).

What happens if an action is filed directly, without applying to the committee?

Where a dispute falls within the threshold that makes application mandatory and an action is filed without applying to the committee, according to the Court of Cassation the action must be dismissed on procedural grounds for want of a condition of action, without examining the merits (Court of Cassation 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2016/9334, K. 2016/14097). The provisions on mediation as a condition of action do not apply to disputes within the committee’s remit (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A/1).
