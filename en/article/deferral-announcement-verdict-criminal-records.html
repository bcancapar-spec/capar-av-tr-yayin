Deferred Verdict (HAGB) and Criminal Records in Türkiye: Conditions, Supervision, Appeal, Erasure of Records

Short answer: Under Turkish law, the announcement of the verdict may be deferred (HAGB) where the sentence imposed is imprisonment of two years or less or a judicial fine. This also requires that the defendant has no prior conviction for an intentional offence, the court is convinced that he or she will not reoffend and the material damage caused by the offence has been fully compensated. The judgment then produces no legal consequence for the defendant, except for provisions on confiscation (CCrP art. 231/5-6). The defendant is subject to a five-year supervision period: if no new intentional offence is committed and the obligations are complied with, the judgment is set aside and the case is dismissed; otherwise the court announces the verdict (CCrP art. 231/8 and 231/10-11). A HAGB decision is recorded not in the criminal record but in a dedicated system, and may be requested only by a public prosecutor, judge or court in connection with an investigation or prosecution (CCrP art. 231/13). HAGB decisions rendered on or after 1.6.2024 are subject to appeal (istinaf), while the objection procedure continues for decisions rendered before that date (CCrP art. 231/12 and provisional art. 6/2). The criminal record of a final conviction, by contrast, is transferred to the archive in cases such as completion of enforcement, and the archive record is, as a rule, fully erased after five years (Law No. 5352 arts. 9 and 12).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

Deferral of the announcement of the verdict under Code of Criminal Procedure art. 231: the two-year limit, absence of a prior conviction for an intentional offence, compensation of material damage and payment in instalments, the court’s conviction and excluded offences; the five-year supervision period and obligations; dismissal or announcement of the verdict; the appeal (istinaf) introduced by Law No. 7499 and the 1.6.2024 transitional rule; recording of HAGB in a dedicated system rather than the criminal record; transfer of criminal records to the archive and the deletion periods in Criminal Records Law art. 12; restoration of forfeited rights (art. 13/A), with verified decisions of the Criminal General Assembly and the 1st, 4th and 10th Criminal Chambers of the Court of Cassation.

A guide to the deferral of the announcement of the verdict (HAGB) and criminal records under Turkish law: conditions and legal effect of HAGB (Law No. 5271 art. 231/5-7), compensation of damage and payment in instalments (Law No. 5271 art. 231/9), the five-year supervision period (Law No. 5271 art. 231/8), dismissal of the case or announcement of the verdict (Law No. 5271 art. 231/10-11), appeal (istinaf) and transitional rules (Law No. 5271 art. 231/12 and Law No. 5271 provisional art. 6), why a HAGB decision is not entered in the criminal record (Law No. 5271 art. 231/13 and Law No. 5352 art. 6), transfer of criminal records to the archive and their erasure (Law No. 5352 art. 9 and Law No. 5352 art. 12), restoration of forfeited rights (Law No. 5352 art. 13/A), with verified decisions of the Criminal General Assembly and criminal chambers of the Turkish Court of Cassation.

What Is HAGB? Nature and Legal Effect (CCrP art. 231/5-7)

The deferral of the announcement of the verdict (hükmün açıklanmasının geri bırakılması, HAGB) means that a conviction rendered at the end of the trial is, under statutory conditions, not announced but left subject to a supervision period. Under the Code, HAGB means that, except for provisions on confiscation, the judgment rendered produces no legal consequence for the defendant; the provisions on settlement are reserved (Law No. 5271 art. 231/5). In a judgment whose announcement is deferred, the prison sentence may not be suspended and, if short, may not be converted into alternative sanctions (Law No. 5271 art. 231/7).

A person subject to HAGB has been tried and a judgment has been rendered, but the judgment is not announced during the supervision period. If the period is completed in compliance with the obligations, the judgment is set aside and the case is dismissed; otherwise the judgment is announced. A HAGB decision is not recorded in the criminal record but in a dedicated system (Law No. 5271 art. 231/13).

Statutory Provision — Code of Criminal Procedure (Law No. 5271) art. 231/5-6 (Deferral of the announcement of the verdict and its conditions) (unofficial translation)

“(5) Where the penalty imposed at the end of the trial for the offence attributed to the defendant is imprisonment of two years or less or a judicial fine, the court may decide to defer the announcement of the verdict. The provisions on settlement are reserved. Deferral of the announcement of the verdict means that, except for provisions on confiscation, the judgment rendered produces no legal consequence for the defendant. (6) For a decision to defer the announcement of the verdict, it is necessary that: a) the defendant has not previously been convicted of an intentional offence, b) the court is convinced, having regard to the defendant’s personal characteristics and conduct at the hearing, that the defendant will not commit another offence, c) the damage suffered by the victim or the public as a result of the offence has been fully compensated by restitution, restoration to the pre-offence state or payment of compensation.”

Conditions of HAGB: Sentence Limit, Criminal Record, Compensation and the Court’s Conviction

Sentence limit. The penalty imposed must be imprisonment of two years or less or a judicial fine (Law No. 5271 art. 231/5).

No previous conviction for an intentional offence. The defendant must not have previously been convicted of an intentional offence (Law No. 5271 art. 231/6-a). The Court of Cassation accepts that a conviction erased from the criminal record, transferred to the archive and eligible for deletion upon expiry of the five-year period does not bar HAGB (decision below).

Compensation of damage. The damage suffered by the victim or the public must be fully compensated by restitution, restoration to the pre-offence state or payment of compensation (Law No. 5271 art. 231/6-c). According to the Criminal General Assembly, the damage concerned is material damage; non-pecuniary damage is excluded, the damage may also be compensated by third parties with the defendant’s knowledge and consent, and the condition is not required for offences incapable of causing damage. If the damage cannot be compensated immediately, HAGB may also be ordered on condition that it is fully paid in monthly instalments during the supervision period (Law No. 5271 art. 231/9); the General Assembly links this possibility to cases where, despite a genuine intention to pay, the damage cannot be paid at once because of its size and the offender’s financial situation (decision below).

The court’s conviction. The court must be convinced, having regard to the defendant’s personal characteristics and conduct at the hearing, that they will not reoffend (Law No. 5271 art. 231/6-b).

Excluded offences. The HAGB provisions do not apply to the offences of torture and torment and to offences committed by public officials because of their duty that may amount to ill-treatment, as listed in Law No. 5271 art. 231/14. Special laws may also exclude HAGB; for example, HAGB does not apply to the offence of issuing a dishonoured cheque (Law No. 5941 art. 5/10).

Consent of the defendant. The current text of Law No. 5271 art. 231 contains no requirement of the defendant’s consent. Law No. 5271 provisional art. 6/2 provides that the requirement of the defendant’s consent continues to apply to HAGB decisions rendered before 1.6.2024.

The Five-Year Supervision Period and Obligations (CCrP art. 231/8-9)

A defendant subject to HAGB is placed under a supervision period of five years. Within this period, for a duration determined by the court not exceeding one year, obligations such as attending a training programme, working under supervision for a wage, or being prohibited from or required to attend certain places may be imposed as probation measures. The limitation period for prosecution is suspended during the supervision period, and no further HAGB decision may be rendered for an intentional offence within that period (Law No. 5271 art. 231/8). For HAGB decisions concerning children, the supervision period is three years (Law No. 5395 art. 23/1).

Statutory Provision — Code of Criminal Procedure (Law No. 5271) art. 231/8 (Supervision period) (unofficial translation)

“(8) Where a decision deferring the announcement of the verdict is rendered, the defendant shall be subject to a supervision period of five years. Within the supervision period, no further decision deferring the announcement of the verdict may be rendered against the person for an intentional offence. Within this period, for a duration determined by the court not exceeding one year, it may be decided, as a probation measure, that the defendant: a) if without a profession or trade, attend a training programme in order to acquire one, b) if having a profession or trade, be employed for a wage in a public institution or privately under the supervision of another person practising the same profession or trade, c) be prohibited from going to certain places, be required to attend certain places, or fulfil another obligation to be determined. The limitation period for prosecution is suspended during the supervision period.”

End of the Supervision Period: Dismissal or Announcement of the Verdict?

If no new intentional offence is committed during the supervision period and the probation obligations are complied with, the deferred judgment is set aside and the case is dismissed (Law No. 5271 art. 231/10). If, however, a new intentional offence is committed or the obligations are breached during the supervision period, the court announces the judgment; assessing the situation of a defendant unable to fulfil the obligations, it may also render a new conviction ordering that up to half of the sentence not be executed or, where the conditions are met, suspending the prison sentence or converting it into alternative sanctions (Law No. 5271 art. 231/11).

Statutory Provision — Code of Criminal Procedure (Law No. 5271) art. 231/10-11 (Dismissal and announcement of the verdict) (unofficial translation)

“(10) Where no new offence is committed intentionally during the supervision period and the obligations of the probation measure are complied with, the judgment whose announcement was deferred shall be set aside and a decision dismissing the case shall be rendered. (11) Where the defendant intentionally commits a new offence during the supervision period or acts contrary to the obligations of the probation measure, the court shall announce the judgment. However, assessing the situation of a defendant unable to fulfil the obligations imposed, the court may render a new conviction by deciding that a part of the sentence, up to half, as determined by it, not be executed or, where the conditions exist, that the prison sentence in the judgment be suspended or converted into alternative sanctions. An objection may be lodged against the judgment announced or newly rendered. The objection authority may conduct an assessment limited to the conditions in this paragraph.”

Since the Code refers to a new offence committed “intentionally”, a negligent offence committed during the supervision period does not lead to the announcement of the judgment. When announcing the judgment, the court must also take into account favourable provisions that entered into force in the meantime: the 4th Criminal Chamber found it unlawful to announce, unchanged, a judgment for insult that had subsequently been brought within the scope of prepayment, without first giving the defendant a prepayment notice (decision below).

Objection or Appeal against a HAGB Decision? (CCrP art. 231/12)

The former system: objection. For a long time, HAGB decisions were subject to an objection (itiraz). As recounted by the Criminal General Assembly, the Constitutional Court annulled the provision on the ground that the objection did not provide effective review, after which Law No. 7499 introduced an appeal (istinaf) against HAGB decisions (decision below).

The current system: appeal. Subject to Law No. 5271 art. 272/3, an appeal (istinaf) may be lodged against a HAGB decision; Law No. 5271 art. 286 applies to decisions of the regional courts of appeal. Where the HAGB decision is rendered by a regional court of appeal or the Court of Cassation acting as a first-instance court, an appeal on points of law (temyiz) is available. On appeal, the decision and the judgment are reviewed for procedural and substantive unlawfulness (Law No. 5271 art. 231/12).

Statutory Provision — Code of Criminal Procedure (Law No. 5271) art. 231/12-14 (Legal remedy, recording and exceptions) (unofficial translation)

“(12) Subject to the provisions of the third paragraph of article 272, an appeal (istinaf) may be lodged against a decision deferring the announcement of the verdict. Article 286 applies to decisions rendered by the regional court of appeal. Subject to the provisions of the third paragraph of article 272, where the decision deferring the announcement of the verdict is rendered by the regional court of appeal or the Court of Cassation acting as a court of first instance, an appeal on points of law may be lodged. In the appeal and the appeal on points of law, the decision and the judgment shall be reviewed for procedural and substantive unlawfulness. (13) The decision deferring the announcement of the verdict shall be recorded in a system dedicated to such decisions. These records may be used for the purpose specified in this article only where requested by the public prosecutor, the judge or the court in connection with an investigation or prosecution. (14) The provisions of this article on deferral of the announcement of the verdict shall not apply to the offences of torture and torment and to offences committed by public officials because of their duty that may be regarded as ill-treatment within the scope of article 17 of the Constitution.”

Transitional rule. Under Law No. 5271 provisional art. 6/2, the changes to the legal remedy apply to HAGB decisions rendered on or after 1.6.2024; for HAGB decisions rendered before that date the objection procedure continues. If an earlier HAGB decision is later announced, the rules on legal remedies in force before the amendment apply. The Criminal General Assembly further held, by majority, that in a case reviewed on appeal and reversed before the regional courts of appeal began to operate, the remedy against a HAGB decision rendered after the reversal is an appeal on points of law (temyiz).

Against the announced judgment. An objection may be lodged against a judgment announced or newly rendered after a breach of the supervision period; the objection authority may conduct an assessment limited to the conditions in Law No. 5271 art. 231/11.

Is HAGB Entered in the Criminal Record?

No. A HAGB decision is recorded not in the criminal record but in a system dedicated to such decisions, and these records may be used only where requested by the public prosecutor, the judge or the court in connection with an investigation or prosecution (Law No. 5271 art. 231/13). The Criminal Records Law likewise provides that decisions on the deferral of public prosecution and on HAGB are recorded only in order to be provided where requested by the court, the judge or the chief public prosecutor’s office in connection with an investigation or prosecution (Law No. 5352 art. 6/1).

By contrast, criminal record information may be provided, with the purpose of use stated, to the person concerned or their attorney and to public institutions and organisations (Law No. 5352 art. 7). The Law does not provide for HAGB records to be given in this way to the person concerned or to public institutions. A similar regime applies to decisions of non-prosecution and dismissal issued upon prepayment (Law No. 5237 art. 75/7). This effect of HAGB depends on the supervision period not being breached: if the judgment is announced and the conviction becomes final, it is entered in the criminal record as a final conviction (Law No. 5352 art. 4).

Can a Criminal Record Be Erased? Criminal Record and Archive Record

What is commonly called a “criminal record” is the criminal record entry under the Criminal Records Law. It disappears in two stages:

  • From the criminal record to the archive: upon completion of the execution of the penalty or security measure, withdrawal of the complaint or effective repentance eliminating the conviction with all its consequences, expiry of the limitation period for execution, or a general amnesty, the information in the criminal record is deleted and transferred to the archive record; upon the death of the person concerned it is deleted entirely (Law No. 5352 art. 9).
  • Complete deletion from the archive: for convictions causing a forfeiture of rights under Constitution art. 76 and laws other than the Penal Code, the record is deleted entirely fifteen years after the conditions for archiving arose, provided that a decision restoring forfeited rights has been obtained, or thirty years later without such a decision; for other convictions it is deleted after five years (Law No. 5352 art. 12/1).
  • Immediate deletion: if the act is decriminalised by law, the related criminal record and archive entries are deleted without any request; if an acquittal or a decision of no penalty rendered after a reversal in the interest of the law or a retrial becomes final, the records of the earlier conviction are deleted entirely (Law No. 5352 art. 12/2-3).
Statutory Provision — Criminal Records Law (Law No. 5352) art. 9/1-2 (Deletion of criminal record information) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Information in the criminal record shall be deleted by the Directorate General for Criminal Records and Statistics and transferred to the archive record in the event of: a) completion of the execution of the penalty or security measure, b) withdrawal of the complaint or effective repentance eliminating the criminal conviction with all its consequences, c) expiry of the limitation period for the execution of the penalty, d) general amnesty. (2) Criminal record information shall be deleted entirely upon the death of the person concerned.”

Statutory Provision — Criminal Records Law (Law No. 5352) art. 12/1-3 (Deletion of criminal record and archive information) (unofficial translation)

“(1) Archive information shall be deleted entirely: a) upon the death of the person concerned, b) for convictions causing a forfeiture of rights under article 76 of the Constitution and laws other than the Turkish Penal Code, from the date on which the conditions for transferring the record to the archive arose: 1. upon the lapse of fifteen years, provided that a decision restoring forfeited rights has been obtained, 2. upon the lapse of thirty years, without the requirement of a decision restoring forfeited rights, c) for other convictions, upon the lapse of five years from the date on which the conditions for transferring the record to the archive arose. (2) Where the act is decriminalised by law, the criminal record and archive entries concerning convictions for that offence shall be deleted entirely without any request. (3) Where an acquittal or a decision of no penalty rendered as a result of a reversal in the interest of the law or a retrial becomes final, the criminal record and archive entries concerning the earlier conviction shall be deleted entirely.”

According to the 1st Criminal Chamber, for convictions to a judicial fine the condition for archiving arises upon execution, and the deletion period runs from the date of archiving; a conviction eligible for deletion does not bar a HAGB decision in a new case (decision below).

Who may request archive records? Archive information may be requested, with the purpose of use stated, by the person concerned or their attorney, by chief public prosecutors’ offices, judges or courts within an investigation or prosecution, by competent electoral boards, and by the relevant public institutions where provided by special laws (Law No. 5352 art. 10/1). Unless expressly provided by law, the criminal record and archive entries of a person’s relative may not be requested or used as a basis for depriving the person of any right (Law No. 5352 art. 10/2). Individuals may query their own criminal record and archive entries using secure identity verification tools (Law No. 5352 art. 13/2).

Restoration of Forfeited Rights

The forfeiture of rights that is the legal consequence of a prison sentence for an intentional offence lasts until the execution of the prison sentence is completed (Law No. 5237 art. 53/2). By contrast, forfeitures of rights attached to a particular offence or penalty by laws other than the Penal Code are removed through the restoration of forfeited rights (memnu hakların iadesi) (Law No. 5352 art. 13/A). The 10th Criminal Chamber holds that such forfeitures arising from special laws do not end automatically upon execution (decision below).

Conditions. Three years must have elapsed since the execution of the sentence was completed, and the court must be convinced that the person has not committed a new offence during this period and has lived with good conduct; Law No. 5237 art. 53/5-6 is reserved. Where the execution ended for a legal reason other than a general amnesty or effective repentance, five years must have elapsed since the judgment became final, and this period may not be shorter than the prison sentence plus three years. Upon request, the court that rendered the judgment or a court of the same degree at the convicted person’s domicile decides on the file or after hearing the public prosecutor and the convicted person; the remedies under the Code of Criminal Procedure are available, and the final decision is entered in the criminal record archive (Law No. 5352 art. 13/A). For convictions causing a forfeiture of rights, a decision restoring forfeited rights also allows the archive record to be deleted after fifteen instead of thirty years (Law No. 5352 art. 12/1).

Statutory Provision — Criminal Records Law (Law No. 5352) art. 13/A (Restoration of forfeited rights) (unofficial translation)

“(1) In order to remove forfeitures of rights attached by laws other than the Turkish Penal Code No. 5237 to conviction for a particular offence or to a particular penalty, the restoration of forfeited rights may be sought. For this purpose, subject to the fifth and sixth paragraphs of article 53 of the Turkish Penal Code, it is necessary that: a) a period of three years has elapsed since the date on which the execution of the sentence was completed, b) the court is convinced that the person has not committed a new offence during this period and has lived with good conduct. (2) Where the execution of the sentence has been ended for a legal reason other than a general amnesty or effective repentance, five years must have elapsed since the judgment became final in order to seek the restoration of forfeited rights. However, this period may not be shorter than the period found by adding three years to the prison sentence imposed. (3) For the restoration of forfeited rights, a decision must be rendered, upon the request of the convicted person or their attorney, by the court that rendered the judgment or a court of the same degree at the convicted person’s domicile.”

Court of Cassation Decisions

The compensation condition concerns material damage; instalments depend on a genuine intention to pay

Court of Cassation Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2012/1315, K. 2012/1871, 25.12.2012 (reasoning of the Criminal General Assembly; the objection was unanimously dismissed in respect of a defendant who showed no intention to compensate)

“The damage meant here is material damage; non-pecuniary damage should not be considered within this scope. The material damage need not be compensated by the defendant personally; it may also be compensated by third parties on the defendant’s behalf, with their knowledge and consent, through payment, restitution or restoration. However, this condition will not be required for offences that cause no damage or are incapable of causing damage. … it is also possible to defer the announcement of the verdict by ordering that the damage be paid in instalments within the supervision period. However, this possibility … may arise where, despite the defendant’s genuine intention to compensate the damage in full, the damage cannot be met at once because the amount is so large as to make immediate payment impossible and because of reasons such as the offender’s financial situation.” (translation)

An old conviction eligible for deletion does not bar HAGB

Court of Cassation Decision
1st Criminal Chamber, E. 2023/5863, K. 2023/6991, 20.11.2023 (reversal in the interest of the law; the Chamber’s reasoning)

“for convictions to a judicial fine, the condition for transfer to the archive arises upon execution, and the deletion period is calculated from the date of transfer to the archive. … it has been determined that, since five years have elapsed since the execution of the judgment in the criminal record, the conditions for deletion under article 12(1)(c) of Law No. 5352 have arisen and that, in this state, it does not constitute an obstacle to the deferral of the announcement of the verdict.” (translation)

When the judgment is announced, favourable later provisions (prepayment) must be assessed

Court of Cassation Decision
4th Criminal Chamber, E. 2026/1798, K. 2026/7004, 14.04.2026 (reversal in the interest of the law; the Chamber’s reasoning)

“Although in the case brought against the defendant for insult (articles 125/1-2-4 of Law No. 5237) a decision deferring the announcement of the verdict was rendered on 25.03.2021, and upon notification that the defendant had committed an intentional offence during the supervision period the judgment was announced unchanged on 12.06.2025; … announcing the judgment unchanged without considering that the offence charged was subject to prepayment and that the court was obliged to give the defendant a prepayment notice in due form and to assess the defendant’s legal situation accordingly was found unlawful.” (translation)

The remedy against HAGB decisions rendered after 1.6.2024 is an appeal (istinaf)

Court of Cassation Decision
Criminal General Assembly, E. 2025/169, K. 2025/381, 08.10.2025 (majority reasoning of the Criminal General Assembly; in the specific case it was held by majority that the remedy against a HAGB decision rendered after a reversal is an appeal on points of law)

“With these new provisions introduced by Law No. 7499, which entered into force on 12.03.2024, it was provided that decisions deferring the announcement of the verdict, previously subject to objection, would be subject to appeal (istinaf) if rendered on or after 01.06.2024. By the same provisions, it was also provided that the remedy against decisions deferring the announcement of the verdict rendered on or after 01.06.2024 by a regional court of appeal or the Court of Cassation acting as a first-instance court is an appeal on points of law, thereby opening an appeal on points of law against decisions deferring the announcement of the verdict, which are not regarded as judgments in our law. … it must be accepted that the remedy available against the decision deferring the announcement of the verdict is also an appeal on points of law.” (translation)

Forfeitures under special laws are removed only by the restoration of forfeited rights

Court of Cassation Decision
10th Criminal Chamber, E. 2020/20399, K. 2022/9396, 21.09.2022 (reversal in the interest of the law; the Chamber’s reasoning)

“Execution of the sentence does not mean the convicted person’s release from prison on conditional release. It means the execution of the entire sentence in the conviction, including completion of the supervision period outside by the conditionally released person. … such forfeitures of rights attached to prison sentences by special laws do not end automatically under article 53/2, since they do not arise from article 53/1 of Law No. 5237, even where the conviction concerns offences in Law No. 5237, and these forfeitures arising from special laws can be removed only through the institution of the restoration of forfeited rights regulated in article 13/A of Law No. 5352” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Is HAGB entered in the criminal record?

No. A HAGB decision is recorded in a dedicated system and may be requested only by the public prosecutor, the judge or the court in connection with an investigation or prosecution (Law No. 5271 art. 231/13; Law No. 5352 art. 6/1). If the supervision period is breached and the judgment is announced and becomes final, the conviction is entered in the criminal record.

How long is the HAGB supervision period?

Five years; the limitation period for prosecution is suspended during this period, and probation obligations not exceeding one year may be imposed (Law No. 5271 art. 231/8). For HAGB decisions concerning children, the supervision period is three years (Law No. 5395 art. 23/1).

Is a HAGB decision challenged by objection or by appeal?

An appeal (istinaf) lies against HAGB decisions rendered on or after 1.6.2024; if the decision was rendered by a regional court of appeal or the Court of Cassation acting as a first-instance court, an appeal on points of law is available (Law No. 5271 art. 231/12). The objection procedure continues for HAGB decisions rendered before that date (Law No. 5271 provisional art. 6/2).

What happens if a new offence is committed during the supervision period?

If a new intentional offence is committed or the obligations are breached during the supervision period, the court announces the judgment; for a defendant unable to fulfil the obligations, it may also order that part of the sentence not be executed, suspend it or convert it into an alternative sanction (Law No. 5271 art. 231/11). If no new intentional offence is committed and the obligations are complied with, the case is dismissed (Law No. 5271 art. 231/10).

When is a criminal record erased?

Upon execution of the sentence, expiry of the limitation period for execution, a general amnesty, or withdrawal of the complaint or effective repentance eliminating the conviction with all its consequences, the entry is deleted from the criminal record and transferred to the archive (Law No. 5352 art. 9). The archive record is deleted entirely after five years for other convictions, and for convictions causing a forfeiture of rights under special laws after fifteen years with a decision restoring forfeited rights or thirty years without it (Law No. 5352 art. 12/1).

How long must one wait for the restoration of forfeited rights?

As a rule, three years must have elapsed since the execution of the sentence was completed, with no new offence and good conduct during that time; if execution ended for a legal reason other than a general amnesty or effective repentance, five years from finality are required (Law No. 5352 art. 13/A). According to the Court of Cassation, execution means execution of the entire sentence, including the supervision period of conditional release.
