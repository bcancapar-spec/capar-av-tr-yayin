Bank Acceleration of Commercial Loans in Türkiye: Acceleration Clauses, Grace Periods, Wrongful Acceleration

Short answer: A bank may accelerate (kat) a commercial loan before maturity if one of the events of default listed in the general loan agreement has occurred. Such events include an unpaid or irregularly paid instalment, cessation of business, or bankruptcy proceedings brought against the borrower. Once the loan account is accelerated, the loan debt becomes due (muaccel) (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/314, K. 2024/3941, 15.05.2024). Acceleration and default clauses are subject to review as standard terms (genel işlem koşulları) even against merchants; the Chamber reversed a decision given without reviewing these clauses under arts. 20 et seq. of the Turkish Code of Obligations (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4499, K. 2022/8102, 21.11.2022). If the bank grants a period for payment (atıfet) in the acceleration notice, in the 2021–2025 decisions of the 11th Civil Chamber enforcement started before that period expires was held contrary to the good-faith rule (Turkish Civil Code art. 2) and the dismissal of the action for annulment of objection was upheld; the Assembly of Civil Chambers, however, held in 2017 that this is relevant only to the calculation of interest, and the issue remains disputed (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/815, K. 2017/1007, 24.05.2017). If the acceleration is wrongful, the borrower may object to the enforcement proceedings, bring a negative declaratory action or an action for recovery and claim back excess interest; an acceleration made in accordance with the agreement and the regulations does not give rise to damages (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6186, K. 2024/7492, 23.10.2024).

This article explains Turkish law. It is an English translation of the Turkish original; in case of any discrepancy, the Turkish text prevails.

In brief

A Turkish bank may accelerate a commercial loan before maturity if an event of default in the loan agreement occurs, such as an unpaid instalment. The article covers standard-terms review of acceleration clauses for merchants, regularly serviced loans, enforcement started before the grace period expires and remedies for wrongful acceleration, with Court of Cassation decisions.

Acceleration of a commercial loan before maturity under Turkish law: acceleration (muacceliyet) clauses in general loan agreements, review of standard terms against merchants (Code of Obligations arts. 20–25), the good-faith rule, regularly serviced loans, the grace period (atıfet süresi) in the acceleration notice, objection, negative declaratory action, refund and damages for wrongful acceleration, and decisions of the 11th Civil Chamber and the Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation.

What Do Acceleration of a Loan and an Acceleration Clause Mean?

Acceleration of a loan (kredinin kat edilmesi) means that the bank, relying on its power under the general loan agreement (genel kredi sözleşmesi), closes the loan account and makes the remaining debt, including instalments not yet due, immediately payable (muaccel). Agreements usually set out this power in clauses headed “acceleration” or “default”; for example, they provide that if the debt is not paid in full on its due date, the customer falls into default without any need for protest, notice or a grace period and must immediately pay all debts that have become due (Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal, 43rd Civil Chamber, E. 2020/190, K. 2021/284, 11.03.2021).

According to the 11th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay), the debt becomes due once the loan account is accelerated; no separate notice to the sureties is required for this (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/314, K. 2024/3941, 15.05.2024). Maturity and default are, however, distinct concepts: the debtor of a debt that has fallen due goes into default upon the creditor’s demand (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 117/1). The 21st Civil Chamber of the Ankara Regional Court of Appeal (Bölge Adliye Mahkemesi) likewise took the end of the three-day period granted in the acceleration notice as the date of default (Ankara Regional Court of Appeal, 21st Civil Chamber, E. 2021/1455, K. 2024/53, 17.01.2024). The content and service of the notice are discussed in the article on the account closure notice, and the surety’s default in the article on the surety’s liability for default interest. For acceleration and enforcement issues in foreign-currency or currency-indexed loans, see our article on acceleration and exchange rates in foreign-currency loans.

In Which Cases May a Bank Accelerate a Loan Before Maturity?

In the decisions, the lawfulness of acceleration is assessed by asking whether the event of default listed in the agreement actually occurred in the case at hand. The most common event is non-payment of instalments. The 11th Civil Chamber upheld a decision accepting that, when instalments are not paid, the entire remaining debt may become due under the agreement and that acceleration for the whole outstanding loan balance is not unlawful (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5065, K. 2026/2229, 21.04.2026; upheld). In a case where an acceleration notice was sent after the 31st and 32nd instalments of a loan guaranteed by the Credit Guarantee Fund (Kredi Garanti Fonu, KGF) went unpaid, it was also accepted that neither the KGF protocol nor the regulation on the classification of loans prevents an acceleration notice from being sent before a certain date (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6186, K. 2024/7492, 23.10.2024; upheld).

Acceleration is also possible where payments have not stopped but are irregular. The 43rd Civil Chamber of the Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal held lawful the acceleration of a 36-instalment loan whose borrower had paid the first 27 instalments, but irregularly, and had paid the instalment due on 25.10.2016, the last before acceleration, on 08.11.2016 (Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal, 43rd Civil Chamber, E. 2020/190, K. 2021/284, 11.03.2021; final). Grounds other than payment may also be relied on. The 11th Civil Chamber upheld a decision finding that a bank which, doubting collection after the borrower company ceased its jewellery business and an overdue debt remained unpaid, accelerated the loan had not acted contrary to the agreement or to good faith (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/5458, K. 2022/2601, 30.03.2022; upheld). In another case, the conditions for acceleration were held to have been met through events listed in a supplementary agreement, such as bankruptcy proceedings started against the borrower by third parties and the borrower’s application for postponement of bankruptcy (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/5, K. 2024/2573, 28.03.2024; upheld).

Is an Acceleration Clause Subject to Standard-Terms Review Against a Merchant?

Yes. Standard terms are contract terms that the drafting party prepares in advance and on its own for use in many similar future contracts and presents to the other party; clauses stating that each term was negotiated and accepted do not on their own take them out of this category (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 20). Terms of which the other party was not clearly informed, or which are alien to the nature of the contract, are deemed not written (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 21). The final stage is review of content:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Code of Obligations No. 6098, art. 25 (Review of content) (unofficial translation)

“Standard terms may not contain provisions that, contrary to the rules of good faith, are against the other party or aggravate its position.”

The 11th Civil Chamber has stated expressly that this review applies to both consumers and merchants and that it does not matter whether the parties are merchants or consumers. The Chamber reversed an appellate decision in favour of a bank that had accelerated the loan of a company claiming to have paid its instalments on time and, on closing the account, had also collected profit amounts not yet due. According to the reasoning, the acceleration clause and the clauses on collection after acceleration constitute standard terms taken as a whole; their validity must be assessed under arts. 20 et seq. of the Code of Obligations and, if they are invalid, it must be considered whether interest or lost profit for the period between the payment date and the date on which payment was due can be claimed (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4499, K. 2022/8102, 21.11.2022).

The review depends on when the contract was concluded. The Chamber applied arts. 20 et seq. of the Code of Obligations to loan agreements signed after 01.07.2012 (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4717, K. 2022/8916, 12.12.2022), while the 13th Civil Chamber of the Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal held that a general loan agreement dated 27.05.2008 was governed by the former Code of Obligations No. 818 and that the standard-terms provisions of the new Code could not be applied retroactively (Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal, 13th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/1492, K. 2023/2109, 28.12.2023; final).

Can a Regularly Serviced Loan Be Accelerated?

The bank’s power to accelerate derives from the agreement, but it too must be exercised in accordance with good faith:

Statutory Provision — Turkish Civil Code No. 4721, art. 2 (Acting in good faith) (unofficial translation)

“Everyone must act in accordance with the rules of good faith in exercising their rights and in performing their obligations. The legal order does not protect the manifest abuse of a right.”

Where instalments are paid on time, acceleration can rest only on an event of default other than non-payment. In the case of the reversal decision mentioned above, the bank had relied on negative economic data concerning the company and on the company’s failure to provide the information and documents it requested (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4499, K. 2022/8102, 21.11.2022).

The bank’s own conduct may also render acceleration wrongful. It was held that a bank which collected TRY 750,000 from the Credit Guarantee Fund and set it off against the unpaid first instalment and the second instalment not yet due had, among the options in art. 106 of the former Code of Obligations No. 818, chosen performance of the contract and compensation for delay, and could not thereafter accelerate the loan; recovery of TRY 179,879.65, the difference between contractual interest and default interest, was ordered. That decision, given in compliance with an earlier reversal, was upheld (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/5506, K. 2021/5763, 27.09.2021; upheld).

Can Enforcement Be Started Before the Payment Period in the Acceleration Notice Expires?

Acceleration notices often grant a period (atıfet) for payment of the debt. According to the 11th Civil Chamber, acceleration makes the debt due; however, if the creditor has granted a grace period, payment is deemed postponed until the end of that period, and starting enforcement proceedings before it expires is contrary to the principle of acting in good faith in art. 2 of the Civil Code. The Chamber stated that, where a notice granting 7 days was served on the principal debtor on 05.01.2018 but enforcement was started on 09.01.2018, the action for annulment of objection based on that enforcement had to be dismissed on this ground as regards the sureties (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/314, K. 2024/3941, 15.05.2024). Dismissals to the same effect were upheld in other cases as well (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/4453, K. 2021/6292, 17.11.2021; Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2022/2991, K. 2023/6726, 22.11.2023; Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/4951, K. 2024/6710, 24.09.2024).

There is no unanimity within the Chamber. Two decisions upholding such dismissals, from 2022 and 2025, were given by majority; the dissenting opinion argued that acceleration of the account is sufficient to start enforcement and that the notice and its service are relevant only to the calculation of default interest (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/8316, K. 2022/3441, 26.04.2022; Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2024/2446, K. 2025/760, 11.02.2025). The Assembly of Civil Chambers decision relied on in the dissents points the same way. The Assembly reversed an insistence decision based on the view that the payment period postpones maturity, and endorsed the reversal decision of the Special Chamber holding that starting enforcement before the period expires is relevant only to the calculation of interest; the view that a bank, as an institution of trust, acts contrary to good faith by starting enforcement before the period it granted has expired was not adopted by the majority of the Assembly (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/815, K. 2017/1007, 24.05.2017; majority).

The debate continues in 2026. As reported in the 11th Civil Chamber’s decision of 29.01.2026, the 13th Civil Chamber of the Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal held enforcement started before the period expired to be contrary to art. 2 of the Civil Code, while its 43rd Civil Chamber held that the only consequence of starting enforcement without waiting for the period is that default interest cannot be claimed until the period expires. The Chamber dismissed on procedural grounds, without examining the merits, the request of the Board of Presidents of the Civil Chambers of the Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal to resolve the conflict, because the decisions concerned were not final by their nature (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2026/496, K. 2026/598, 29.01.2026). The search carried out for this article did not identify a unification-of-case-law decision settling the issue with binding effect. Given this uncertainty, it is advisable for the bank to wait for the period to expire and for the borrower to raise this objection expressly.

What Remedies Does a Borrower Have if the Loan Is Wrongfully Accelerated?

If after acceleration the bank starts non-judgment enforcement proceedings and the borrower objects to the payment order, the action for annulment of objection brought by the bank examines whether the acceleration complied with the agreement and whether enforcement was started before the grace period expired (Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal, 43rd Civil Chamber, E. 2020/190, K. 2021/284, 11.03.2021). The borrower may also bring a negative declaratory action (menfi tespit davası) before or during enforcement to prove that it owes no debt; if no interim measure is obtained and the debt is paid, the action continues as an action for recovery (istirdat davası) (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law art. 72; see negative declaratory action and action for recovery).

Refund of excess interest or profit share may also be claimed. A decision rejecting the claim of a company that paid the entire debt within two days after acceleration for refund of TRY 97,906.09, representing one year’s interest, was reversed (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4499, K. 2022/8102, 21.11.2022), and in another case where the acceleration was found wrongful, recovery of the difference between contractual and default interest was ordered (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/5506, K. 2021/5763, 27.09.2021).

If it is proven that the acceleration was unlawful and caused loss, damages may also be claimed under the general rules: where an obligation is not performed at all or not properly performed, the obligor must compensate the resulting loss unless it proves that no fault is attributable to it (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 112). If the acceleration complied with the agreement and the regulations, the claim is dismissed: the action of a company seeking TRY 1,000,000 in non-pecuniary and TRY 10,000 in pecuniary damages for loss of reputation and the blocking of its accounts at other banks was dismissed on the ground that acceleration after two unpaid instalments did not constitute a tort, and the decision was upheld (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6186, K. 2024/7492, 23.10.2024). The limited search carried out for this article did not identify a recent decision, upheld by the Court of Cassation, awarding damages to a borrower for wrongful acceleration.

Court of Cassation and Regional Court of Appeal Decisions

The 2017 decision of the Assembly of Civil Chambers differs from the majority view of the 11th Civil Chamber on the grace-period issue.

Where a grace period is granted, payment is postponed until it ends; enforcement started before it expires is contrary to good faith

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/314, K. 2024/3941, 15.05.2024 (decision upheld with amended reasoning; unanimous)

“…it must be accepted that, upon acceleration of the loan account by the claimant bank, the debt becomes due even if no notice has been given to the defendant sureties, but that, where the creditor grants the debtor a grace period, payment of the debt is postponed until the end of the grace period. Accordingly, starting enforcement proceedings before the grace period granted has expired is contrary to the principle of ‘acting in good faith’ set out in Article 2 of the Turkish Civil Code No. 4721 (Law No. 4721)…” (translation)

Assembly of Civil Chambers: accelerating the account is sufficient for enforcement; enforcement started before the period expires matters only for the calculation of interest

Court of Cassation Assembly of Civil Chambers Decision
Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/815, K. 2017/1007, 24.05.2017 (reversal of the insistence decision; Special Chamber’s reversal reasoning endorsed by the Assembly; majority)

“Accelerating the account is sufficient for the claimant creditor to start enforcement proceedings, and if enforcement is started before the period granted for payment in the acceleration notice, this must be taken into account only in the calculation of interest.” (translation)

Standard-terms review also applies to merchants; acceleration clauses and clauses on collection after acceleration are subject to it

Court of Cassation Decision
11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4499, K. 2022/8102, 21.11.2022 (Chamber’s reasons for reversal; unanimous)

“Review of standard terms, which applies to both consumers and merchants, protects the party to the contract that is in the weaker position at the signing stage within the framework of the rules of good faith. … Accordingly, in the present case, when the provision on acceleration of the account in the contract between the parties is considered as a whole together with the clauses on collection of the bank’s receivable after the acceleration date, they constitute standard terms…” (translation)

Acceleration against a borrower who paid instalments irregularly and late complies with the law

Regional Court of Appeal Decision
Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal, 43rd Civil Chamber, E. 2020/190, K. 2021/284, 11.03.2021 (appeal dismissed on the merits; final; unanimous)

“In light of these explanations, since it is established that the defendant debtor generally paid its instalment commercial loan debt after the due dates shown in the payment plan, made irregular payments and indeed paid the instalment dated 25.10.2016 preceding the acceleration date on 08.11.2016, and since the defendant acted in breach of the above contractual provision, no breach of the contract or of the law was found in the claimant bank’s acceleration of the account…” (translation)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the bank accelerate my loan even though I have paid my instalments?

If instalments are paid on time, the bank must show that another event of default provided for in the agreement (for example, cessation of business or bankruptcy proceedings) has occurred (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/5458, K. 2022/2601). Paying instalments irregularly and late may, however, justify acceleration (Istanbul Regional Court of Appeal, 43rd Civil Chamber, E. 2020/190, K. 2021/284).

Is it lawful for the bank to demand the whole loan after one or two instalments go unpaid?

Yes, if the agreement provides non-payment of instalments as an event of default. The 11th Civil Chamber upheld a decision accepting that the entire remaining debt may become due (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2025/5065, K. 2026/2229) and a decision finding lawful the acceleration of a KGF-guaranteed loan after two unpaid instalments (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6186, K. 2024/7492).

What happens if enforcement is started before the 7-day period granted in the acceleration notice expires?

Under the 2021–2025 majority decisions of the 11th Civil Chamber, enforcement before the period expires is contrary to art. 2 of the Civil Code and the action for annulment of objection is dismissed (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/314, K. 2024/3941). The Assembly of Civil Chambers held in 2017, however, that this is relevant only to the calculation of interest (Court of Cassation, Assembly of Civil Chambers, E. 2017/815, K. 2017/1007).

Can damages be claimed from the bank if the loan was wrongfully accelerated?

They may be claimed under the general rules if unlawfulness, loss and causation are proven (Turkish Code of Obligations art. 112). If the acceleration complied with the agreement, the damages claim is dismissed (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2023/6186, K. 2024/7492). Refund of excess interest may be claimed separately (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2020/5506, K. 2021/5763).

As a merchant, can I challenge the acceleration clause in a general loan agreement?

Yes. Terms of which the other party was not clearly informed, or which are alien to the nature of the contract, are deemed not written; provisions that are contrary to good faith and aggravate the other party’s position may not be included (Turkish Code of Obligations arts. 21, 25). The 11th Civil Chamber has accepted that this review also applies to merchants and that acceleration clauses and clauses on collection after acceleration must be examined within it (Court of Cassation, 11th Civil Chamber, E. 2021/4499, K. 2022/8102).
