Where to Apply: Routes for Common Disputes under Turkish Law

Under Turkish law, many disputes cannot go straight to court, or an application to an authority must come first. This guide sets out, with statutory references, where to apply, the legal basis, the time limits and the fee rules stated in the law for common disputes in Türkiye. Turkish names of institutions are given in brackets.

Short answer: Under Turkish law, applying to a mediator (arabulucu) before filing suit is mandatory in labour disputes, commercial disputes concerning a sum of money, consumer, lease, partition, condominium and neighbour-law disputes. In 2026, consumer disputes worth less than TRY 186,000 must go to a consumer arbitration committee (tüketici hakem heyeti). Social security disputes must first go to the Social Security Institution (SGK), and personal-data requests first to the data controller. In administrative matters, an application to the superior authority stops the time limit for filing suit.

Mandatory mediation before filing suit (arabuluculuk)

In the disputes below, applying to a mediator before filing a lawsuit is mandatory under Turkish law. The claimant must attach the final mediation report showing that no agreement was reached to the statement of claim.

DisputeScopeLegal basisMediator's time limit
Labour disputesEmployee or employer receivables and compensation based on law or on an individual or collective employment contract, and reinstatement claims, together with related actions to set aside an objection, negative declaratory actions and restitution actions. Compensation claims arising from work accidents and occupational diseases are excluded.Labour Courts Law (Law No. 7036) art. 33 weeks (extendable by up to 1 week where necessary)
Commercial disputesCommercial cases concerning a sum of money: claims for receivables or compensation, actions to set aside an objection, negative declaratory actions and restitution actions.Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 5/A6 weeks (extendable by up to 2 weeks where necessary)
Consumer disputesDisputes heard by consumer courts, except the statutory exceptions such as disputes within the remit of consumer arbitration committees, objections to their decisions and disputes over rights in rem in real estate in consumer transactions.Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 73/A3 weeks (extendable by up to 1 week where necessary)
Lease, partition, condominium, neighbour lawDisputes arising from a lease (except eviction through enforcement without a judgment); division of movables and immovables and partition of co-owned property; disputes under the Condominium Ownership Law and neighbour-law disputes.Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes (Law No. 6325) art. 18/B3 weeks (extendable by up to 1 week where necessary)
Where to apply
The mediation office (arabuluculuk bürosu) at the place of the court competent for the dispute; where no office has been set up, the registry of the designated civil court of peace (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A/4). In labour disputes, the application is made to the mediation office at the other party's place of residence or where the work is performed (Law No. 7036 art. 3/5).
Time limits
The mediator must conclude the application within three weeks of appointment, extendable by up to one week where necessary (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A/9). In commercial disputes the period is six weeks, extendable by up to two weeks (Law No. 6102 art. 5/A/2). From the application to the mediation office until the final report, limitation periods are suspended and forfeiture periods do not run (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A/15). For reinstatement, the employee must apply to a mediator within one month of notification of the termination; if no agreement is reached, a lawsuit may be filed in the labour court within two weeks of the final report (Labour Law No. 4857 art. 20/1).
Fees and court costs
If the parties cannot be reached, no meeting takes place because they do not attend, or no agreement is reached after meetings lasting less than two hours, a two-hour fee is paid from the Ministry of Justice budget; if the parties agree, the fee is shared equally unless agreed otherwise and is set under the Mediation Minimum Fee Tariff (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A/12–13). In consumer disputes, the mediation fee payable by the consumer is covered by the Ministry of Justice budget, up to the two-hour amount (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A/3).
Legal basis
Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes (Law No. 6325) art. 18/A · Law on Mediation in Civil Disputes (Law No. 6325) art. 18/B · Labour Courts Law (Law No. 7036) art. 3 · Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102) art. 5/A · Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 73/A · Labour Law (Law No. 4857) art. 20
Note
A party who fails to attend the first meeting without valid excuse is named in the final report; even if that party wins the case, it bears half of the costs payable by the other side and is awarded only half of the attorney's fee (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A/11). This rule does not apply against consumers (Law No. 6502 art. 73/A/2).
Note
The mandatory mediation rules do not apply where a special law requires arbitration or another alternative dispute-resolution route, or where there is an arbitration agreement (Law No. 6325 art. 18/A/18).

Consumer arbitration committee (tüketici hakem heyeti) and consumer court

Under Turkish law, consumer disputes below the statutory monetary limit must go to a consumer arbitration committee, and disputes above it cannot be brought before a committee (Consumer Protection Law No. 6502 art. 68/1). The limit is increased each year by the revaluation rate (art. 68/4); for applications in 2026 it is TRY 186,000.

Where to apply
The provincial or district consumer arbitration committee where the consumer resides or where the consumer transaction took place; where there is no committee, the district governor's office (kaymakamlık) (Law No. 6502 art. 68/3). Above the limit, the consumer court (tüketici mahkemesi); consumers may also sue in the consumer court of their place of residence (art. 73/1, 73/5). Applying to a mediator before suing in the consumer court is mandatory (art. 73/A).
Time limits
The parties may object to a committee decision before the consumer court of the committee's location or of the consumer's residence within two weeks of notification; the objection does not suspend enforcement of the decision, but the judge may stay it by interim measure on request (Law No. 6502 art. 70/3). The court's decision on the objection is final (art. 70/5).
Fees and court costs
Lawsuits filed by consumers in the consumer courts are exempt from court fees (Law No. 6502 art. 73/2). Where a committee rules against the consumer, notification and expert fees are covered by the Ministry (art. 70/7). Consumer arbitration committees may not award attorney's fees (art. 70/1).
Legal basis
Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 68 · Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 70 · Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 73 · Consumer Protection Law (Law No. 6502) art. 73/A · Communiqué on the monetary limits for consumer arbitration committees (Official Gazette, 23 December 2025)
Note
Committee decisions are binding on the parties and are enforced under the rules for enforcing court judgments (Law No. 6502 art. 70/1–2).

Application to the administration and administrative lawsuits (Administrative Procedure Law arts. 10 and 11)

Under Turkish law, before suing against an administrative act, a person may ask the administration to revoke or amend it; this application stops the time limit for filing suit. A person may also apply to the administration to request an act or action that could later be the subject of an administrative lawsuit.

Where to apply
The superior authority or, if there is none, the authority that made the act (Administrative Procedure Law No. 2577 art. 11/1). A request for an act or action is made to the relevant administrative authority (art. 10/1).
Time limits
The application to the superior authority must be made within the time limit for filing suit. If no answer is given within thirty days, the request is deemed rejected; the time limit then resumes and the period elapsed before the application is counted (Law No. 2577 art. 11/2–3). Where a request for an act receives no answer within thirty days, it is deemed rejected; if the administration gives a non-final answer, the waiting period for a final answer may not exceed four months from the application (art. 10/2). Unless a special law provides otherwise, the time limit for filing suit is sixty days before the Council of State and administrative courts and thirty days before tax courts (art. 7/1).
Legal basis
Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 10 · Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 11 · Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 7
Note
If the administration answers after the thirty-day period and no lawsuit has been filed or the lawsuit was dismissed as out of time, a lawsuit may be filed within sixty days of notification of that answer (Law No. 2577 art. 10/2).

Ombudsman Institution (Kamu Denetçiliği Kurumu)

Under Turkish law, once administrative application routes have been exhausted, an application may be made to the Ombudsman Institution. The Institution notifies the authority concerned and the applicant of the outcome of its examination and of any recommendations.

Where to apply
The Ombudsman Institution (Kamu Denetçiliği Kurumu). Natural and legal persons may apply, by petition in Turkish or electronically under the conditions in the regulation; applications may also be lodged through governors' offices in provinces and district governors' offices (Law No. 6328 art. 17/1, 17/2, 17/5).
Time limits
The application must be made within six months of notification of the administration's answer or, if the administration does not answer within sixty days, of the end of that period (Law No. 6328 art. 17/7). An application made within the time limit for filing suit stops that time limit (art. 17/8). The Institution concludes its examination within six months of the application at the latest (art. 20/1); if it rejects the application, the suspended time limit for filing suit resumes from notification of the reasoned rejection (art. 21/1).
Fees and court costs
No fee is charged for applications (Law No. 6328 art. 17/6).
Legal basis
Law on the Ombudsman Institution (Law No. 6328) art. 17 · Law on the Ombudsman Institution (Law No. 6328) art. 20 · Law on the Ombudsman Institution (Law No. 6328) art. 21
Note
Applications made before administrative routes are exhausted are forwarded to the authority concerned; where there is a risk of damage that is difficult or impossible to remedy, the Institution may accept them (Law No. 6328 art. 17/4).
Note
Applications concerning disputes pending before, or decided by, the courts are not examined (art. 17/3-b).

Objections to decisions of the Social Security Institution (Sosyal Güvenlik Kurumu, SGK)

Under Turkish law, in disputes arising from social security legislation an application to the Social Security Institution is mandatory before filing suit. Claims to establish compulsory insurance periods based on an employment contract are excluded from this requirement (Labour Courts Law No. 7036 art. 4/1).

Where to apply
The Social Security Institution; if the request is rejected or deemed rejected, the courts. For administrative fines imposed by the Institution, the objection is made to the Institution and an action against its rejection is brought before the competent administrative court (Law No. 5510 art. 102). An insured person whose work was not reported to the Institution applies to the labour court to establish it (Law No. 5510 art. 86).
Time limits
If the Institution does not answer within sixty days, the request is deemed rejected; the time spent on the application is not counted for limitation and forfeiture periods (Law No. 7036 art. 4/1). An administrative fine may be challenged before the Institution within fifteen days of notification; if the objection is rejected, an action may be brought before the competent administrative court within thirty days of notification of the rejection, failing which the fine becomes final (Law No. 5510 art. 102). An action to establish unreported employment must be filed in the labour court within five years from the end of the year in which the work was performed (Law No. 5510 art. 86).
Fees and court costs
If an administrative fine is paid in advance within fifteen days of notification, before any objection or court action, three quarters of it is collected; advance payment does not affect the right to go to court (Law No. 5510 art. 102).
Legal basis
Labour Courts Law (Law No. 7036) art. 4 · Social Insurance and General Health Insurance Law (Law No. 5510) art. 102 · Social Insurance and General Health Insurance Law (Law No. 5510) art. 86
Note
An objection to an administrative fine suspends collection; a court action does not suspend its enforcement and collection (Law No. 5510 art. 102).

Settlement (uzlaşma) of tax penalties and tax lawsuits

Under Turkish law, for tax loss penalties relating to taxes assessed additionally, ex officio or by the administration, and for irregularity penalties above the statutory amount, a taxpayer relying on one of the grounds in the law may request settlement with the administration (Tax Procedure Law No. 213, additional art. 1).

Where to apply
The settlement commission within the tax administration (Law No. 213, additional art. 1/2); if no settlement is reached, the tax court.
Time limits
The settlement request must be made within thirty days of notification of the tax notice; if settlement fails or cannot be arranged, it cannot be requested again. If the taxpayer notifies in writing, before the time limit for filing suit expires, that it accepts the penalty last offered by the administration, settlement is deemed reached (Law No. 213, additional art. 1). A taxpayer who requested settlement in time may sue over that penalty only if settlement is not reached (additional art. 7). Unless a special law provides otherwise, the time limit for suing in the tax court is thirty days (Law No. 2577 art. 7/1).
Legal basis
Tax Procedure Law (Law No. 213) additional art. 1 · Tax Procedure Law (Law No. 213) additional art. 7 · Tax Procedure Law (Law No. 213) additional art. 11 · Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577) art. 7
Note
Where settlement is reached before assessment, no lawsuit may be filed on the matter recorded in the minutes; if pre-assessment settlement fails, settlement cannot be requested after the penalty is imposed (Law No. 213, additional art. 11).

Complaints against enforcement office acts and objections to payment orders

Under Turkish law, acts of the enforcement and bankruptcy offices (icra ve iflas daireleri) that are unlawful or inappropriate to the case may be challenged by complaint (şikâyet) to the enforcement court, except for matters the law leaves to the ordinary courts (Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law No. 2004 art. 16).

Where to apply
For complaints, the enforcement court (icra mahkemesi) (Law No. 2004 art. 16). For objections to a payment order in enforcement without a judgment, the enforcement office (icra dairesi) (art. 62).
Time limits
A complaint must be made within seven days of learning of the act; where a right is not carried out or is delayed without reason, a complaint may be made at any time (Law No. 2004 art. 16). An objection to a payment order must be lodged with the enforcement office, in writing or orally, within seven days of service of the order (art. 62).
Fees and court costs
A debtor who objects receives a free, stamp-duty-exempt document confirming the objection (Law No. 2004 art. 62).
Legal basis
Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 16 · Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004) art. 62
Note
A debtor objecting to part of the debt must state that part clearly; a debtor denying the signature on the instrument must say so separately and expressly in the objection, otherwise the signature is deemed accepted for enforcement purposes (Law No. 2004 art. 62).

Legal aid (adli yardım) and bar legal aid offices

Under Turkish law, persons unable to pay the costs of proceedings may ask the court for legal aid if their request is not manifestly unfounded; those who cannot afford a lawyer receive legal services through the legal aid office of the bar association (Code of Civil Procedure No. 6100 art. 334; Attorneys Act No. 1136 art. 176).

Where to apply
For court costs: the court that will decide the main claim; in enforcement proceedings, the enforcement court where enforcement is pursued; on appeal, the regional court of appeal (bölge adliye mahkemesi) or the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay) (Law No. 6100 art. 336/1, 336/3). For a lawyer: the bar legal aid office (baro adli yardım bürosu) or its representatives; if the request is refused, the applicant may apply to the president of the bar in writing or orally, whose decision is final (Law No. 1136 arts. 177, 178).
Time limits
Legal aid continues until the judgment becomes final (Law No. 6100 art. 335/3).
Fees and court costs
A legal aid decision provides temporary exemption from the costs of proceedings and enforcement, exemption from providing security, advance payment of costs by the State and, where representation is required, a lawyer whose fee is paid later; the court may grant only some of these (Law No. 6100 art. 335). Documents relating to a legal aid request are exempt from all fees and taxes (art. 336/4).
Legal basis
Code of Civil Procedure (Law No. 6100) art. 334 · Code of Civil Procedure (Law No. 6100) art. 335 · Code of Civil Procedure (Law No. 6100) art. 336 · Attorneys Act (Law No. 1136) art. 176 · Attorneys Act (Law No. 1136) art. 177 · Attorneys Act (Law No. 1136) art. 178
Note
The applicant must submit a summary of the claim, the evidence and documents on their financial situation showing that they cannot meet the costs (Law No. 6100 art. 336/2). In a request to the bar, the applicant must likewise show with evidence that the request is well-founded (Law No. 1136 art. 178).
Note
Legal aid for foreigners is additionally subject to reciprocity (Law No. 6100 art. 334/3).

Personal data: application to the data controller and complaint to the Board (KVKK)

Under Turkish law, a data subject first submits requests concerning their personal data to the data controller; a complaint to the Personal Data Protection Board (Kişisel Verileri Koruma Kurulu) cannot be made before this route is exhausted (Personal Data Protection Law No. 6698 arts. 13, 14/2).

Where to apply
First the data controller (in writing or by other methods set by the Board); then the Personal Data Protection Board (Law No. 6698 art. 13/1, art. 14).
Time limits
The data controller must conclude the request as soon as possible according to its nature and within thirty days at the latest (Law No. 6698 art. 13/2). If the request is rejected, the answer is insufficient or no answer is given in time, a complaint may be lodged with the Board within thirty days of learning of the answer and in any event within sixty days of the application (art. 14/1).
Fees and court costs
The data controller handles requests free of charge; if the action entails an additional cost, a fee under the tariff set by the Board may be charged and is refunded if the request resulted from the controller's error (Law No. 6698 art. 13/2–3).
Legal basis
Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 6698) art. 13 · Personal Data Protection Law (Law No. 6698) art. 14
Note
Persons whose personality rights have been infringed retain their right to compensation under the general provisions (Law No. 6698 art. 14/3).

CİMER, the right to petition and the right to information

Under Turkish law, Turkish citizens may apply in writing to the competent authorities with requests and complaints concerning themselves or the public (Law No. 3071 art. 3). The Presidential Communication Centre (Cumhurbaşkanlığı İletişim Merkezi, CİMER) is the channel through which petitions and requests for information are forwarded to public bodies (CİMER Regulation arts. 1, 5/1).

Where to apply
The competent administrative authority, or CİMER. Applications to CİMER can be made through its electronic and mobile service, the e-Devlet gateway, the ALO 150 telephone line, by letter or post, or in person (CİMER Regulation art. 6/1).
Time limits
The petitioner receives a reasoned answer on the outcome or progress of the request or complaint within thirty days at the latest (Law No. 3071 art. 7). For requests for information, the body provides access within fifteen working days; if the information must come from another unit or body, or another body's opinion is needed, the period is thirty working days and the extension and its reasons are notified (Right to Information Law No. 4982 art. 11).
Fees and court costs
If access to information or documents involves a cost, the administration notifies the applicant of the amount; if it is not paid within fifteen working days, the request is deemed withdrawn (Law No. 4982 art. 11).
Legal basis
Law on the Exercise of the Right to Petition (Law No. 3071) art. 3 · Law on the Exercise of the Right to Petition (Law No. 3071) art. 7 · Right to Information Law (Law No. 4982) art. 11 · Regulation on the Presidential Communication Centre (CİMER)
Note
CİMER does not process applications concerning legislative or judicial activity, claims arising from private-law relationships, or reports of crimes that fall solely within the remit of the judicial authorities (CİMER Regulation art. 5/2).
Note
Foreigners residing in Türkiye may exercise the right to petition on a reciprocal basis, provided their petitions are written in Turkish (Law No. 3071 art. 3).

Insurance Arbitration Commission (Sigorta Tahkim Komisyonu)

Under Turkish law, disputes arising from an insurance contract may go to arbitration even without a clause in the contract if the insurer is a member of the arbitration system; for disputes arising from compulsory insurance, claimants may use this route even if the insurer is not a member (Insurance Law No. 5684 art. 30/1).

Where to apply
The Insurance Arbitration Commission (Sigorta Tahkim Komisyonu) (Law No. 5684 art. 30/1).
Time limits
Before applying, the claimant must have applied to the insurer and be able to show that the claim was wholly or partly refused; failure by the insurer to answer in writing within fifteen working days also allows an application (Law No. 5684 art. 30/13). Arbitrators must decide within four months of their appointment at the latest (art. 30/16).
Fees and court costs
An application fee is charged to those applying to the Commission (Law No. 5684 art. 30/12); the fee is set after obtaining the Commission's opinion (art. 30/18).
Legal basis
Insurance Law (Law No. 5684) art. 30
Note
A dispute that has been brought before a court or a consumer arbitration committee cannot be taken to the Commission (Law No. 5684 art. 30/14).

Individual application to the Constitutional Court (Anayasa Mahkemesi)

Under Turkish law, once the available remedies have been exhausted, an individual application may be made to the Constitutional Court regarding the right or freedom allegedly violated; the application must state the grounds for the violation and the steps taken to exhaust remedies (Law No. 6216 art. 47/3).

Where to apply
The Constitutional Court; the application may be lodged directly or through courts or Turkish missions abroad (Law No. 6216 art. 47/1).
Time limits
The application must be made within thirty days of exhausting the remedies or, where no remedy is provided, of learning of the violation. An applicant prevented by a valid excuse may apply within fifteen days of the excuse ceasing, with evidence of it (Law No. 6216 art. 47/5).
Fees and court costs
Individual applications are subject to a fee; proof of payment must be attached to the application (Law No. 6216 art. 47/2–3).
Legal basis
Law on the Establishment and Rules of Procedure of the Constitutional Court (Law No. 6216) art. 47
Note
If the applicant is represented by a lawyer, a power of attorney must be submitted; for missing documents, a period not exceeding fifteen days is granted (Law No. 6216 art. 47/4, 47/6).
