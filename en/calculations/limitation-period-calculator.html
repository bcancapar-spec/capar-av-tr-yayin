Limitation Period Calculator under Turkish Law: Last Day, Suspension and Interruption

Go to the calculator

Choose the type of claim or action and enter the dates: the calculator shows, under Turkish law (the law of the Republic of Türkiye), whether the time limit is a limitation period or a preclusive period, its length, its start and its last day, with the legal basis. It takes into account time spent in mediation, the pandemic suspension (13 March – 15 June 2020) and interruption by acknowledgement, part payment, action or enforcement, and moves a last day falling on a holiday to the next working day (TBK art.92, 93, 146–161).

Short answer: Unless the law provides otherwise, claims become time-barred after ten years (Turkish Code of Obligations, TBK art.146); periodic payments such as rent, interest and wages and claims under mandate and contracts for work after five years (TBK art.147; Labour Law No. 4857 art.32); tort and unjust enrichment claims two years from knowledge and in any event ten years (TBK art.72, 82). The first day is not counted and a last day falling on a holiday moves to the next working day (TBK art.93, 151); suspension periods are added and interruption starts a new period (TBK art.153–157). Preclusive periods (e.g. one month for reinstatement, one year for reduction of dispositions) are neither suspended nor interrupted and are applied by the court of its own motion.

Information tool; not legal advice. Risk of missing the deadline: missing a time limit causes loss of rights; check the result against your documents and do not leave the step until the last day.

How to use: choose the area, then the type of claim or action; enter the dates requested; add any suspension periods such as mediation and interruption events such as acknowledgement, payment or an action, and press the calculate button. The result appears on this page, just below the form.

The information you enter is calculated in your browser and is not sent to the server.

How the calculation works

Abbreviations: TBK Turkish Code of Obligations (Law No. 6098); TMK Turkish Civil Code (Law No. 4721); TTK Turkish Commercial Code (Law No. 6102); KTK Highway Traffic Law (Law No. 2918); İİK Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law (Law No. 2004); İYUK Administrative Procedure Law (Law No. 2577); CBK Presidential Decree; HGK Assembly of Civil Chambers; HD Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation (Yargıtay); E. / K. docket and decision numbers.

  1. Classification: first, whether the time limit is a limitation period or a preclusive period. Limitation is a defence the debtor must raise (TBK art.161) and is suspended and interrupted; a preclusive period is applied by the court of its own motion and is neither suspended nor interrupted.
  2. Start: limitation starts when the claim falls due (TBK art.149); for periodic payments each instalment starts separately. In tort, unjust enrichment, reduction and similar types the start depends on knowledge and there is also an outer limit that runs in any event; whichever ends first applies. Where the start depends on a fact such as knowledge, the calculation uses the date you enter.
  3. Counting: the first day is not counted; a period in years or months ends on the corresponding day of the last month, or on the last day of that month if there is none (TBK art.92, 151). If the last day is a weekend, official holiday or religious holiday it moves to the next working day (TBK art.93; Law No. 2429 art.2); half days such as the eve of a religious holiday or 28 October do not move it.
  4. Suspension: in the cases of TBK art.153 limitation does not start or is suspended; from the application to the mediation office until the final minutes, limitation is suspended and preclusive periods do not run (Law No. 6325 art.18/A; Law No. 7036 art.3/17). Suspended days are added to the last day.
  5. Statutory suspensions: under Law No. 7226 provisional art.1 and Presidential Decision No. 2480 time limits were suspended from 13 March 2020 to 15 June 2020 (from 22 March 2020 for periods under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law); periods with fifteen days or less remaining are deemed extended by fifteen days from 16 June 2020. After the earthquakes of 6 February 2023, Presidential Decree No. 120 art.2 suspended time limits from 6 February to 6 April 2023 for persons in the affected region and lawyers registered with those bars; this suspension is optional in the calculator.
  6. Interruption: acknowledgement, payment of interest or part of the debt, a pledge or surety, an action, defence, enforcement or a claim in bankruptcy interrupt limitation; a new period starts, which is always ten years if the debt was acknowledged in writing or established by a decision (TBK art.154, 156). During an action or enforcement the period runs again after every step (TBK art.157). An acknowledgement after expiry does not interrupt.
  7. Criminal limitation: if the claim arises from a crime for which criminal law provides a longer period, that period applies (TBK art.72; KTK art.109/2). The calculator does not determine the criminal period; you enter it in years.

Actions without a time limit, or outside the scope

  • Cancellation of title and registration: an action based on an unlawful registration (Turkish Civil Code, TMK art.1025) and an action for simulated transfers by the deceased (muris muvazaası) are not subject to a time limit; those based on fraud or exploitation are subject to one year.
  • Partition of co-owned property: any co-owner may ask at any time; there is no time limit (TMK art.698).
  • De facto expropriation (kamulaştırmasız el atma): as the Constitutional Court annulled art.38 of Expropriation Law No. 2942, the action is not subject to a time limit while the occupation continues.
  • Compensation for unlawful occupation (ecrimisil): in Court of Cassation practice at most five years before the action may be claimed; older periods are open to the limitation defence.
  • Eviction and termination periods in a lease: see the lease termination period calculator.
  • Medical error in a state or university hospital: you must first apply to the administration (Administrative Procedure Law, İYUK art.13); not calculated here.
  • Time limits for actions before administrative and tax courts; for tax limitation see the article on tax assessment limitation in Türkiye.
  • Criminal prosecution and sentence limitation; procedural time limits (appeal, objection, reply).
  • File-specific questions such as increasing a partial claim, set-off with a time-barred claim and the effect on joint debtors and sureties (TBK art.155).
  • For a general explanation see the article on limitation and preclusive periods.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a limitation period and a preclusive period?

When a limitation period expires the claim still exists but can no longer be enforced in court, and the judge does not take it into account unless the debtor raises it (Law No. 6098 art.161, Turkish Code of Obligations, TBK). When a preclusive period expires the right itself is extinguished and the court applies this of its own motion. Limitation is suspended and interrupted (TBK art.153–154); a preclusive period as a rule is not. Time spent in mediation counts for neither (Law No. 6325 art.18/A; Law No. 7036 art.3/17).

When does the period start and how is the last day found?

Limitation starts when the claim falls due (Law No. 6098 art.149); in tort and unjust enrichment the date of knowledge and, in any event, the date of the act or enrichment are decisive (TBK art.72, 82). The first day is not counted; a period in years or months ends on the corresponding day of the last month, or on its last day if there is no such day (TBK art.92, 151). A last day falling on a weekend or official holiday moves to the next working day (TBK art.93). Religious holiday dates follow the Presidency of Religious Affairs (Diyanet) calendar; if the last day is close to a religious holiday, confirm it separately.

How is a limitation period interrupted?

Acknowledgement of the debt, in particular payment of interest or of part of the debt, a pledge or a surety, and the creditor's action, defence, enforcement or claim in bankruptcy interrupt limitation (Law No. 6098 art.154). A new period then starts; if the debt was acknowledged in writing or established by a court or arbitral decision, the new period is always ten years (TBK art.156). A notice letter does not interrupt limitation, and an acknowledgement after expiry has no effect.

My debt is time-barred: what should I do, and can I recover what I paid?

Limitation does not extinguish the debt. If the creditor sues or starts enforcement, you must raise limitation in time in your reply or objection; otherwise the court does not consider it (Law No. 6098 art.161). Acknowledging or paying after expiry may be treated as a waiver, so consult your lawyer first. What was paid on a time-barred debt cannot be recovered (TBK art.78/2). Time in mediation is added to the period (Law No. 6325 art.18/A).

Were periods suspended during the Covid-19 pandemic and after the 2023 earthquakes?

Under Law No. 7226 provisional art.1 and Presidential Decision No. 2480, limitation and preclusive periods were suspended from 13 March 2020 to 15 June 2020 (time limits to sue under the Enforcement and Bankruptcy Law, i.e. annulment of objection, recovery and avoidance actions, fall under sub-paragraph (a) and stopped from 13 March 2020: for annulment of objection, Court of Cassation 3rd Civil Chamber, 24.11.2022, 2022/6068 E., 2022/8936 K.; periods within enforcement proceedings, i.e. removal of objection and enforcing a judgment, fall under (b) and stopped from 22 March 2020, as no decision was found the shorter suspension is applied); periods with fifteen days or less remaining are deemed extended by fifteen days from 16 June 2020. The calculator applies this automatically. Under Presidential Decree No. 120 art.2, periods were suspended from 6 February to 6 April 2023 for persons in the provinces under state of emergency and lawyers registered with those bars; tick the earthquake option if this applies.

How does the longer criminal limitation period apply?

If the claim arises from an act that is a crime for which criminal law provides a longer limitation period, that period applies (Law No. 6098 art.72; Law No. 2918 art.109/2); criminal proceedings or a conviction are not required. The calculator does not determine the criminal period: you enter it in years and it runs from the date of the act. The pandemic and earthquake suspensions are added to it as in the 2025 decisions of the 4th Civil Chamber (2024/8493 E., 2025/16938 K.); in 2023/2982 E., 2023/10325 K. the majority of the same chamber did not add them, and the calculator shows that earlier date in a note.
