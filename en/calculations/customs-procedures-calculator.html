Customs Procedures Calculator under Turkish Law: Penalties, Deadlines, Interest, Procedures, Origin and Value

Go to the calculator

Choose what you would like to calculate in customs matters under Turkish law (the law of the Republic of Türkiye): a customs penalty, objection and action deadlines, interest at the late-payment rate, temporary storage and liquidation periods, exceeding the period under temporary import or inward processing, compensatory duty, outward processing, the preferential origin percentage rule, customs value adjustments or the repayment of duties. The calculator shows the steps, a timeline, the legal basis and related Council of State decisions.

Short answer: A customs penalty under Turkish law linked to a loss of duties is a multiple of the duty difference (Customs Law No. 4458 art. 234: three times if the difference exceeds 5%), a penalty for prohibited or licence-bound goods is a multiple of the duty-paid value (art. 235), a breach of a customs procedure is twice the duty-paid value or the duties plus interest at the late-payment rate (art. 238), and an irregularity is a multiple of an amount revalued every year (art. 241; 1,494 TL in 2026). An objection or a settlement request is filed within 15 days of notification; an action against the rejection is brought before the tax court within 30 days.

Information tool; not legal advice. It does not replace a customs declaration. Customs declarations and indirect representation are reserved to licensed customs brokers (Customs Law No. 4458 art. 5 and 225); the customs office sets the final amount of duties and penalties.

How to use: first choose what you would like to calculate, then enter the amounts and dates requested and press the calculate button. Amounts can be written as 125,000.50. The result appears on this page, just below the form.

The information you enter is calculated in your browser and is not sent to the server.

How the calculation works

  1. Penalties linked to a loss of duties: the difference between declared and due duties is found; for discrepancies in tariff elements the penalty is three times the difference if it exceeds 5% of the declared duties, three times for undervaluation, half for quantity differences up to 5% and calculation errors (art. 234/1). With voluntary disclosure only 10% applies; the penalty cannot be lower than the art. 241/1 amount (art. 234/3, 6). According to the Council of State no such penalty is calculated on anti-dumping duty.
  2. Prohibited and licence-bound goods: four times the duty-paid value for prohibited imports, twice for licence-bound imports; twice for prohibited exports and one tenth for licence-bound exports (art. 235). 10% with voluntary disclosure.
  3. Procedure breaches and delays: under temporary import, twice the art. 241/1 amount if the goods are discharged within one month after the period, four times within two months, six times within three months for vehicles; afterwards the duties plus interest at the late-payment rate if the goods are under supervision, or twice the duty-paid value if their fate is unknown (art. 238, 241). The same tiers apply to inward processing.
  4. Irregularity penalty: the art. 241/1 amount is revalued every year; the amount in force on the date of the act applies, multiplied by two, four, six or eight depending on the paragraph.
  5. Deadlines: objection and settlement within 15 days of notification; the administration decides within 30 days, otherwise the objection is deemed rejected; the action is brought before the tax court within 30 days, moved to the next working day if the last day is a holiday and to seven days after the judicial recess if it falls in the recess (Law No. 2577 art. 7, 8). Settlement stops the period; at least five days remain (art. 244).
  6. Interest at the late-payment rate: for each rate period, full months at the monthly rate and fractions daily (Law No. 6183 art. 51); the monthly rate has been 3.7% since 13 November 2025.
  7. Compensatory duty: when a product obtained under inward processing is exported to a preferential partner, the customs duty of the third-country input is calculated per export declaration at the exchange and duty rates of its registration date; interest applies if paid after exit, and a penalty of one quarter if detected by customs (art. 194, 234/5).
  8. Origin and value: for the origin percentage rule the non-originating content is compared with the list-rule threshold, and the tolerance under the revised Pan-Euro-Mediterranean rules is 15%; for customs value the additions of art. 27 are added to the price and the costs of art. 28 are deducted.

Not covered

  • Customs duty, additional duty, special consumption tax and VAT by tariff code, postal and express consignments, passengers' goods: see the tariff code (GTİP) calculator in the Calculations section.
  • Relief for returning residents (household effects and vehicles): not calculated in this version because the conditions depend on frequently amended provisions.
  • Customs broker fees: not added because no tariff in force verified from an official source was found.
  • Smuggling offences (Law No. 5607) and related penalties; settlement does not apply to them (art. 244/2).

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a customs penalty calculated in Türkiye?

Under Customs Law No. 4458 art. 234/1-a, if the declaration and the examination differ in tariff elements and the duty difference exceeds 5% of the declared duties, the penalty is three times the difference; under art. 234/1-b an undervalued declaration attracts three times the duty difference. If the declarant discloses the discrepancy before detection, only 10% applies (art. 234/3), and the penalty cannot be lower than the art. 241/1 amount (art. 234/6).

What is the customs irregularity penalty in 2026?

Under Customs Law No. 4458 art. 241/1 the irregularity penalty is 1,494 TL from 1 January 2026 (Customs General Communiqué (Customs Procedures) Serial No. 217); depending on the paragraph, two, four, six or eight times this amount applies. The amount in force on the date of the act is used.

How long do I have to object to a customs penalty?

Under Customs Law No. 4458 art. 242/1, duties, penalties and administrative decisions may be objected to within 15 days of notification. The administration decides within 30 days (art. 242/2); according to the Council of State, if no decision is notified in that period the objection is deemed rejected and the 30-day action period before the tax court starts (Law No. 2577 art. 7).

Does a customs settlement request stop the objection period?

Yes. Under Customs Law No. 4458 art. 244/1, if settlement is requested within 15 days of notification the objection or action period stops; if no settlement is reached the period resumes, and if fewer than five days remain it is extended to five days. No advance-payment reduction applies to a settled penalty (art. 244/6).

What happens if the temporary import period is exceeded?

Under Customs Law No. 4458 art. 241/3-l, 4-g and 5-b, re-export within one month after the period attracts twice the irregularity amount, within two months four times, and for vehicles within three months six times. For longer delays the penalty is the duties plus interest at the late-payment rate if the goods are under supervision (art. 238/1-d), or twice the duty-paid value if their fate is unknown (art. 238/1-a).

Until when can overpaid customs duties be reclaimed?

Under Customs Law No. 4458 art. 211/2, duties that were not legally owed are repaid on application within three years of notification; for defective goods the period is one year (art. 213/4). If a repayment decision is not implemented within four months of notification, statutory interest is paid on request (art. 216/2).
