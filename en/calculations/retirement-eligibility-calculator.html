When Can I Retire in Türkiye? Retirement Eligibility Calculator under Turkish Law: Age, Premium Days and Insurance Period

Go to the calculator

Determines, under Turkish law (the law of the Republic of Türkiye), the conditions for an old-age or retirement pension according to your date of first insurance and shows the earliest date: for persons insured on or before 8 September 1999, provisional art. 81 of Law No. 506, provisional art. 10 of Law No. 1479 and provisional art. 205 of Law No. 5434, with the age condition removed by Law No. 7438; for 9 September 1999 – 30 April 2008, provisional art. 9 of Law No. 5510; afterwards, art. 28. It shows the insurance period, premium day and age conditions separately, what is missing and when it will be completed; it takes into account military service and childbirth credits and, for service in more than one status, the status under which the pension is granted. Each step is explained with the article number.

Short answer: Under Turkish law, retirement conditions depend on the date of first insurance. On or before 8 September 1999: provisional art. 81 of Law No. 506, provisional art. 10 of Law No. 1479 and provisional art. 205 of Law No. 5434 apply, and provisional art. 95 of Law No. 5510 (added by Law No. 7438) removed the age condition for the full pension. From 9 September 1999 to 30 April 2008: age 58 for women and 60 for men with 7,000 premium days under 4/a or 9,000 under 4/b and 4/c (Law No. 5510 provisional art. 9). From 1 May 2008: 7,200 days under 4/a or 9,000 under 4/b and 4/c, and an age rising from 58/60 to 65 according to the date the day condition is met (Law No. 5510 art. 28).

Information tool; not legal advice. This result may change according to the SGK service record and the pension award decision.

How to use: open your SGK service record on e-Devlet; enter your sex, date of birth, status, date of first insurance and total premium days, add any credits and press the calculate button. The result appears on this page, just below the form. Results are preliminary estimates.

The information you enter is calculated in your browser and is not sent to the server.

Required fields: sex, date of birth, status, date of first insurance and premium days. Enter day counts in digits. No name, identity number or registration number is requested.

How the calculation works

  1. Start of insurance: the date a person first becomes subject to invalidity, old-age and survivors' insurance (Law No. 5510 art. 38/1). If military service before the first entry is credited, the start moves back by the number of days credited (art. 41/3). Where insurance began before age 18, the period starts on the 18th birthday and the earlier days are added to the premium days (art. 38/2); persons registered under Law No. 506 before 1 April 1981 are excepted (provisional art. 6/1). The band of provisional art. 81/B of Law No. 506 is also selected by the 18th birthday (former Law No. 506 art. 60/G; Court of Cassation 10th Civil Chamber E. 2018/344, K. 2019/7721; E. 2024/1567, K. 2024/4001; E. 2024/8453, K. 2024/9513); since the Social Security Institution uses the date of first entry, the tool also shows that band.
  2. Entry on or before 8 September 1999, 4/a: a graded transition applies according to the insurance period on 23 May 2002 (Law No. 506 provisional art. 81/B): an insurance period of 20 years for women or 25 for men and 5,000 to 5,975 premium days depending on the entry date. Under provisional art. 95 of Law No. 5510, added by Law No. 7438, the age condition in these bands no longer applies. Persons with a period of 18 years (women) or 23 years (men) on 8 September 1999 remain subject to the earlier rules (provisional art. 81/A).
  3. Entry on or before 8 September 1999, 4/b and 4/c: 20 full years for women or 25 for men, that is 7,200 or 9,000 days; the age bands were removed by provisional art. 95 (Law No. 1479 provisional art. 10/2; Law No. 5434 provisional art. 205).
  4. Entry from 9 September 1999 to 30 April 2008: age 58 for women or 60 for men with 7,000 days under 4/a or 9,000 days under 4/b and 4/c; with fewer days, under 4/a the same age, a 25-year insurance period and 4,500 days, under 4/b age 60 for women or 62 for men and 5,400 days, under 4/c age 61 and 5,400 days (Law No. 5510 provisional art. 9).
  5. Entry on or after 1 May 2008: 7,200 days under 4/a or 9,000 under 4/b and 4/c and age 58 for women or 60 for men; if the day condition is met after 1 January 2036 the age rises every two years up to 65, and the age limit in force on the date the day condition is met applies (Law No. 5510 art. 28/2). For a pension with fewer days three years are added to that age, not exceeding 65, and 5,400 days are required (art. 28/3); under 4/a the number of days starts from 4,600 depending on the year of entry (provisional art. 6).
  6. Partial pension, entry on or before 8 September 1999: under 4/a a 15-year insurance period, 3,600 days and an age graded by the date the conditions are met (Law No. 506 provisional art. 81/C); under 4/b 15 full years and a graded age (Law No. 1479 provisional art. 10/3). On this route the age condition was not removed by Law No. 7438.
  7. Agricultural workers (Law No. 2925): for entries on or before 8 September 1999, a 15-year insurance period and 3,600 days with no age condition (Law No. 2925 provisional art. 2; Law No. 5510 provisional art. 95); for entries between 9 September 1999 and 30 April 2008, age 58 (women) or 60 (men) is added (Law No. 5510 provisional art. 9/4); after that the 4/a conditions apply.
  8. Loss of working capacity and persons disabled before starting work: 16 years and 4,320 days for a 50–59% loss, 18 years and 4,680 days for a 40–49% loss (art. 28/5; fewer days under 4/a depending on the year of entry, provisional art. 6); 15 years and 3,960 days for persons disabled before starting work (art. 28/4), 15 years and 3,600 days for those insured under 4/a before 1 October 2008 (provisional art. 10/1), and 5,400 days for 4/c members who joined before 1 October 2008 (provisional art. 4); no age condition.
  9. More than one status: for persons insured before 1 October 2008, the status in which most service fell within the last seven years of actual service (2,520 days), or the last status if equal (repealed Law No. 2829 art. 8; Law No. 5510 provisional art. 2); for persons insured later, the status with the most service (Law No. 5510 art. 53).
  10. Earliest date: the day each condition is met is found separately and the latest one applies. In the continue-working scenario 30 premium days are assumed every month; if work stops, missing premium days are not completed.

What is not included

  • The amount of the pension and the pension rate.
  • Actual service increments (Law No. 5510 art. 40) and notional service (art. 49, provisional art. 7), underground mining work, early ageing (art. 28/6, art. 28/7).
  • The added days and age reduction for insured women with a severely disabled child in need of constant care (art. 28/8).
  • Self-employed farmers (repealed Law No. 2926), details of voluntary insurance, the bank and stock exchange funds under provisional art. 20 of Law No. 506.
  • Service abroad and its crediting (Law No. 3201), and the start of insurance under social security agreements.
  • Special age limits such as those for military personnel, judges and prosecutors and academics; the partial pension under 4/c for entries on or before 8 September 1999, the 25-year rule in paragraphs (c) and (f) of repealed art. 39 of Law No. 5434 and retirement for lack of a post (Law No. 5510 provisional art. 4).
  • The invalidity pension (art. 25–27), working after retirement (art. 30) and, for persons insured before 1 October 2008 whose pension is granted under 4/b, the pension of persons disabled before starting work.
  • No personal data is requested; the calculation is made in your browser.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is covered by the EYT reform?

Provisional art. 95 of Law No. 5510, added by Law No. 7438, covers persons whose pension conditions are set by provisional art. 81/B of Law No. 506, provisional art. 10/2 of Law No. 1479, provisional art. 2/B of Law No. 2925 and provisional art. 205 of Law No. 5434, that is, persons first insured on or before 8 September 1999. For them only the conditions other than age apply: under 4/a, an insurance period of 20 years for women or 25 for men and 5,000 to 5,975 premium days depending on the entry date; under 4/b and 4/c, 7,200 days for women and 9,000 for men; for agricultural workers (2925), a 15-year insurance period and 3,600 days. The provision applies to applications made after 3 March 2023 and gives no retroactive payment. The partial pension rules in provisional art. 81/C of Law No. 506 and provisional art. 10/3 of Law No. 1479 are not covered; the age condition remains there.

Can one retire with 3,600 or 5,400 premium days?

Under 4/a, persons first insured on or before 8 September 1999 qualify with a 15-year insurance period and 3,600 premium days at an age graded by the date the conditions are met (Law No. 506 provisional art. 81/C): 58 for women who meet them after 24 May 2011 and 60 for men who meet them after 24 May 2014. For those first insured between 9 September 1999 and 30 April 2008 this route requires age 58 (women) or 60 (men), a 25-year insurance period and 4,500 days (Law No. 5510 provisional art. 9/1). Under 4/b, 15 full years, that is 5,400 days, are required (Law No. 1479 provisional art. 10/3; Law No. 5510 provisional art. 9/2). For those first insured after 30 April 2008, 5,400 days and three years added to the full-pension age are required (Law No. 5510 art. 28/3); under 4/a the number of days starts from 4,600 depending on the year of entry (provisional art. 6).

Does crediting military service bring retirement forward?

The credited days are added to the premium days. If the military service preceded the first insurance entry, the start of insurance is moved back by the number of days credited (Law No. 5510 art. 41/3). If the start moves to 8 September 1999 or earlier, or to an earlier band of provisional art. 81 of Law No. 506, the conditions change as well. In its decision of 9 October 2025 (E. 2025/7865, K. 2025/13351) the 10th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation upheld moving the start from 1 April 1998 to 1 October 1996 through a 540-day credit and granting the pension accordingly. If the service came after the first entry, only the days are added. The Social Security Institution accepts that for pensions granted under 4/c (the former Retirement Fund) crediting military service does not move the start back; statutory basis: Law No. 5510 provisional art. 7 (SGK Circular 2023/13; the circular's text has not been separately verified).

Does internship or apprenticeship insurance count as the start for retirement?

The insurance period starts on the date a person first becomes subject to invalidity, old-age and survivors' insurance (Law No. 5510 art. 38/1). For apprentices and student interns only short-term insurance premiums are paid; that registration is not a start (repealed Law No. 506 art. 3/II-B). In its decision of 25 March 2024 (E. 2024/1231, K. 2024/3117) the 10th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation upheld not treating a vocational high school internship as the start; in its decision of 19 March 2024 (E. 2023/14230, K. 2024/2913) it held that a person who actually takes part in production cannot be treated as an apprentice and that the nature of the work must be examined. Where insurance began before age 18, the period starts on the 18th birthday and the earlier days are added to the premium days (art. 38/2).

Which status applies to someone who moved between Bağ-Kur and SSK?

For persons insured before 1 October 2008, when Law No. 5510 entered into force, the pension is granted under the conditions of the status in which most service fell within the last seven years of actual service (2,520 days); if equal, the last status applies (repealed Law No. 2829 art. 8; Law No. 5510 provisional art. 2). For persons first insured after that date, the status with the most service applies (Law No. 5510 art. 53). Voluntary insurance days count as 4/b (Law No. 5510 art. 51); in its decision of 6 November 2024 (E. 2023/371, K. 2024/543) the General Assembly of Civil Chambers of the Court of Cassation held that voluntary premiums are also counted in the last seven years.

How does crediting childbirth periods affect women's retirement?

An insured woman may credit, for up to three births, the periods she did not work, not exceeding two years after each birth; the child must be alive and she must not have been covered by long-term insurance in that period (Law No. 5510 art. 41/1-a). The credited days are added to the premium days. In its decision of 12 January 2023 (E. 2022/13806, K. 2023/301) the 10th Civil Chamber of the Court of Cassation accepted that a woman who was not insured on the date of the birth cannot credit that birth. If the credited birth precedes the first entry used for retirement (only short-term registration at the time of the birth), the start of insurance is moved back by the credited days (Law No. 5510 art. 41; judgment affirmed by Court of Cassation 10th Civil Chamber, 25 March 2024, E. 2024/1231, K. 2024/3117: entry on 10 February 2006 moved to 9 February 2003 with 1,081 days). The calculator applies this according to the question in section 3.
